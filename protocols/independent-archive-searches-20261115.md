# Independent archival searches, 2026-11-15

The opening review of main at e67f02d, its results directory, the branch names, and the open-PR endpoint found no open PRs and no dedicated results on comet–comet plasma-tail geometry or the August 2026 Washington fireball in regional seismic data. The simultaneous microlensing, K2, pulsar, exoplanet, Cepheid and lunar seismic searches are outside this campaign.

* For the Washington fireball, use the public CNEOS time and location to predict an acoustic arrival window before reading the regional waveforms. Compare with off-time windows and require consistent travel times at separate sites before claiming a counterpart.
* For comet pairs, propagate distinct nuclei and check close approaches against JPL ephemerides, fragment families, the direction and speed of solar wind, and prior pair-specific reports. A projected alignment alone does not establish physical interaction.
* For independent high-energy transients, require a physically admissible light-travel delay, a measurement from a second detector, an explicit background comparison, and a check of existing identification lists before claiming a new association.

This checkpoint records questions and tests. Results and selection decisions belong in target-specific evidence packets.

## Outcomes

* [Sims Corner and interior Alaska](../results/sims-corner-seismoacoustic/README.md). The Washington satellite and seismic data support a low-altitude main pulse. The Alaska pressure and seismic records provide a second event with four co-located station pairs. The event-selection details and holdouts are in the evidence packet.
* [2024 XS16 entering Earth's shadow](../results/2024-xs16-earth-shadow/README.md). A separate close-approach search found a five-minute sequence of Sicilian photometry with the predicted direction of change and approximate amplitude. Two orbit solutions place totality after the measured ingress.
* [3I/ATLAS and 383P/Christensen](../results/3i-atlas-383p-radial-crossing/README.md). Three orbit calculations recover a common radial direction on different dates. The transport-speed and nearby wind checks leave physical transfer unverified.
* A possible wide companion to the high-mass binary MWC 656 was rejected as a novelty claim. The two Gaia source IDs already occur together in row 199491 of the Shariat et al. wide-binary catalog. Gaia already identifies the neighboring star's oscillation. No new companion or pulsation is claimed.
