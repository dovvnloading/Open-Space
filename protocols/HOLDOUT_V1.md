# Fixed predictions for a new SDSS sample

Can the strongest groups from the first 12 galaxies be detected with the same sign in galaxies from other fields and nights? This protocol freezes the analysis before inspecting any held-out spectra. The discovery product, exact detector and rest wavelengths are identified by digest. [Machine-readable predictions](holdout-v1.json)

**Primary family.** Take ranks 1–8 in the published rest-frame group table: the entire leading tier supported by at least six of the 12 discovery targets. Keep each group's published direction and median rest wavelength. Use Ca II K at 3934.7751 vacuum Å as a separate known absorption control. All nine predictions are reported, including failures. The primary question is reproducibility of narrow contrast, independent of catalog label.

**Sampling.** Select the first three eligible good BOSS main-program plate visits above 6497 ordered by plate and MJD. Require distinct plates and MJDs, plate centers at least 8° from the launch field and previously selected fields, and at least six galaxies satisfying the launch selection. Take the first eight eligible distinct sky targets in fiber order per field, or six or seven if fewer are available. Exclude target duplicates within 2 arcsec across discovery and holdout. Freeze the observation IDs from metadata before examining pixel values. Sky position and night differ across fields. All use the same survey, spectrograph family and reduction.

**Coverage.** Shift each prediction into the observed frame using that galaxy's measured systemic redshift. The nearest native pixel to the prediction must be a searchable center under the original detector, which requires twelve eligible pixels on either side and no gap in that window. One usable prediction/galaxy pair contributes once to the denominator. Record unsearchable pairs explicitly. Geometric coverage alone does not guarantee sensitivity to faint features.

**Primary readout.** Search the entire spectrum with the frozen detector, including its threshold of 6. For each covered prediction, count at most one feature of the specified sign within ±150 km/s using the logarithmic wavelength difference. Include any catalog classification and record the closest qualifying centroid, its score and source flags. Resolve exact ties by higher score then pixel index. Distinct targets and distinct plate/MJD visits with hits are counted. Call a wavelength *recurrent in this holdout* if it has hits in at least three different galaxies across at least two new visits. This is an operational threshold for follow-up, with no calibrated error rate.

**Controls and robustness.** Repeat the measurement at offsets of −1200 and +1200 km/s from each rest wavelength. Match with the same sign and tolerance and use separate coverage denominators. The shifted windows can contain real lines and are descriptive controls. Re-run the primary windows with all nonzero OR-mask pixels excluded. At each baseline hit, test whether an alternative continuum using sidebands 9–20 pixels away preserves the sign of the core contrast. Preserve incomplete alternative fits as unknown. These are secondary readouts and cannot change the primary definition. Leave signal identity, common astrophysical structure, correlated noise, and residual calibration effects open to further tests.

The redshift permutation control on the discovery sample and the same-target repeat-observation search address separate questions. Neither changes the frozen positions or the decision threshold.
