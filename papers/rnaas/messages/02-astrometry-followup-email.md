Dear Dr. [Name],

Your work on [OGLE-2011-BLG-0462 / OGLE-2024-BLG-0062] prompted me to write about a new candidate that may suit astrometric follow-up in 2027.

**The event.** OGLE-2026-BLG-0058 (also KMT-2026-BLG-0521) is at RA 17:52:38.09, Dec −31:47:36.1 (J2000; l = 358.33°, b = −2.77°). It peaked in June 2026 at I ≈ 17.25 from a baseline of I = 18.13.

**The fit to the public OGLE photometry:**
- tE = 277 (+35/−31) d and πE = 0.038 ± 0.006 for u0 > 0;
- tE = 273 (+33/−30) d and πE = 0.057 ± 0.009 for u0 < 0;
- source I ≈ 18.4–18.5;
- unmagnified light about 20–30% of the baseline, consistent with zero.

**The lens interpretation.** A Galactic model with stellar remnants favours the u0 > 0 geometry, which aligns with disc rotation. For it, the black-hole probability is 0.93–0.98, with a black-hole mass near 11 solar masses and θE ≈ 3.5 mas. Combining the geometries using that Galactic model gives 0.83–0.93; weighting the two equally gives 0.6–0.7. These are model-dependent estimates, and neither θE nor the mass has been measured.

**The astrometric signal and timing.** Evaluating the frozen annual-parallax chains gives median dates of 6 May 2027 (u0 > 0; 95% interval 1 March–21 July) and 11 April (u0 < 0; 21 February–18 June) for the unblended source-centroid maximum. For an assumed θE = 3.5 mas its maximum is 1.24 mas; unresolved blending changes the measured photocenter. Solar-elongation and GTVT checks support JWST windows around mid-March–early May and early August–late September. The packet documents a one-day date-alignment issue in GTVT 1.2 and preserves both its original output and a corrected-date audit. July is outside JWST's field of regard for this target. APT, guide-star and exposure-time checks are still needed; these are planning estimates. The source deflection remains large in the late-summer window.

I would welcome a feasibility assessment for several astrometric epochs spanning spring and late summer 2027, with later seasons to constrain the unlensed proper motion and parallax, plus two-color imaging to test the blend. The calculation and input hashes are in the follow-up packet linked below.

**Caveats:**
- The non-zero parallax rests partly on seven late-2026 points. The 2027 OGLE season tests it with a frozen forecast.
- The mass depends on the relative proper motion, which only astrometry can measure.
- The astrometric signal can remain substantial after the photometric event fades; a late-2028 image should not be assumed to be an unlensed baseline.
