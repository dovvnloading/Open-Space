Your work on [OGLE-2011-BLG-0462 / OGLE-2024-BLG-0062] prompted me to write about a new candidate that may suit astrometric follow-up in 2027.

**The event.** OGLE-2026-BLG-0058 (also KMT-2026-BLG-0521) is at RA 17:52:38.09, Dec −31:47:36.1 (J2000; l = 358.33°, b = −2.77°). It peaked on 2026 June 21 at I ≈ 17.25 from a baseline of I = 18.13.

**The fit to the public OGLE photometry:**
- tE = 277 (+35/−31) d and πE = 0.038 ± 0.006 for u0 > 0;
- tE = 273 (+33/−30) d and πE = 0.057 ± 0.009 for u0 < 0;
- source I ≈ 18.4–18.5;
- unmagnified light about 20–30% of the baseline, consistent with zero.

**The likely lens.** A Galactic model with stellar remnants favours the u0 > 0 geometry, which aligns with disc rotation. For it, the lens is a black hole of about 11 solar masses (probability 0.93–0.98), with θE ≈ 3.5 mas and a distance of about 4 kpc.

**The astrometric signal.** The centroid shift should be up to about 1 mas, largest around July 2027, roughly 1.36 tE after the peak.

**Caveats:**
- The non-zero parallax rests partly on seven late-2026 points. The 2027 OGLE season tests it with a frozen forecast.
- The mass depends on the relative proper motion, which only astrometry can measure.
