**Title:** OGLE-2026-BLG-0058: a candidate isolated stellar-mass black hole, with a photometric test in 2027 and an astrometric opportunity

---

M. Wesney (Open-Space project) reports:

The OGLE-IV Early Warning System event OGLE-2026-BLG-0058 (RA 17:52:38.09, Dec −31:47:36.1, J2000; also KMT-2026-BLG-0521) is a strong candidate for an isolated stellar-mass black-hole lens. It peaked on 2026 June 21 at I ≈ 17.25 from a baseline of I = 18.13.

I fitted a point-lens model with annual parallax to 1,900 public OGLE I-band points (2017–2026; Udalski et al. 2015, AcA 65, 1). The two solutions fit equally well:

- tE = 277 (+35/−31) d and πE = 0.038 ± 0.006 for u0 > 0;
- tE = 273 (+33/−30) d and πE = 0.057 ± 0.009 for u0 < 0.

The 95% upper limits on πE are 0.048 and 0.072. These imply 2.5 or 1.6 solar masses per mas/yr of relative proper motion, and the unmagnified light excludes a main-sequence lens of that mass. A Galactic model with stellar remnants gives a black hole of about 11 solar masses with probability 0.83–0.93.

The non-zero parallax rests partly on seven points after a July–September 2026 gap. Without parallax the source should be at I = 17.885 on 2027 February 15 and 17.950 on April 1. The two parallax solutions predict 17.934 and 17.945 on February 15 (95% ranges 17.917–17.950 and 17.924–17.966), and 17.985 and 17.995 on April 1. The difference falls to about 0.02 mag by June, so photometry early in the 2027 season tests it.

For θE ≈ 3.5 mas, the source centroid should shift by up to about 1 mas, largest around July 2027. Astrometric observations with HST, JWST or ground-based adaptive optics in 2027 could measure the lens mass directly. Follow-up is encouraged.

The analysis was carried out with AI research agents under the author's direction and checked with an independent re-analysis. Data, code, posteriors and the forecast:
