**To:** the OGLE team. The contact is listed on https://ogle.astrouw.edu.pl/ (Andrzej Udalski leads OGLE).
**Subject:** OGLE-2026-BLG-0058: planned Research Note on a candidate isolated black hole, based on EWS photometry

Dear Professor Udalski and the OGLE team,

I am writing as a courtesy before publishing a short analysis based on your public Early Warning System photometry of OGLE-2026-BLG-0058.

Fitting the 1,900 EWS I-band points from 2017–2026 with a point-lens model with annual parallax gives:

- an Einstein crossing time of about 275 days;
- a microlens parallax of 0.038 ± 0.006 for u0 > 0, or 0.057 ± 0.009 for u0 < 0, with 95% upper limits of 0.048 and 0.072.

Weighted with a Galactic model that includes stellar remnants, the lens is most likely a black hole of about 11 solar masses (probability 0.83–0.93).

The non-zero parallax rests partly on the seven points taken after the July–September 2026 gap. I therefore also give a frozen forecast for the 2027 season: I = 17.934 or 17.945 on 2027 February 15 with parallax, against 17.885 without.

I plan to submit this as a Research Note of the AAS. It credits the Early Warning System and cites Udalski, Szymański & Szymański (2015). The analysis, code and data are public:

- Result page: https://github.com/dovvnloading/Open-Space/blob/main/results/ogle-dark-lenses/README.md
- Deposited photometry and posteriors are in the same folder.

The analysis was carried out with AI research agents under my direction and checked with an independent re-analysis of the same photometry.

I would be grateful for any correction. If anything about the late-2026 photometry makes the parallax unreliable, please tell me. If you would prefer that I coordinate with your own work on this event, I am happy to wait before submitting.

Thank you for making the EWS photometry public.
