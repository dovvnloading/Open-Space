Dear Dr. [Name],

Your work on mid-infrared-selected tidal disruption events led me to write about two flares that may be worth a spectrum.

I searched NEOWISE photometry of the 43,916 well-sampled galaxies of the 2MASS Redshift Survey for nuclear mid-infrared flares. Seven flares were absent from the Transient Name Server and the published infrared-flare samples queried on 2026 November 14–15 (Masterson et al. 2024; Jiang et al. 2021; Necker et al. 2025). That is a dated search result, and priority checks need refreshing before submission. The search recovers all six Masterson et al. events in 2MRS galaxies that began after a baseline exists. Two stand out:

**UGC 2653 (MCG+06-08-012)**: 03:18:38.23 +37:36:29.0, z = 0.0231
- The flare began in 2022, with 760 K dust and a peak of 4.1 × 10⁴² erg/s.
- 73% of the 4.6 μm excess remained at the last NEOWISE visit in 2024; this is not a measurement of its current brightness.
- SPHEREx colours from 2025–26 show a +0.48 mag excess, larger than all 65 same-tile comparison galaxies. The residual is about nine times the comparison scatter, not a calibrated Gaussian false-alarm significance.
- VLASS gives 0.7–0.8 mJy/beam in 2019–2021 and 1.24 mJy/beam in 2024. The increase is tentative: including illustrative independent 10% epoch calibration errors lowers the statistical contrast from about 3.2 to 2.4 standard deviations. Matched-field calibration is still needed. SN 2007D, 6.3″ away, is undetected.

**CGCG 539-025 (IRAS 02237+4127)**: 02:26:53.16 +41:41:22.2, z = 0.0144
- Its outburst began between August 2016 and January 2017, and it was 7.1 times brighter at 4.6 μm by January 2018.
- A two-band blackbody interpretation gives 1,040 K dust, a peak of 1.4 × 10⁴³ erg/s and 1.5 × 10⁵¹ erg integrated through the 2024 NEOWISE record.
- No optical transient was reported, and the pre-flare W1 − W2 was 0.10 mag.

Optical spectra would test the tidal-disruption interpretation against an obscured interacting supernova or variable active nucleus. Coronal lines ([Fe VII] 6087 Å, [Fe X] 6375 Å, [Fe XIV] 5303 Å) and He II are useful diagnostics, but coronal emission also occurs in interacting supernovae such as SN 2005ip ([Smith et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1088/0004-637X/695/2/1334)). Classification needs line profiles, ratios and evolution together with source localization. Multi-frequency radio imaging and repeat epochs would test whether UGC 2653's apparent increase is a persistent new component.
