**Subject:** Two unreported mid-infrared nuclear flares for follow-up: UGC 2653 (still bright) and CGCG 539-025

Dear Dr. [Name],

Your work on mid-infrared-selected tidal disruption events led me to write about two flares that may be worth a spectrum.

I searched NEOWISE photometry of the 43,916 well-sampled galaxies of the 2MASS Redshift Survey for nuclear mid-infrared flares. Seven flares are absent from the Transient Name Server and from the published infrared-flare samples (Masterson et al. 2024; Jiang et al. 2021; Necker et al. 2025). The search recovers all six Masterson et al. events in 2MRS galaxies that began after a baseline exists, which gives me some confidence in it. Two stand out:

**UGC 2653 (MCG+06-08-012)**: 03:18:38.23 +37:36:29.0, z = 0.0231
- The flare began in 2022, with 760 K dust and a peak of 4.1 × 10⁴² erg/s.
- 73% of the 4.6 μm excess remained at the last NEOWISE visit.
- SPHEREx colours from 2025–26 still show the hot dust (+0.48 mag, about 9σ).
- In VLASS the nucleus brightened from 0.7–0.8 mJy/beam in 2019–2021 to 1.24 mJy/beam in 2024 (about 3σ). SN 2007D, 6.3″ away, is undetected.

**CGCG 539-025 (IRAS 02237+4127)**: 02:26:53.16 +41:41:22.2, z = 0.0144
- Its outburst began between August 2016 and January 2017, and it was 7.1 times brighter at 4.6 μm by January 2018.
- The excess has 1,040 K dust, a peak of 1.4 × 10⁴³ erg/s and at least 1.5 × 10⁵¹ erg so far.
- No optical transient was reported, and the pre-flare W1 − W2 was 0.10 mag.

An optical spectrum would test the tidal-disruption interpretation against an obscured supernova or a waking active nucleus. The coronal lines ([Fe VII] 6087 Å, [Fe X] 6375 Å, [Fe XIV] 5303 Å) and He II are the obvious diagnostics. Multi-frequency radio imaging of UGC 2653 would test whether its 2024 brightening is a new outflow.
