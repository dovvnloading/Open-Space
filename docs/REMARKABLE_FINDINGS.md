# Remarkable findings

This log keeps results that would matter beyond this repository from getting lost: discovery-level candidates and alarming results. An entry needs a claim that survived the result's own validation, an explicit evidence level and a link to the full record. Routine measurements, controls and null results stay in their result folders. A few that are worth flagging but fall below this bar are listed under "Notable measurements".

## Candidates

**DESI DR1, Gaia DR3 1717410430709663616 — evidence level: spectroscopically supported candidate, single epoch (binary motion untested).** Five stellar absorption lines support a corrected heliocentric RV of +501.74 ± 4.65 km/s. The inferred Galactocentric speed is 697 km/s (691–703 km/s) on the reference distance scale: all 4,096 draws exceed escape speed in McMillan 2017 and Gala 2022, but a published higher-mass halo binds all 4,096 reference draws. The 256-draw backward orbit ensemble crosses the disk 7.40 kpc from the Galactic centre about 10.9 Myr ago, with no reference orbit approaching within 1 kpc of Sgr A* or 5 kpc of the moving LMC centre. **No Sgr A* or LMC-centre ejection is established**, and neither a >1,000 km/s black-hole ejection nor an LMC black-hole mass constraint was found. A second independent RV epoch is essential to test the systemic velocity; discovery priority remains open after a finite 21-source literature comparison. ## Notable measurements

**Perdita crossed the nominal known-moon masks in independent JWST observations in 2025 and 2026.** The evidence combines multi-epoch native-detector astrometry, a subsequent fixed-track check and an independent identification in the discovery team’s labeled image. The source was a median 0.50 arcsec from JPL URA184 in February 2025 and 1.17 arcsec away in October 2025. A new blind search in two October 2026 visits recovered a source at Perdita’s radius about 1.9 arcsec from nominal, and a subsequent same-day visit found a corresponding source. The 2026 source is consistent with Perdita but is not uniquely identified by those data; the deposited record does not establish a prospective freeze. The median projected longitude residual reached −19.7° across four 2026 exposures. Earlier 2023–24 images give residuals of order one degree. The 2025-only 387-cycle orbit misses the 2024 position by 14.24 pixels RMS, so the six-epoch history is descriptive rather than a validated global ephemeris. The observing team previously reported a qualitative discrepancy. These measurements quantify the offset and show how an ordinary catalog mask can admit a known moon through a multi-visit discovery search. [Astrometry, identity, independent tests and limits](../results/ice-giant-moon-search/README.md) · [Prior report](https://scixplorer.org/abs/2025AGUFMP41G.2685H/abstract)
