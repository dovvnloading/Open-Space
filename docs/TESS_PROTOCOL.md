# Two-sector short-period transit screen, version 1

Before searching the pilot light curves, fix the sample to the first 24 TICs in the SHA-256 ordering of the joint Sector 1/2 two-minute SPOC inventories, with the seed `open-space-tess-v1`. Use Sector 1 for the search and Sector 2 for a prediction at fixed epoch, period and duration. Separately run the same algorithm on WASP-18 b in Sectors 2 and 3 as a positive control. Keep every target, including quiet, flagged and inconclusive records. The parent population comprises selected two-minute TESS targets observed in both sectors.

- Use finite TDB times, positive PDCSAP flux uncertainties, finite PDCSAP fluxes and QUALITY = 0. Preserve time gaps. Bin at 10 minutes; require three good cadences. Split at gaps longer than 0.35 days and remove slow variations with interpolated local medians. Do not excise transit-like flux points. A segment too short to estimate a trend is unsearchable.
- Search 0.75–8 days in the first sector with weighted Box Least Squares, three box durations (1, 2 and 4 hours) and a grid fine enough to keep cumulative phase drift below about half the shortest duration over the sector. Preserve the best peak as well as alternatives. Fit a fixed-ephemeris depth in the second sector; do not scan phase there. Compare same- and half-phase depths, odd and even depths, and individual event depths. Assess phase coverage and magnitude of single-event influence.
- Select a *two-sector periodic dip* only if discovery depth/error is at least 7, fixed validation depth/error is at least 5, and at least three cycles in each sector have majority event coverage and a local depth/error of at least 2. Flag a deep secondary (at least five error units and greater than 20% of the corresponding primary depth), an odd/even difference above four error units, depth above 5%, or a validation/discovery depth ratio outside 0.4–2.5 for astrophysical or artifact review. Rank uncatalogued objects for further investigation. This is a selection rule for follow-up, without an empirical planet probability.
- Use an inverted-flux screen per target under identical settings as an upwards-event diagnostic, plus seeded 1.7, 3.1 and 5.3 day synthetic transit sequences added to a real exploratory star's raw flux before detrending and validation. These controls test sensitivity on measured backgrounds. The 24-target sample cannot establish survey-scale reliability. Stellar variability, eclipsing binaries, aperture blends, imperfect detrending and unmodeled ephemeris changes can produce or suppress hits.
