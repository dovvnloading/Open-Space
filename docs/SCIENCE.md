# Research protocol for unidentified optical spectral features

Open-Space asks whether a feature in a public astronomical spectrum can survive independent observation, instrumental checks and a documented search for known explanations. A useful result can be a rediscovered transition, a calibration artifact, a measured sensitivity limit or a short list for follow-up. The project's first tractable target is a narrow optical emission or absorption feature. The launch detector ranks deviations from a local continuum. The protocol below specifies how a research team can qualify that detector for a population survey and investigate its output.

A wavelength alone does not determine atomic number. Discovery of a chemical element requires experimental evidence for a nuclide with a previously unidentified atomic number. Identifying even a known ion in a new environment requires a defensible assignment of its transitions. Reserve the description *unassigned in the references searched* for candidates accompanied by those search records. [IUPAC/IUPAP discovery report, §6.1](https://doi.org/10.1515/pac-2020-2926)

## Start with one archive and one question

For a first population study, select SDSS DR17 narrow-line galaxies with reliable systemic redshifts in the range 0.02–0.10. Ask how often a ranked feature recurs at the same rest wavelength in independent data. This redshift interval puts Hβ and both bright [O III] lines within the optical passband and moves source lines relative to terrestrial emission. Stratify by redshift, continuum signal-to-noise and wavelength coverage. Record the catalog, query, reduction, exclusion counts and the list of observations before tuning. The pilot coadd comes from plate 6497, MJD 56329, fiber 0139. SDSS classifies it as a starburst galaxy at z = 0.0856685 with ZWARNING = 0. That puts the stronger [O III] component at about 5437.3 Å on its observed grid. This prediction is a positive control for the search.

Use the full spectrum products when individual exposures matter. The full and lite versions of a spectrum use the same coadd and count as one observation. Record the release, `RUN2D`, plate, MJD, fiber, sky position, target identity, parent exposure IDs, file origin and digest. Keep the input bytes or a durable route to the identical bytes. [SDSS download guide](https://www.sdss4.org/dr17/data_access/bulk/) · [Full example spectrum](https://data.sdss.org/sas/dr17/eboss/spectro/redux/v5_13_2/spectra/full/6497/spec-6497-56329-0139.fits)

SDSS supplies heliocentric vacuum wavelengths. Convert `loglam` with `10**loglam` to obtain Å. For a systemic redshift `z` in the same velocity convention, compute `lambda_rest = lambda_observed / (1 + z)`. Record redshift uncertainty and the spectral resolution at the candidate. Keep native wavelength sampling, calibrated flux, inverse variance and quality bits. A zero inverse variance excludes a pixel. The OR mask records a problem in at least one contributing exposure and the AND mask records problems common to every exposure. State which masks the analysis used. Record gaps and the wavelength range that remains searchable after masking. [SDSS wavelength convention and units](https://www.sdss4.org/dr17/spectro/spectro_basics/) · [Mask definitions](https://www.sdss4.org/dr17/spectro/quality/)

## Qualify the search before increasing its scale

This is a proposed blind evaluation. The demonstration pipeline and its synthetic fixture exercise local detection and matching. The calibration command also measures artificial-line recovery on a real spectral background. A survey performance claim requires the following measurements on held-out observations. [Real-background calibration results](CALIBRATION.md)

| Experiment | Design | Quantity to report |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Known-line recovery | Use an independently labeled set of galaxies with measurable Hβ and [O III]. Reserve labels until after the blind peak search. The DR17 reference wavelengths in vacuum Å are 4862.683, 4960.295 and 5008.239. | Recovery fraction at a prespecified centroid tolerance, coverage denominator, velocity error and failures by signal-to-noise. |
| Blind injection | Add artificial lines of both signs at random unmasked wavelengths to real spectra. Sample width from the measured line-spread function and vary integrated flux. Include edge, blend and sky-adjacent strata. Use disjoint targets and plates for tuning and evaluation. | Recovery versus injected flux, width and wavelength with binomial intervals. Report missing coverage and positional bias. Keep simulated injections out of the observational candidate register. |
| Null search | Run the identical search, including all positions and template widths, on blank-sky controls and independently constructed exposure differences with propagated variance. Match wavelength coverage and noise. Inspect each control type separately. | Fraction of control spectra yielding at least one candidate above threshold. Report counts, uncertainty and clustering by night and plate. Correlated noise makes a pixel score unsuitable as a survey false-alarm probability. |
| Independent repetition | Freeze a candidate's coordinate, rest wavelength, expected sign, width and tolerance before inspecting a second observation. Match the object by sky position and stable ID. Check the sets of exposure IDs for overlap. | Whether the line recurs, the sensitivity to the predicted flux and the estimated random-coincidence rate. Record an inconclusive result when the repeat lacks sensitivity. |

As a concrete initial operating target, select a ranking threshold using development controls, then test it against a predeclared false-alarm budget of 1% of eligible null spectra. A holdout with zero triggers in 300 statistically independent null spectra has a one-sided 95% binomial upper bound of `1 - 0.05**(1/300)`, about 0.994%. Shared observing conditions can reduce the effective sample size. Report plate/night-resampled bounds and additional controls if the independence assumption fails. An all-null alarm rate alone does not establish the fraction of false entries in a candidate list from the sky. Freeze the tested threshold, survey size and code revision. A change in masking, width grid, search region or threshold needs a fresh holdout.

## Investigate an unassigned feature

1. **Inspect the pixels.** Plot calibrated flux, uncertainty, signed residual, masks, sky spectrum and line-spread function in the detection window. Refit with a different continuum and wider sidebands. Check whether the centroid, sign and width are stable. Report integrated flux with uncertainty and the local detection limit. A three-pixel score is a ranking measurement.
2. **Try an instrumental explanation.** Inspect individual exposures and neighboring fibers on the same plate. Test for residuals at fixed terrestrial wavelength, detector defects, cosmic rays, camera overlap and flux-calibration structure. Use the observation's velocity correction when mapping sky lines into the product's grid. Bright night-sky lines, the red OH forest and correlated errors between coadd bins are documented SDSS failure modes. Record the check even when it removes a candidate. [SDSS spectral caveats](https://www.sdss4.org/dr17/spectro/caveats/)
3. **Try a known carrier.** Search the appropriate ions in NIST ASD using a consistent wavelength medium. Preserve observed versus Ritz (energy-level-derived) values, uncertainties, database version, query and references. Evaluate neighboring lines, plausible velocity components, common blends, molecules and foreground absorption. Predict companion transitions within the instrument's coverage and evaluate their presence or upper limits. The small bundled line table is a first-pass screen. [NIST ASD line search documentation](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/ASD/Html/lineshelp.html)
4. **Search prior work.** Search object aliases, coordinates, ion names, both air and vacuum wavelength conventions, and rounded wavelengths across the uncertainty interval. Record the database, query text, date, results and relevant bibcodes or DOIs. Read the relevant spectra, tables or figure captions. Record unsearched resources explicitly. [NASA ADS search guidance](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/help/search/)
5. **Seek independent evidence.** Prefer a different telescope or spectrograph. Another night with disjoint exposures can rule out a transient sensor defect while leaving shared calibration errors plausible. Reprocessing the same exposure tests method sensitivity. An independent astrophysical explanation should predict another detectable feature or a specific observation that can distinguish it from its competitors.

## Evidence handed to a reviewer

A candidate report should identify the object and retained exposures, wavelength medium and velocity frame, observing and rest wavelengths with uncertainties, sign, width, flux, masks, local sensitivity and search configuration. Attach the source files or their verified hashes and locations, the command and code revision, the control results, the catalog and literature searches, competing explanations and the cheapest observation that could resolve them. State which evidence is absent. A second agent's agreement on the same input supplies an interpretation check. Independent exposures supply a new measurement.

Keep rejected and inconclusive cases, with reasons, alongside promising ones. They let a future agent recognize a recurring skyline or test whether a model revision actually improves sensitivity. For presentations or publications using SDSS data, follow the survey's credit instructions and cite the release used. [SDSS data releases and citation guidance](https://www.sdss4.org/dr17/)
