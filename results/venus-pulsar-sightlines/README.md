# Venus crosses two radio-pulsar sightlines

**Using fixed catalogue coordinates, Venus is predicted to occult the direction of PSR J1944−1750 on 2054 March 2 from both MeerKAT in South Africa and the GMRT in India.** **The pulsar's proper motion is unmeasured**, so the actual future occultation remains conditional on the source's position in 2054. The two sites share at least 84 seconds of nominal solid-disk coverage even when taking the intersection over three source positions and two planetary ephemerides. A second, much nearer event on **2027 February 6** puts PSR J1813−2113 behind Venus at the South Pole and McMurdo for all three published position estimates tested. Whether the 2027 shadow also reaches radio dishes in Argentina and New Zealand depends on a roughly ten-arcsecond disagreement between timing solutions. These are conditional geometric predictions, not confirmed occultations or guarantees of radio detectability. No pulse disappearance has been observed in this work.

## 2054 March 2 · PSR J1944−1750 (B1941−17)

This 0.841-second pulsar provides a periodic background signal. The ATNF timing position, an ASKAP RACS-low2 unresolved component (1.031 ± 0.175 mJy/beam), and a centroid test against a 150 MHz GMRT TGSS image all give visible chords at MeerKAT and GMRT. The ASKAP and TGSS positions differ by about 4.6 arcseconds. The TGSS peak close to the timing position is only about five local image RMS. Its centroid is sensitive to beam shape and background, so it is included as a stress test. Pulsations establish the catalogued radio source. A continuum image alone does not identify a pulsar.

| Site | Nominal UTC interval with ASKAP position and JPL DE441 | Minimum–maximum altitude over 04:00–04:32 UTC |
|---|---|---|
| MeerKAT | 04:06:45–04:16:47 | 38.5°–45.4° |
| GMRT | 04:10:57–04:22:03 | 52.8°–53.2° |

The common interval across both sites, all three source positions and both ephemerides is **04:12:16–04:13:40 UTC**. Taking the ASKAP position alone gives 5 min 50 s of joint coverage with DE441. Parkes is a grazing alternative whose classification changes with the radio position. FAST has a geometric chord but the source lies outside its tracking range at this declination from its latitude. No observation or facility operation in 2054 is assumed. ATNF v2.9.0 reports no measured proper motion for this pulsar. The fixed-position table therefore describes a conditional forecast, not a formal probability of occultation. Starting at the 2022 ASKAP component, a 32-year propagation at 100 mas/yr retains both chords with at least 300.5 seconds of overlap in all 72 sampled position angles. At 150 mas/yr the minimum is 242.1 seconds. At 200 mas/yr two of the 72 directions lose joint coverage. Those direction counts describe the grid, not a prior on the velocity. For the nominal ASKAP point, the solid limb clears the closest ray by 6.36 arcseconds at MeerKAT and 8.46 arcseconds at GMRT. ASKAP quotes component coordinate errors of about 0.8 arcsecond per axis. A new radio position closer to 2054 would tighten the forecast. Future rotation of the Earth introduces a separate uncertainty. Miriade and Horizons place the 2054 contacts about 26 seconds apart, so sub-minute UTC agreement should not be assumed. These alternatives are a sensitivity test, not a formal probability interval.

## 2027 February 6 · PSR J1813−2113

The Parkes discovery table gives −21°13′00″ (7″ formal error). Jayacumaar's 2023 MeerKAT thesis gives −21°13′00.4″, and the peer-reviewed 2024 MeerKAT timing release gives −21°13′10.227″ (2.816″ formal error). The last two fits use overlapping measurements. This low-ecliptic-latitude object has a poorly constrained timing declination. The table gives unrefracted solid-body geometry for each point estimate. At the IAR the Sun–Venus separation is about 44°.

ASKAP RACS images supply an independent check. At the newer timing position the 887.5 and 1367.5 MHz images give 0.765 and 0.402 mJy/beam (2.98 and 2.33 times robust local RMS). The thesis position gives 0.213 and 0.078. Only 0.135% of uniformly sampled comparison positions on the same image tile exceed both newer-position intensities. A paired-offset test comparing points separated by exactly the same vector gives 1.77% with a larger summed normalized difference. The controls preserve real sources and noise shared between bands. These are correlated sky-area fractions, not independent trials or a blind-discovery false-alarm probability. The brightnesses favor the 2024 solution without precisely locating the pulsar. The frequencies have different synthesized beams, and unresolved confusing sources could affect either brightness. None of four checked ASKAP catalogues contained a formal entry within 72 arcseconds. Continental-site predictions remain conditional. The Antarctic point predictions agree across the fitted positions, although the full astrometric uncertainty allows other paths.

## Geometry and priority

The discovery screen compared geocentric JPL astrometric paths of Mercury through Neptune (excluding Earth) for 2025–2055 with all 4,503 ATNF v2.9.0 positions. A two-day ephemeris interpolated to six-hour spacing selected close candidates for topocentric refinement. It uses nearest neighbours and fixed nominal positions, and is incomplete for fast inner planets, crowded sightlines and parallax-assisted near misses. The reported events were individually recomputed at one-minute cadence, with independent JPL DE441 and IMCCE INPOP19 topocentric outputs. Ranges and a Venus radius of 6,051.8 km set the IMCCE angular radius. The calculation compares astrometric ICRF directions; differential gravitational bending, atmospheric refraction, finite radio opacity, terrestrial horizon masks, and telescope signal-to-noise are outside the contact model. The angular guard against a miss is the apparent radius minus the minimum separation. Contacts identify Venus's solid limb; atmospheric effects can alter received pulses outside it.

An exact-name search (both J designations, B1941−17, Venus and event years, November 2026) located no earlier event-specific prediction. The 1988 Venus occultation of P 0507+17 was observed at 2.3 GHz and showed ionospheric refraction. The present calculation concerns different background sources. This search does not establish publication priority.

## Reproduce

Run `python replay.py > replay.csv` and compare with `contacts.csv` (Python 3, NumPy, SciPy). Every site, input direction, minute of both ephemerides, minimum distance and tested position is retained. Fractions of a second describe the interpolation, not accuracy. Run `python archive_positions.py` with Astropy, SciPy and NumPy to download the three hash-verified map products, replay the paired sky controls, and fit the TGSS centroid. Run `python motion_test.py` for the 32-year transverse-motion test in `motion.csv`. The second ASKAP catalogue epoch and its quoted coordinate and uncertainties are in `ASKAP_J1944m1750.csv`; its corrected declination was used. `image_products.csv` identifies the exact archive tiles. `archive_queries.py` records the retrieval parameters for the ephemerides and the catalogue query.
