# Early-morning Arsia Mons brightening in a repeat Odyssey strip

This plan was frozen on 18 November 2026 against ef9a7e19 before opening imagery arrays. It tests whether the newly archived 18 January 2026 THEMIS strip contains a spatially broad brightening on the same ground as a 6 November 2025 visit. Both are in Mars Year 38 and precede the main AMEC season documented by earlier observers. The unit is a common ground cell in two observed detector channels. A shared two-band brightening counts as a candidate for inspection. An onset date, a water-ice identification and a comparison with the entire cloud head require different evidence. [Release 97](https://pds.nasa.gov/datasearch/subscription-service/SS-Release.shtml) · [Earlier AMEC observations](https://doi.org/10.1029/2020JE006517) · [2026 microphysics study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41561-026-02089-9)

- Primary earlier exposure VA5997004, 6 November 2025, Ls 167.050°, image-center local solar time 06:51:21.6. Primary later exposure VA6883007, 18 January 2026, Ls 209.025°, 06:49:26.4. Use project-corrected VIS-EQR 0.42 and 0.75 µm planes, their XML labels and exact science bytes. The archive's single-band ALB products select different source filters for these two visits. Do not compare their albedo amplitudes. The independent adjacent ground-pair control is VA5423003 on 19 September and VA6596006 on 25 December, also before the usual season. This control differs in illumination and is not assumed cloud-free.
- Use the XML coordinate encoding, dimensions, byte offsets and wavelength centers. Verify EQR's array organization with its embedded ISIS3 header plus spatial metadata. Reproject equal-cell footprints into one shared 720 m grid in the native equirectangular map using positive finite samples. Require at least 70% of the nominal native sample count in both channels and dates per cell. Use only the geographic area covered by both projected visits. Longitude is east-positive and latitude planetocentric. Confirm that valid-map extents are consistent with the ODE footprints. Metadata screening includes all six broad-box February/early-season release-97 candidates and the wider westward source query.
- Use a common latitude search interval of −10° to −8.5°. Use shoulder intervals of −10.55° to −10.05° and −8.45° to −7.95° as foreground controls. Take the log of the later-to-earlier ratio per common cell in each band. On each shoulder, compute the median log ratio and median latitude. Interpolate a linear baseline between those medians, remove it and convert the residual to a fractional change. The alternating baseline is the constant median of both shoulders. Require at least 30 common cells in each shoulder for both methods.
- Bin the mapped residuals into consecutive 0.05° latitude blocks. Use medians over at least ten common cells per block. A candidate requires both bands to exceed +10% in at least six consecutive search blocks, above the largest positive median among qualifying shoulder blocks in the respective band. Repeat the screen with the constant baseline, a 360 m grid, and a 1,440 m grid. Support on the primary and all three sensitivity configurations is a replication requirement within the same photography. Shift the later image by ±1 primary grid cell east, west, north and south as a geolocation sensitivity. Keep their per-run coverage and outcomes.
- Test sensitivity by injecting a circular Gaussian brightening in the later mapped array at latitude −9.25°. Set the center x from the common covered cell nearest that latitude and the median x of valid cells within 0.075°. The full width at half maximum is 40 km, with equal peak multipliers of 5%, 10%, 20% and 40% in both bands. Preserve the actual sampling, noise and earlier image. Use the same screening gate separately for each injection. This tests those synthetic amplitudes and morphology only. Injected patches are not independent scenes.
- Interpret all contrast measurements as relative structure in projected products. The archive applies cosmetic framelet adjustments that can alter local radiance. Differences in solar angle, surface relief and atmospheric scattering can produce color-correlated contrasts. Treat a favorable result as unresolved until it survives a calibrated, independently observed or dynamically reconstructed counterfactual. MARCI's afternoon glimpses cannot falsify a morning-only cloud on the same Earth date. A newer independent morning observation has not been established.
