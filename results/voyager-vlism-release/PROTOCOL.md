# Voyager v2 archive time and magnetic-field checks

Frozen on 18 November 2026 at repository base 764748b3. Timestamp-only preflight found differences between the public Voyager v2 catalog pages and the native XML. It also established the number and dates of late quasi-thermal-noise selections, without opening density values or magnetic fields. This experiment tests catalog time behavior and the transfer of a simple fixed density-to-field relation to the newest overlapping Voyager 1 observations. [September 2026 PDS release](https://pds.nasa.gov/datasearch/subscription-service/SS-20260930.shtml) · [PWS dataset paper](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4365/ae10bd)

## Catalog test

- Compare UTC start/end across exact v2.0 bundle and data-collection source labels, NASA PDS search API JSON, and the public ds-view response. Compare both Voyager product labels to UTC extrema and record counts re-read from byte-verified CSVs. Verify the inventory includes both product LIDVIDs. Recompute the number of records outside each literal inclusive time range, identifying the affected spacecraft and epochs. This measures what a client using the displayed range would exclude. It does not assume the search API applies those displayed values.
- Hold out three unrelated-by-product public bundle controls chosen before inspecting their public page times. These are the September release counterparts Voyager 1 interplanetary ephemeris and Psyche SPICE, and the currently returned MAVEN calibrated SEP bundle. For each, pin matching versions across XML, JSON and HTML. Report any version mismatch as insufficient for using that control. Compare each end against ordinary 24-hour UTC and the date-preserving 12-hour rendering without a meridiem marker. Record agreements and differences at both ends, including midnight controls. Leave mechanism and prevalence unassigned beyond the checked interfaces.
- Check that the published electron densities reproduce from reported plasma frequency using n = (fpe / 8980 Hz)^2 in particles per cubic centimetre. Preserve the narrowband QTN and plasma-oscillation EPO selections separately. Compare at reported precision and report numerical departures before making any calibration claims. This is an internal arithmetic test.

## Independent-instrument test

- For Voyager 1, use PWS QTN rows with finite positive density and valid published bounds, and direct definitive VIM F1 from the three public 48-second MAG files covering parts of 2023, 2024 and 2025. Use each MAG interval midpoint, finite positive F1 less than 10 nT, and no inferred gap coverage. Use a symmetric ±12 h window around each PWS UTC epoch with at least 450 valid 48 s records. Report ±6 h with at least 225 records as a sensitivity. Preserve actual matched record counts and gaps. Collapse repeats on each UTC day to one median density and one median field per valid day. Retain EPO coverage separately, without including EPO in the fit. Voyager 2 has too few contemporaneous samples for this fit.
- Estimate a single fixed proportionality coefficient as median density divided by field on matched 2023 days. Fit a stationary density baseline as the median density in the same sample. Use 2024 as a descriptive comparison and reserve January through April 2025 QTN for scoring both predictions. Score absolute log errors, the number of predictions inside the reported density bounds, and the number of distinct occupied 14-day blocks. Require at least four held-out matched days in at least three occupied blocks for an informative comparison. Require a simultaneous-fit median error at least 20% below the stationary baseline and lower error than all shared-coverage shifted controls for model support.
- Compare the fixed fit with MAG shifted by −14, −7, +7 and +14 days. Score these offsets on their intersections with the simultaneous covered sample and require at least four common days and three blocks per comparison. Make no p-value claim. The dates may share physical structure and are selected by wave detectability. For rows with an embedded valid Bmag value, report absolute error against direct MAG at the native SCET and at ±12 h, on a shared-coverage subset. This is an archive consistency control, since both can derive from the same physical MAG observation.
- The known 2020 density and magnetic compression motivates a fixed-ratio comparison. Its transfer to later samples is a separate question. Prior work already describes the field through mid-2025 and the earlier compression. Report departures as descriptive measurements for observed windows. The test cannot identify a new shock or distinguish spatial from temporal gradients.
