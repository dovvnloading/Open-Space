# Independent 144 MHz detection along the WISE J0458+6434AB track

The LOFAR Two-metre Sky Survey DR3 catalog lists **ILTJ045854.31+643455.8** at ICRS (74.72630277°, +64.58218255°). The 144 MHz Stokes I peak is **0.903 ± 0.086 mJy/beam**, with **1.167 ± 0.178 mJy** integrated and local island noise of 0.082 mJy/beam. This is a previously catalogued radio source. The proposed association with the moving brown-dwarf binary is the finding under investigation. Name and coordinate searches did not identify an earlier report of that association. [LoTSS DR3](https://lofar-surveys.org/dr3.html) · [Catalog column definitions](https://vo.astron.nl/__system__/dc_tables/show/tableinfo/lotss_dr3.main_sources) · [Survey paper](https://www.oa.uj.edu.pl/press_release/LoTSS_DR3/LoTSS_DR3_AAaccepted.pdf)

A direct measurement in the primary 6-arcsec HP-151 mosaic gives **0.964 mJy/beam** at the published LOFAR catalog coordinate, against a matched-control spread of **0.099 mJy/beam**. A free circular-beam fit returns (74.72628444°, +64.58214196°), 0.15 arcsec from the catalog fit. The centroid is **0.48 arcsec from the binary's predicted position** at the full mosaic metadata date of 2023-01-26. The source catalog reports positional errors of 0.33 arcsec east and 0.24 arcsec north. These omit the binary photocenter orbital uncertainty. The 2023 VLASS radio centroid and the LOFAR catalog position agree to approximately 0.22 arcsec.

The full mosaic metadata include 28 observation IDs between 2017-08-09 and 2023-09-04. All five constituent pointings with centers within 3 degrees of the target date to December 2022–August 2023. The two nearest are P072+64 (1.08°, 2023-07-14) and P078+64 (1.60°, 2022-12-29). Their individual visibility weights at this sky position are unknown. The metadata date is an approximate reference epoch for the mosaic, and radio fluxes at different facilities were measured at different times. [Mosaic metadata](mosaic-coverage.csv) · [Nearby pointing metadata](pointings-within-3deg.csv)

The fitted LOFAR ellipse is 7.63 × 6.09 arcsec with a 6-arcsec restoring beam. The catalog lists deconvolved axes of 4.71 ± 0.80 and 1.06 ± 0.53 arcsec. A single mosaic cannot resolve the approximately 3-arcsec VLASS end-to-end motion. At positions propagated to 2017, January 2023 and September 2025 it returns 0.913, 0.948 and 0.863 mJy/beam. Most of the evidence for motion is in the time-separated VLASS data. LOFAR establishes independent low-frequency emission on the sightline. Blending, an unrelated background source, intrinsic variability and the multi-year mosaic remain relevant to source association, morphology and any interpretation of the non-simultaneous spectrum.

Both legacy cutouts have finite image data at the test position. Neither provides a significant detection. The LoTSS DR2 cutout service returned “No suitable mosaic found.” SkyView returned a no-data product for FIRST. Those requests supply no flux constraint.

The inputs and their SHA-256 hashes are in [sources.csv](sources.csv). Retrieve the DR3 cutout using the [official API](https://www.lofar-surveys.org/cutout_api_details.html). The [catalog snapshot](lotss-source-cone.csv) comes from a 0.0139° cone around (74.7262°, +64.5822°). The second record is the binary's proposed counterpart. The other record lies approximately 37 arcsec away. The catalog, the cutout and the independent circular-beam fit share LOFAR measurements.

Run `python verify.py` in this directory with NumPy, SciPy and Astropy to reproduce [the measurements](independent-measurements.csv). Values use fixed restoring-beam fits with a local constant and slopes. The control scatter is 1.4826 times the MAD of 76 off-target fits. It is a local noise estimate and excludes any survey-wide flux calibration error. ## Dated pointing and circular-polarization check

Two public LOFAR pointings yielded both total intensity (Stokes I) and circular polarization (Stokes V) at fixed, epoch-propagated coordinates. The P072+64 pointing on **2023-07-14** gives **+0.43 mJy/beam** in restored Stokes I, against a 0.15 mJy/beam local robust scale. Its primary-beam-corrected dirty-image pair gives I = +0.48 (0.14) and V = −0.31 (0.10) mJy/beam. The V control standard deviation is 0.13 mJy/beam, and one of 58 field controls is more negative. The P072+67 observation on **2022-12-28** gives I = +0.31 (0.31) and V = −0.05 (0.21) mJy/beam. Numbers in parentheses are 1.4826 times the MAD among 58 offset apertures. The Stokes V hint is marginal, sensitive to the choice of scatter statistic, and lacks corroboration. The data do not establish circular polarization or an emission mechanism.

The paired dirty images have a FITS Stokes axis containing I (code 1) and V (code 4). Each archived file in [the manifest](single-pointing-products.csv) preserves the correct plane separately with its WCS. The 61 × 61 pixel patches were extracted from uncompressed SURF tar members using validated HTTP byte ranges. The first FITS header begins at the recorded archive byte offset. The center is the nearest pixel to the LOFAR catalog position, with 30 pixels on each side. `pointing_check.py` verifies the crop hashes and plane labels and evaluates a 6-arcsec circular template. Restored I provides the beam reference. The dirty V response may differ. All products from one observation use shared visibilities.
