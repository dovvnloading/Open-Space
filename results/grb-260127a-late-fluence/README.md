# Late soft-X-ray fluence of GRB 260127A

In two Swift/XRT visits around 3.0 and 4.5 hours after this burst, the absorption-corrected 0.3–10 keV fluence accumulated during **3,605.5 s of exposure** was **1.551 × 10⁻⁶ erg cm⁻²**. This equals **0.674** times the prompt 15–150 keV Swift/BAT fluence of 2.3 × 10⁻⁶ erg cm⁻². Using the independent Fermi/GBM prompt measurement of 3.8 × 10⁻⁶ erg cm⁻² over 10–1000 keV gives **0.408**. Both ratios compare different observer-frame bands. The X-ray numerator includes the underlying afterglow at those times. **Comparison sample.** The frozen archive intersection contains 355 target IDs for 354 named bursts, and 310 IDs supplied both PC products. Of the 228 bursts with at least one complete positive-flux bin at 10–40 ks, GRB 260127A ranks first with **0.679**. GRB 220706A is second with **0.204**. The observed fluence ratios differ by a factor of 3.32. Exposure accounts for much of that separation. The two bursts had 5,208 s and 1,790 s respectively, and dividing each ratio by its exposure reduces their lead ratio to 1.14. They still rank first and second in this window. The highest accumulated ratio also belongs to GRB 260127A in the 5–30, 10–20 and 10–100 ks windows (243, 209 and 235 bursts). In the 10–100 ks window GRB 220706A leads on the exposure-normalized measure among bursts with at least 1,000 s of exposure.

**Physical limits.** The bright visits occupy only 3.61 ks of the 10–20 ks window. Sampling between spacecraft orbits leaves the rise, peak time, and uninterrupted energy release undetermined. No integration crosses these gaps. The XRT Burst Analyser interpolates a count-to-flux conversion from hardness and assumes an absorption column and a spectral model. Using the constant *observed* conversion 3.99 × 10⁻¹¹ erg cm⁻² per count from the ordinary XRT light-curve page gives a two-visit ratio of **0.518** to BAT. The numerator convention and the spectral treatment both differ in that check. BAT reports a 90% uncertainty of ±0.2 × 10⁻⁶, which alone spans ratios of 0.62–0.74 around 0.674. Pile-up corrections, absorption, correlated spectral-conversion errors, and incomplete temporal coverage limit a physical energy inference. No redshift is listed. [Burst Analyser methodology](https://www.swift.ac.uk/burst_analyser/docs.php) · [XRT count conversion](https://www.swift.ac.uk/xrt_curves/01444088/) · [Swift burst table](https://swift.gsfc.nasa.gov/archive/grb_table/260127A)

**Event-list check.** The raw cleaned photon-counting data retain 262, 2,292 and 1,304 counts in a 10–30 pixel annulus around the published XRT position during the first visit, first bright visit and second bright visit. The time columns in the event CSV subtract the FITS TRIGTIME. The UKSSDC light curves use a time origin about 32 s earlier for this burst. The visits correspond by exposure and chronological order. The corresponding 60–100 pixel annulus has 92, 332 and 170 counts. Scaling the comparison annulus by the geometric area ratio of 0.125 gives approximate net wing rates of 0.155, 1.245 and 0.713 counts s⁻¹. The wing increase across visits survives excluding the piled-up core and using independent event extraction. This check uses the same XRT photons as the public products. It does not correct detector defects, vignetting or PSF losses. The source PSF can also contribute to the comparison annulus. [Event archive](https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/FTP/swift/data/obs/2026_01/01444088001/xrt/event/)

**Prior work and novelty.** The Leicester live catalog already flags a 1.36–30 ks flare interval. Keating et al. plot the X-ray light curve alongside their millimeter measurement in Figure 3. A GCN circular describes a preliminary late-time fit. Searches for the burst name with “X-ray”, “flare”, “fluence” and “rebrightening” found no explicit two-visit fluence and recent-cohort ranking. The result here is that measurement and its rank. Large X-ray flare-to-prompt ratios in other GRBs are well established, including GRB 050502B. ## Reproduction

Run the commands below in this directory. The sample is the intersection of the Swift 2020–2026 target index through GRB 261017A and the NASA Swift-only table with positive numeric BAT fluence. Both queries were read 2026-11-14. The frozen manifest supplies the target IDs, BAT denominator, source-file hashes, and status of every row. A missing-product row means the public product was unavailable at retrieval. A second target ID for GRB 221009A lacks the required count-rate data.

The main calculation joins on the center and both time errors of each PC bin. It sums the time-dependent unabsorbed flux multiplied by the reported *on-source exposure*. For sensitivity to window choice, the all-burst calculation is repeated at 5–30, 10–20, 10–40 and 10–100 ks. Only entire bins inside a window enter that window, so several late bins can be lost in sparsely binned curves. Upper limits do not contribute to the measured fluences. Sample ranks depend on exposure, adaptive binning, spectral-model availability and instrument response. The windows and the fluence comparison were chosen after screening for an X-ray rebound. The ranks describe this release and supply no trial-corrected population significance. [GRB target index](https://www.swift.ac.uk/xrt_curves/grb.list) · [NASA table query](https://swift.gsfc.nasa.gov/archive/grb_table/table.php?obs=Swift&year=All+Years&restrict=none&bat_fluence=1&bat_err_fluence=1)

```sh
python measure.py --download --output reproduced.csv
cmp measured.csv reproduced.csv
python check_visits.py --download --output reproduced_visits.csv
cmp event_visits.csv reproduced_visits.csv
```
