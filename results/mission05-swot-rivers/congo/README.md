# Congo River at the outlet of Malebo Pool

The downstream reach widened and its water surface flattened during the high flow of December 2023 and January 2024. The river surface rose substantially more in the rapids than near the pool outlet. The return toward earlier gradients during falling water favors a stage-dependent hydraulic response. The record does not establish a persistent change in the rating relationship. Staff gauges on opposite sides of Kinsuka Falls recorded a greater rise downstream in 1954. Historical surveys also mapped the bedrock control. The dated 2024 profile supplies new measurements of this documented behavior. [Historical and scholarly sources](literature.md)

## Measurements

We screened 11 SWORD v16 mainstem reaches from immediately below the pool through its upstream approach in the Chenal. NASA Hydrocron returned 674 reach records, 592 with a timestamp, from the query period March 2023 to September 2025. The V3 source ends in May 2025. For geometry we required valid time and WSE, a good or suspect reach flag (0/1), a complete reach, at least 80% node coverage, and reported WSE uncertainty of 0–0.30 m. Of 274 passing records, all had flag 1 (suspect). Only reach 13217000061 had a valid V3 consensus, on 47 raw and 37 screened visits. Other reaches sometimes had individual algorithm estimates. All six downloaded fixed nodes had 53 records each. Counts, field-level flags, CRIDs, units and L2 granule names are archived.

Per-orbit comparisons below use pass 111 on 8 January and 13 July 2024. Both focal reaches had 100% node coverage, `partial_f=0` and `reach_q_b=14` on both dates. July is the low-stage comparison. Dates are observation dates, separate from the independently measured peak on 10 January.

The 10.7414 m difference between the two water-level drops accompanies a 15.6779 m rise downstream and a 4.9365 m rise upstream. The root-sum-square of the four published reach WSE uncertainties is 0.181 m under an independent-error approximation. Shared instrument errors, seasonal shifts in observed support and processor effects fall outside that approximation. The between-reach values use reach-representative WSE. They measure elevation drop, with velocity head unmeasured. The SWORD static centers are 9.22 km apart in a straight line. Projecting the centers independently onto the reprojected v16 reach polylines and following the connecting lines, including their 30 m gap, gives approximately 11.53 km. The centers sit 718 m and 295 m from their respective polylines. We report the measured drop in meters to avoid treating centers as surveyed hydraulic sections.

Four screened visits from 13 December to 28 January span 46 days, with drops of 8.79–9.86 m. On the other orbit (pass 570) the drop ranged from 9.8550 m on 13 December 2023 to 19.5243 m on 20 August 2024. The secondary pass did not observe the event maximum. A change in the observed along-reach centroid (`loc_offset`) between the January and July pass-111 comparisons was <0.5 m at either location. The nominally fixed nodes moved 21–124 m in latitude/longitude between dates. The three downstream node elevations were 16.23–16.71 m higher in January, while the three upstream nodes were 4.63–5.17 m higher. The central pair yielded a 11.45 m change in drop. All six had node quality 1 on both dates. The range across three fixed node indices per reach tests sensitivity to along-channel sampling, with remaining sensitivity to across-channel water surface differences and correlated KaRIn errors.

## Statistical and physical interpretation

To avoid a difference being regressed on one of its own terms, we used reach 13219000041 in the Chenal, about 130 km upstream in SWORD outlet distance from reach 13217000061, as the separate stage indicator. We used no spatial or temporal interpolation. Seventeen quality-screened, exactly matched cycle/pass-111 visits gave Spearman correlation −0.993 and a Theil–Sen slope of −1.64 m of elevation drop per meter of Chenal stage rise. A moving-block sensitivity interval with three consecutive observed passes, 1,500 resamples and fixed seed was −1.96 to −1.41 m/m. It preserves short-range temporal dependence but does not account for exploratory selection of the site, longer dependence or irregular gaps. Separate PGC0, PIC0 and PIC2 estimates were −1.41, −1.78 and −1.56 m/m on 4, 6 and 7 visits. The dates in the table span PGC0 and PIC0. The sign recurs within each processing period. The Chenal check shares SWOT hardware and is an internal control. Rank results with upstream WSE as predictor are also archived, but that predictor contributes to the elevation difference and can have algebraic coupling.

For a residual check, a line fitted on 14 pass-111 visits outside 1 December 2023–31 January 2024 predicted the same direction of flattening. The three held-out visits had drops 0.84–1.28 m greater than predicted. All three Chenal stages (279.17–279.93 m) exceeded the entire calibration range (273.53–277.78 m). Extrapolation, serial dependence and only two observed annual high-water seasons prevent a defensible significance test of a persistent change. No climatological return period follows from these data. Relative to the pool, the downstream rise is consistent with partial submergence of a hydraulic control or a stage-sensitive response of the gorge. Resolving the mechanism requires velocity and bed-elevation measurements across the active control.

## Independent observations

- **Flow and water level.** Four boat crossings on 10 January with two independent ADCP instruments produced about 75,000 m³/s ±5% across the river near Brazzaville–Kinshasa. Beach gauge stage was 5.47 m above its local datum. The field team reported the second-largest event since 1902 and a crest roughly 15 days later than usual. This corroborates high water. The unconstrained V3 Q was 41% higher two days earlier at a different, downstream reach below the Djoué tributary. Thus the ADCP does not validate that absolute Q value. Its published uncertainty and the single offset match do not determine a statistical discharge bias. The boat cross-sectional width and SWOT equivalent reach width have different support. [Original field report, pp. 2–3](https://gdh-hydrometrie.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/NL_GDH9.pdf), [INRAE](https://www.inrae.fr/actualites/fleuve-congo-objet-dune-collaboration-fructueuse).
- **Co-located inundation and width.** Sentinel-2B L2A images on 5 January and 13 July used the same tile, platform, processing baseline 05.10 and 20 m grid. We chose the nearest January acquisition with at least half the fixed corridor clear and used the July acquisition on the SWOT date. The corridor extends 1 km to either side of the SWORD v16 centerline. Among 42,574 pixels classed as vegetation, bare ground, or water on both dates (62.6% of the corridor), 26,087 were classed water in January and 20,616 in July. The common-support water area increased from 8.2464 to 10.4348 km² (+2.1884 km², +26.5%). Optical water classification confirms the direction of widening. Clouds exclude 37.4% of the corridor, classification may miss turbid or shadowed water, and no extrapolation fills the missing area. Identifiers, hashes, source URLs, the original class values cut to the study area and the mask are in `independent/`. Regional WorldView-3 imagery on 13 January independently mapped about 4 km² flooded in 130 km² of Kinshasa. [UNOSAT](https://unosat.org/static/unosat_filesystem/3786/UNOSAT_A3_Natural_Landscape_FL20231229COD_Kinshasa_DRC.pdf).
- **Independent surface elevation and slope.** The IRIS v2.9 release is explicitly matched to SWORD v16. It records one downstream laser-derived slope of 371 mm/km, dated 7 October 2021, and six upstream slopes spanning 9–523 mm/km, with the last observation dated 13 March 2024. That sparse, differently supported sample does not verify the two-date drop. Papa and colleagues’ 1995–2021 Congo nadir archive has no station on either reach. The closest is on the pool, about 21 km away at 15.45°E. ENVISAT at KM0521 recorded a 4.12 m within-series range in 2002–2010, and SARAL at KM0521 recorded 3.77 m in 2013–2016. Both corroborate the scale of variability in the pool, not the larger downstream rise. [IRIS v2.9, Scherer et al.](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.14616357), [Congo Basin nadir elevations, Papa et al.](https://doi.org/10.23708/ASY30O).
- **Rainfall and reservoir operations.** Independent GPM IMERG and CPC precipitation observations document consecutive positive daily precipitation anomalies during 21–25 November 2023. IMERG recorded three successive days above one standard deviation. The Djoué tributary enters the main river above the downstream reach. The World Bank reported no generating capacity available at its plant in 2024. That is operating context rather than an event-level release record. Gate/spill hydrographs are unverified. [Zhao et al., precipitation analysis and Fig. 2](https://doi.org/10.5194/acp-25-17301-2025), [World Bank](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099060624192033880/pdf/BOSIB16a3432200241912f1ebde92db41c6.pdf).
- **Prior reporting.** Historical gauging established the Djoué rapids as the pool outlet control. USGS field surveys documented strong bedrock control in the Lower Congo. The 2024 flood had already been independently measured and its propagation targeted for SWOT research. Published 1954 gauge readings already contain the same directional response. The priority of the modern reach-resolved values remains unestablished. [Dated literature audit](literature.md). [Laraque et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1080/27678490.2022.2082338), [Oberg et al. 2009](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70156715), [LEGOS Congo flood study](https://www.legos.omp.eu/understanding-flood-processes-in-the-congo-river-basin-and-their-large-scale-triggers-the-caseof-the-2024-event-using-swot/).

## Sources and reproduction

`manifest.json` and `node-manifest.json` contain exact queries, UTC retrieval times, byte lengths, row counts and SHA-256 for all 17 unmodified NASA Hydrocron responses in `raw/`. The queried collection is `SWOT_L2_HR_RiverSP_2.0`, matched to SWORD v16 and SoS V3. `sos_consensus_q` is the L4 consensus. The African file is `af_sword_v16_SOS_results_unconstrained_20230502T204408_20250502T204408_20251219T163700.nc`, CMR `G3927135650-POCLOUD`, revision 4. At retrieval the collection contained six unconstrained continental results. This file resolves the distinction between the generic collection description and the actual branch used here. The independent ADCP observation did not constrain this run. [NASA SoS V3](https://doi.org/10.5067/SWOT-SOS-RD3), [Hydrocron discharge field mapping](https://podaac.github.io/hydrocron/user-guide/discharge/), [CMR inventory](https://cmr.earthdata.nasa.gov/search/granules.umm_json?collection_concept_id=C3905028734-POCLOUD&page_size=50).

`python analyze.py` verifies all archived NASA raw response hashes, screens the records and regenerates the numerical result tables. It requires NumPy, pandas and SciPy. `python fetch.py` and `python fetch_nodes.py` populate missing raw files from NASA. `python optical.py` uses GDAL/OGR and NumPy to fetch and verify the two optical rasters, recreate the three-band GeoTIFF (January SCL, July SCL, study corridor), and recompute the area counts. See `source-details.json` for exact external file and member identifiers. Source tables `sword-v16.csv` and `iris-v2.9.csv` retain original source field names and units. Sentinel and solar illumination, optical pixel labels, radar reach widths and ADCP cross-section widths use different spatial supports. Independent measurements constrain the interpretation at the locations they actually sample.
