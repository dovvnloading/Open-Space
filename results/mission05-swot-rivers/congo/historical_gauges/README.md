# Independent historical stage observations at Kinsuka

In 1955, E.-J. Devroey published daily stage readings on opposite sides of the Kinsuka rapids. Boye's staff gauges were on the left bank of the Congo just below the falls, at the Boye confluence. The upstream Kinsuka recorder was on the left bank near the Léopoldville-Ouest–Kinsuka road. The stated zeros for published readings were 246.94 m downstream and 265.00 m upstream. Differences of successive readings within either station cancel its constant datum. The pages give local descriptions without modern surveyed coordinates. Consequently this is historical evidence for the same hydraulic setting, rather than an exact validation of the modern reach-mean slope or its 2024 amplitude. No SWOT instrument, algorithm, ML prior, or gauge rating curve enters this stage comparison.

The transcription starts on 24 October 1954, when observations resumed at Boye, and ends on 31 December. Two of 69 Boye entries (15 and 28 November) were blank in the scan. All other 67 dates were compared without interpolation. Both December tabulations sum to the printed checksums (24,729 cm at Boye and 28,129 cm at Kinsuka-amont). `source.json` records the PDF, printed and PDF page numbers, and SHA-256. `python analyze.py` reproduces the time ranges, net changes, and Theil–Sen descriptive slope of lower against upper stage. The split date is the maximum recorded downstream stage. The two limbs include this date. Daily values form one serially dependent rise and recession, and no independent-day significance test is reported.
