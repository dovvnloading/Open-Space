# Evidence of prior reporting at the Malebo Pool outlet

**Search date 16 November 2026.** Historical staff gauges already recorded a greater high-water rise downstream than upstream of Kinsuka Falls. The public sources examined did not report the two 2024 SWORD reach-to-reach water-surface drops or the distribution of their seasonal difference along the contemporary radar profile. Earlier surveys, engineering studies and local knowledge limit any claim of priority.

## Measurements and surveys

- **E.-J. Devroey (1955), hydrological yearbook for 1954, printed pp. 74–75.** Its daily staff-gauge tables at Boye, below Kinsuka, and Kinsuka-amont provide direct antecedents. Between 24 October and 4 December the lower and upper gauges rose 3.95 m and 1.02 m, implying a 2.93 m contraction in their relative drop. By 31 December the drop had recovered 1.22 m. Changes within each staff series cancel its gauge zero. The 1954 sites, dates and stage range differ from the SWOT reach averages. [Académie royale des sciences coloniales yearbook](https://www.kaowarsom.be/documents/MEMOIRES_VERHANDELINGEN/Sciences_techniques/Tech.Sc.%28NS%29_T.III%2C1_DEVROEY%20E.-J._Annuaire%20hydrologique%20du%20Congo%20belge%20et%20du%20Ruandi-Urundi%281954%29_1955.pdf)
- **E. Devroey (1941), Le bassin hydrographique congolais.** Printed p. 148, §567 gives high- and low-water slopes of 5.2 and 6.7 cm/km in Stanley Pool upstream of Pointe Kalina. Pages 32–35 identify Ntamo among the cataracts and trace surveys to R. Thys (1911 fieldwork, published 1912), M. Benson and P. Wall (1913 typescript, former Ministry of Colonies library no. 12,261) and SYNEBA (1930–31). The discussion of a several-metre late-flood drop on p. 35 concerns Massese, about 80 km downstream. Those original survey profiles and plates were not all retrieved. [1941 monograph](https://www.vliz.be/imisdocs/publications/ocrd/231552.pdf)
- **Robert Arnould (2005), La canalisation du fleuve Congo à l’aval de Kinshasa.** Printed p. 101 names Kintamo among the northern cataracts. Figure 4 gives a Kinshasa–ocean profile. The numerical comparison of 100 m at very low water with 96 m at very high water, pp. 103–104, applies across 26 km at Inga. [Université de Liège article and figures](https://popups.uliege.be/0770-7576/index.php?file=1&id=2014)
- **F. E. O’Loughlin, M. A. Trigg, G. J.-P. Schumann and P. D. Bates (2013).** Three seasonal ICESat profiles give slopes over 25 km windows for the middle river. Section 22 identifies the constriction downstream of Malebo Pool (Con. A) and explains that their ICESat observations ended upstream of it, leaving its slope effect unresolved. A. B. Carr et al. (2019) add boat GNSS and altimetry over 650 km of the middle river, including longitudinal variability upstream to and through the pool. [O’Loughlin et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/wrcr.20398), [Carr et al.](https://doi.org/10.1029/2019GL083720)
- **A. Laraque et al. (2022), Courbes de tarage du fleuve Congo à Brazzaville-Kinshasa.** The authors compile 154 gaugings and describe a stable Djoué outlet control. Their Fig. 1 shows long profiles and gauging sections and their annex shows photographs of the rapids. Their historical rating is for the pool gauge. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1080/27678490.2022.2082338)
- **World Bank (2021), two national site-screening reports, and Groupe Forrest (2026).** The 2021 study identifies Tractebel’s 2019 work at Île de Kuidu, cites a 10–15 m natural fall and locates the Kinsuka project at 15.1854°E, 4.33972°S. The developer later reported about 15 m over 10 km. The summaries give no observation dates or seasonal profiles. The full 2019 engineering study was not located. [World Bank Fig. 50](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/565251623814093686/pdf/Priorisation-des-Sites-Hydro%C3%A9lectriques-et-Solaires-T%C3%A2che-2-Rapport-d-Analyse.pdf), [inventory LT-0183](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/979591623813495233/pdf/Rapport-de-Cartographie-des-Sites-Hydro%C3%A9lectriques-et-Solaires-Photovolta%C3%AFques-Potentiels-T%C3%A2che-1.pdf), [developer’s site description](https://forrestgroup.com/en/kinsuka-power-a-900-mw-private-hydropower-project/)
- **CICOS, GIE-SCEVN, RVF and INRAE (2024).** The January flood had a direct ADCP measurement and a published hydrograph. LEGOS publicly identified the 2024 flood and its propagation as a SWOT research topic. [Field report, pp. 2–3](https://gdh-hydrometrie.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/NL_GDH9.pdf), [LEGOS project](https://www.legos.omp.eu/understanding-flood-processes-in-the-congo-river-basin-and-their-large-scale-triggers-the-caseof-the-2024-event-using-swot/)
