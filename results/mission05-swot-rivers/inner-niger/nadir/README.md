# Independent satellite water levels

The preserved CSVs are published numeric chart series from DGFI-TUM DAHITI, with observation times, elevation and reported uncertainty, for eight examined virtual stations. The attached Hydroweb ASCII file contains the original header and pass-level observations. Exact source links, hashes, instrument and track metadata are in `provenance.json`. DAHITI uses retracked satellite altimetry and filtering. Hydroweb and DAHITI both use Sentinel-3A at the downstream study crossing, so the outputs are processing sensitivities. The primary downstream-versus-upstream test uses separate tracks of Sentinel-3A, independent of SWOT and the Mali staff gauges. Neither satellite product is used to validate constrained SWOT discharge.

Run `python analyze.py` with NumPy, pandas and SciPy to verify the preserved input hashes and regenerate all three tables. Campaign years begin in October here. January and February belong to the preceding year's flood. We test October–February and November–January, require at least two observations with reported uncertainty ≤0.30 m per site/season and retain both the highest and second-highest sampled stage. These statistics can miss the true crest. For the July/January excursion comparisons, each endpoint must fall within 15 calendar days of the stated SWOT date, on one DAHITI track for the entire pair. Rows failing this window retain observation times and values for the access audit. Reporting the two input uncertainties separately avoids assuming independence of errors between passes.

DAHITI 13763 (16.589°N, 3.117°W, Niger) is about 46 km in a straight line from Diré. Station 14345 (15.473°N, 4.284°W) is about 11 km from Akka. Their 2016–24 paired peaks give two seasons before 2018 and seven after. A linear model of downstream sampled peak on upstream sampled peak plus a 2018–24 indicator estimates a −1.5 cm indicator coefficient, with classical OLS 95% interval −9.4 to +6.3 cm. The November–January estimate is +2.7 cm (−8.9 to +14.3). With only two baseline years, these conditional intervals assume independent homoscedastic residuals and do not include site-transfer, aliasing or time-selection uncertainty. At 14657 about 36 km from Diré, classified by DAHITI as an unnamed river, the comparable October–February estimate is −0.1 cm. These satellite series support the large regional flood but offer limited evidence about an increase in local Diré stage at given Akka stage.

The 2018 and 2025 gauge peaks at Akka were both 518 cm. The satellite proxy 15109, about 21 km from Diré, observed 262.201 and 262.180 m respectively. The corresponding Akka proxy 14345 observed 263.339 and 263.426 m. This comparison has only three usable outlet-proxy visits in 2018. At station 13763, July-to-January stage increases were 5.010 m for 2024–25 and 4.156 m for 2025–26. At 14345 they were 3.433 m and 1.300 m. At a second Akka proxy, 17276, estimates were 3.656 m and 0.206 m. Magnitude varies among crossings, especially at low water. The closer Diré proxy 15109 missed the matching window for one endpoint in both seasons.

Diré-series 1493 and 1494 end in 2016, before the gauge candidate begins. Station 15109 (Sentinel-3B) starts in December 2018 near the separate 1494 crossing (Envisat/SARAL), making an uncalibrated splice unreliable. The more distant Jason/Sentinel-6 record 1610 has implausible abrupt jumps in low-water periods and a weak association between sampled peaks and the Diré gauge (r=0.52 over 18 campaigns). It is excluded from the conditional model. The Hydroweb 7695 cross-check uses an archived copy through August 2024, allowing eight complete October–February windows. The operational product header lists EGM2008. Between-site tests use a constant intercept to absorb fixed reference differences.

Measurement attribution and methods are described by [Schwatke et al. (2015)](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-19-4345-2015) and [CNES/Theia Hydroweb](https://doi.org/10.24400/329360/HYDROWEB_WATER_LEVEL).
