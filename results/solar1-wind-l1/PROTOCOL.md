# SOLAR-1 and Wind magnetic timing

Frozen on 2026-11-18 at repository base `3d7995aaec1de6b4886ebc523e6d69b937c5b246`, before decoding the field or plasma outcomes. The observing unit is a matched UTC minute and its five-minute vector change. The question is whether a single effective planar phase-front direction fitted on 5–6 November improves 7 November agreement over simultaneous comparison and radial propagation, using NOAA MAG L3 and NASA Wind MFI H0. Standard phase-front timing and SOLAR-1 multipoint work are prior art. [Weimer et al.](https://doi.org/10.1029/2001JA009102) · [Raptis et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18314)

## Fixed design

- Use the 5–7 November 2026 UTC files with the exact checksums in the source receipts. Assign timestamps to one-minute UTC bins and timestamp each vector at its center. Average only finite provider-range GSE components with absolute values under 1000 nT. Require 45 distinct good 1-second SOLAR-1 windows with `flags_summary == 0` or 15 distinct 3-second Wind windows with `NUM3_PTS > 0`. NOAA timestamps label the start of each 1-second mean and Wind labels the center of each 3-second mean. [NOAA MAG readme](https://archive.data.noaa.gov/satellite-spaceweather/SWFO/docs/MAG/SWFO_MAG_Provisional_ReadMe_V1.0.pdf)
- Use the NOAA EME2000 predictive ephemeris issued 4 November and NASA Wind predicted-orbit GSE positions. Interpolate NOAA on eight consecutive 10-minute points with degree seven and rotate to GSE using the Earth–Sun geometrical direction and mean ecliptic pole of date. Interpolate each Wind position component linearly with at most a 10-minute bracket. Retain validation of the GSE transform against NASA's paired predicted inertial and native GSE coordinates.
- For each minute center require at least eight Wind SWE observations within 15 minutes, `DQF == 0`, negative (V_x) from −1000 to −200 km/s, and finite GSE components within ±1500 km/s. Take component medians. Retain limited-tracking observations and report their counts. Check a fixed GSE velocity of (−450, 0, 0) km/s at the same slope as a sensitivity. [NASA SWE quality guide](https://cdaweb.gsfc.nasa.gov/wind_swe_quality.html)
- At SOLAR-1 time (t), set predicted Wind offset in seconds to ((r_W(t)-r_S(t))\cdot(1,q,0)/(V_W(t)\cdot(1,q,0))). Reject offsets outside ±60 minutes and normal flow less than 100 km/s in magnitude. Compare no offset, radial (q=0), and slopes from −1.5 to +1.5 in steps of 0.1. Wind vectors at predicted times use linear interpolation between adjacent valid minute centers. At an exact minute center use that minute alone. Both ends of every five-minute difference must remain in their UTC day, including its mapped endpoints.
- Center the five-minute vector changes on their component means and score their normalized dot product over the identical eligible minute pairs for all models. Fit one slope using 5–6 November scores and break ties by smaller absolute slope then smaller slope. Freeze its value before evaluating 7 November. Report full days plus two-hour UTC blocks with at least 80 eligible changes each and absolute raw vector RMS as a secondary metric. The improvement gate is held-out gain of at least 0.05 score over both no shift and radial, and a higher score than both in a majority of qualifying 7 November blocks.
- Negative timing controls circularly shift 7 November Wind B and its mask by 120 to 1320 minutes in 30-minute increments. Use the same frozen models, complete shared coverage for the three control models within each offset, and report coverage and the largest control correlation. Require at least 600 pairs per offset for summary inclusion and held-out fitted score above all eligible controls as a safeguard. The shifts are descriptive with serially correlated and partly shared observations. No significance level or population rate follows.
- Before matching Wind rotations, identify up to five SOLAR-1 times on 7 November with angle at least 35° between component medians from minute offsets −7 through −2 and +2 through +7, with at least four valid vectors per side and norms ≥2 nT. Rank by descending angle then earlier UTC, keeping events ≥60 minutes apart. Characterize the Wind rotation direction and amplitude at zero, radial and fitted offsets rounded to minutes, using the same windows and eligibility. A diagnostic checks offsets ±10 minutes from each rounded prediction and reports the strongest matching vector-change direction; those searched matches carry no confirmatory weight.
- Run synthetic controls on the lag sign, minute interpolation and one missing sample. A failed improvement gate falsifies this benefit for the tested days and models. Differences in tilt performance can arise from evolving fronts, different upstream samples, field and clock calibration, and ephemeris or SWE errors. Two spacecraft supply one delay constraint per structure and cannot uniquely measure its orientation. Wind SWE is retrospective input. This is a timing test for the specified days.
