# Frozen Okinahama shoreline change test

How much optically exposed new ground can be mapped near Okinahama in the October–November 2026 eruption, and how sensitive is an apparent connection to cloud, surf, resolution and shoreline registration? The observing unit is a surface-reflectance grid cell in a named overpass. A published Sentinel-2 interpretation already describes attachment on 11 and 16 November. The Japan Coast Guard observed attachment from its 13 November flight. The new work tests that interpretation against separate spectral bands and across sensors and measures the apparent footprint under declared rules. [JCG survey](https://www.kaiho.mlit.go.jp/info/kouhou/post-1363.html) · [Published Sentinel-2 interpretation](https://news.livedoor.com/article/detail/32839287/) · [GSI ALOS corroboration](https://www.gsi.go.jp/uchusokuchi/20261116ioto.html)

- Freeze the 22 October Sentinel-2C and 26 October Landsat 9 baselines, 27 October Sentinel-2B, 1 November Sentinel-2C, 3 November Landsat 8, 6 November Sentinel-2B, 11 November Landsat 9 and Sentinel-2C, and 16 November Sentinel-2B. The 27 October scene followed the reported onset at roughly 21:00 UTC on 26 October. Preserve every planned scene even if it is covered by clouds, missing, or spatially poor. Treat the near-same-day pair on 11 November as separate images of nearly the same conditions.
- Use the JCG's approximate vent point (141.335°, 24.763333°) and EPSG:32654. Read only the native COG windows needed around a fixed 2.88 km square, from easting 532440 to 535320 m and northing 2737320 to 2740200 m. Freeze a circular 1.1 km quantification region around the vent and a 450 m radius vent neighborhood. The cited vent coordinates have limited precision. Keep the full input crop for coast and ocean controls.
- Use the provider's per-asset reflectance transform. Landsat uses a scale of 0.0000275 and an offset of −0.2. Check Sentinel's effective transform on deep-water shortwave reflectance and document any conflict with the provider's metadata. Withhold conclusions from any scene that fails that check. Read optical and SWIR bands and the product QA. Mask Landsat QA_PIXEL bits 0–5 and nonzero QA_RADSAT. Mask Sentinel SCL 0, 1, 3, 8–11. Do not turn product water flags into land/water labels. Repeat the main test with an extra one-analysis-pixel cloud/shadow dilation. Use 20 m analysis pixels for Sentinel, averaging 10 m bands and keeping SWIR resolution, and 30 m for Landsat. Aggregate the 11 November comparison to 60 m, requiring at least two-thirds valid and two-thirds-classified support from each sensor.
- Define a dry-surface proxy with reflectance thresholds for SWIR1, NIR and SWIR2 of at least 0.035, 0.055 and 0.020, respectively, and modified NDWI at most 0.18. Define water as SWIR1 at most 0.020 and either NIR at most 0.055 or modified NDWI at least 0.30. Leave intervening cases unclassified. Modified NDWI is (green minus SWIR1) / (green plus SWIR1), using nonnegative reflectances. Dry here means land-like spectral response. Wet sediment, floating debris and partly submerged ground remain alternatives. Test relaxed thresholds (0.025/0.045/0.014 and NDWI ≤0.28) and strict thresholds (0.048/0.067/0.030 and NDWI ≤0.10).
- Define old land and old water by agreement of both baseline classifications. Measure new ground only where it replaces baseline water, lies inside the region, is more than 40 m from baseline land, and the later SWIR1 increased by at least 0.02 over both baseline values. Retain components of at least 0.006 km², require at least one cell in the vent neighborhood to assign the component to the eruptive feature, and record all removed or distant components. Repeat with the alternate dry thresholds, a 20–60 m baseline exclusion band and a one-cell cloud buffer. Report coverage and areas rather than interpreting obscured cells as water. The baseline exclusion band means areas omit some newly exposed ground near old shore.
- A positive contact test requires a four-neighbor path in a continuous post-event dry mask from a qualifying new-ground component to baseline land at least 100 m inland. Require the path after one native analysis-pixel erosion for robust contact. Check stability with the strict and relaxed thresholds and cloud dilation. No path gives an unresolved result unless a clear-water barrier can be separately established. Estimate registration over unchanged inland control pixels with local texture and independent held-out registration checks where coverage allows. Preserve poorer geolocation and report boundary sensitivity. Do not infer exact first contact, terrain above arbitrary tidal datum or the point at which a coastline became permanently attached.
- Estimate deep-water and stable-land behavior outside the event region; list shortwave reflectance and false changes on the baseline-to-baseline comparison and post-event controls. Exercise the connectivity logic on a planted robust neck and a one-pixel bridge. Compare the source observations, reflectance composites and published aerial and radar evidence, keeping time, reading conditions and sensor dependencies explicit. No trustworthy local tide gauge or beach-slope survey is assumed.
