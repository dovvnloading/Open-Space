# O4b coherent burst search methods

## Observing unit and overlap

The observing unit is a one-second Hanford arrival interval with valid Hanford and Livingston strain, spectral normalization and filter guards. We test whether simultaneous strain contains more transient power with consistent intersite phase and delay than the same two detectors at artificial offsets. A high score can be produced by chance or by instrumental noise.

The repository’s existing GWTC study examined 5,356 pairs of 104 published event posterior summaries and sky maps. At initial review the open PRs concerned SPHEREx (#102), Juno (#104), MAVEN (#105), a Rubin binary (#106), and XMM neutron stars (#108). Outside this repository, the LVK O4a short-burst search calibrated the sensitivity of four analyses. GWTC-5.0 includes an O4b cWB-BBH analysis as well as compact-binary template searches. This contribution measures a rate for a specified waveform, public-data selection and independent statistic. It does not establish priority or a stronger bound than the LVK's other surveys. ## Selection and calibration

GWOSC's O4b observing interval is GPS 1396796418 to 1422118818. We use final R1 4,096 Hz HDF5 files. The release identifies the Hanford and Livingston source channels as DCS-CALIB_STRAIN_CLEAN_AR01. The 32–896 Hz analysis lies within the published valid passband. File identity, lengths, GPS epochs, native sample spacing, 1 Hz flag alignment, finite values and official file MD5 are checked. The manifests identify each input byte stream and the metadata used to select it. [GWOSC O4b open data, DOI 10.7935/8emv-ag54](https://gwosc.org/O4/O4b/)

The first survey uses one aligned 4,096 s frame per O4b calendar month, the first with complete H/L DATA on or after the 15th. In July no eligible frame remained after the 15th, so the last full frame ending before then was selected. This fallback was fixed from the DATA timelines before science processing. All available Virgo files were fetched. GW250114 is a separate positive control.

The disjoint extension was fixed after the first survey. We divided each UTC calendar week into four slots, beginning on Monday, Wednesday, Friday and Sunday at 00:00. For each slot the first complete aligned H/L DATA frame wholly within the slot and the observing run was selected. The ten initial frames and control were ineligible. The 168 slots tile the observing run. Of these, 125 slots in 36 ISO weeks yielded a frame, and 43 yielded none. Virgo availability played no role in selection. Both campaign results are reported separately. [First protocol](https://github.com/dovvnloading/Open-Space/pull/114#issuecomment-6923893538) · [Extension protocol](https://github.com/dovvnloading/Open-Space/pull/114#issuecomment-6924145706) · [125-frame selection and empty slots](results/holdout_selection.json)

Within each detector we intersect DATA, BURST_CAT1, BURST_CAT2, BURST_CAT3, NO_CBC_HW_INJ, NO_BURST_HW_INJ, NO_DETCHAR_HW_INJ and NO_STOCH_HW_INJ. The released observing times have no hardware injections of those four types. Low-amplitude continuous-wave hardware injections were active through much of O4b. They are retained and checked against the published frequencies during follow-up. We then excise whole seconds intersecting ±32 s of any frozen public-event time in both detector streams, before shifting or spectral estimation. The three sources cover 1,857 LVK search-summary rows, 160 GWOSC event versions and 1,820 public GraceDB production superevents within O4b, with overlaps. Seven windows touch the initial ten survey frames. Published-event vetoes exclude known signals and some noise coincidences. The bounds concern additional events in the remaining experiment. ## Network statistic and background

Resample each detector to 2,048 Hz using a polyphase antialias filter, then high-pass it with a 513-tap FIR at 24 Hz. Exclude a second on each side of invalid data and the file edges. At each duration (0.125, 0.5 and 1 s), use 50%-overlapped Hann windows. The four bands are [32,96), [96,224), [224,448) and [448,896) Hz. At each resolution exclude bins within the greater of 1 Hz and one frequency bin of each 60 Hz harmonic. Normalize each complex Fourier coefficient by the square root of the local median bin power divided by ln(2). The PSD estimate for a 16 s half-epoch comes from the opposite half of its 32 s epoch, with at least eight eligible interior training seconds. Target and training data share no central signal window.

For each site let E be the summed squared magnitude of the normalized coefficients across B retained frequencies; define z=(E−B)/sqrt(2B). Both sites must reach z≥1.5. For a time delay τ we compute the absolute real projection of H times conjugate(L) times exp(−2πifτ), summed across the band. Normalize this by sqrt(E_H E_L) to get the coherence C. The score is min(z_H,z_L) times C times 2 sqrt(E_H E_L)/(E_H+E_L). The grid contains 41 delays from −10 to +10 ms in 0.5 ms steps. The absolute projection tests both polarities. A positive residual lag indicates later Livingston arrival. The score has an empirical noise distribution. H/L must see sufficiently similar waveforms up to scale, sign and delay, so strong polarization mixtures can be missed.

Select the largest score per eligible second over all 88 tiles, signs and lags. Seconds without a double-gate crossing have score zero. The same search operates at offsets ±4, ±8, …, ±256 s, with the shifted Livingston segment restricted to the same file and its own live mask. No offset wraps, crosses a file boundary or contributes to physical observing time. For each offset take the maximum across the *whole* campaign. The descriptive campaign tail is (1 + number of shifted maxima ≥ foreground maximum)/129. This includes the window, frequency, lag and sign trials. Offsets share detector data, can align with the PSD training cycle, and have slightly shorter live exposure than zero lag. The rank measures empirical background support and has a floor of 1/129. It cannot establish a discovery-level false-alarm probability. The prespecified follow-up flag is 0.01. A two-campaign claim would require 0.005 after Bonferroni correction. We also report time-normalized counts of shifted seconds reaching the observed maximum. [Implementation](core.py) · [Backgrounds and control](results/survey.json)

## Controls, follow-up and efficiency

The known GW250114 control uses the archive's bulk calibration and detector DQ. Its own published time mask is bypassed in the separate control frame. The synthetic controls use independent colored noise for each detector, 192 independent noise pairs, 64 common H1 seconds in each comparison and 17 different alignments. A fixed broadband random pulse of opposite polarity was recovered at the prescribed intersite delay. The same pulse placed in H1 alone gave zero score around the target time. This tests code and trial behavior under a simplified noise population. The measured FAR uses shifted *observations*. [Synthetic controls](noise_controls.json) · [Published GW250114](https://gwosc.org/eventapi/html/GWTC-5.0/GW250114_082203/)

Independent follow-up uses the original 4,096 Hz strain, Butterworth bandpass, separate autoregressive filters fitted to off-source data in each detector, and direct time-domain rolling correlation. It shares the on-source strain and primary band selection. Virgo energy, local comparisons, public auxiliary witnesses, active instrumental lines, continuous-wave injection frequencies and calibration envelopes supply additional checks. The line catalogue and public auxiliary channels sample only part of the instrumental environment. Calibration envelopes summarize the released per-epoch percentiles. Amplitudes and bounds use nominal calibration. [Instrumental lines](https://dcc.ligo.org/T2500212/public) · [Calibration release](https://dcc.ligo.org/T2500288/public)

The primary signal is a **linearly polarized sine-Gaussian**, with Q=9, central frequency 150 Hz, and incident root-sum-square strain 1e−21 Hz⁻¹ᐟ², before detector projection. Its Gaussian envelope has σ=Q/(2πf). The measured integral of the squared incident waveform is normalized to hrss². Draw sky positions isotropically in Earth-fixed coordinates, uniform polarization, phase and arrival time within the chosen Hanford second. Project onto both detector tensors using the same sky and polarization. Apply the physical fractional delay from the relative site positions. This calibrates sensitivity to sky nulls, detector imbalance and the finite search grid. [LAL detector constants](https://lscsoft.docs.ligo.org/lalsuite/7.26/lal/_l_a_l_detectors_8h_source.html)

A recovery requires score ≥20 in the injected Hanford second and a lower score than 20 in that same second before injection. For the first campaign, 2,048 primary draws sample the actual live seconds with replacement. Three additional 512-trial ensembles characterize sensitivities at 150 Hz and 2e−22 or 5e−22, and 350 Hz and 1e−21. For the extension, 8,192 independent primary draws sample *all* scheduled seconds with replacement. A vetoed draw counts as a miss. Accordingly the exposure factor uses live time for the first campaign and scheduled time for the extension. A full-run spectral reference is used to evaluate injected raw crops. Forty pilot trials concentrated around thresholds, time boundaries and extreme lags were replayed using full local signal-plus-noise spectral re-estimation.

For a sparse stationary Poisson population of isolated additional bursts, the expected detected count is R ε T. The reported count includes every foreground second at or above 20 regardless of interpretation, with zero background subtraction. For k recoveries in n trials, use the exact one-sided beta quantile Beta(α; k, n+1−k) as a lower bound on ε. Upper-bound the mean count by one half of the 1−α chi-squared quantile with 2(N+1) degrees of freedom for N observed occupied seconds. Divide the upper count by the lower effective exposure. For N=0, the numerator equals −ln(α). Setting α=.05 for each term gives at least 90% pointwise confidence by a union bound; α=.025 gives at least 90% joint coverage for the two primary campaign results. Bounds assume stable nominal calibration, arrivals independent of the fixed public-event masks, and negligible overlapping astrophysical bursts. They do not describe all waveform strengths, all source orientations for a particular object, a distance or a volumetric rate.
