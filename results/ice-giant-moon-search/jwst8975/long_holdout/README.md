# Perdita in the independent 2026 long-exposure visits

**The October 2026 F140M short-exposure family also appears in two 20-pixel-long trails in visit 005, at about −19.6° relative to nominal Perdita.** The two full native-trail fits at the frozen −19.8° short-exposure prediction give longitude residuals of −19.74° and −19.42° relative to nominal Perdita, fitted PSF sigmas of 0.74 and 0.84 native pixel, and formal amplitude signal-to-error ratios of 14.82 and 15.25. The visit 007 offset stack scores −5.14, and the two native fits with detector coverage give formal ratios of 0.96 and 0.02. The noisier second long visit supplies no positive detection. Four other exposures across these two visits placed the predicted position off chip. The six frozen trial orbits from short visits 006 and 008, propagated to the independent long exposures, score 10.79–17.38 in visit 005 and −6.41 to −5.13 in visit 007. These are the exact radii and phases specified by the short-visit screen, at emission epoch JD TDB 2461321.533281779. The nearest nominal known-moon mask was well separated from the trial paths. Rotating Perdita's projected catalog orbit to −19.75° gives 20.78 in long visit 005. The highest nearby score is 20.96 at −19.50°, with a worst leave-one-exposure-out score of 14.49. These extended-exposure detections provide independent measurements for the subsequently identified short-visit source. Their native centroids and formal errors use a trailed Gaussian plus a fitted local background plane. The strongest source is Perdita at the altered longitude. ## October 2026 F140M photometry

The four accepted detections give a median **1.020 μJy in F140M (AB 23.879)**. The between-exposure scaled median absolute deviation is **0.078 μJy**. Applying the same measurement at aperture radii of 2.5, 3.5 and 5.0 pixels with planar and quadratic local backgrounds gives medians from 0.995 to 1.020 μJy.
