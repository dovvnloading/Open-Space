# SO/PHI-FDT magnetic repeatability: pre-pixel preparation

Status: metadata and geometry only. Neither development nor held-out science arrays have been analyzed for this task. The development and held-out evaluators are implemented and synthetically tested; native inversion quality, cross-instrument checks and real-background calibration remain unresolved. This packet does not itself authorize opening any science arrays. Commit and independently review the full executable protocol before development; freeze a numerical prediction and record its digest before any holdout array is opened.

## Question and falsification

Can a large, spatially coherent change in a fixed photospheric magnetic patch between two 2026-08-13 FDT measurements be followed at the same rotating solar location on both 2026-08-15 scans? The physical motivation is evolution of a localized magnetic feature; stable geometry-adjusted signal would be a consistency observation, while sign reversal, absence beyond calibrated uncertainty, or failure of shared-footprint/quality gates defeats the registered persistence prediction. Strong changes, polar features, reconnection, or emergence are not established from a single LOS magnetogram. A negative or insensitive comparison is a useful result. At present there is no numerical prediction or discovery.

## Observations and independence

Use L2 `blos` v03 maps in [inventory.json](inventory.json). Candidate development pair: Aug 13 10:25:09–10:29:32 and 16:25:09–16:29:37 UTC. Candidate held-out pair: Aug 15 16:25:09–16:29:36 and 19:25:09–19:29:36 UTC. These are separate four-minute acquisition windows; prove disjointness using actual individual exposure timestamps, duration convention, input parent identifiers and source bytes. A map, not each of its 360 metadata rows, is the observing unit. `OBS_ID=SPHI_240A_LF2_112_Rt1U_126` is reused and is not evidence of a unique exposure. The same-time bmag/binc/bazi/stokes siblings are derived from the same acquisition, not independent measurements. Both holdout scans share telescope, pipeline, calibration, inversion and solar evolution; two images do not eliminate common systematics. The four maps have been downloaded and hashed without reading primary science arrays. Their per-image time and recorded nonzero operand sets are pairwise disjoint; full raw-parent interpretation and exposure duration semantics remain limited. SOAR `LAST_PRODUCT` is mutable, so compare exact SHA-256 before any science read.

## Pre-pixel spatial proposal

Use a deterministic grid of 25 reference centers in the Aug 13 16:25 image, with normalized observer-plane offsets x/Rsun,y/Rsun in {−0.4,−0.2,0,0.2,0.4}². Grid and eligibility must be constructed from headers/WCS only. At each center define a finite, 317-node surface lattice within 0.02 radians of angular radius; map its *full footprint* into all four scans with differential rotation, actual observer coordinates and finite disk visibility. Require every contributing point to have μ ≥ 0.65 and a common finite, unmasked footprint; exclude any patch intersecting the dust/defect mask or image edge. Eligible patches are not labeled quiet until measured. No candidate/prediction is frozen now, and no patch may be picked using the Aug 15 pixels.

Once software, inputs and controls are separately reviewed, use the two development images alone to compute each eligible patch's signed median change in B_LOS/μ, where B_LOS is in gauss. Choose the largest absolute development change, retaining all tested patches and ties broken by grid order. Dividing by μ is a *radial-field approximation*, not a measurement of the true radial magnetic field: horizontal fields, changing viewing geometry, resolution and inversion can cause apparent changes. Before either holdout image, freeze the chosen heliographic footprint, development reference, predicted sign, code/input hashes, registration choices, robust control-based uncertainty and a rejection threshold. The preregistered sign test requires both Aug 15 patch medians to remain on the same side of the Aug 13 10:25 reference, with each difference exceeding three predeclared control-scale units; report the actual differences and all control outcomes. This is a conditional screening rule, not a calibrated p-value: spatial correlation, grid selection and solar evolution violate naive per-pixel independence. If fewer than eight usable, spatially disjoint patches or either scan cannot support the chosen footprint, report inconclusive; do not silently select a replacement. The two Aug 15 maps are repeat scans, not two independent instruments.

## Geometry, calibration and controls

FDT files contain HPLN/HPLT TAN WCS, nontrivial PC rotation, solar-observer ephemeris, Gauss units and DATE-BEG/AVG/END UTC. Astropy WCS transforms these angular axes to degrees, including longitude wrap, even when original CUNIT says arcsec; use units from WCS rather than assuming the header value survives. `geometry.py` uses zero-based pixel coordinates, SunPy observer-aware 3-D helioprojective coordinates, a finite-distance μ, the Howard rotation model and target visibility. It is prerequisite code only, not a complete remapping, uncertainty or flux measurement pipeline. Validate both directions with synthetic sources, off-disk rejection, far-side checks, a nonzero PC rotation and real header-only geometry before analysis; coordinate transformations at 3-D time changes and roundtrip errors must be documented.

A scan covers about 4m27s and cycles through wavelengths and modulation states; DATE-AVG is a convenience and not a simultaneous instant for every state. Account for the solar emission time by comparing DATE-AVG minus observer–Sun light travel time for independent observatories while retaining the spacecraft ephemeris at the actual observation time; validate this convention against mission documentation before interpreting sub-hour timing. At spacecraft–Earth separation nearly 90° on Aug 13, only a restricted common surface is visible, and direct SO/PHI and HMI B_LOS magnitudes are not identical even for an unchanged vector. Define HMI projected-field/structural controls only in geometry-verified common view, accounting for viewing angle, time, resolution and disambiguation; e.g. do not require μ≥0.8 from both viewpoints. Same-spacecraft EUI FSI 174 Å is a coronal morphology control, not an independent magnetic measurement. Both external controls remain contingent on exact metadata, co-registration and source provenance.

Inspect all relevant native quality metadata, finite/invalid/saturation masks, cosmic/pointing, inversion quality, spatial covariance, registration residuals and cadence before any claim. Match resolutions, and compare the SO/PHI blank-region noise/pointing against shifted/noncandidate controls and the two individual Aug 15 scans. The [instrument release notes](https://www.mps.mpg.de/solar-physics/solar-orbiter-phi/data-releases) document v03 artifacts including the moving detector dust grain and residual etalon/ghost patterns; they also warn FDT LOS velocities have offsets and are not directly calibrated. No velocity inference is proposed. Their dust formula returns FITS one-based coordinates; mask at least the documented 30×30-pixel footprint plus a prespecified margin in detector coordinates for every scan, not just a fixed solar coordinate. The `geometry.py` dust formula converts to zero-based pixels. Review bad-pixel/uncertainty metadata rather than assuming nominal quality bits.

## Executable development gate

The preliminary implementation comprises `footprints.py`, `development.py`, and `prediction.py`. Each grid patch is a finite 317-node surface lattice, and every node plus bilinear interpolation stencil is checked. This does not prove a continuum-wide circular footprint. Full four-scan geometry checks occur using headers only; all four filename/hash pairs and metadata-table timestamp and recorded operand disjointness are verified before development pixels are opened. Eight or more usable disjoint patches are required; these patches share calibration and are not statistically independent experiments. The earlier-reference and development maps are the only primary images the development reader will open. Holdout paths are hashed and their headers and acquisition tables read but their science arrays are not accessed.

For each usable patch, take the median B_LOS/μ in the two development scans. Select the greatest absolute raw change (grid order breaks ties), then subtract the median change among the remaining usable patches. Define control scale as max(1 gauss, 1.4826 × MAD of those other changes). Admit the proposal only if its absolute centered change is at least five control-scale units and the sign remains the same when the later image is shifted by each of ±1 pixel in x or y. Controls include genuine solar evolution and spatial covariance; this threshold is a deterministic screening rule, not a false-alarm probability. Invalid, non-finite, near-saturated (|B_LOS|≥4900 G), or incomplete bilinear stencils exclude the patch with a recorded reason. Native inversion and instrument-specific quality flags remain a mandatory separate review; finite-value checks do not establish quality.

If admitted, freeze the exact candidate patch, development code SHA, input hashes, four tracked lattices, sign, reference median, control median and scale in `prediction.py` before inspecting any holdout pixels. The prediction tests each holdout against the reference after subtracting the frozen control median, requiring the registered sign and a magnitude above three frozen control-scale units. Record a fixed-physical-detector-coordinate control separately: it samples a different solar surface and is an artifact diagnostic, not a second independent hypothesis test. No rescue using this secondary control or unsigned/coherence measures is predeclared. Report all 25 patch dispositions and masks, including dust-excluded g13, input failures and the number usable, and retain a no-candidate/inconclusive result.

Before a candidate is interpreted physically, compare detector-frame changes, rotation/registration alternatives, remaining quality flags, inversion artifacts and matched controls. Calibrate using injected known changes and null controls on independent/real backgrounds after the code freeze; current synthetic Gaussian noise tests are implementation checks only. Do not treat a local control MAD or per-pixel count as a calibrated global false-alarm probability, or infer actual flux emergence from the radial proxy.

The future command `development.py EARLY LATE --holdout-meta-a HOLD_A --holdout-meta-b HOLD_B --run-id UNIQUE --code-sha EXACT_COMMIT --out UNIQUE.json` validates the code packet, all input hashes and four metadata records before opening only the two August 13 primary arrays. Root review and explicit development authorization remain required. After that run, `prediction.py UNIQUE.json --out NEW_PREDICTION.json` records a selected candidate or the no-prediction result without touching FITS. If and only if an independently reviewed, hashed prediction exists and root authorizes that exact code SHA and prediction digest, `validate_repeat.py` can evaluate the two sealed August 15 scans. The latter checks the authorization record before opening either held-out array.

The observer-frame control freezes the same physical detector-coordinate nodes from the later development scan using PXBEG offsets, recalculates μ and quality for each other scan, and records its change separately. If an observer-frame footprint is off-disk, dust-contaminated, clipped or invalid, it is reported as unavailable and cannot rescue or replace the tracked primary. The fixed detector patch samples a different solar location as the Sun rotates. The 25-patch selection is a research screen; its synthetic-null behavior does not establish a global false-alarm rate.
