# Resolved-satellite search: control fields and conditional sensitivity

The DR11 search recovered the known BoÃ¶tes I control but did not meet its joint spatial-and-distance recovery rule for Coma Berenices. One primary peak was retained, at BoÃ¶tes I. A follow-up search outside 26 arcmin of the known controls retained zero peaks under its conditional shuffled-catalogue model; it was designed after the primary results and is exploratory. **No new satellite or astrophysical survey null is established.**

The two small fields were chosen around known systems, not sampled as an unbiased sky survey. The reported Monte Carlo probabilities are conditional on the observed catalogue, search grid and within-cell color/magnitude shuffles; shared stars and masks are not independent observations. Missing coverage, crowding, depth, morphology, blends and background-model choices limit interpretation. The injections are artificial catalogues using model stellar populations, nearest-source depth estimates and approximate noise, not end-to-end image completeness or an intrinsic satellite occurrence rate. Their center sampling and exact trial counts are recorded alongside results. Bootes and Coma recovery do not establish sensitivity to all stellar populations.

Gaia, RR Lyrae and catalogue checks are supporting follow-up of these same fields, not independent detections. The original protocol is preserved as historical context; the pre-access timing is a local record, not a cryptographic registration.
