# AKARI scan-time provenance

The published [Phan et al. paper](https://doi.org/10.1017/pasa.2025.10024), PDF SHA-256 `ddc78cd212287f13b2db480498797a6c26f08eee1dd425fb6060ef209739d0f1`, page 8 Figure 6, labels two December 26, 2006 scans at 15:59:18.072 and 17:38:34.553 UTC. The authors report December detections. These are paper-supported scan-time alternatives, not independently verified catalog contributor times or the catalog centroid epoch. The three June scan labels are counterfactual seasonal sensitivity cases, not supported contributing detections.

Existing orbit-dependent outputs, including figures, searches, trajectory coverage and injection recovery, used 21:03:07.5 UTC. The earlier report called it the midpoint of 20:13:46 and 21:52:29; these claimed scan times contradict Figure 6. This historical input and all generated outputs remain unchanged so that those analyses can be replayed. These searches already inspected real images; none of the scenario comparisons is prospective or an independent observation.

`python timing_scenarios.py --scenario dec1 --out D:/new-exclusive-directory` performs metadata-only propagation; `dec2`, `jun1`, `jun2`, `jun3`, and `historical` are also available. It records the input hash and scenario role. The two DES epoch times are the archive inventory timestamps plus 45 seconds for 90-second exposures; the Euclid time is the documented 07:30 UTC sequence reference. Positions use a geocentric observer, not the actual Euclid spacecraft location. These calculations do not recompute masks, image coverage, moving-source searches, injection recoveries or calibration. Those effects need a separately reviewed analysis before applying the old headline fractions to either December scenario.
