# Independent referee audit — Planet Nine far-infrared pairing (17 November 2026)

**Disposition: conditional null search; no discovery or unconditional exclusion of the published orbit family.** The measured result is that no moving source passed the stated DES/Euclid, NEOWISE or SPHEREx tests in the actual public fields searched. On a *chosen finite sampling* of 400 bound trajectories through the two published centroids, injecting a compact **5 Earth-mass, 2.71 Earth-radius** body with constant optical **geometric albedo 0.10** would have passed DES or Euclid on **397/400** realizations (**99.25%**, Wilson 95% **97.82–99.74%**). Repeating with deliberately perturbed historical centroids gives **388/400** (**97.00%**, Wilson 95% **94.83–98.28%**). These are injection-recovery efficiencies and conditional tested-hypothesis exclusions when no source is seen, **not** exclusions of 99.25% or 97% of the continuum defined by the published measurements. No scientifically justified posterior measure was supplied for that continuum, and the original AKARI scan epoch was not verified.

## Independent checks and what the deposited bytes permit

1. **Matched optical trials and statistics:** `referee_audit.py` independently joins the complete 800 gzipped per-orbit rows with the deposited DES sample and recomputes DES, Euclid, intersection, union, missing count and *Wilson* intervals, rather than reading percentages from the README. The central dark case is DES 379, Euclid 362, both 344, union 397, neither 3; the centroid-stress case is 371, 271, 254, 388 and 12. The 5 M⊕, 2.94 R⊕ and 10 M⊕, 3.70 R⊕ albedo-0.30 examples are also checked. The 95% intervals describe sampling only; the published positions do not define a probability distribution.
2. **2025 N395:** The exact 15 dark-model DES/Euclid joint misses have been matched by `experiment, draw` to `n395_cal/paired_misses.csv`. One of three published-centroid and six of twelve perturbed draws is usable. The maximum measured real-pixel S/N is **+1.0173**, with **zero** recoveries on the original seeded injections; 64 repeats per usable site recovered only three seeds at a single perturbed site. The independently calibrated fixed-magnitude N395 tests give **126/129** at AB 23.25 and **91/129** at AB 23.50. Native N395 FITS pixels were downloaded during the study but **not deposited**, so neither the photometric calibration nor the original pixel-level injection can be independently rerun from Git alone.
3. **1983 IRAS, stationary-source alternative:** With the empirically validated SCANPI scan-axis orientation, the July 60 µm fixed-position matched amplitude is **+0.2809687 Jy** and late August/September is **+0.2310513 Jy**. The **711** July-compatible moving tracks give **−0.0812053 to −0.0127743 Jy** late, with a fixed-position offset-control robust scatter of **0.052899 Jy**. The archive's second sky-centred request independently checks the scan sign. The original per-detector SCANPI tables **are deposited** inside a tarball, and their uncompressed member lengths and SHA-256 hashes can be checked against the 200-status acquisition receipts. A regenerated scan test in an isolated scratch directory is part of `planet-nine-referee.yml`. A known W4-bright AllWISE object **WISEA J022301.05−483040.5**, listed as a galaxy by NED with photometric redshift 0.066250 (not a spectroscopic redshift), is **32.856″** from the IRAS centroid, within the large far-IR beam. Its W4 magnitude 6.899 and S/N 15.6 are in the deposited catalog response. It is a **candidate contributor, not a secure identification**. The scan fits favor stationary emission but cannot establish an astrophysical identity or exclude a faint moving component in the beam.
4. **SPHEREx:** The six detector summaries cover **572 acquired and analysed image products** with nominal usable tracks **2,551/3,617**. A centroid/range/rate stress sample has **10,464/15,833** usable trajectories and **zero** motion triggers. Those **10,464** indicate geometric/data eligibility, **not** physical-model exclusion. Detector-level trial tables, source-recovery counts and a reproducible combination script are committed. Exactly **one D3 acquisition entry** has a time, bytes and SHA-256 but no HTTP status; the other 571 used frames report status 200. Original large FITS data were not committed. The script validates presence and syntax of each acquired image checksum, but cannot independently hash an absent image.
5. **Integrity and archive receipts:** `verify_results.py` checks the DES compressed input SHA-256, per-trial joins, manifest hashes and ancillary outputs. `referee_audit.py` additionally hashes each deposited SCANPI source-table member, the three committed SHA256SUMS manifests, and checks source URLs, byte counts and hashes recorded for upstream IRIS, ISSA, AKARI, Euclid, DES, WISE, N395 and SPHEREx downloads. **A recorded upstream hash is a receipt, not a new byte-for-byte validation when those original pixels are absent.** The GitHub Actions `Verify claims` job separately checks committed hashes and catalog links. The first generic offline replay was not a complete native-image rerun: it reported drift after network-dependent WISE/JPL receipt refreshes failed and generated an empty ZTF output with missing native FITS. **This review fixed both fail-open behaviours:** `wise_vet/catalogs.py` now propagates a download error without overwriting a previously verified receipt, and `other_optical/ztf_tracks.py` raises on absent SHA-verified ZTF image triplets rather than generating spurious zero counts. These missing native images still cannot be reprocessed offline and remain explicitly pending.
6. **2026 embargoed observations:** Twelve 900 s r-band DECam exposures from proposal 2026B-649930 are listed with image ID and MD5 in `archives/dedicated_2026.csv`: **four each on September 13, October 28 and November 15, 2026**, with public-release dates **March 5, April 19 and May 7, 2028** respectively. A direct November image request returned HTTP 403. They have **not** been analysed as public science pixels; no discovery/exclusion is claimed from them.

## Material issue: AKARI timing provenance

The paper by **Phan et al. (2025, PASA)** [doi:10.1017/pasa.2025.10024](https://doi.org/10.1017/pasa.2025.10024) presents two positive December 26, 2006 single scans at **15:59:18.072** and **17:38:34.553 UTC**, and three earlier June scans without convincing signal. The stored orbit input instead assumes **2006-12-26 21:03:07.5 UTC**, described in the first draft as the midpoint of two times not supported by the paper's Figure 6. The paper's catalog-contributor assignment and centroid effective epoch cannot be independently recovered from the public coadd and FIS BSC v1; the monthly unconfirmed-source list and original positive scan images were unavailable. The auxiliary `orbits/timing_scenarios.py` propagates paper-supported times **without** rerunning public image masks, spacecraft observers or recovery tests. Consequently the 397/400 and 388/400 results are **historical, conditional calculations** and must not be generalized to a calibrated exclusion of Phan et al.'s full family. This discrepancy is disclosed in the campaign README and PR body, not hidden in a footnote. [Detailed timing provenance](orbits/TIMING.md)

## Pending independent measurements

Reacquire and hash the native optical, Euclid and SPHEREx pixels; confirm AKARI's positive-scan timestamp/centroid and the unavailable 2006 single scans; redo the full matched orbit search and sky-mask intersections for each supported AKARI timing alternative; test broader distances and albedo/color/atmosphere distributions; open the 2026 DECam exposures at their respective release dates. The DES–Euclid union cannot be treated as two independent survey probabilities because the draws are identical and partly share background reference data. Other published candidates have sparse footprints and do not receive family-wide exclusion claims.

**Label:** *Null result.* This is an exclusion/sensitivity exercise with missing critical inputs, not a validated discovery-level or alarming finding. The PR title must not carry `[REMARKABLE]`, and `docs/REMARKABLE_FINDINGS.md` is unchanged.

## Reproduction

`python results/planet-nine-fir/verify_results.py` (standard library) and `python results/planet-nine-fir/referee_audit.py` reproduce the numeric and integrity assertions from committed files. The scoped Actions job `.github/workflows/planet-nine-referee.yml` installs NumPy, SciPy and Astropy, independently regenerates `joint/join.py`, both `spherex/combine.py` outputs, `historical/verify_scan_sign.py`, `historical/scan_tracks.py` and `historical/all_scan_tracks.py` in a copied scratch directory, then compares outputs to the deposited tables. This gives a genuine source-level replay for those products while plainly delimiting the unavailable native FITS pixels.

## Additional reproducibility fixes from the review

- The ZTF replay previously produced an all-zero candidate summary if none of its native difference images were mounted. It now aborts without touching the validated results.
- The WISE, Gaia and JPL catalog refresh functions previously overwrote archived success receipts with transient network errors during offline replay. They now record a new receipt **only after** downloading the response successfully; an inaccessible endpoint raises and leaves prior bytes intact.
- One SPHEREx D3 acquisition record lacks an explicit HTTP status despite its length, UTC and SHA-256. The 572-frame total is the count of recorded acquired images and detector products, not 572 independently verified HTTP-200 receipts.
