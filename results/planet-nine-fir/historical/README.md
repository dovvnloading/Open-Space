# Far-infrared endpoints

The public IRAS Faint Source Catalog reproduces the first endpoint. The two AKARI single-scan fluxes and their astrometry remain author measurements. The public AKARI sky map shows no significant signal at the AKARI endpoint. July and late-August/September IRAS detector data favor persistent emission at the catalogued position over the expected several-arcminute displacement of a July-compatible planet. A WISE-bright galaxy lies inside the beam.

The IRIS/ISSA measurements fit a fixed-width circular Gaussian and tilted planar background within two beam FWHM, with 4.0′ and 4.3′ beams at 60 and 100 μm. The AKARI measurements use a 90″ radius aperture, a 120–300″ median-sky annulus, and the published response factors 0.627 (65 μm) and 0.696 (90 μm). ## Inputs and reproduction

Run `python measure.py --cache /path/to/cache` with NumPy and Astropy. `inputs.json` lists the source URL, acquisition time, byte count and SHA-256 of every input. The downloaded products include the arXiv paper, full CDS II/156A and II/298 catalogues, native IRIS FITS plates from IRSA, ISSA field 45 from NASA LAMBDA and AKARI all-sky v2.1 bundles from JAXA DARTS. ## Time-resolved IRAS test

IRSA SCANPI returned 12 individual 60 μm detector timelines and 14 at 100 μm from June 26 through September 1, 1983. The archive's median coadd gives point-source template amplitudes of 0.30 Jy (60 μm) and 0.52 Jy (100 μm), with peak/noise ratios of 7.14 and 6.58. Six 60 μm detector crossings on July 9–10 each give a template amplitude of 0.26–0.53 Jy and a displacement within 0.43′ along the scan. In the last three crossings the native central-source finder returned two missing fits and one 0.22 Jy fit (September 1 at 02:27 UT, archive peak/noise 3.11). Its search radius at 60 μm is only 3.2′. [SCANPI guide](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/applications/Scanpi/docs/overview.html) · [Parameter definitions](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/applications/Scanpi/docs/ScanpiProgramInterface.html)

Of the 3,617 nominal bound orbit grid points, 711 pass a descriptive July consistency cut of |along-scan position| <0.5′ on July 9 at 23:50 UT. By September 1 they predict offsets of −5.28′ to −3.83′ along the detector scans and +0.91′ to +1.72′ perpendicular to them. In a second SCANPI request centered on the WISE galaxy, five common detector profiles from June, July, August and September shift along the scan by the signed amount predicted from their reported sky position angles, to within 0.010′ (0.6″). The cross-scan offset changes by the negative of the projected shift, to within 0.002′. The second request reuses the original photons and serves as an orientation check.

The late 60 μm fits at the fixed position individually give 0.366, 0.179 and 0.228 Jy. The galaxy projects only +0.23′ to +0.27′ along those scans. Its separation from the FSC position is below the 1.5′ in-scan width. After applying the cross-scan sign measured in the recentering test, all July-compatible models cross the three late detectors within 1.76′ of their centers. The nominal 60 μm detector field spans 4.7′ cross-scan, although individual detectors have different widths and response profiles. At 100 μm the three late time groups combined across five detector crossings give 0.309 Jy fixed, and −0.383 to −0.207 Jy following the selected moving models, with a 0.163 Jy control scatter. [IRAS detector dimensions](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/IRASdocs/exp.sup/ch2/C4.html) · [Detector response calibrations](https://lambda.gsfc.nasa.gov/data/interfaces/iras/docs/exp.sup/ch4/A.html)

Without conditioning on the July position, `all_scan_tracks.py` also measured the two interval fluxes on **every one of the 3,617 nominal moving tracks**. The largest value of the smaller of the two interval fluxes was 0.0267 Jy. Zero tracks reached 0.05 Jy in both intervals. Of 902 trajectories measuring at least 0.20 Jy in July, the highest late-interval measurement was 0.0132 Jy. The fixed position yielded 0.281 and 0.231 Jy. These are signed matched-filter amplitudes on the available detector strips. They are a direct test of whether the recovered IRAS profile peaks trace the predicted motion. They are not upper limits on an additional source's intrinsic flux: throughput changes across a detector, and subtracting the local baseline can depress flux beside a stationary peak. Across nine alternative filter fits (Gaussian FWHM 1.2′, 1.5′ and 1.8′ with sky windows of ±3, ±6 and ±9 FWHM), the largest smaller interval flux stays below 0.033 Jy, while the fixed-position late flux spans 0.208–0.268 Jy. Run `python all_scan_tracks.py --orbit-dir /path/to/orbit/directory` for the nominal comparison or `python scan_sensitivity.py --orbit-dir /path/to/orbit/directory` for the nine fits.

A scrambled Sobol draw of 65,536 continuous range, range-rate and effective-date combinations gave 31,686 bound nominal fits. Their highest smaller interval flux was 0.0454 Jy. Adding the FSC 18″ × 7″ Gaussian centroid ellipse and drawing the AKARI centroid uniformly in a 40″ radius disk gave 31,656 bound states and a maximum of 0.0750 Jy. Replacing the effective IRAS observer with an endpoint-weighted coadd at the same mean date gave 31,035 bound states and 0.0730 Jy. The 40″ disk is an explicit stress assumption. These trials support the interpretation that the two bright IRAS time groups trace persistent sky flux even after perturbing the input coordinates. Reproduce them with `all_scan_tracks.py --family sobol`, `--family stress` and `--family stress-coadd`, passing a different `--output` path for each.

The refit uses the actual sky-subtracted SCANPI samples out to ±60′. The flux estimator fits a unit-peak Gaussian in scan coordinates with FWHM of 1.5′ or 3.0′ and a local constant and slope inside ±3 FWHM. The weighted visit means use an inverse variance estimated at 16 common translated positions, ±12′ through ±40′ in steps of 4′, with a 0.04 Jy scatter floor per detector. Applying the same translations to all scans in a visit preserves common spatial structures in the controls. The reported MAD scales describe those controls and have no independent-Gaussian interpretation. The coadds, raw scans, both recenterings and two IRAS image atlases share underlying data. The field contains real sky background. Detector throughput for the displaced object, its flux distribution, the uncertainty in associating the July source with a planet, and astrometric uncertainty beyond the nominal grid preclude a calibrated fraction excluded by these IRAS scans.

Reproduce the numbers with `python verify_scan_sign.py` and `python scan_tracks.py --orbit-dir /path/to/orbit/directory`. The original SCANPI tables are archived in `scanpi/profiles.tar.gz` and `scanpi/galaxy_profiles.tar.gz`, with per-table hashes and HTTP receipts. `python requery_scanpi.py scanpi/request.json` obtains a fresh archive job if the original temporary result URLs have expired. The recentering inputs are in `scanpi/galaxy_request.json`. `track_summary.json` retains both orientation calculations for audit. The empirically validated orientation is `sign+1`.

## AKARI access limits

The complete public FIS BSC v1 was searched at both positions. The JAXA v2 preliminary catalog directory returned HTTP 401, and the JAXA release page requests email registration. The v2 main and supplemental tables were not obtained. Its quoted 0.44 Jy uniform 90 μm sensitivity is also brighter than the published 0.27 Jy single-scan source. `bsc_v2_access.json` records the endpoints. Public coadd photometry and either catalogue cannot substitute for the author's monthly-unconfirmed source determination. ## Possible stationary identification

NED lists the galaxy WISEA J022301.05−483040.5 at (35.75440°, −48.51127°) with photometric redshift 0.066250. Its AllWISE W4 (22 μm) measurement is 6.899 Vega mag at S/N 15.6, with cc_flags 0000. Its separation from the published IRAS position is 32.856″, PA 82.245°. Projecting onto the FSC error ellipse (18″ × 7″, PA 79°) gives an elliptical radius of 1.84, near its major axis. The optical/mid-infrared object can contribute to the far-infrared flux inside the IRAS beam. It remains a proposed counterpart until higher-resolution far-infrared observations or time-resolved astrometry can distinguish it.

The one-degree AllWISE control cone contains 22 sources with W4 S/N ≥10 and W4 magnitude ≤6.899. Its surface density is 7.00 per square degree. For a uniform independent field the expected count inside an ellipse of this size is 0.00073. The threshold and candidate were selected after inspecting the field, and galaxies cluster. This count describes local crowding. It is neither a posterior association probability nor a false-alarm rate for the original sky search. The raw queries and returned rows are in `counterpart_queries.json` and the adjacent text files. [NED redshift flags](https://ned.ipac.caltech.edu/Documents/Guides/Database)
