# Testing the two confirmed warm red continua with all four WISE bands

**Gaia DR3 3017430205815280384 has much more 12 µm light than the single-temperature excess implied by its 3.4 and 4.6 µm fluxes. Gaia DR3 6359523376149191936 has a smaller discrepancy that is sensitive to the assumed photosphere.** Both can be explained by natural emission or blending. The DR1 comparison flags are descriptive, and the published selection already required a WISE excess.

| Gaia DR3 target | Distance | Published fit | AllWISE W1/W2 excess color temperature | AllWISE W2/W3 excess color temperature | Measured W3 / total predicted from W1/W2 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 3017430205815280384 (FGK18) | 708 pc | 550 K | 814 K | 428 K | 5.07 |
| 6359523376149191936 (FGK31) | 529 pc | 530 K | 609 K | 487 K | 1.88 |

W3 is a detection with catalog magnitude uncertainty 0.030 mag in both objects. Replacing the short-band photometry by CatWISE gives W3 ratios of 4.44 and 1.84. Keeping the published temperature fixed and normalizing the excess at W2 gives ratios of 2.28 and 1.30 with AllWISE. W4 has greater uncertainty (0.357 and 0.218 mag). The catalog W4 values and full prediction grid are available for inspection. No W4 datum is treated as a high-confidence color-temperature constraint.

The stellar flux predictions for W1 and W2 come individually from the authors' model, translated from native unWISE to the AllWISE magnitude scale. The central comparison extends a 5500 K stellar Planck spectrum from the predicted W1 flux into W3/W4. For each band pair we subtract the stellar contribution before solving for its excess color temperature. The two-parameter fit to the W1/W2 residuals predicts an independent total W3 flux (star plus excess). A single featureless blackbody sharing the two short-band colors and excess normalization would give a measured/predicted ratio of one. We integrate the photon response at each wavelength and use the published Vega-system isophotal flux zero points. [Published stellar predictions](../literature/fgk_baseline.csv) · [WISE response functions](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/data/WISE/docs/release/All-Sky/expsup/sec4_4h.html) · [Zero points and calibration](https://doi.org/10.1088/0004-6256/140/6/1868)

In a sensitivity test we varied the stellar extrapolation between 4500, 5500 and 6500 K, used either short-band catalog, and independently shifted each W1/W2 photospheric prediction by up to 0.10 mag on a five-point grid. The W3 ratios span 2.41–8.12 (FGK18) and 0.61–3.56 (FGK31). These ranges quantify the stated model choices and carry no confidence level. The bands combine different observation epochs and the short-band predictions have imperfect systematic calibration. Multiple dust temperatures, emissivity structure and superposition are all compatible with these unresolved broadband measurements. Broadband color temperatures cannot classify the emitter.

The two spectra each have two W1/W2-quality comparisons and are redder than both in both DR1 quality masks. Their field-specific results, including independent temperature selection and every undetermined star, are in [all_53.csv](../fgk_spectra/all_53.csv). CatWISE finds one source within 12 arcsec in each neighborhood. Each object had just one qualifying final-year NEOWISE visit per band. Both 15 arcsec SIMBAD queries returned zero rows. ZTF observed FGK18 in two overlapping fields with 33 and 39 qualifying nights per object ID and no night more than 0.041 mag fainter than that field's median. There are zero ZTF r-band rows at FGK31's southern position. Independent SkyMapper g/r observations give 12 unique sampled nights at FGK31 and eight at FGK18. No retained night in either filter had a 0.20 mag dip relative to the sampled median. FGK31 has gaps exceeding 1,000 days.
