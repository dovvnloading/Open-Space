# Blind nearby-star pilot

No excess met the WISE threshold among 404 main-sequence proxies in a 12.565 deg² spherical cap. The overlapping, brighter SPHEREx cohort yielded zero 4.42–4.90 µm excess triggers among 55 stars with usable data and matched controls. These two denominators overlap. A featureless, cold or modestly luminous emitter could pass both screens.

## Completed measurements

The field was fixed at ICRS (180°, +30°), radius 2°, before examining infrared colors. Of 596 Gaia DR3 sources passing the broad distance, G and BP−RP limits, 115 failed the astrometric or color–absolute-magnitude cuts. Another 19 had no AllWISE association, five had ambiguous associations and 53 failed W1 or morphology requirements. All 404 remaining stars had a usable signed W3 measurement and a local comparison set. Of these, 324 also had usable W4 data. W3 reached S/N≥5 for 205 stars. None of the 324 W4 measurements reached S/N≥5. The largest excess significances were 3.23σ in W3 and 2.83σ in W4. Criteria, coordinates, cuts and field selection were recorded in [selection.md](selection.md).

The official Gaia DR3 best-neighbour table supplied the AllWISE designations. Signed AllWISE profile-fit fluxes, including negative measurements, were converted to common units. Comparing bright catalog magnitudes with raw fluxes recovered instrumental magnitude zero points of 18.00006 (W3, 325 checks) and 12.99993 (W4, 13 checks), with all residuals below 0.0006 mag. The per-band photosphere was estimated from the median signed long-band/W1 ratio of 5–25 other field stars matched in Gaia color and W1 magnitude. Residual uncertainties include propagated measurement errors, a robust intrinsic-scatter estimate with a 3% floor and the finite comparison sample. The excess threshold requires ≥5σ band and excess detections and a color excess of at least 0.25 mag. Each association, comparison set and tested star is recoverable from the CSV files. [Gaia DR3 documentation](https://gea.esac.esa.int/archive/documentation/GDR3/) · [AllWISE column definitions](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/data/WISE/docs/release/AllWISE/expsup/sec2_1a.html)

The independent DR1 spectral screen used 58 W1<12 stars inside tile 7119. Of these, 56 had sufficient photometry and blank-sky apertures, and 55 had at least four matched stars. The strict mask and the repeat permitting OUTLIER-flagged pixels each yielded zero triggers. The observable was the ratio of median Fν at 4.42–4.90 µm to 1.51–1.72 µm relative to Gaia-color- and G-matched peers. The detector required a ≥25% increase and ≥5σ measurements of the long band, H anchor and excess. The 19″ aperture, 36–60″ sky annulus and per-target sky apertures were applied to the DR1 cube. Spatial structure, unresolved neighbors and stellar molecular features can affect this ratio. The fixed cohort, cuts, observations and uses of the two quality masks are documented in [dr1_selection.md](dr1_selection.md). [SPHEREx DR1 cube](https://doi.org/10.26131/IRSA679)

## Injection recovery

The WISE screen had 400 within-3σ comparison stars and 334 with a Gaia temperature in the adopted range. A bolometric excess fraction is L_excess/L_star, using the unobscured, photosphere-only luminosity in the denominator. For each measured star, the final code normalizes one stellar Planck spectrum at W1 and adds one blackbody with the same normalization across bands. Photon integrals use the measured W1, W3 and W4 relative spectral responses with the Wright et al. isophotal Fλ zero points. The added W1 light also changes the observed color and comparison-star matching. A preliminary calculation scaled each long-band injection to its local peer median separately; noise in the W4 median could distort the input color. The final single-normalization calculation supersedes those preliminary recoveries. The measured-star selection and excess counts were unchanged by this correction. [WISE calibration and passbands](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/data/WISE/docs/release/All-Sky/expsup/sec4_4h.html) · [Wright et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1088/0004-6256/140/6/1868)

For SPHEREx, 43 real spectra with measured temperatures and modest residuals received added 100–600 K blackbodies, normalized once to the H continuum. Both masks were rerun after adding the spectrum to the measured channel fluxes. At 1% / 10% luminosity the strict DR1 screen recovered 0 / 0 of 43 at 100 K, 0 / 22 at 300 K, and 25 / 43 at 600 K. The temperature and luminosity grids with all recovered counts are in [injections.csv](injections.csv) and [dr1_injections.csv](dr1_injections.csv). These tests reuse measured sky noise and retain fixed catalog membership, quality flags and spatial morphology. Source detection, deblending, stellar atmosphere uncertainty, bandpass systematics for red sources and optical extinction are outside these efficiencies. The quoted fractions describe the tested controls. SPHEREx at ≤5 µm samples the Wien tail of cool emission; W4 depth is a limiting factor for the WISE screen.

## Published classifications across the field

The 404 WISE-tested stars were checked against SIMBAD identities and 12″ neighborhoods, Gaia non-single-star and variability results, all 11,671 Konkoly Optical YSO entries, and both Cotten–Song infrared-excess lists (505 prime and 1,257 reserved entries). One star, TYC 2524-1730-1 (Gaia DR3 4020583280230980224), has a Gaia non-single-star solution and a SIMBAD spectroscopic-binary classification. Its two flags count as one star. Six other sightlines have possible contaminants. Three include separately identified galaxies or a quasar 7.15–11.21″ away. The secure veto leaves 403 stars, and setting the six ambiguous sightlines aside leaves 397. No photometric excess survives. In the overlapping 55-star spectral sample, one sightline lies within 4.31″ of both components of TYC 1987-1955, leaving 54 under the conservative cut. No tested star matched a published KYSO or either Cotten–Song list. SIMBAD exact Gaia IDs exist for 88/404 WISE and 13/55 DR1 stars. The remaining 316 and 42 lack an exact entry and their astrophysical classifications remain incomplete. ## Limits and pending checks

No blind star reached the photometric trigger for additional imaging or optical dimming follow-up. The quality cuts themselves do not resolve background galaxies or all companions. The parent loses faint red dwarfs, saturated W1 sources, poor astrometric solutions and rows with inadequate associations. Catalog upper limits, null triggers, and absence of short-wavelength features do not rule out dust or bound an all-sky population. A deeper search needs more fields and measurements at wavelengths where cold components peak; image-level recovery and source-specific follow-up would be needed for a physical exclusion.

## Reproduce

From this directory, run `python screen.py` and `python dr1_screen.py` with NumPy 2 or newer. The supplied astronomical inputs suffice for offline replay. `catalog_receipts.json` records request URLs, ADQL, UTC, byte sizes and SHA-256 digests; `screen.py` checks those hashes. `stars.adql` is the parent sample query, which has a separate COUNT check. `7119.json` records the tile identity, SHA-256, ETag, size, epochs and WCS coordinates for every aperture. `dr1_spectra_and_blanks.npz` contains wavelengths and, for each Gaia ID, rows for the strict spectrum, OUTLIER-permitted spectrum and 24 local sky aperture spectra, all in mJy. To fetch new copies and repeat the reduction, run `python catalogs.py`, `python dr1_positions.py`, then `python dr1_acquire.py` with Astropy and the adjacent repository extraction scripts available, followed by both screen scripts.
