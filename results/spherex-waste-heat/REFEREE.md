# Independent referee review — SPHEREx stellar waste-heat search

**Disposition: a reproducible, sensitivity-bounded null, not a discovery.** This review used independently executed scripts on the committed input responses and newly downloaded SPHEREx DR1 FITS objects. It does not identify any excess source whose dust, companion or background-source explanations have all been excluded. No `[REMARKABLE]` title or remarkable-findings log entry is warranted.

## Completed review checks

1. **Blind selection and injections.** Running `python results/spherex-waste-heat/blind/screen.py` against the deposited Gaia/AllWISE rows first checks their receipt SHA-256 digests, then reproduces 596 broad Gaia positions, 404 WISE-tested stars, 324 with W4 measurements, **0/404 WISE excess triggers**, and 334 injection controls. The independent SPHEREx screen replays 58 attempted, 56 with adequate photometry, **0/55** with controls and a qualifying trigger, and 43 injection controls. Conservative binary/neighbor vetoes leave **0/397 WISE** and **0/54 SPHEREx**. The two samples overlap. [Selection protocol](blind/README.md) · [Catalog receipts](blind/catalog_receipts.json) · [Vetting counts](blind/classifications/counts.json).

2. **Measured recovery, not a Galaxy-wide rate.** At **1% stellar bolometric luminosity**, WISE recovers **84/334 (25%)**, **202/334 (60%)**, **157/334 (47%)** injected 100/300/600 K continua. Strict SPHEREx DR1 at 4.42–4.90 µm recovers **0/43 (0%)**, **0/43 (0%)**, **25/43 (58%)** at those temperatures. At 10%, WISE recovers 232/334, 334/334, 330/334; SPHEREx 0/43, 22/43, 43/43. These runs use measured control stars, preserve their quality masks and selection and do not quantify source detection or deblending losses. W4 sensitivity is weak (zero of 324 W4 sources at ≥5σ). A cold or faint thermal component cannot be ruled out by a ≤5 µm scan. [WISE injection table](blind/injections.csv) · [SPHEREx injection table](blind/dr1_injections.csv).

3. **Independent WISE and optical reductions.** The archive contains **40 HTTP 200 WISE responses** with matched queries, byte counts, row counts and SHA-256 digests, all verified by a fresh replay of `wise/measure.py` without the legacy-status override. Reconstructed photometry, final-year visits and variability tables match the deposited tables. `wise/within_visit.py` reproduces the W1 excess scatter toward Hephaistos C but cannot attribute it to the star. The ZTF `optical/nightly.py` and `optical/d_science.py` replays match the archived r-band nights and image apertures. D's two faint exposures are real image-level aperture deficits (0.203/0.337 mag for 6 pixels) with uncertain origin; the mid-IR excess at D is independently explained by a galaxy. [WISE query receipts](wise/queries.json) · [ZTF pixel-image receipts](optical/d_science_receipts.json).

4. **Fresh retrieval of the frozen SPHEREx products.** A separate run fetched the original **16 order-5 DR1 tiles** through HTTP byte ranges, compared the FITS SHA-256 digests and metadata with the deposited receipts, and reproduced the four original spectral tables from 47 positions. It also refetched the two tiles containing FGK18 and FGK31, reproducing all six target/comparison positions (612 channels), and independently refetched tile 7119, reproducing the complete 58-position blind spectra and local background-aperture arrays. These replays use the same astronomical observations. [Original tile hashes](spectra/cube_receipts/) · [Warm-field receipts](fgk_spectra/cube_receipts/) · [Blind tile hash](blind/7119.json).

5. **Literature dispositions.** All ten Hephaistos A–J positions have separate evidence-based interpretations with primary citations, and the [53-star FGK ledger](literature/fgk_dispositions.csv) explicitly distinguishes 5 red, 13 below-threshold and 35 indeterminate spectra. A and F and the weaker H/I/J are unresolved; B and C likely have blended infrared light; D and E are resolved background galaxies; G has an offset radio AGN strongly favored for its mid-IR emission. In the FGK list, the only two objects inside 300 pc are published natural disk systems. [Ten positions and original papers](literature/README.md) · [FGK dispositions and limitations](literature/fgk_dispositions.md).

6. **Unresolved warm systems.** FGK18 (Gaia DR3 3017430205815280384, **708 pc**) has a red DR1 continuum and a W3 flux **5.07×** the W1/W2-derived one-temperature prediction. FGK31 (Gaia DR3 6359523376149191936, **529 pc**) has a model-sensitive **1.88×** discrepancy and its AllWISE **12 µm centroid is offset 0.665 arcsec** from Gaia, with nearby Gaia sources. Both have only **one qualifying final-year NEOWISE visit per band**, and the SPHEREx DR1 cube coadds scans, preventing a genuine between-visit variability test. Multi-temperature dust, companions and unresolved background galaxies remain plausible. [SED sensitivity](warm_fgk/WISE_colors.md) · [Native images](fgk_spectra/README.md) · [SkyMapper nights](warm_fgk/skymapper/README.md).

## Remaining scientific checks

Point-source-resolved mid-IR imaging/spectroscopy for ambiguous objects, stellar-atmosphere SED uncertainty, genuine independent temporal sampling and complete source-association vetting are **not finished**. The 19-arcsec SPHEREx aperture cannot separate subarcsecond galaxies from M dwarfs, and low Gaia RUWE is not proof that a companion is absent. A scan-coadded DR1 spectrum is not an independent series of visits. Faint 100–300 K excesses can escape the measured 1%-luminosity SPHEREx screen entirely. The finite 12.565-square-degree pilot yields no Galaxy-wide occurrence bound.
