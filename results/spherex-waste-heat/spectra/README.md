# SPHEREx DR1 aperture spectra

None of the observed features identifies the excess as intrinsic to a star. The seven original Hephaistos fields have 99–100 populated channels each when propagated OUTLIER bits are allowed. The stricter aperture mask retains 27–96 channels per field (492 total). The DR1 cube pixel area is equivalent to a 12.88 arcsec square, much larger than the angular separations of several known contaminants. The 19 arcsec radius spectra measure the combined light inside the aperture. [IRSA DR1 cube, DOI 10.26131/IRSA679](https://doi.org/10.26131/IRSA679) · [SPHEREx Explanatory Supplement, sections 2.5 and 3.4](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/data/SPHEREx/docs/SPHEREx_Expsupp.pdf)

## Measurements

The 47 extracted positions comprise Hephaistos A–J, five Contardo & Hogg FGK stars, two Gaia-matched comparisons for each of these 15 published targets, and one independently chosen blind-field pair. FGK1–3 are the first three published table entries. FGK4–5 are the only two additional entries with Gaia parallax above 1000/300 mas. The ecliptic scans are coadded in the cube and individual observing dates vary by tile. See `positions*.csv` for exact IDs and motion-propagated ICRS positions, and `cube_receipts/` for the complete FITS object URLs, byte counts, timestamps, ETags, SHA-256 hashes and pixel coordinates. The W1 check integrates the reduced cube spectrum through the published per-photon response, dividing by the response integral for a flat Fν. It uses the less restrictive mask for spectral coverage, linear interpolation, a 309.54 Jy zero point for AllWISE, and no source-specific WISE color or SPHEREx aperture correction. The A–J ratios span 0.817–1.155. Broadband agreement gives a check on tile orientation and surface-brightness units, with room for substantial aperture, epoch and source-level systematics. [WISE calibration](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/data/WISE/docs/release/All-Sky/expsup/sec4_4h.html)

**Observed spectral contrasts.** The E sightline has a strict 3.293 µm excess of +0.430 mJy relative to a linear local continuum of 1.279 mJy. The empirical sky scatter is 0.092 mJy from 19 off-target apertures. Only one channel lies in the central window. JWST resolves a background galaxy at z = 0.410. Its rest-frame 3.29 µm PAH complex would be observed at 4.64 µm. At that wavelength the strict extraction gives +0.297 mJy, with sky scatter 0.153 mJy (1.89 local scatter units after subtracting the blank median). Permitting OUTLIER gives +0.245 mJy and 0.296 mJy, respectively. The expected wavelength also overlaps stellar CO. For D at z = 0.922 the same band would be at 6.32 µm, beyond DR1. [JWST/MIRI D and E](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09460)

The putative 4.27 µm absorption contrast in D is dominated by a single unphysical, background-subtracted −8.42 mJy channel at 4.257 µm. Neighboring channels are positive. I's apparent CO-region excess is dominated by one +8.86 mJy channel at 4.635 µm, compared with neighboring fluxes around 1 mJy. These fail a spectral-coherence check. A's 4.27 µm depression (−0.652 mJy versus a 3.899 mJy local continuum) persists under both masks. Its 0.122 mJy local sky scatter excludes uncertainties from discretely sampled PSFs, source photon noise and the stellar spectrum. The ratio alone does not establish an ice band. `feature_tests.csv` contains every window for the candidates and comparisons, including strict windows with missing results. Broad molecular absorption can arise in M dwarfs and the archive flags scan-dependent stripes and spatial/spectral mixing. A smooth spectrum cannot exclude dust.

**Continuum and controls.** `continuum.csv` records the median flux density in 1.51–1.72, 3.16–3.62 and 4.42–4.90 µm windows. The last-to-first ratio tests red continuum against the observed stellar light. With the inclusive mask A gives 0.269, compared with 0.25 and 0.28 for its two field comparison stars. D gives 0.40 compared with 0.23 and 0.41. The nearer FGK4 source gives 0.29 compared with 0.19 and 0.16, consistent with a redder aperture spectrum. The two bright FGK fields have informative independent identifications. FGK4 (Gaia DR3 3183786280737192704) is TIC 43488669, a published optical dipper with an extreme debris disk interpretation. Its strict ratio is 0.289 ± 0.005 (sky only), against 0.189 ± 0.005 and 0.162 ± 0.006 in two W1–W3-clean comparisons. It fails the quiet-star criterion. FGK5 (Gaia DR3 2942418533272205312) is TYC 5940−1510−1, previously identified as a warm extreme debris disk. Follow-up 10 µm photometry found a silicate-like feature, and the two published blackbody temperatures for FGK4 and FGK5 in the Contardo & Hogg table are 815 and 835 K, respectively, above the specified 600 K upper bound. The prior identifications are independent of the DR1 extraction. [Tajiri et al.](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4365/abbc17) · [Greenberg](https://doi.org/10.3847/2515-5172/ae7322) · [Moór et al., Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00568) · [10 µm follow-up](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stae155)

The aperture comparisons alone cannot determine whether the infrared light comes from a disk, companion or superposed source. Neighboring field stars were matched before inspecting infrared data by Gaia G, BP−RP and absolute G. AllWISE quality and colors are reported individually in `comparison_wise.csv`. Of the 30 comparisons, five have quality-passing photospheric W1–W3 colors, twenty have photospheric W1–W2 colors with W3 unconstrained, and five have ambiguous quality or color. Do not use an ambiguous counterpart as a verified normal comparator. The field pilot has its own photometric controls in the blind analysis.

## Extraction and replication

1. From the results directory's `spectra/` subdirectory, run `python select_fields.py`, `python select_fields.py --fgk`, and `python select_pilot.py`. These use the target lists in `../literature/` and the blind sample in `../blind/`. Exact Gaia ADQL and response digests are in `gaia_queries*.json`. Positions are propagated from J2016.0 to J2025.9 using Gaia μα* and μδ. Changing from this representative epoch to an individual DR1 frame makes only a small fraction of a cube pixel for these targets.
2. With NumPy and Astropy installed, run `python read_cubes.py /path/to/cache --positions positions.csv` and repeat for `positions_fgk.csv` and `positions_pilot.csv`. The code uses the repository's nested HEALPix routine and archive tile URLs. It verifies Content-Range, ETag, tile identity, image units and WCS. Each tile is read once per position list, in sequential byte ranges, to measure multiple widely separated stars and their local sky on the same tile.
3. Run `python photometry.py /path/to/cache`. Subpixel WCS quadrature gives fractional weights for a 19 arcsec circular aperture. Subtract the median sky from a 36–60 arcsec annulus. Each order-14 pixel subtends 4π/(12 × 16384²) sr. The output is integrated aperture flux density, in mJy. An aperture needs ≥97% weighted valid area, ≥12 finite sky pixels, and near-complete geometric source coverage. The strict extraction requires positive NHITS, finite brightness, and flags zero or SOURCE alone. The alternative also permits propagated OUTLIER (bit 19). Dichroic channels 51 and 52 are all-NaN by design.
4. `channel_spectra.csv` contains all 102 archived channel centers per position, both masks, empirical sky scatter and blank counts. Up to 24 identically sized off-target apertures sample the same neighborhood. Sky-only uncertainties are 1.4826 × MAD with ≥8 eligible apertures, using the inclusive flag mask so that sparse bit-by-bit overlap does not erase the control data. Off-target photometry includes confusion. It cannot calibrate wavelength-dependent point-source losses. Feature windows require ≥half their nominal channel counts in each sideband and center. The centers and actual signed contrasts are retained before any interpretation. Local scatter units have no global significance interpretation. The color-error column comes from perturbing the measured spectrum by each off-target spectrum and remeasuring the median color. The calculation propagates correlated local sky residuals.
5. Run `python check_controls.py` for PM-aware AllWISE comparison-star associations and `python w1_validation.py` after the candidate AllWISE tables in `../wise/archive/` have been obtained. `comparison_queries.json` and `w1_response.json` hold the request URLs and input digests.

## Limits and pending checks

Deblended level-2 forced photometry with explicit companion and background positions, time-resolved confirmation of individual channels, empirical aperture corrections, and source-specific stellar-atmosphere fits would be needed to attribute a feature to one emitter. W3/W4 and longer-wavelength spectra are essential for the colder models. The reported near-infrared aperture data do not rule out a cold component or determine a population prevalence.

This publication makes use of data products from the Spectro-Photometer for the History of the Universe, Epoch of Reionization and Ices Explorer (SPHEREx), which is a joint project of the Jet Propulsion Laboratory and the California Institute of Technology, and is funded by the National Aeronautics and Space Administration.
