# Literature screen for HD 53143 and HD 38206

The bright HD 53143 field source already has a published background classification. Published HD 38206 disk and planet-sculpting studies establish prior imaging and model predictions. Neither field yielded an identified publication claiming a confirmed substellar companion at the positions examined in this follow-up.

The pre-reduction screen on 17 November 2026 checked the merged MAST PaperTrack replies for programs 3973, 8142 and 6012 (zero indexed papers for each), object-linked imaging references, and targeted ADS/arXiv/publisher and observing-program searches with the HD, HIP and GJ/HR names, the program numbers, and companion, candidate, background, disk and JWST terms. The complete SIMBAD object-linked bibliography subsequently retrieved by Gaia DR3 identifier is frozen with query and response hashes. SIMBAD returned 192 and 123 references respectively. Program and object indexing can lag and neither is exhaustive. The recent releases warrant a new search before asserting priority.

## HD 53143

Identifiers include HIP 33690, GJ 260 and Gaia DR3 5479222240596469632. Wahhaj et al. measured NICI candidate 1 at 4.479 ± 0.009 arcsec, PA 286.2 ± 0.2 degrees in 2009.03. The second epoch and the strong differential motion favor the stationary background interpretation. Bonavita et al. explicitly identified the same source as background in the COPAINS survey. This is a measured historical position and classification. [Wahhaj et al., Tables 6–7](https://doi.org/10.1088/0004-637X/773/2/179) · [Bonavita et al., target notes](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stac1250)

HST GO 9475/ACS (September 2004), the multiepoch STIS program and the 2021 STIS GO 16202 observations reveal the extended disk. Stark et al. mask a southeast background object. MacGregor et al. distinguish the stellar millimeter source, an asymmetric outer belt and interior residual emission. Those structures are prior disk detections. [HST/ACS observation](https://science.nasa.gov/asset/hubble/circumstellar-debris-disk-hd-53143/) · [Stark et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07370) · [MacGregor et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05856)

The NIRCam planet-search observations belong to GO 3973 and the later MIRI F2550W direct imaging to GO 8142 (JEDIS). The MIRI observation date is 9 November 2025, while target exposures became public on 10 November 2026. The offset background field is a separate pointing. The very wide WDS system TOK 507 is outside the coronagraphic annulus. [Cycle 2 observing programs](https://www.stsci.edu/jwst/science-execution/approved-programs/general-observers/cycle-2-go) · [Cycle 4 observing programs](https://www.stsci.edu/jwst/science-execution/approved-programs/general-observers/cycle-4-go)

## HD 38206

Identifiers include HIP 26966, HR 1975 and Gaia DR3 2967287596507432704. Gemini/NICI measured H-band sensitivities and VLT/NaCo surveyed the star. HST/ACS HRC imaged it on 1 November 2005 (GO 10539) and reported a disk nondetection. Later VLT/SPHERE scattered-light imaging resolved the disk. Engler et al. report a radius near 144 au, inclination near 87 degrees and PA near 85 degrees. The ALMA 1.3 mm ring/gap analysis infers possible planet configurations under assumptions about clearing. Its 0.7 Jupiter-mass outer planet estimate is a dynamical prediction. [Wahhaj et al.](https://doi.org/10.1088/0004-637X/773/2/179) · [Rameau et al.](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201220984) · [Krist et al., Table 1](https://openknowledge.nau.edu/368/7/Krist_JE_etal_2010_Hst_and_spitzer_observations_hd_207129%281%29.pdf) · [Booth et al.](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/staa3362) · [Engler et al.](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202554953)

A targeted DIVA+ search for HIP 26966 returned three curated reductions. Two Keck/NIRC2 Ks epochs (4 November 2008 and 2 November 2009) and one VLT/NaCo L-prime epoch (27 November 2009) each have zero catalogued detections. The archive metadata and source-characterization queries are frozen in the DIVA tables. The independent Gemini/NICI pixels and published completeness constrain the actual historical JWST loci. [DIVA+](https://data.lam.fr/divap/)

The JWST GO 6012 coronagraphic observations include F210M and F444W. HD 32309 is the PSF reference. The 23 January and 24 October 2025 science images use markedly different detector orientations, which allows a test for detector-fixed peaks. [GO 6012](https://www.stsci.edu/jwst-program-info/program/?program=6012)
