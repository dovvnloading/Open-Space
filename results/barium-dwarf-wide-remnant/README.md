# A candidate wide white-dwarf tertiary to the barium dwarf HD 72223

HD 72223 contains a barium-enriched star and an unseen companion on a 249.23-day orbit. A second, **resolved white-dwarf candidate, WDJ083100.66−190310.14, shares the system's motion at 17.82 arcseconds**, or 2,427 au in projection using the inner orbit's parallax. This supplies a candidate evolved tertiary in a specific barium-star sample for which Rekhi et al. reported no wide companions. The authors explicitly noted reduced sensitivity beyond G ≈ 19. The candidate has Gaia DR3 G = 20.61. [Rekhi et al., §4.3 and Table C2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13413)

The inner orbit, stellar chemistry, visual pair and outer object's white-dwarf classification are all prior measurements. The contribution here is identifying the two-remnant architecture **in the enriched sample**, testing it with the primary's orbital astrometric solution, and checking the fainter object's earlier positions against independent Pan-STARRS exposures. The outer component has no radial velocity or spectroscopic classification in the evidence here. A bound triple remains an interpretation.

## Selection and prior work

An initial GALAH DR4 heavy-element screen of wide pairs pointed to the already-published barium star. This prompted a retrospective test of the published barium-star sample. Tables C1 and C2 of Rekhi et al. contain 30 newly analyzed systems (including chemically ordinary controls) and 25 previously reported enriched systems. An exact integer-ID join on **both sides** of the El-Badry et al. catalog gives six wide-pair records. Only the HD 72223 row is flagged WDMS. All 55 input IDs and all six matches, including the one with chance-alignment statistic above one, are retained. The counts are conditional on those catalogs' selections and do not estimate a tertiary occurrence rate. Another row has an unclassified companion. [55 source identifiers](inputs/rekhi_ids.csv) · [All six matches and their comparison statistics](measurements.json)

Rekhi et al. give the bright component [Ba/Fe] = +1.12 ± 0.04, [Y/Fe] = +1.22 ± 0.05, and an inner companion mass of 0.60 ± 0.01 solar masses. GALAH DR4 recovers +1.11 and +1.02 and measures [La/Fe] = +1.29 and [Ce/Fe] = +1.07. Its row has zero stellar and per-element quality flags for these quantities. The GALAH releases reuse HERMES spectroscopy. Enrichment supports prior mass transfer in the inner system. It does not identify which companion supplied the material. [GALAH DR4](https://www.galah-survey.org/dr4/the_catalogues/) · [Frozen target row](inputs/galah_target.csv)

The older wide-pair record and white-dwarf catalog already contain the two visual components. Hallakoun et al. tabulated wide companions to their separately selected Gaia K/M binary sample. Neither HD 72223 Gaia ID occurs in their Table 2. We found no earlier explicit target-level identification of this visual white dwarf as the tertiary of the 249-day barium system in the sources examined. This literature check cannot establish historical priority. Hwang (2023) had already combined Gaia NSS astrometry and wide-pair searches to study triple populations. Its astrometric-binary selection used the `Orbital` solution type. HD 72223 has `AstroSpectroSB1`. [Hwang, §2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02257)

A spectrum of the faint source could test its white-dwarf classification. Independent relative astrometry and a WD radial velocity with gravitational-redshift correction would tighten the test of binding. [Hallakoun et al., Table 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17145)
