# Prior reports and the scope of the new result

On 2026-11-16 we checked the discovery circular for (556068) 2014 JR80, Johnston's Archive, object-name queries in OpenAlex and indexed literature, and the repository's main and parallel research. Search strings included `"2014 JR80" "mutual orbit"`, `"2014 JR80" satellite mutual orbit mass Rubin`, the numbered designation 556068 with the same terms, and the name with the candidate 103-day period. The OpenAlex exact-name full-text query returned one work, *From planetesimals to dwarf planets by pebble accretion*, published in 2025. Searches by name can miss a table in a paper that does not name the object in its indexed metadata; unpublished analyses are inaccessible to this check.

Proudfoot, Grundy, Ragozzine, Holler, Rommel, Fernandez-Valenzuela and Gomez-Limon discovered the companion. Their 2026 September 14 telegram gives HST astrometry and a flux ratio for June 13 and reports six ground-based epochs from 2019–23. Those epochs established that the companion is real. Their circular gives a range of angular separations for the earlier epochs. It does not give their vectors, an orbit fit or system mass. The fetched circular's SHA-256 was `8f8e794477936305cc38188c82c869789e2611b189a55bf1d891208269e9071b`. [CBET 5739](http://www.cbat.eps.harvard.edu/iau/cbet/005700/CBET005739.txt)

Johnston's secondary-orbit entry gives a 24,300 km projected separation, a 150-day period flagged as estimated, and unknown mass and eccentricity. The measured heliocentric period in the same catalog is a different orbit. [Johnston's Archive for 2014 JR80](https://johnstonsarchive.net/astro/astmoons/am-556068.html)

The contribution here is the relative motion measured in 2026 ALeRCE-selected Rubin exposures, its prediction from independent telescope data, and quantitative constraints on the mutual orbit and mass. Several historical exposures can overlap the discovery team's pooled ground observations. We claim no new companion, first ground-based detection, or novel physical class.
