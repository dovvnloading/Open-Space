# LYACAL exposure identity and scattering sensitivity

The primary dark comparison has been run. This follow-up uses the distinct LYACAL exposure sets and tests an empirical scattering subtraction before examining the template-corrected line. The six October 2007 sky candidates match the published exposure lengths and mean Galactic direction. The ten open 3,600-second 2023 LYACAL exposures match the comparison sample in Table 4 by integration time, size and pointing. Extract their boresights from FITS headers. Record the first MET in the Table 2 LYACAL row separately, with its integration, Galactic coordinate and separation from the two-epoch template pointings. Subtract the exposure-weighted mean raw histogram rates for 2007 and 2023 on the native detector grid. Normalize their difference by the difference in Stem Lyα rates measured over a single fixed detector mask. Build that mask from the median 2023 wavelength map and the 1,195–1,235 Å window. Nothing is regridded in the subtraction. Report 2007 versus 2023 wavelength-map offsets over that native mask and near the lines as a calibration diagnostic. The same pooled dark cancels in the difference. As a separate sensitivity check use the three contemporary 2007 darks and the compatible 2023 LYACAL darks for separate dark backgrounds.

Project the template into each science exposure's fixed line fit and scale it by that exposure's bracket-subtracted Lyα rate. Propagate Poisson counting variance through the ratio by a first-order derivative, with the same template applied to all science fields. Report the common template error separately from the conditional science count error. Verify that the science and template integrations are disjoint by MET. Report half-sequence template choices with three older and five newer sky exposures on each side of the time split. The trial estimates share inputs. Mean boresights and their offsets describe pointing differences. Full slit-footprint overlap and roll are outside this calculation. The procedure is in count space with its own fixed Lyα normalization and models only scattering proportional to the chosen Lyα count rate. The measured contrasts precede dead-time and effective-area corrections.
