# Detector controls for the New Horizons Alice 1,135 Å feature

The observing unit is a time-tagged Alice histogram exposure. Murthy et al. reported a diffuse feature near 1,135 Å in the narrow Stem aperture, consistent with neutral nitrogen. Their published extraction subtracts a pooled 2023 dark spectrum and an empirical heliospheric Lyα scattering template. This audit asks how the count-space contrast changes when each exposure uses its two bracketing darks. The observing geometry and published feature fix the wavelength in advance. This is a reanalysis of shared observations. [2025 reduction and field definitions](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00787v1) · [2026 line report](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.20942v1) · [June 2026 PDS collection](https://doi.org/10.26007/kb7p-pq79)

## Population and controls

Select the 2023 survey fields listed in the 2026 Stem analysis. Recover the NCOB, DCAL and SHOCK sequences from their Table 2 start times in the reduction paper. Leave out the 2021 field for the 2023-only test. Retain every planned observation identity, and record any missing, saturated, mismatched or wrongly configured exposure before evaluating contrasts. Identify sky and dark integrations by the instrument aperture-door state. Match darks with the same histogram configuration and use the closest before and after within the observing sequence. The first and last darks in a sequence can bound multiple sky exposures. Preserve dark weights so each reused dark contributes once with its combined coefficient in the statistical error.

Use detector rows 6 through 15 inclusive for the Stem on the native product grid, following the published reduction. Split the ten rows into two equal groups as a spatial check. Inspection of the product's instrument geometry must establish the indexing convention and verify two off-aperture row groups before extraction. Those same-detector groups can carry scattered light. Treat them as checks for detector structure, not measurements of blank sky. Use disjoint northern and southern survey fields to evaluate sensitivity of the aggregate to the footprint. The split repeats the published field grouping.

## Fixed contrasts

Use the integer raw-product histograms and the two-dimensional wavelength maps from their exact calibrated counterparts. Check data units, axes and wavelength mapping against the FITS and PDS labels. Estimate a local linear continuum and fixed Gaussian at 1,134.6 Å. Use 9 Å FWHM for the main diagnostic and 15 Å for the width sensitivity check. Include the core within 0.8 FWHM and continuum sidebands 1.6 to four FWHM from line center. Fit the selected pixels jointly in each detector row, then average the row contrasts within each aperture. Use ordinary least squares so the contrast weights depend only on the released wavelength map. Also test a quadratic baseline. Compare 1,549.5 Å as a published narrow-line positive comparison, the intense illuminated Lyα core between 1,195 and 1,235 Å as a pointing and wavelength check, and 830 Å below the Lyman limit as a dark-plus-scatter comparison. Keep the line and controls even if they show weak or negative residuals.

Compare linear midpoint interpolation between the nearest preceding and succeeding closed exposures in the same field with the exposure-weighted pool of compatible closed exposures in the selected fields. Require the previous dark to end before the sky starts and the next dark to start after the sky ends. Use the same matched sky exposures and wavelengths in both analyses. Report a rate contrast with count propagation, the effect of shared dark images and the change induced by the alternate subtraction. Report field-to-field variation separately from formal count errors. These measurements concern count contrast. Absolute nitrogen flux and location require further calibration.

An empirical Lyα template can be tested separately if the matching 2007 and 2023 calibration observations are available and identified by positions and setup. Use the published same-sightline difference scaled by a consistently defined Lyα normalization. Keep the empirical template branch separate from the dark-subtraction comparison. The spatial match and dark stability are assumptions to test. A local continuum does not remove a narrow scatter residual at the line position.

On-chip differences can falsify a simple sky-only interpretation. A result confined to the published Stem aperture and insensitive to measured dark variation supports repeatability in these detector data. Identifying nitrogen or locating its origin requires evidence that also distinguishes astrophysical emission from Lyα scattering or fixed instrumental structure.
