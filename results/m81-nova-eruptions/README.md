# Two M81 nova eruptions separated by 19.6 years at one position

M81N 2000-12a and M81N 2020-07a are a candidate recurrent nova. Two July 2020 images from the independent Rozhen follow-up place the eruption within **0.15 arcsec in R and 0.27 arcsec in Hα** of the source seen in December 2000. A third image, taken two days earlier at another observatory, gives a larger 0.62-arcsec offset. The two catalogue discovery dates are 7,145.23 days (19.56 years) apart. Their true eruption dates are less precisely known.

Kamil Hornoch and colleagues discovered both transients and identified them as nova candidates. Kurtenkov and Donchev independently detected hydrogen emission in 2020. The contribution here is the proposed association of the two eruptions, a measurement on four published image panels, and a test of how often the catalogue produces a coincidence as good as this one. A spectrum establishing the transient class for both events, or astrometry resolving a common progenitor, would strengthen the identification. [2000 eruption, Table 6](https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.5276) · [Ondřejov discovery](https://www.astronomerstelegram.org/?read=13889) · [Rozhen observations](https://www.astronomerstelegram.org/?read=13908)

## The catalogue search

The frozen MPE/Pietsch catalogue contains 249 entries through May 2022. The search excludes the red variable flagged “no M81 nova” and the 2020-02a row with a malformed right ascension. Of 29,498 eligible pairs of entries more than 90 days apart, this is the closest at 0.659 arcsec. The next closest pairs are 2003-06a/2020-11b (1.178 arcsec) and 1950-12a/2008-03f (2.011 arcsec). The contribution makes no source association for either. Restoring 2020-02a at the independently measured position of 09h55m48.50s, +69°07′33.2″ gives 248 entries and 29,742 pairs. The closest added pair is 28.568 arcsec away and the ranking of the leading pair is unchanged. Only the leading MPE pair received image follow-up here. [Independent 2020-02a astrometry](https://www.astronomerstelegram.org/?read=13440) [MPE catalogue](https://www.mpe.mpg.de/~m31novae/opt/m81/M81_table.html) · [All 247 accepted entries](catalogue.tsv)

The position lies 3.6 arcmin from the M81 nucleus. Within 60 arcsec of the 2020 position, the catalogue contains two other earlier eruptions besides the proposed counterpart. Scaling the annular area to the measured catalogue offset gives 0.00024 expected unrelated earlier entries for this *one position*. Using 30 or 120 arcsec gives 0.00048 or 0.00073. These density estimates are noisy in the outer field.

To account for selecting the closest pair in the whole catalogue, displace each later event by the same 5, 10 or 20 arcsec at 720 equally spaced position angles and repeat the eligible comparison with earlier positions. The fractions of directions giving at least one equally close pair anywhere are **18.6%, 30.6% and 28.9%**, respectively. Ranking each pair by its offset divided by the distance to the eleventh closest earlier event still puts this pair first. Recomputing that density-scaled rank under the same shifts gives equal-or-better coincidence fractions of 4.3%, 9.4% and 19.3%. Using the sixth and twenty-first neighbors gives the same top-ranked observed pair. These displaced catalogues share entries and sky structure. The fractions describe the control experiment and do not give the probability that the two eruptions share a star.

An exploratory comparison with Rochester's additional 2025 M81 entries also found the 0.83-arcsec 2013-03a/2025-04a pair. Hornoch et al. had already demonstrated that those two novae are distinct on difference images. That example shows why subarcsecond catalogue separation alone cannot assign recurrence. In a parallel M33 cross-check, the close 2021-08a/2025-10a match had already been identified and confirmed by Shafter and colleagues. Neither pair contributes to the numerical M81 screen above. [Distinct M81 eruptions](https://www.astronomerstelegram.org/?read=17154) · [M33 recurrent nova](https://www.astronomerstelegram.org/?read=17435) · [Rochester compilation](https://www.rochesterastronomy.org/snimages/novanameall.html)

## Registering the sky independently of the reported coordinates

The astrometry uses the original observers' published finding charts. For each image, a rough position, rotation and scale locate Gaia DR3 reference stars. A circular Gaussian plus a sloping background estimates their centroids. The fit excludes chart marks, edges, the transient's immediate vicinity and obvious detector streaks. Position residual thresholds of 1.2, 0.75 and 0.55 arcsec reject mismatches from a six-coefficient affine fit. Proper motions, where measured, propagate Gaia positions from 2016.0 to each observing epoch. The Subaru and Ondřejov transient fits use the same Gaussian model. The two stretched Rozhen panels have clipped point-source cores, so their transient positions use a radius-six-pixel background-subtracted centre of light. The two bands share a date, observatory and comparison field. [Gaia DR3 archive](https://gea.esac.esa.int/archive/) · [Reference centroids](references.tsv) · [Transient centroids](targets.tsv)

Coordinates use east-positive ICRS right ascension and north-positive declination. The table displays west and south offsets about 09h55m54.39s, +69°00′50.2″ for convenience. Each held-out residual removes one retained reference star and predicts its sky position. Those residuals characterize reference-star registration after clipping. They do not include all transient-centroid systematics. The 0.62″ difference on the shallower Ondřejov chart, image stretches, colour-dependent backgrounds and the larger field of that telescope limit the precision achievable here. The public Hubble Source Catalog v3 lists three distinct MatchIDs within 0.5″ of the 2000 position in quiescence. Ground-based images cannot distinguish a single progenitor from neighboring binaries. HSC match identifiers may also split detections across observations. [Hubble Source Catalog](https://archive.stsci.edu/hst/hsc/) · [HSC field measurements](hubble.tsv)

## What the light measurements establish

The 2000 source rose above a prior V-band limit of 22.4 and was measured at V = 21.93 and B ≈ 23.2 on 24 December. The Isaac Newton Telescope subsequently recorded it at Hα = 20.76 at JD 2451912.626. The MPE catalogue flags this as Hα-confirmed. The published 2000 measurements lack a simultaneous continuum measurement, so an equivalent width or a 2000 Hα excess cannot be derived here. Later data include V > 24.0. In July 2020, Ondřejov measured an unfiltered magnitude of 20.2 ± 0.3 on the 17th and 19.6 ± 0.15 on the 22nd after a nondetection on the 14th. On the 24th, Rozhen measured R = 19.27 ± 0.03 and Hα = 18.16 ± 0.03 with a 3-nm filter, independently identifying enhanced line emission. The observations sampled different bands and phases. Neither light curve establishes a repeat interval or a matching decline rate. [Hornoch et al., Table 6](https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.5276) · [ATel 13889](https://www.astronomerstelegram.org/?read=13889) · [ATel 13908](https://www.astronomerstelegram.org/?read=13908)

A decisive next measurement would register the original linear 2000 and 2020 science frames against a common Hubble field with proper-motion correction and image subtraction. The 2000 Subaru visit includes 200-second exposures SUPE00028160 and SUPE00028180 on 24 December. The source-association hypothesis predicts the same residual position at both epochs. A repeatable differential offset larger than the measured local and injected-source uncertainties would disfavor it. The published images alone leave two unrelated novae or other recurrent outbursts possible. [CADC Subaru observation metadata](https://www.cadc-ccda.hia-iha.nrc-cnrc.gc.ca/en/search/) · [Subaru legacy archive](https://www.cadc-ccda.hia-iha.nrc-cnrc.gc.ca/en/scla/)

## Reproduction and provenance

Run the following in this directory with Python 3, NumPy, SciPy, Pillow and lxml. The default uses the committed numerical measurements. The second command downloads the primary images and catalogues into an adjacent `inputs` directory, verifies their fingerprints, rebuilds the tables and repeats the analysis. Gaia/HSC CSV hashes use LF line endings and sorted data rows because TAP and cone searches may vary their row order. The script records the exact source URLs and queries. The image files remain with their providers. [Analysis](analysis.py) · [Input hashes](source-hashes.tsv)

```sh
python analysis.py
python analysis.py --from-images
```

The research literature check on 14 November 2026 used the names “M81N 2000-12a”, “M81N 2020-07a”, “PNV J09555439+6900502” and their combinations with “recurrent nova” in web-indexed papers and Astronomer's Telegram reports. The 2008 paper, the two 2020 observing reports and the MPE entries found in that search identify the eruptions individually. No prior association surfaced in that search. Both eruptions and the original photometry remain discoveries of their respective observers. The two comparison pairs discussed above have explicit prior identifications.
