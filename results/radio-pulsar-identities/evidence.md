# Independent archival checks for PSR J1611−01 and J1611−0114

## Green Bank pulses

Karako-Argaman et al. (2015), Table 2 gives the 350 MHz discovery, and Table 5 gives 51 ± 36 pulses per hour in the approximately 2-minute discovery observation and 60 ± 19 per hour in 10 minutes of 350-MHz follow-up. With the stated Poisson counting convention these are consistent with two and ten detected pulses. The 15-minute LOFAR pointing was blank. The paper has no published J1611 individual arrival times or single-pulse diagram. Section 4.3 says they first use a greatest common divisor and refine the period with TEMPO2 in a single observation. A signal that emits only once per 2.594 s still fits timestamps on a 1.297-s grid if they occupy only one parity. A true greatest common divisor of such pulse-to-pulse intervals would be *at least* 2.594 s. Sparse observations alone do not explain a period half the fundamental under an exact GCD. Check actual burst TOAs and both parities. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.05170

The current RRATalog leaves Pepoch blank. ATNF supplies 56657, an epoch shared by 46 entries, including other 2015 drift-scan RRATs. DSS's complete UTC schedule for 2014-01-01 (MJD 56657) lists no RRAT visit. Its one pulsar project targeted J1643−1224. https://dss.gb.nrao.edu/schedule/public?tz=UTC&start=01/01/2014&days=1

The original survey was in the northern summer of 2007. For the follow-up, an observatory lead is GBT12A-433, V. Kaspi (PI), C. Karako (co-I), specifically approved for follow-ups of the 2007 drift-scan RRATs. Its eight sessions at 0.29–0.40 GHz in the 190 days starting 2012-02-01 were 2012-02-23 13:00–14:15, 02-25 13:30–14:45, 02-26 14:45–16:00, 02-27 13:30–14:45, 03-03 13:30–14:45, 04-03 11:30–12:45, 04-26 10:00–12:00, 05-27 07:45–09:00 UTC. These are program-level records. A point-by-point scan log or observer data are needed for attribution. GBO says the pulsar teams have their own distribution routes. https://dss.gb.nrao.edu/project/GBT12A-433/public and https://dss.gb.nrao.edu/schedule/public/printerFriendly?tz=UTC&start=02/01/2012&days=190 and https://www.gb.nrao.edu/scienceDocs/Archive/

## Independent low-frequency follow-up

Taylor et al. 2016 list a 2-hour 2014-01-08, 72-MHz LWA1 observation under project LM002, with no reported detection of J1611−01. The public LDAdb project list omits LM002, and the public Pulsar Archive index does not list J1611. https://doi.org/10.3847/0004-637X/831/2/140 and https://lda10g.alliance.unm.edu/ldadb/observations/search/ and https://lda10g.alliance.unm.edu/PulsarArchive/

## FAST follow-up and pulse morphology

Zhou et al. 2023 Table 7 reports a 5-minute nondetection for J1611−01 in J1611−0100-M01 on 2021-12-10, under PT2021_0151. The FAST DR12 official telescope log row 766 puts its nominal center at 16:11:00, −01:28:00, about 13.15 arcmin from the precise timing location. It records all 19 beams in SnapShot mode, 4113 GB. The normal FAST snapshot program uses four translated pointings. Its nominal 3.0 arcmin steps and roughly 6 arcmin pitch mean that an outer beam could illuminate the proposed position; exact coordinates, beam rotation, per-beam gains and per-beam searches are unverified. Both a coverage claim and an exclusion by the boresight offset are unsupported. Archive transfer currently requires an emailed application. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.17279 and https://nadc.china-vo.org/res/r100693/?lang=en and https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.08460 and https://english.nao.cas.cn/newsevents/newsupdates/202610/t20261026_1209335.html

The separate 2021-01-31 PT2020_0164 log row 1980 is a single beam1 targeted tracking observation centered on the old position. File size 114 GB. No pulse result recovered for it. https://fast.bao.ac.cn/static/uploadfiles/FASTDocument/%E5%BC%80%E6%94%BE%E6%95%B0%E6%8D%AE%E8%A7%82%E6%B5%8B%E6%BA%90%E8%A1%A8/20201101-20210131-Observed%20Sources.pdf

Liu et al. reported 25 FAST epochs for J1611−0114 at 1.05–1.45 GHz, with a 2021-06-17 pulse profile plotted in Fig. 1 (left). I extracted the published *vector path* in the positive pulse-phase window with PyMuPDF, calibrating against 220 and 230 degree x-axis ticks and the 0 and 1 intensity ticks. The shoulder at 257° has 0.724 of the peak at 260°, dipping only to 0.721 at 258° in between. Their separation is approximately 3°, or 21.6 ms. The panel spans only 220–295° so cannot constrain opposite-phase emission. The two reported components themselves cannot explain phases 180° apart. The paper's Table 1 gives erroneous J1611 astrometry and DM compared with the phase-connected solution cited by its authors. https://www.raa-journal.org/issues/all/2024/v24n11/202411/P020241204801030490057.pdf

`read_fast_profile.py` and `liu2024_fig1_j1611_profile.csv` reproduce the morphology measurement from the pinned PDF. It is an analysis of published measurements, not an independent reduction of telescope voltages. The corrected Wu 2025 Fig. 7 J1611 intensity profile also crops out the opposite half-period. https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/staf1986

## Archive identification of the FAST profile observation
