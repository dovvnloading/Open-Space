# A likely but unconfirmed repeat Pushchino entry for pulsar J1958+2214

The 2024 PUMPS catalog reports **J2000+22** as one of 39 new pulsars. Its period, dispersion measure and discovery position agree with both **J1958+2213**, published by the same telescope four years earlier, and the independently timed LOFAR pulsar **J1958+2214**. FAST localized the 2020 source 3.4 arcsec from the LOFAR timing position. Its paper explicitly credits the 2020 Pushchino discovery. **The identification of the 2024 entry with the same emitter is likely but unconfirmed.** If confirmed, that would mean one fewer distinct discovery among the 39 entries. No observing team or catalog maintainer has confirmed the proposed correction. The separate Green Bank half-period candidate discussed below remains unresolved and is not counted as a duplicate. ## Which tests distinguish these records?

- **Independent timing position.** The LOFAR timing solution, based on 26 time-of-arrival measurements, falls inside *both* original positional error boxes. The 2024 right ascension differs by 74.72 seconds of time against its stated ±90 s precision. Declination differs by 1.44 arcmin against ±10 arcmin. The LOFAR period is 0.000120 s from the 2024 value and 0.000180 s from the 2020 value, both within each paper's individual precision. Both original DM intervals include 87.665. The 2020 position uncertainty is ±30 s in RA and ±15 arcmin in declination. LOFAR also describes how strong far-sidelobe detections initially complicated its own localization. [LOFAR timing analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11080)
- **Published detection diagnostics.** The two Pushchino source pages remain separate. Both published power spectra label the fundamental at Fourier index **389, P = 1.05235 s**, with a comb of harmonics. The displayed peak index is discretized and both analyses use the same instrument and potentially overlapping survey observations. The two dispersiograms peak at 85 and 86. These plots establish which periodic signal each listing measured. The independent evidence comes from LOFAR and FAST. [2020 source and spectra](https://bsa-analytics.prao.ru/en/pulsars/info/251/) · [2024 source and spectra](https://bsa-analytics.prao.ru/en/pulsars/info/495/)
- **Complete comparison between the two papers.** All 5 × 39 = **195** cross-publication pairs were compared using the reported intervals. This is the only pair whose positional boxes overlap. It also agrees in both period and DM. Both recipient lists were chosen after examining the broader catalog. This is an audit of the two releases, with no survey-wide false-positive rate inferred. [All 195 comparisons](pushchino-pairs.csv)
- **Population context.** The ATNF v2.9.0 snapshot contains **three separate named records** for this group, J1958+2214, J1958+22_P and J2000+22. The FAST row is an update of a known source. The provisional suffix, survey rows and independent detections alone cannot count physical pulsars. Of 4,503 database records, 4,322 have a period, DM and convertible position. An exploratory 1.1° screen yielded 17,568 pairs and 2,213 with |ΔDM| ≤ 3. Excluding pairs with a catalogued globular-cluster association left 272. Fifty-five passed the period/harmonic test; the median randomized count was 2, maximum 6, across 1,000 period shuffles stratified by declination and log DM. Repeated entries and survey selection can both contribute to that excess. This packet makes an individual identification for the Pushchino case only. [All passing pairs including clusters](screen.csv) · [Counts and controls](summary.json)
- **Falsifier.** Two adjacent pulsars with similar periods and DMs could masquerade as one coarse-beam detection. A phase-connected, independently localized signal at the J2000+22 pointing that remains after subtracting the timed J1958+2214 signal would defeat the one-source interpretation. The existing positional and period measurements favor a duplicate. No observing team or catalog maintainer has confirmed a correction here.

## The separate Green Bank half-period candidate

GBT's RRAT J1611−01 is 13.15 arcmin from the timing position of FAST J1611−0114, within the GBT position errors. Its 1.29687(2) s period is just 7.22 microseconds above half the 2.59372556 s FAST period. Its DM of 27.21(7) differs from FAST's 27.70(10) by **4.0 combined formal standard errors**. A published two-component FAST profile has peaks/shoulders only about 3° apart in the displayed window. This cannot account for alternating phases 180° apart. Missing pulses alone cannot make the greatest common divisor of inter-pulse intervals smaller than the spin period for one window per rotation. The phase parity of the original 350 MHz bursts is decisive, and the arrival times were not recovered. A separate published FAST nondetection has uncertain multi-beam coverage at the timing position. We retain this pair as unresolved. [Observations, dates and profile measurement](evidence.md) · [GBT discovery paper](https://doi.org/10.1088/0004-637X/809/1/67) · [FAST timing](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stad1323)

The J0942−5552/J0948−5549 and J1858+0215/J1858+0310_P harmonics are incidental leads in the output. The former would require an off-axis detection 0.84° away and lacks a verified epoch/Doppler correction here. The latter is 440 ppm from a 2:1 ratio. Neither meets the evidence standard of the Pushchino result.

## Reproduction and provenance

The protocol commit predates the first ranking. The numerical cuts, named primary-paper comparison and highlighted associations are exploratory. The period screen allows ratios 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 8 with fractional residual at most 0.0005. It compares catalog values at their reported precision. Doppler, binary motion and epoch differences are unmodeled in this screen. Shifting all field positions by ±4°, ±8° and ±12° in RA yielded 0, 1 and 0 matches respectively for each sign, versus 110 directed matches at zero shift (55 undirected pairs). These controls apply to the population screen rather than an individual posterior probability.
