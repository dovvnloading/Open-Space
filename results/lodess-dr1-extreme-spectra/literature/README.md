# Prior classifications and independent source structure

## NVSS J112022+502936

This is a previously catalogued radio source. The LoLSS position (170.092166°, +50.493238°) lies 0.32″ from the SIMBAD radio counterpart. Its aliases include DRAOP 21P11, 6C 111733+504632, WN B1117.5+5046 and GB6 B1117+5046. Neither the object-linked bibliography nor the coordinate search below located a prior identification of its proposed low-frequency turnover. Cordun et al. had already reported a tail of possible turnovers in the LoDeSS population. They did not name this object in that discussion. [LoDeSS DR1, §5.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04698v1)

SPECFIND v3 already groups the source under spectrum 144715. Within the 30″ cone, its legacy per-entry power-law indices span −0.60 to −0.63. Past measurements include 0.430 ± 0.086 Jy at 74 MHz (VLSSr), 0.341 ± 0.068 Jy at 150 MHz (TGSS) and 0.0841 ± 0.017 Jy at 1.4 GHz (FIRST). The quoted errors here are SPECFIND's. Its MHz and GHz break candidate lists contain no entry within 60″. Catalogues such as NED also index several of these underlying surveys. Repeated listings of one measurement should be counted once. [SPECFIND v3](https://cdsarc.cds.unistra.fr/viz-bin/cat/VIII/104) · [NED object references](https://ned.ipac.caltech.edu/NED::API/ReferencesOfObject?TARGET=NVSS%20J112022%2B502936)

The nearest selected Slob and Ballieux MPS sources are both about 29.8′ away. The closest Ballieux GPS entry is 16.6′ away. The UTR-2 source is 2.01° away. The Callingham GLEAM study covered declinations below approximately +30°. This field is outside its coverage. The LoTSS DR2 optical-identification catalogue has ILTJ112022.15+502935.7 at 0.30″ with 347.86 ± 2.44 mJy (formal 144 MHz error), no adopted optical counterpart and no redshift. SDSS DR16Q contains no quasar within 30″, excluding this object from the DR16Q-selected parent sample used by Zhai et al. Vallée & Roger (1989) report radio sources in the direction of Abell 1314. That field identification supplies no distance or cluster membership for this source. [Hardcastle et al.](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202347333) · [Zhai et al.](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202452979) · [Vallée & Roger](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1989A%26AS...77...31V/abstract)

An independent LOFAR Long-Baseline Calibrator Survey observation on 2015-03-18 (L333048) is centred 2.08″ away. All eight participating baselines recorded positive fringes at around 140 MHz. This establishes a subarcsecond compact component. The survey records anomalous amplitudes (flag A), so the row is used for morphology only. The LoLSS DR1 authors also find their in-band slopes biased positive, typically by 0.2–0.3. Applying a +0.30 bias to this source's measured +0.644 ± 0.179 gives +0.344 ± 0.179 before uncertainty in that correction. The separate survey measurements support testing for spectral curvature. They do not isolate which emitting component turns over, discriminate synchrotron self-absorption from external absorption, or supply a second decametre epoch. [LBCS table and flags](https://vo.astron.nl/tableinfo/lbcs.main) · [Jackson et al., Table 2](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202140756) · [de Gasperin et al., §5.5](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202245389)

The northern source has a separate Miyun observation in field 153 from 1988-02-27. MY0953+816 has a peak of 2.13 Jy/beam at 232 MHz, signal-to-noise 41.8, and local noise of 0.051 Jy/beam. The field background is 0.045 Jy/beam. The catalogue's integrated flux field is blank. Its 3.8′ beam and calibration limit its use in fitting narrow spectral features. Vollmer et al. give 5% nominal survey accuracy and 16.6% empirical flux scatter for bright Miyun sources, compared with composite spectra. Combining that scatter with the local noise gives 0.357 Jy as a sensitivity-test uncertainty. The original record has no per-source total flux error. A five-local-rms threshold at this position is 0.255 Jy, far below this detection. A field-specific completeness fraction is unknown. The 0.25 Jy completeness result for the two-field Miyun pilot does not apply directly to field 153. The 2.13 Jy point is nominally below 2.541 ± 0.102 Jy at 325 MHz from WENSS as compiled by Mingaliev et al. 2001. The difference is 1.1 standard deviations with the empirical Miyun error. The earlier 2001 polar survey records an ordinary steep cm spectrum with index 1.02 in its positive-steep sign convention. These three searches constrain earlier GHz-peaker claims. ## Search and reproduction

The 27 ICRS positions in `positions.csv` include catalogue detections, forced-photometry candidates and positive controls. Integer IDs refer to LoDeSS catalogue row indices in this analysis. The audit computes the nearest angular separation in each complete published list. Its 60″ search radius permits examination of legacy positions with larger errors. For UTR-2, 40′ marks a coarse neighbourhood for inspection. A UTR neighbour can include many sources. Coordinate and bibliography searches have incomplete sky coverage and incomplete indexing, so an unmatched row constrains only the lists searched.

Run from this directory with Python 3. Both scripts use the standard library. Choose scratch directories for full catalogue caches.

```sh
python recheck_catalogues.py /tmp/lodess-priors /tmp/catalogue_checks.csv
python query_context.py /tmp/lodess-context
```

`catalogue_checks.csv` contains one minimum angular distance per source and table. `simbad_associations.csv` records SIMBAD aliases and object-specific bibcodes for neighbourhood entries within 25″, including possible unrelated objects. For the three sources examined in detail, `dr2_counterparts.csv` records the DR2 catalogue within 30″, and `long_baseline_observations.csv` records LBCS within 60″. The 30″ search of [van Weeren et al.](https://doi.org/10.1088/0004-637X/793/2/82) at 34, 46 and 62 MHz returned no entries for the three positions. No upper limits were assigned from this query. `legacy_spectrum_j112022.csv` records SPECFIND legacy measurements inside 30″. `ned_references.csv` lists returned object-specific references, and `ned_radio_photometry.csv` uses the NED VOTable's standardized Jy columns. These reference measurements have different beams, epochs and flux conventions. The numerical target labels in both NED files are the six-digit RA portions of the source names. Archives were queried on 2026-11-16. This research used the NASA/IPAC Extragalactic Database, operated by Caltech and funded by NASA, and the CDS SIMBAD and VizieR services.
