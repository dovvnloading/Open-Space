# Ross 154 — a two-epoch MIRI F2100W search

No point source crossed the blind detection threshold in the searched portions of the 3–9 arcsec annulus around Ross 154 (GJ 729). This covers projected radii of 8.9–26.8 au at the Gaia distance of 2.98 pc. At 5.25 arcsec, independent reference-star injections reached ≥90% recovery among the tested positions at flux ratios of 0.000332 (21/21, April), 0.000427 (19/20, August first roll) and 0.000532 (22/22, August second roll). The August visits are 59 minutes apart and constitute one epoch.

**Coverage matters.** Within 4.5–6 arcsec, 80.3% of the annular area was searched at least once and 15.2% in both epochs. At 7.5–9 arcsec these fractions fell to 58.5% and 8.1%. The contrast data report empirical detection performance only for the accepted footprint of each visit. Visit-level injections use up to 48 fixed sky angles at each radius. A ratio such as 7/7 at the outermost radius describes seven tested angles.

## Completed

* Used five SHA-256-verified, public MAST level-3 F2100W mosaics from program 6122, including three science visits and two Wolf 359 reference visits. The Ross 154 science times were 2025-04-11 and 2025-08-20 UTC. The August mosaics entered public access on 2026-08-21. Exact UTC MJDs, exposure ranges, release times, product URIs and byte counts are in `inputs.json`.
* Registered the cores of the three science images and two references using a Gaussian centroid fit. Removed a per-image sky median, averaged the registered reference stars in detector coordinates, and fitted a reference scale plus background to each science image using a robust loss within 19 pixels. The two references are independent observations of the same star. The central pixels in the three science mosaics retain 99.2%–99.4% of maximum mosaic weight. Starting from i2d products preserves the archive's detector calibration, cosmic ray rejection and resampling.
* Required a finite 21×21-pixel footprint with weight above 30% of the image maximum in every contributing frame. Applied a zero-sum matched filter constructed from each science star's empirical core in a radius of 10 pixels. Searched four prespecified radial bins from 3 to 9 arcsec. The detection threshold uses an annular median, MAD scale, and a one-sided Student-t factor for the nominal Gaussian 5-sigma tail with independent resolution-element count reduced by valid annular coverage. MAD, spatially varying structure and resampling make the quoted threshold an operational detection rule. The injections measure its performance.
* Injected a different star's observed PSF (Wolf 359, observation 013), normalized to the background-subtracted core aperture of each science star. Applied fractional-pixel shifts. Each recovery requires a local maximum within half a FWHM above threshold and no original detection at that location. The injections model the stellar PSF core of an unresolved source. Their radius excludes flux beyond the padded core stamp. The science RDI coefficients depend only on pixels inside the innermost injection's support. The filtered response is therefore linear in the injected flux. Explicit pre-RDI image injection at one angle in every visit/radial bin verifies the shortcut in `linearity_check.csv`. Full trial list and per-visit contrast curves are in `injection_trials.csv` and `contrast_recovery.csv`.
* Used Gaia DR3 source 4075141768785646848, with parallax 336.027 mas and proper motion (+639.368, −193.958) mas/yr. At each MJD the JWST barycentric position in the science header provides the differential parallax factor. A distant stationary source moves approximately (+0.349, +0.122) arcsec relative to the host between the epochs. This 0.37 arcsec displacement is smaller than the roughly 0.70 arcsec PSF FWHM. There are zero threshold detections to associate or fit. `visits.csv` and the header-only `peaks.csv` record the calculation and the empty source list.
* SIMBAD confirms GJ 729, HIP 92403 and V1216 Sgr are aliases. The host-specific Exoplanet Archive query and Gaia field cone are in `catalogue/`. NACO survey 097.C-0972(A) previously observed HIP 92403. The 2026 RedDots RV study reported an ambiguous 7-day signal outside this imaging search's radial range. The GO 6122 sensitivity paper presents results for Wolf 359 and EV Lac. Searches for “Ross 154” / “GJ 729” with “6122”, “JWST”, “F2100W”, “companion”, and “direct imaging” yielded no target-specific earlier JWST companion result in consulted sources on 2026-11-17. [SIMBAD](https://simbad.u-strasbg.fr/simbad/sim-basic?Ident=V1216+Sgr), [NACO](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stz882), [RedDots](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stag1294), [GO 6122 sensitivity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15995).

## Limits and remaining checks

The footprint omits low-weight regions, the stellar core and all separations outside 3–9 arcsec. A fainter source, a source at an inaccessible PA or blended emission could remain present. The curves use measured point-source core contrast at 21 μm; companion atmosphere and temperature models are required for mass limits. The procedure does not measure false-alarm probabilities for every independent search trial, fit a population occurrence rate, or address subthreshold features. Cross-epoch colours and astrometric tests of a particular point source would require an identified source and usable flux measurements. No individual source passed the first detection step in this annulus.

## Reproduction

Run `python ross_rdi.py /path/to/fits-cache /path/to/output` from this folder with NumPy, SciPy and Astropy installed. `inputs.json` determines the five input files. The script verifies their hashes and retrieves missing files. The Gaia field record in `catalogue/gaia_dr3.csv` supplies the host parameters. To refresh catalogue responses, run `python ross_catalog.py`. NumPy, SciPy and Astropy versions used are recorded in `versions.json`. A resolved detection would trigger candidate photometry, background modelling and comparison to the Gaia field; the reported contrast measurements are independent of those population models.
