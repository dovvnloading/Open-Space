# G29−38 (ZZ Psc), GO 9077, MIRI direct imaging

**One detected field source, zero new companions in the searched portions of 3–9 arcsec.** MIRI recovered the previously catalogued Spitzer source SSTSL2 J232846.52+051448.4 at 6.55 arcsec from the dusty white dwarf in F2100W. Its F2550W centroid agrees within 0.08 arcsec in host-relative coordinates. The multi-year Pan-STARRS, Legacy Surveys and Spitzer positions support a stationary background object. The two MIRI filters belong to one visit. At the Gaia parallax distance of 17.52 pc, the search spans projected separations of approximately 53–158 au. [Spitzer catalogue receipt](catalogue/seip.csv), [campaign archival vetting](../vetting/), [detections](out/peaks.csv).

## Completed

- Queried public CAOM Level 3 MIRI imagery. GO 9077 has two SUB256 mosaics of ZZ Psc on 2025-11-13, both public on 2026-11-14, with 2,693.88 s effective exposure per band. The Phase II science objective was the central debris disk. Selected the closest preceding and following same-band, same-subarray observations of photometric standard BD+60 1753 in calibration program 7671. They bracket the visit by 50 and 57 days. The reduction verifies SHA-256 for all six input FITS files. [GO 9077 Phase II](https://www.stsci.edu/jwst-program-info/download/jwst/pdf/9077/), [product metadata and hashes](inputs.json), [dated requests and response hashes](mast_receipts.json).
- Registered each star by fitting its core within eight pixels of the archive position. Built a circularly averaged PSF profile from the two standards, normalized to their background-subtracted core fluxes, and fitted its scale and a background term inside 14 science pixels. Azimuthal medians suppress imprinting unrelated sources and thermal stripes from the reference field. The noncircular reference PSF of the earlier standard delivered the zero-sum matched filter. Searched four radial bins with local median/MAD noise and a Student-t threshold for a nominal one-sided Gaussian 5-sigma tail, reducing the count of independent resolution elements for partial coverage. Each accepted center has a 29×29 finite footprint at over 30% of the peak science weight. Local artifacts and variable thermal background limit any Gaussian interpretation of the detection statistic, especially in F2550W. [Reduction](reduce.py), [MIRI PSF characterization](https://jwst-docs.stsci.edu/jwst-mid-infrared-instrument/miri-performance/miri-point-spread-functions).
- Injected the late standard's independently measured, subpixel-shifted PSF core at up to 48 sky angles in each band and radial bin, with six flux levels. Recovery required crossing the threshold at a local maximum within half a FWHM with no original detection there. The 12-pixel model core is truncated. All injection stamps lie outside the host-fit domain. Per bin, a direct pre-subtraction injection agrees with the linear matched-filter response to less than 3×10⁻¹⁷ in contrast units. [Verification](out/linearity_check.csv).
- F2100W detected the field source at east/north (−6.466, +1.015) arcsec, S/N 19.97, and F2550W at (−6.519, +1.071) arcsec, S/N 8.36. In a separate 70%-encircled-energy aperture analysis, the corrected source fluxes are 125 ± 15 and 118 ± 50 µJy. The uncertainties are same-radius blank-aperture MAD scales. The median blank signal is +3 µJy at 21 µm and −26 µJy at 25.5 µm, indicating a local background systematic. The corresponding 80%-energy measurements are 122 ± 21 and 101 ± 61 µJy. The filtered detection statistic and aperture scatter use different estimators. [Aperture measurements](out/source_photometry.csv), [calibration anchors](../miri/calibration_inputs.json).
- Spitzer IRAC catalogued the source within 0.18 arcsec of the absolute F2100W location. The 3.6, 4.5 and 8 µm point-source-corrected fluxes are 23.53, 28.21 and 45.58 µJy. SEIP's MIPS 24 µm fit forced at the catalogue position gives 174.9 ± 37.2 µJy. Pan-STARRS DR2 object 114293521937476539 and a Legacy Surveys DR10 Tractor source fall within 0.09 arcsec of JWST. In the intervening years the white dwarf moved south-westward at 479 mas yr⁻¹. The known northeast object C1 measured by Debes et al. is a different background source, at (+4.91, +2.03) arcsec in its discovery epoch. The point-source-like MIRI appearance does not establish the physical class of the object. [SEIP data](catalogue/seip.csv), [Spitzer flux conventions](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/knowledgebase/spitzer_seip.html), [Debes et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0412668), [Gaia query](catalogue/gaia.csv).
- At the white dwarf's assumed distance, 119 tabulated cloud-free Sonora Bobcat models across three metallicities (effective temperature 475–1,300 K) fall within twice the empirical aperture scatter in **both** MIRI bands. These predict 246–1,215 µJy in the actual Spitzer IRAC 4.5 µm band, compared with its catalogue flux of 28.21 ± 0.35 µJy. These are model checks conditioned on association. The archival positions carry the common-motion test. The Exoplanet Archive returned no confirmed planets for the queried host and aliases. A 5-arcsec search at the field source returned zero CatWISE 2020 and 2MASS PSC entries. [Grids](out/model_best_and_2sigma.csv), [Sonora Bobcat model release](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.5063476), [queries](catalogue/receipts.json).
- Counted 122 SEIP 24 µm sources with PSF flux ≥150 µJy and zero MIPS flux flag in a 40.72 arcmin² GOODS-S control cone, or 3.00 per arcmin². This projects to 0.188 objects in a complete 3–9 arcsec annulus. At ≥100 µJy the corresponding number is 0.213. The local Gaia 0.1° cone gives 1.07 optical sources per arcmin² (0.067 per complete annulus). The two counts use different bands and populations, cannot be added to estimate a false-association probability, and omit completeness and field-to-field corrections. [Counts](out/control_counts.csv), [control catalogue](catalogue/mips_control.csv).
- Prior targeted searches include Keck speckle and HST/NICMOS plus Gemini AO. The latter rejected C1 by proper motion. The Cycle 4 project and a 2026 conference presentation characterize the central dusty system. A search on 2026-11-17 for G29−38/ZZ Psc, GO 9077, companion and the Spitzer identifier found no peer-reviewed orbit or companion claim for the western source. [Keck study](https://web.gps.caltech.edu/~mbrown/papers/ps/g2938.pdf), [HST and Gemini](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0412668), [2026 conference program](https://www.ast.cam.ac.uk/debris-disk-connections-workshop-programme).

## Limits and pending checks

The study measures sensitivity within a partial footprint and an empirical range of angles, flux levels, and PSF shapes. The normalization includes the disk. F2550W has higher thermal structure and a smaller usable footprint than F2100W. Objects inside 3 arcsec, outside 9 arcsec, within rejected footprint locations, or below the tabulated sensitivity remain unconstrained. Mass and completeness over all orbital orientations were not inferred. GO 9077 has one public direct-imaging epoch in this inventory. The physical class of the known background source would require further spectroscopy or morphology.

## Reproduction

From this directory, run the following with the versions in `versions.json`. The normal reduction downloads and verifies the manifest when the cache is empty. The two calibration/model files used by the independent photometry check are shared with the adjacent MIRI white-dwarf analysis.

```sh
python reduce.py /path/to/fits-cache out
python photometry.py /path/to/fits-cache out
python catalogs.py
python counts.py
```
