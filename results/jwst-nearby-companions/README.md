# Public JWST companion search around nearby stars

**No uncatalogued planet or brown-dwarf companion passed validation in the ten stellar fields searched.** The three field sources highlighted below each have an independently identified archival counterpart. Nine additional white-dwarf-field peaks matched stationary positions between JWST epochs. Two additional NIRCam peaks around Ross 619 fell below threshold when we changed the reference stars. The sensitivity measurements apply to the footprints, filters, separations and recovery rules below.

## Completed

- Froze the MAST release inventory on 2026-11-17 at 06:51:31 UTC. Across the three overlapping query selections, 9,056 distinct exposure filesets map to 2,653 visit/filter groups and 240 programs. The Gaia Catalogue of Nearby Stars crossmatch selected 792 groups associated with 331 possible nearby hosts in 70 programs. Rights, public-release and observing dates, program, target, filter, exposure mode and MAST IDs are retained. Papertrack returned 438 program–paper associations with 334 publications. A separate SIMBAD title screen found object-linked imaging or companion-search literature leads for 214 of the 331 possible hosts. The four newest coronagraphic targets all have earlier ground- or space-based companion-search references. [Inventory, target coverage and exact predicates](inventory/inventory-notes.md) · [Visit ledger](inventory/archive/visits.csv) · [Program bibliography](inventory/archive/program-papers.csv) · [Per-target prior-search ledger](inventory/archive/target-search-status.csv)
- Prioritized the latest release in the adopted nearby ordinary-imaging selection, the newest public science coronagraphic release, other targets with usable references, and repeated fields where host motion could be measured. A completed reduction is identified separately from an inventory entry. The previous JWST transient search studied extragalactic fields. We reused its archive product URL builder for the white-dwarf mosaics and kept the SPHEREx and small-body programs outside this work. [Previous campaign](../jwst-repeat-imaging-transients/README.md)
- For the retained detections, checked morphology, colours, independently dated astrometry, catalogues and literature. For L 788−34, a 0.9″-radius aperture gives F200W−F444W = −0.93 AB mag. Cool substellar models matching the F444W brightness at the host distance are much redder. The previous optical position and the resolved two-band morphology support a background galaxy. The two known RV planets of GJ 4274 orbit far inside the imaged separations. [Source-vetting record](vetting/README.md) · [Colour and galaxy-count comparison](vetting/background/README.md)
- The five F444W reductions used 11,880 artificial-source trials. Across six tested radii from 2.65″ to 5.35″ around G 99−49, the initial detection cut reached ≥95% recovery at companion/host ratios of 1.77–2.70 × 10⁻⁶. The extended source raises the threshold locally around L 788−34. The instrument folders contain the scripts, exact inputs with SHA-256, checks of the reference libraries and visit structure, per-source measurements, and per-target point-source injection/recovery. Artificial signals and previously published results are identified separately from measurements on the science data.

## Remaining scope and limits

This is a selected ten-field experiment. The full inventory includes science targets, references, calibrations and archive associations. The nearby sample can miss stars with incomplete Gaia astrometry, short observations outside the supplementary programs, proposer misclassifications and serendipitous stellar fields. Program-level publication links do not establish whether a specific field was searched. Individual legacy searches and image coverage for other hosts require review.

Completeness depends on band, field, angular separation, stellar-flux normalization, and which azimuths were observable. NIRCam injections measure the blind detection stage before independent-library vetting. The two-epoch MIRI white-dwarf trials include the motion criterion. The seven unmatched white-dwarf peaks and the two reference-dependent Ross 619 peaks have no validated astrophysical classification. No survey-wide upper bound on planet occurrence, universal mass limit, or bound companion follows from these measurements.
