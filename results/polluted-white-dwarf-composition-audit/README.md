# A published selection changes in WD 1232+563 and debris at WD J2236−5548 is sodium-poor relative to CI material

## The nickel measurement belongs to titanium

The March 2026 PEWDD snapshot assigns log(Ni/He) = −8.92 ± 0.15 to WD 1232+563 in the Badenas-Agusti 2024 row and leaves titanium blank. The observing paper's Table 5 places that number and uncertainty under **Ti**, and reports that Ni was undetected. The error creates a physically consequential anomaly, an apparent factor **13.6** Ni/Fe depletion against the Orgueil CI chondrite under early accretion, or **14.5** under steady supply. The model uses the observed Fe abundance of −6.51 ± 0.15. If the two reported errors were independent, the false early-phase deficit would nominally exceed 5σ relative to the tabulated reference; this calculation is illustrative, with no uncertainty assigned to the reference. [Badenas-Agusti et al. 2024, Table 5](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stae421) · [Source catalog snapshot](https://github.com/jamietwilliams/PEWDD/tree/023dba3176ac367c6cf2b5968847a80d79e3c594)

Differential settling cannot produce this deficit from material with the Orgueil ratio **in the specified diffusion model**, regardless of the history of nonnegative accretion of fixed composition onto an initially metal-free atmosphere. At the catalog temperature of 11,777.8 K and log(g) of 8.24, interpolation in the SV helium grid gives τNi/τFe = 1.06786. An input parcel of age *a* contributes a ratio multiplied by exp[*a*(1/τFe − 1/τNi)] ≥ 1. The sum of such parcels therefore has an atmospheric Ni/Fe at least equal to the input ratio. This establishes why a geology analysis could misinterpret this error as real nickel depletion. The valid log(Ti/Ca) is −1.48 ± 0.21, or −1.407 after the steady-state correction, versus −1.443 for the Orgueil reference. A higher-resolution study measured titanium independently at −8.96 ± 0.11 and placed only an upper limit of −7.3 on nickel. That upper limit permits a chondritic Ni/Fe ratio. [SV grid](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17313701) · [Xu et al. 2019, Table 4](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10163609)

### Consequence for a published sample

Buchan et al. included the Badenas-Agusti entry (ID 1828) in their Test 5 comparison of the SN and KN diffusion grids. Their footnote 7 identifies a specific December 2024 PEWDD snapshot. The erroneous nickel identification appears in that exact snapshot, as well as the newer March 2026 file. The paper requires at least four modelled elements and a selection metric of 2.0 or greater. Correcting the column removes this entry from the published selection. ## A settling-independent sodium bound for WD J2236−5548

At the published abundance values, the parent material has **at least 3.42 times less sodium per magnesium atom** than in either complete Orgueil CI-chondrite assay, for any nonnegative accretion history of fixed composition in the two tested diffusion models. The photospheric log(Na/Mg) = −1.75, compared with −1.216 and −1.206 for the two Orgueil assays. At 5,350 K and log(g) = 8.17, τNa/τMg is 1.0172 for zero overshoot and 1.0094 for one pressure scale height of overshoot. Sodium's longer lifetime means every parcel contributes at least its original Na/Mg ratio. An initially clean atmosphere accumulating such parcels must have atmospheric Na/Mg greater than or equal to the supplied ratio. If the source composition varied, at least one contributing parcel must meet this bound. The point estimate therefore rules out a history made solely of Orgueil-composition material in either model. This conclusion is independent of any proposed meteorite match. [O’Brien et al. 2023, Table 7](https://academic.oup.com/view-large/383732263) · [Diffusion grids](https://github.com/andrewmbuchan4/PyllutedWD_Public/tree/9738ca37ca83710c9aca4b943701373686e07033/data)

The lifetime ratio stays greater than one (1.0062 to 1.0199) across 54 illustrative grid evaluations spanning 5,150–5,550 K, log(g) 8.10–8.24 and log(Ca/He) −9.40 to −8.90. The independent formal errors imply 0.014 dex in the measured log ratio. Allowing independent 0.15-dex abundance systematics in quadrature makes the smaller 0.534-dex CI offset 2.51σ; a 0.20-dex floor gives 1.89σ. The 3.42 factor is a bound at the reported abundance values, rather than a confidence limit. Element-specific measurement or diffusion model errors beyond the tested range could weaken it. A separate mass-budget test below addresses a grid with reversed lifetimes. The recent SV helium grid starts at 7,000 K, so it cannot independently verify the sign for this cool star. The result establishes a chemical difference from the two CI references within the models and stated uncertainties. Ordinary chondrite compositions vary enough that other members can fit. Target-name, Gaia-identifier, and paired abundance/meteorite literature searches located the published spectra but no earlier accretion-history bound for this star.

### An independent bound when the lifetime order reverses

An additional archived helium diffusion table varies temperature and gravity and omits the calcium dimension. At the same stellar parameters it gives τNa/τMg = 0.99230, reversing the order. Pure differential decay after a single CI pulse would now require at least **158.5 Mg sinking times**. A mass-conservation argument extends the test to arbitrary nonnegative supply histories in this alternative model. The table gives log₁₀(Mmix/MWD) = −4.7755, so the measured abundance implies a current Mg reservoir W = 3.96 × 10⁻¹² white-dwarf masses, approximating the helium-dominated atmosphere by helium alone. [Alternate tabulation and source reader](https://github.com/andrewmbuchan4/PyllutedWD_Public/blob/9738ca37ca83710c9aca4b943701373686e07033/src/detlev_timescale_extractor.py)

Let F be all delivered Mg and x = exp(−age/τMg) the surviving fraction of Mg for one parcel. For fixed source composition, the retained Na/Mg ratio divided by the source ratio is E[x^(1+k)]/E[x], where the expectation is Mg-input-weighted and k = τMg/τNa − 1 = 0.00776. Since k > 0, convexity gives E[x^(1+k)]/E[x] ≥ E[x]^k = (W/F)^k. To obtain the observed 0.534-dex deficit from CI composition would therefore require **at least 10^57.45 white-dwarf masses of supplied magnesium**, which rules out differential settling as a physical explanation in this grid at the reported abundances. Even allowing one entire white-dwarf mass of supplied Mg yields an input Na/Mg at least 2.79 times below the poorer-Na of the two Orgueil assays. The full numerical error budget of cool, strongly nonideal atmospheres extends beyond these three tabulations.

### Five-element analogues

O’Brien et al. measured five photospheric elements. The Mg/Fe number ratio is 17.0. Among 1,014 complete five-element laboratory/reference analyses (840 distinct names), the Roda diogenite provides the closest fit under either of the two tested cool-helium diffusion grids, over a grid of constant-rate accretion episodes followed by free decline. Roda's inferred declining phase is approximately 1.3 Mg sinking times with a short supply pulse. Only one of the three Roda database analyses measures all five of the required elements. The fit identifies a specific analogue for targeted tests of this well-measured system. At a sensitivity floor of 0.15 dex per abundance in quadrature, the one-pressure-scale-height overshoot grid yields profiled χ² values of 0.174 for Roda, 2.44 for an ordinary LL chondrite measurement (Ragland), 4.60 for model bulk Earth, 8.03 for model silicate Earth, and 11.42 and 12.73 for the two complete Orgueil measurements. A generic chondrite reference gives 9.84. At a 0.20 dex floor, the better Orgueil fit drops to 6.66 and bulk Earth to 2.82. These are residual fit statistics, with laboratory compositions held fixed. The experiment selects the best of 1,014 entries, includes fitted nuisance parameters and uses abundance floors chosen for sensitivity tests. It cannot assign an identification probability to any meteorite class. The zero-overshoot grid produces the same full-sample winner. Omitting Na, Mg, Ca, Cr or Fe in turn gives Johnstown, Jalanash, Y82049, A881725 or Roda, respectively, on the overshoot grid at a 0.15-dex floor. Four of these five omissions change the best individual match. The spectroscopy supplies no Si measurement or limit for this target. The family of plausible compositions includes ordinary chondrites. There is no secure evidence here for a differentiated parent body or for a Vesta analogue.

The physics model fits a common dilution scale, a constant supply duration from zero (the impulse limit) to 120 Mg sinking times, and an unbounded nonnegative elapsed time after shutoff. Each abundance is proportional to source abundance × τ × [1−exp(−duration/τ)] × exp(−time_since_shutoff/τ). The log diffusion times are interpolated in effective temperature, log(g), and log(Ca/He) at the reported values, 5,350 K, 8.17, and −9.17. The forward model can be tested against both compared meteorite hypotheses once more elements are measured. Searches of the source paper, the target's name with “Roda” and “diogenite,” and the comparison literature located no earlier Roda identification. This is a scoped literature check.

## Reproduction

Run `python reproduce.py` with Python 3 and NumPy, or pass `--cache PATH` to reuse downloaded inputs. The script validates the thirteen source files against the SHA-256 digests in `sources.json` and emits JSON. `measurements.json` contains the complete numerical output, including fits with four systematic-error settings, both grids, leave-one-element-out tests, and the Ni/Ti correction. Outputs use logarithmic number abundances and timescales in years.
