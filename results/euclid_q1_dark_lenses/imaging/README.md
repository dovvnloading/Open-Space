# Calibrated pixel measurements

Every one of the 312 grade A/B Discovery Engine candidates with a positive effective Einstein radius and finite mass centre has VIS emission inside 0.30″ of that centre after subtracting the released MGE lensed-source image. The weakest formal core detection is 19.74σ. The median is 127.99σ. These significances propagate local sky noise and the RMS image. Source-model mistakes and foreground/background ambiguity remain astrophysical uncertainties.

The single valid H core below 5σ has S/N 4.9995. The archive also includes eight positive-radius grade-C controls and two zero-radius cases with reported centres. One zero-radius case, 102042915_NEG528545016288651034, has a straight VIS streak crossing the centre and no matching narrow NIR feature. The VIS core is +44.6σ in the data and −19.0σ after model source subtraction. Its J and H residuals are +6.0σ and +6.4σ. Its effective radius is zero. Its cutout and other displayed panels appear alongside this file.

For 183 other primary A/B catalogue systems lacking a finite centre/radius pair, 174 have usable four-band cutouts in the archive. ## Photometry and checks

[The release](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15025832) supplies co-registered 0.1″ science/RMS images, filter-specific PSFs, and fitted image-plane MGE light from the foreground and lensed source. We downloaded specific ZIP members with HTTP Range, checked each against its ZIP directory CRC and byte count, and recorded SHA-256 in `input_members.csv` (1,288 members). The archive-wide MD5 in `cutouts.py` is the publisher's value. Partial requests do not independently verify that whole-file digest. The additional stamps and their hashes are in `unmodelled_*.csv`.

The forced-aperture centres are the published median mass centres relative to the middle of the science array. Apertures use whole pixels and the header zeropoint. Core radius is 0.30″. The second aperture has radius equal to the published effective Einstein radius. We subtract the MGE lensed-source image inside each aperture and subtract the median flux in identically shaped displaced apertures. Blank centres are separated by at least one aperture diameter in a 7–13.5″ annulus with allowance for aperture radius. The denominator of S/N is the larger of the blank-aperture MAD × 1.4826 and quadrature-summed RMS. At least 12 blank apertures are required. Nonfinite pixels, zero/negative RMS and the Q1 no-coverage RMS sentinel around 10¹⁶ invalidate an aperture. `coverage_ok` reports this separately from missing model bands. The reported 5σ depths measure the flux threshold for that specific aperture. Extended-profile total luminosity needs a profile-dependent aperture correction.

`registration.csv` compares the observed VIS data to the combined foreground/source image in a 4″ radius for the original ten priority objects. Median correlation is 0.928 in the recorded FITS orientation and 0.681 after reversing the y-axis. The median distance between the light-map peak and the published light centre is 0.049″ with the recorded convention and 0.289″ with y reversed. Nine of ten objects favour the direct convention by this distance. A low correlation or large single-system offset remains a model-quality warning. The PNG panels show independent display stretches, north approximately up, with a width of 7.1″ per panel in the Discovery Engine stamps. Source colours must be measured on the linear FITS data.

## Two apparent mass-to-light outliers

**102158887_2655306065654633189** has a bright central component (VIS 56.6σ, H 54.3σ) and three VIS residual maxima around the centre. To test colours we subtract the released foreground in each band, cross-convolve VIS with the NIR PSF and NIR with the VIS PSF, and use identical 0.23″ apertures at VIS residual maxima between 0.65 and 1.42 Einstein radii. A 0.6-pixel smoothed image and five-pixel neighbourhood select peaks higher than 20% of the brightest peak in the annulus. VIS−J colours are 0.77, 0.82, 0.77 AB. VIS−H colours are 0.95, 1.08, 1.16 AB with formal sky-only errors of 0.02–0.03 mag. VIS−Y varies by 0.32 mag. These apertures have finite PSF overlap, arc features may sample different source regions, and imperfect foreground subtraction contributes systematic error. The three knots do not establish identical source colours. A bright companion is visible to the southwest.

**102158888_2659753895653871052** has an elongated central component (VIS 49.0σ, H 22.2σ) and a crescent on one side of it. Only one VIS residual maximum meets the same annulus and relative-peak criteria. Its H signal is 4.3σ. A same-colour multiple-image test is unavailable. `knot_colours.csv` gives the measurements for both objects.

### Independent stress test of 102158888_2659753895653871052

The leading grade-B candidate retains a high conditional H-band mass-to-light ratio across two geometries. Its status depends on whether the crescent is multiply imaged and on the foreground redshift. The published mass and MGE light centroids differ by 0.214″. The raw and MGE-source-subtracted VIS core have S/N 49.09 and 48.96. The corresponding H values are 23.57 and 22.22. Total H magnitudes from the published MGE and Sérsic fits are 20.787 and 20.774 AB.

Holding the published 0.936″ Einstein aperture fixed, assigning every observed H photon to the foreground gives 21.779 AB. Subtracting the published source gives 21.908 AB. Direct integrals of the MGE and Sérsic foreground images give 21.888 and 21.725 AB. This spans −15.5% to 0% of the conditional mass-to-light estimate relative to the source-subtracted aperture. The Sérsic model integral exceeds the local sky-subtracted observed light, illustrating model uncertainty.

A point-source, circular SIS at the published mass centre gives a test independent of the published source reconstruction. The brightest VIS knot is 1.229″ out. With the published effective radius, the opposite image would fall 0.643″ from the centre and have 0.523 times the flux of the outer knot. In matched 0.30″ apertures the primary is 21.86σ in VIS, the predicted counterimage is 11.46σ and the measured counterimage is −1.19σ. The discrepancy is 11.2 formal noise σ after propagating both aperture noises. Apertures of 0.20″ and 0.40″ give discrepancies of 11.6σ and 9.5σ. In H the expected signal is only 1.94σ in the 0.30″ aperture. The equal-aperture ratio approximates an unresolved source and the circular SIS is more restrictive than the published SIE plus shear. Extended-source structure, differential extinction and foreground residuals can change this comparison.

We fitted the raw VIS pixels independently with a free elliptical Sérsic foreground, nonnegative source and foreground amplitudes, a constant sky and the released VIS PSF. A free-centre SIS with shear lenses an elliptical Gaussian source. The alternative uses an intrinsic curved knot with free curvature centre, curvature radius and angular and radial widths. Both fits use a 2.1″-radius region and 2 × 2 subpixel integration. The lens has three starts, the arc has two. Their uncorrelated-pixel χ² values are 916 and 1,027 for 1,396 pixels, with 19 and 15 free parameters. The SIS fit moves the mass centre 0.766″ from the light centre and needs shear 0.254. Its radius is only 0.480″. The drop in diagonal χ² favours this specific lens fit over this specific arc shape. Correlated noise, unmodelled foreground structure, and the small source family prevent interpreting this as a calibrated Bayes factor. A second contaminant fit adds a separately placed elliptical Gaussian knot to the intrinsic arc. It converges at χ² 920.07 for 21 parameters, versus 915.91 for the 19-parameter lens, on the same pixels. Its minor knot width reaches the 0.4-pixel floor. This explicit arc-plus-knot alternative reproduces essentially all of the residual improvement. The residual panels and fitted parameters are saved.

In the alternative lens fit, two strong predicted peaks sit along the bright crescent. The third predicted VIS peak is 0.22″ from its new mass centre. A 0.23″ aperture there gives 11.8σ after subtracting the independently fitted Sérsic foreground but only 3.5σ after subtracting the published MGE foreground. Those are post-fit positions; this component cannot establish a distinct counterimage independent of the foreground model. The image-plane excess alone also cannot assign a common source spectrum.

After fitting each band's amplitudes at this independent geometry and remeasuring the smaller, offset aperture, the conditional H result at assumed lens redshift 0.17 and β=1 is **84.3 solar units** (M = 1.689 × 10¹⁰ solar masses, H = 23.277 AB). Crediting all observed photons to the lens gives 70.3. The published geometry gives 91.0 and 80.8, respectively. Both geometries select similar mass-to-enclosed-light despite very different radius and mass. The source redshift, secure foreground redshift, resolved counterimage and source-colour confirmation remain missing. The raw inputs, fitted geometries, aperture counterimage tests, refit peak measurements and detector/filter dependence are recorded in `secondary_*.csv`.

## Independent fits of AgileLens candidates

We inspected new A/B systems from the [independent AgileLens catalogue](https://cdsarc.cds.unistra.fr/viz-bin/cat/J/A+A/712/A207) at its faint end. Five additional catalogue positions show blended VIS and H emission, tabulated in `agile_followup.csv` with their true two-band cutouts. All five have VIS core S/N 15.3–68.3 and H S/N 11.6–34.0. Two visually distinct rings received full four-band pixel fits, including one of those five.

| System | Free centre, RA and Dec (deg) | SIS+shear radius | VIS core after source subtraction | H core after source subtraction | H magnitude within Einstein radius |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| EUCLJ035520.57−490933.17, grade B, faintest catalogue H | 58.8363042, −49.1587454 | 0.566″ | 18.3σ, 25.55 AB | 23.4σ, 22.38 AB | 21.70 AB |
| EUCLJ035505.11−501633.65, grade A, faint catalogue VIS | 58.7714066, −50.2760041 | 0.900″ | 13.3σ, 25.98 AB | 39.9σ, 22.31 AB | 20.97 AB |

The grade-B ring lies about 2.2″ from the published catalogue coordinate. Both centres were fitted on VIS without fixing them to the input catalogue position. We used native IRSA Q1 MER science and RMS images and the normalized band PSFs of the nearest available successfully modelled stamp in the same tile. Each model is convolved with the measured PSF after 3 × 3 subpixel integration. VIS fits simultaneously estimate a circular Gaussian foreground, sky, and either a singular isothermal sphere with external shear lensing an elliptical Gaussian source or a competing elliptical intrinsic ring with variable brightness in the first two angular harmonics. The foreground centre is tied to the fitted mass/ring centre. Two starting conditions per family are used. The filters share VIS foreground and source geometries with independent nonnegative amplitudes and independent constant backgrounds. This is a restricted morphology test and may miss alternate fits, internal colour gradients or distinct foreground morphologies.

Uncorrelated-pixel residual χ² in the B case is 1,120 for the lens and 1,924 for the intrinsic ring (441 pixels, 14 fitted parameters each). For the A case, the values are 1,050 and 1,548 (1,257 pixels each). Resampling correlations, PSF variation, foreground flexibility and contamination affect these comparisons. The B source minor width hits the lower 0.20-pixel fit bound. The lens family wins these specific comparisons but does not verify lensing or rule out every tidal or ring morphology. The source-subtracted core is detected in all four filters in both candidate geometries.

`agile*_geometry_sensitivity.csv` reports the Einstein aperture mass and light for an explicit redshift grid using the same cosmology and shifted solar bandpasses as `../inference/infer.py`. With assumed source redshift 4, the H ratios at assumed lens redshift 0.15, 0.6, and 1.5 are 34.6, 7.39, 2.64 in solar units for the B target and 44.6, 9.51, 3.39 for the A target. At the fitted B centre, Euclid MER neighbours lie 0.45″ and 0.47″ away with photo-z 1.71 and 0.46. The photo-z 2.95 catalogue object is 2.20″ away. At the A centre two neighbours at 0.23″ and 0.70″ have photo-z 1.69 and 0.16. DESI DR1 has no spectrum within 3.5″ of either fitted centre. The geometry scenarios carry no posterior probability. A positional match and secure foreground/source redshifts would be needed to infer physical outliers. The individual IRSA cutout URLs, downloaded FITS hashes, PSF provenance, fitted parameters and local depth estimates accompany the results. Parameters in `agile*_models.csv` are x, y, radius, foreground width, foreground amplitude, source amplitude, six family-specific shape/position parameters, orientation and sky, in the order implemented by `structures`. Position units are pixels in the fitted window.

## Reproduce

From this directory, with NumPy, SciPy, Pillow and the original source CSV/TSV files in `$INPUT`, run the following. The index cache and native IRSA cutout cache may be empty. For a fresh rebuild, use an empty `--out` for `measure.py` or remove previously generated aperture and manifest CSVs. The `--all` run resumes complete object rows present in those output files.

```sh
python measure.py "$INPUT/modeling_lens_mass.csv" --all --index-cache /tmp/euclid-index
python registration.py "$INPUT/modeling_lens_mge.csv" --index-cache /tmp/euclid-index
python center_screen.py "$INPUT/q1_discovery_engine_lens_catalog.csv" "$INPUT/modeling_lens_mass.csv" --index-cache /tmp/euclid-index
python knot_colours.py "$INPUT/modeling_lens_mass.csv" --index-cache /tmp/euclid-index
python secondary_fit.py "$INPUT/modeling_lens_mass.csv" --index-cache /tmp/euclid-index
python secondary_reaperture.py --index-cache /tmp/euclid-index
python secondary_blend.py --index-cache /tmp/euclid-index
python agile_followup.py "$INPUT/agilelens.tsv"
python agile_fit.py "$INPUT/q1_discovery_engine_lens_catalog.csv" --index-cache /tmp/euclid-index --cache /tmp/euclid-native
python agile_fit.py "$INPUT/q1_discovery_engine_lens_catalog.csv" --index-cache /tmp/euclid-index --cache /tmp/euclid-native --faint-vis-a
```

The three native IRSA code paths use the [Q1 MER server-side cutout interface](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/ibe/cutouts.html). Acquisition hashes refer to the science products actually read. The center-screen step verifies even zero-byte ZIP members against their directory records, which distinguishes empty released files from download failures.
