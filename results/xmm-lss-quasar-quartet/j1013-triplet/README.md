# A 220-kpc spectroscopic quasar triplet around J1013−0148

The second quartet in the [survey ranking](../survey-quartets.csv) contains a much tighter core. Three DESI quasar targets form a nearly equilateral triangle at **z ≈ 1.51**, centered on RA 153.41510°, Dec −1.80675°. Its three sides are 23.54, 24.57 and 26.00 arcseconds, or approximately **199, 208 and 220 proper kpc** under the parent cosmology. The three full-spectrum redshifts span **939 km/s**. Separate fits to the Mg II blend span about **862 km/s**. The compact configuration is consistent with three active nuclei sharing an environment. Spectroscopy of the host galaxies and an independent lens test would distinguish a physical triple from projection or multiple imaging.

All three C IV, C III] and Mg II regions were extracted directly from the public spectra. B's Mg II line is more than three times as broad as either A's or C's. With the fitted Gaussian-plus-continuum model fixed and 120 Gaussian per-pixel noise realizations, the respective central 95% width intervals are A 2,479–3,544, B 8,518–10,809 and C 2,270–2,958 km/s. For the two narrower profiles, the same noise simulations give Mg II centroid redshift intervals of 1.50277–1.50531 (A) and 1.51001–1.51220 (C). This rejects a simple scaled copy of a single line profile for B versus A/C under the noise model. The intervals omit line blending, absorption and model error. Microlensing, variability across a time delay, or a lens affecting only two images require separate tests. **Fourth-object check.** DESI's catalog assigns target 39627745021400845 at ~1.34 Mpc maximum projected separation z = 1.51237, but its Δχ² is only 10.08. Its Mg II fit reaches a width of ~13,200 km/s with centroid z = 1.48824. It is listed in the survey output but excluded from the spectroscopically stronger three-member interpretation.

**Foreground-lens check.** A 10-arcminute search of the 1.58-million-system Wen & Han catalog found four cluster centers. The closest, J101345.1−014727, has spectroscopic redshift 0.5698, estimated M₅₀₀ = 6.0 × 10¹³ solar masses and R₅₀₀ = 0.544 Mpc. Its catalog center is 99.65 arcseconds from the triangle center (0.650 Mpc at the foreground redshift), well outside the 26-arcsecond triangle. The SDSS DR8 redMaPPer catalog returns no cluster center in the same 10-arcminute cone. A separate DESI query of the central 0.04° square identifies the three quasars as its three nearest spectra to the centroid. The nearest cataloged foreground galaxy is 21.8 arcseconds away at a tentative z = 0.9753 (Δχ² = 9.1). These checks find no cataloged cluster centered on the three images. They leave room for a fainter or miscentered deflector, environmental shear, and incomplete central spectroscopy. **Survey controls.** Using rounded, post-selection caps of 225 proper kpc and 1,000 km/s, two triangles qualified in 1,372,972 unique quality-screened targets. The other lies around J1120−0333. Across 1,000 complete within-sky-cell redshift permutations, five trials produced one such triangle and zero produced two. A stricter control retained each real velocity-coherent pair in a geometrically suitable triangle and randomized the possible third member within its own cell. Among 13 possible real-pair anchors, 98 of 1,000 realizations yielded one completed triangle and four yielded two. Thus **10.2%** had at least one chance completion at survey scale, versus **0.4%** with two. The search was exploratory and the physical status of each individual system needs independent evidence.

**Priority check.** All three spectra are public DESI measurements. Milliquas v8 returns zero entries within 210 arcseconds of the center. The 2026 GraL compilation of confirmed lensed quasars returns zero in 600 arcseconds. The closest member of the 1,842-row Jing et al. pair compilation is 34.38 arcminutes distant. The nearest parseable component in the Big MAC DR1 main table lies 0.89° away and the nearest in its triple table is 5.37° away. The compact 2013 triplet reported at the same rounded redshift by Farina et al. is at J1519+0627, in a different part of the sky. Searches for J1013−0148, the individual sexagesimal names, triple/quadruple/lens and the field coordinates found no earlier report of this specific triple. These checks do not establish field-wide publication priority.
