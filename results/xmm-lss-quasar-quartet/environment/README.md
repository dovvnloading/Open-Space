# Galaxy counts around the XMM-LSS quartet

The four quasars give a spectroscopic position and redshift for a separate test of their environment. Chen et al. report photometric redshifts for 390,900 galaxies selected in the near-infrared. Their uncertainties are much wider than the redshift interval between these quasars. At the quartet center, 19 of 355 catalog objects within 2 arcmin have a photometric redshift within 0.10 of 1.9295 (5.35%). The median for 555 nearby comparison apertures is 2.84%. Forty-six (8.29%) have at least the field's fraction. This modest excess does not establish an underlying galaxy cluster. [Chen et al. 2018, Appendix C](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/sty1036)

The grid uses 2-arcmin spacing in a tangent-plane approximation within 5–34 arcmin of the center, with detector coverage and source density approximated by retaining apertures with total counts within 20% of the target aperture. Neighboring apertures overlap. The ranks are descriptive and depend on radius and redshift width. The nine chosen combinations span 8.3–25.5% of control apertures at least as high. A 2-arcsec removal around the four quasars excluded zero entries. These are objects with published high-quality photometric redshifts, a selection that can miss active nuclei. [Nine aperture comparisons](apertures.csv)

The independent VVDS final catalog has 172 redshifts in a 6-arcmin cone, with zero within 0.045 of the quartet redshift. That magnitude-limited sample has poor coverage at this redshift. XXL's optical counterpart table already lists three of the four sources at z = 1.9200, 1.9290 and 1.9286. These historical rows credit earlier individual identifications.
