# A July 2040 candidate lacks independent clean WISE astrometry

The 237-lens search also selected WISEA J061557.21+152626.1, a T8.5 dwarf, and Gaia DR3 3345037250158664576 (G = 19.20, RUWE = 1.06). Fitting the 24 published positions gives a 43 mas separation on 17 July 2040 and a 0.49 mas peak dark-lens centroid shift for 0.05 solar masses. The conditional 5th–95th percentile of minimum separation spans 5–133 mas. Fitting the 12 Spitzer positions alone moves the minimum to 10 July at 50 mas. The 12 published WISE positions alone give a physically implausible negative parallax with an uncertainty of 166 mas. The first two fits share Spitzer data. [Kirkpatrick et al. observations, Tables 4 and 5](https://vizier.cfa.harvard.edu/viz-bin/VizieR?-source=J/ApJS/253/7)

We also extracted W2-only positions from all 16 unTimely epochs covering the target in 2010–2020. Every nearest detection carries the geometric diffraction-spike flag (128), and only two have signal-to-noise above ten. A sensitivity fit retaining all flagged detections has 680 mas RMS and a nominal closest approach of 1,426 mas. Its uncertainty and contamination prevent it from adjudicating a 43 mas approach. We retain this field as a preliminary candidate. This uses the parent forecast module, all available spacecraft coordinates and the original per-epoch errors. The bootstrap-free intervals use 5,000 Gaussian draws of the lens and source covariance, with negative source parallaxes clipped at zero. The uncertain, negative-parallax WISE-only solution gives no physical lensing prediction. The target-specific catalogue selection was `PName=0615+1526` in VizieR J/ApJS/253/7/table4.
