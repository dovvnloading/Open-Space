# A 2030 radio-source chord through Saturn’s A ring

On **28 November 2030**, Saturn’s A ring is predicted to pass in front of the compact radio source **RFC J0413+1903** as seen from Europe and Asia. Effelsberg has about **76 minutes** between outer A-ring contacts. Badary has about **60 minutes** with the source 47–54° above the horizon and clear of the projected planetary limb. The geocentric sightline misses the A ring by **2,206 km** in the ring plane. This is an observing prediction. The underlying detection is a 2026 VLBI observation of the background source.

## What an observer could test

Ingress should occur first at Effelsberg, then at Badary, then at Urumqi. The broad ring should attenuate the compact background source and the Encke gap should interrupt the attenuation. Simultaneous calibrated measurements spanning the event and adjacent unocculted baselines could test the station dependence. A VLBI baseline measures correlated flux formed from the signals at **both** antennas. A station-specific chord would need a clear reference antenna or a jointly modeled set of baselines. Effective sensitivity against Saturn, flux variability and telescope availability remain unmeasured.

## Evidence and independent checks

- **Measured source.** The 2026c Radio Fundamental Catalog gives J2000 coordinates 04h 13m 27.977792s, +19° 03′ 14.83330″. All 28 delays came from one session on 5 April 2026, experiment BP280C. The catalog lists 61, 55 and 40 mJy at X band on short, intermediate and long projected baselines. At C band the corresponding values are 56, 54 and 47 mJy. This supplies a compact continuum background without an assumption about its flux in 2030. [RFC release](https://astrogeo.org/sol/rfc/rfc_2026c/) · [Source, session, station rows and hashes](evidence/catalog.json)
- **Two ephemerides.** JPL Horizons uses DE441 for Earth and sat441l for Saturn. IMCCE Miriade separately returned INPOP19 astrometric positions for the geocenter, Effelsberg, Urumqi and Yebes. All four A-ring hit/miss decisions agree. The closest ring-plane radii differ by 151–153 km. At these telescope sites the maximum outer-edge time difference is 40 s. Both solutions use the same RFC source position and ring model. [JPL observer tables](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/manual.html) · [IMCCE ephemerides](https://ssp.imcce.fr/webservices/miriade/api/ephemcc/) · [Exact queries and hashes](evidence/manifest.json)
- **An observed historical event.** We applied the straight-ray geometry to the 3 July 1989 occultation of 28 Sgr at Palomar using the stellar direction adopted in the published analysis, a nominal B-ring edge at 117,530 km and the contemporary Horizons solution. Predicted ingress was 1.75 s earlier and egress 0.70 s later than the measured 06:16:26.670 and 09:10:42.380 UTC. These residuals validate the broad-scale geometry at one measured event, including its time and longitude conventions. [French et al., Tables 1 and 5](https://doi.org/10.1088/0004-6256/139/4/1649)
- **Astrometric and physical sensitivity.** The formal RFC errors are 0.64 mas in right ascension and 1.16 mas in declination, with correlation 0.259. Moving the source around a 72-point ellipse of Mahalanobis radius three shifts outer A-ring contacts by at most 11 s among the computed chords. This excludes catalog systematics. A separate first-order monopole lens test displaces the Urumqi closest radius outward by about 65 km, enough to leave the JPL ray only about 4 km inside the nominal inner edge of the Encke gap. The JPL and INPOP solutions accordingly give very different durations interior to the gap. Treat that fine structure as a discriminating measurement, with no guarantee of four gap-edge contacts.
- **Prior work.** The 2010 Pulkovo publication and its expanded 2015 event table list a 2028 encounter with J0225+1134. The coarse search recovers that date and source. The tabulated 2028 comparison sites in this package miss the modern nominal A-ring boundary. The older description “by ring” does not specify that boundary. The earlier list includes a **different** Saturn appulse on 1 December 2030, with 0409+188 at 306 arcsec. Its listed targets and dates do not include the 28 November J0413+1903 event. The French–Souami 2023 survey selected primarily bright near-infrared stars. Those checks establish the scope of the literature comparison. [2010 VLBI predictions](https://ivscc.gsfc.nasa.gov/publications/gm2010/lvov.pdf) · [2015 event table](https://www.gaoran.ru/english/as/ac_vlbi/occultations_approaches.pdf) · [French & Souami optical/IR survey](https://doi.org/10.3847/PSJ/aced50)

## Reproduction

From this directory run `python reproduce.py > /tmp/saturn-predictions.json` and compare the output with `predictions.json`. Python 3, NumPy and SciPy suffice. All ingested ephemeris responses are included in lossless gzip form with hashes of the uncompressed bytes. To rerun the catalog-wide candidate generation, `python screen.py` downloads the pinned RFC 2026c catalog and the 2026–2036 Horizons geocentric grid. Its output is in [screening.txt](screening.txt). The candidate search uses 12-hour samples with local quadratic interpolation and a deliberately generous angular cut of 1.35 planet diameters plus 3 arcsec. The four hits are coarse appulses. The screen makes no claim of a complete occultation census.

For each sampled arrival time the solver intersects the line through the source with the equatorial plane at Saturn’s light-time-corrected position. It solves the one-bar oblate limb separately. The source direction and the Saturn pole are in ICRF. The Saturn pole uses NAIF’s linear RA and declination terms. Radii are cubic-interpolated to find crossings. WGS84 station coordinates were calculated from RFC geocentric positions. The source solution, public ephemerides and physical ring edges dominate over the precision of numerical root finding. The optional Saturn monopole test uses the primary image of the standard point-lens equation. The prediction omits detailed gravitational multipoles, atmospheric refraction, scattering, wave propagation, local ring-edge shape, changes to the source’s radio structure, and future Earth-orientation corrections.
