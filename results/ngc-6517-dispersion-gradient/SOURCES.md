## Earlier work and selection

Lynch et al. identified the low DM of D, discussed cluster membership and argued that the offset from the mean was plausible in light of DM spreads in other clusters. Pan et al. found that E, F and G had higher DMs than their earlier sample. Their positions and timing used some of the same FAST data as Yin et al. Yin et al. reported the full 11.2 pc cm⁻³ range and discussed possible cluster and Galactic origins. Lian et al. (2025) list preliminary DMs for S–V and give a YMW16 cluster-center prediction of 214 pc cm⁻³ at 10.6 kpc versus a cluster average of 182.1. Dai et al. report the individual stack-search discoveries and the separate DM reduction. [Lian et al., Tables 1 and 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07970) P–R adopted the cluster center for their 2024 solutions, and S–V have no individually constrained positions in the 2026 paper. J is a harmonic of B. None enter the 14-sightline spatial fit. [Lynch et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1101.1467) · [Pan et al. on E–G](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14927) · [Pan et al. on H and I](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08559) · [Yin et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18228) · [Dai et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08268)

A 2023 catalogue study compared maximum-minus-minimum DM across clusters and listed NGC 6517. Earlier pulsar work in Terzan 5 studied the angular structure function of electron density fluctuations with a different sample. Searches for NGC 6517 DM gradients, positions, structure functions and predictive maps located the NGC 6517 papers above. Zhang et al. (2025) fit angular gradients of the line-of-sight magnetic field in other clusters, including Terzan 5 and M62. Their eight-cluster sample excludes NGC 6517. This is a bounded literature search. [Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08291) [2023 cluster DM range comparison](https://www.raa-journal.org/issues/all/2023/v23n5/202305/P020240604449452177516.pdf) · [Ransom et al. on Terzan 5](https://www.aspbooks.org/a/volumes/article_details/?paper_id=4525)

The exploratory screen fitted a two-coordinate plane to catalogue groups with at least five objects, a globular-cluster association, DM and printed sexagesimal coordinates (two colons per field, RA seconds string length at least four and Dec seconds length at least three). Fifteen groups passed. These deliberately recorded *initial* criteria are an imperfect proxy for astrometric quality. NGC 6624 also ranked highly, but some of its coordinates lack quoted errors and independent original timing samples there are small. Only NGC 6517 is assessed here because its positions are traceable to 14 precision solutions and it has a separate historic telescope sample. Search stages and models were chosen retrospectively. The per-target randomization probabilities in the results are conditional diagnostics. A simple 15-target Bonferroni correction would give approximately 0.0873 (15 × 0.005818), **not** 0.00003. Even that correction does not account for other hypotheses and analyses explored; no survey-wide significance is claimed.

## Reproduction

Run `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python analyze.py` in this directory, or use its absolute path from another directory. Python 3, NumPy and SciPy suffice. The source tables are bundled and no network access is required. Run `python plot_radio.py` with Matplotlib installed to regenerate the radio figure from the frozen tables and JSON. The analysis run writes `radio-analysis.json` including all leave-one-out fits and the exact rank-permutation count. To replay source selection or the smooth Galaxy comparison, run `python screen.py /path/to/psrcat.db` or `python smooth_galaxy.py /path/to/ymw16_v1.3`. The latter also requires a C compiler and writes `smooth-galaxy.csv`. The distance grid brackets 9.26 kpc and includes the 10.6 kpc distance used in earlier pulsar-dynamics work. [Baumgardt distance measurements](https://people.smp.uq.edu.au/HolgerBaumgardt/globular/fits/disfit/ngc6517_dist.pdf) · [NGC 6517 timing-dynamics study](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stad3598)

## Reading the tests

The sky-plane fit minimizes unweighted squared residuals. Target-to-target differences dominate the catalogued formal errors of 0.002–0.020 pc cm⁻³. The HC3 standard errors in the output account for unequal leverage and residual variance under independent residuals. They do not propagate an unknown spatial covariance. The east gradient alone has lower leave-one-out error than the two-coordinate model. The fitted north term is smaller than its HC3 standard error. Removing any single pulsar leaves the fitted variance fraction between 0.937 and 0.976. Removing D predicts its DM as 176.092 pc cm⁻³ versus the measured 174.537, an error of 1.555. The model captures substantial angular variation while missing structure at the level of individual lines of sight.

The GBT prediction uses the direction from the centroid of A/B/C to D, as set by the original 2011 positions. The intercept and one slope use only those four GBT positions and DMs. The ten new FAST pulsars are ten other astrophysical sources. GBT-only predictions are low by 0.77 pc cm⁻³ on average on the ten. The exact two-sided rank test permutes all 10! later-source DMs against predicted values, making no distributional assumption about DM amplitudes. The cohort split was examined retrospectively. A second split by discovery era within Yin et al. trains on A–I and tests K–O. It shares a telescope and timing analysis.

For the spatial nulls, the simulation draws mean-zero Gaussian fields on the measured angular positions, with isotropic structure function proportional to angular separation raised to 1, 5/3 or 1.95. Double-centering the pairwise variogram gives the covariance of the field minus its sample mean. The amplitude cancels out of the fitted R² statistic. Each null uses 1,000,000 draws without tuning the exponent. The Kolmogorov case produces at least as high an R² in 26.24% of draws. This tests whether a smooth-looking fit identifies a special structure beyond ordinary correlated fluctuations. It does not test the distance of the electrons or the absolute turbulence amplitude.

Yin et al. used a common 170–190 pc cm⁻³ search band for the field. The precise 174.537–185.740 range lies inside those boundaries. Detection thresholds, angular beam sensitivity, scattering and sparse spatial coverage may still affect which pulsars are present. Every measurement integrates electrons all the way to its pulsar, so cluster gas and localized intervening material both remain possible. As a limited depth diagnostic, fractional spin derivatives and residual DMs have Spearman ρ = −0.169 (nominal p = 0.56). Intrinsic spin-down, cluster dynamics and systematics limit inference from that proxy. See [Yin et al., Section 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18228).

The smooth-Galaxy test uses four common distances for all sources and evaluates the catalogue sky positions individually. The DM differences across those positions are 0.16, 0.16, 0.18 and 0.20 pc cm⁻³, respectively. At 9.26 kpc the predicted D and L values are 204.22 and 204.06 pc cm⁻³. The observed D and L values are 174.537 and 185.740 pc cm⁻³. An additive model offset cannot fix the sign or magnitude of the differential. An unresolved fluctuation in the Galactic foreground remains consistent with the turbulent-field simulation.
