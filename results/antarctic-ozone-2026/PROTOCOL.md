# Cross-sensor persistence of deep Antarctic ozone depletion in November 2026

Frozen 18 November 2026 on main 7e8e21ba before any outcome computation. Header and row-format scouting displayed a few early rows of one file. The NASA and NOAA season reports already document the large 2026 ozone hole. The question is how much agreement there is on deep depletion at fixed locations late in the available November record, relative to the early November footprint and to the same dates of two earlier seasons. [NASA 2026 report](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/2026-ozone-hole/) · [NOAA 2026 report](https://www.noaa.gov/news-release/noaa-nasa-release-measurements-of-2026-ozone-hole)

## Input and support

- Freeze daily Aura OMI OMTO3d and NOAA-20 OMPS nadir-mapper total ozone grids, 1–15 November in 2024, 2025 and 2026. Use the published 18 September 2026 maximum-extent day as a retrieval and area check outside the November comparison, if both grids are available. Resolve filenames and revisions from the producer indexes before reading outcomes. Use the archive column in Dobson units directly. Verify same lattice and day, preserve version and receipt, and exclude zero or missing values. The daily products provide values on an aggregation grid and do not establish exact common overpass time or identical upstream retrieval masks. Preserve all daily coverage counts and nominal local crossing times.
- The geographic domain is the cap south of 60°S. The broader cap south of 40°S is a daily area sensitivity comparable to the geographic scope of NASA's 220 DU definition. Use cell area from a sphere of radius 6,371 km and latitude edges with 1° longitude width. No spatial filling or weighting by raw pixel count. Include independent single-sensor areas, each daily intersection of valid data, and lower and upper bounds for threshold area when the unobserved area has unknown classification. Use the same <220 DU convention on both sensors, and never interpret missing as ozone rich.
- Use early 1–5 November and late 11–15 November as two distinct windows. Make a fixed support by intersecting both sensors' available grid cells across all ten days for each year. For interannual comparison intersect those fixed footprints across all three years. Report area retained by year, latitude bands and longitude sectors. Treat fixed-footprint conclusions as insufficient for a cap-wide inference if the all-year common support is smaller than half of the 60°S cap. Show results on the within-year fixed grids, a fixed geographic cap south of 70°S, and the relaxed within-year footprint with valid pairs on at least four days of each five-day window. No day beyond 15 November enters the comparison.

## Tests

- The primary threshold is **180 DU**, an operational definition of deep depletion. Score the area whose ozone is below 180 DU in both daily products on at least four of five early days and on all five late days. Divide its area by the area that qualifies in the early window. Report area and fraction on the all-year fixed footprint and each within-year footprint. Record early-only, late-only and shared regions. Repeat with thresholds of 170, 190, 200, 220 and 240 DU, and contrast early and late total occupied areas. A fixed location may be traversed by different air masses. No continuous 15-day persistence claim follows from the two-window statistic.
- For each daily matched footprint, report area under the threshold for each sensor, union, intersection and directional disagreement, the count of both sensors agreeing on low ozone, and the latitude-weighted median OMI minus OMPS. For the 220 DU diagnostic also report disagreement where the mean of the two measurements is within 15 DU of the threshold. Record the ozone deficit against 220 DU in DU km² as an area-depth statistic. Report maximum area from single-sensor data as a coverage check and compare September with the published estimate while preserving differences in method. Adjacent cells, days and sensor retrievals share sources of error, so the comparisons are descriptive and use no pixel-level significance.
- Screen daily November geometries for two comparably supported low-ozone regions at 220 DU south of 40°S. Use edge-connected cells, periodic longitude and exact areas. A split candidate requires a second consensus component of at least 0.5 million km² on each of two consecutive dates. Its corresponding two components must also be distinct after allowing any jointly missing cell to connect them. Report exclusion because of unresolved bridges and preserve the individual-sensor masks. This is a gridded column-ozone test and cannot identify vortex breakup by itself.
- Compare the ground record only where a contemporaneous observation is available, preserving each timestamp and the point-versus-grid difference. The accessible South Pole Dobson archive stops before November; a November 3 balloon profile alone cannot independently follow November 11–15. Review NASA, CSIRO and BAS seasonal monitoring for previously reported structures before assigning a noteworthy label. Long-term recovery and chemistry require a much longer and more complete design.
