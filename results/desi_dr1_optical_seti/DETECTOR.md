### Search, injections, and units

Each invocation of `scan_target` covers one star and one camera arm. Supply the independent survey EXPID inventory, including exposures absent from a downloaded subset. Missing or duplicate exposure records raise an error. A missing arm or masked region is a loss of sensitivity. Retain the complete resulting rest-frame intervals, per-exposure triggers, coverage count, and each vetting decision.

DESI cframes have barycentric vacuum wavelength grids and a fibre-specific resolution matrix. The observed wavelength is divided by the star's relativistic Doppler factor to obtain its rest wavelength. If using redshift, convert with `beta = ((1+z)**2-1)/((1+z)**2+1)`. Confirm the supplied RV definition for the catalogue used. To test terrestrial lines, separately divide barycentric wavelengths by exposure-header HELIOCOR. Fit or supply an independent stellar template in the correct frame, convolved exactly once to the exposure resolution. A template fit directly to the test star's coadd can absorb persistent features. Assess this loss by refitting raw-flux injections through the same template procedure, or label limits as conditional on a fixed template.

The detector subtracts the supplied stellar model. On each pair of adjacent native input wavelength samples it fits an affine baseline and a positive integrated line flux. The unit-area response is a continuous linear mixture of the two normalized DESI resolution-matrix columns. Centroid fitting evaluates the two endpoints and the analytic interior stationary point of the weighted matched-filter S/N. The fitting window contains both columns' supports and four baseline samples on each side. Every sample in that window must have zero mask and positive finite inverse variance and finite residual. The algorithm uses diagonal extraction inverse variance. Unmodelled correlated residuals can alter the reference chi-square distribution. The S/N threshold is a detection rule, without a survey-wide false-alarm probability attached.

An exposure trigger requires S/N ≥7. The LSF fit must have shape p≥0.001 after fitting a local intercept, slope, centroid, and amplitude. A positive one-pixel response or a broadened LSF that improves chi-square by >9 rejects the event. The broadened model convolves the local LSF with a Gaussian having its positive-profile second-moment width. For a constant rest wavelength every exposure pair must agree within three combined centroid errors and half the narrower effective FWHM. Centroid errors include the quoted independent per-exposure RV error and a calibration floor of 0.12 native pixels. These cuts do not simulate all possible blends or detector defects. The output includes centres, shape tests, flux uncertainties, and per-exposure significance even when a trigger fails. A persistent hit still requires sky, trace-neighbour, detector, stellar and background-object vetting.

`recovery.measure_completeness` draws iid uniform rest wavelengths in the explicitly specified interval for one star/arm. It injects a unit-area local instrumental response at random subpixel phase and a specified received line flux into each independently acquired calibrated spectrum, then repeats the search. Keep the random seed, interval, sampled wavelengths, fluxes, outcome and stage for every trial. The same wavelength draws are paired across flux levels. Exclusions and pre-existing coincidences count as misses in the full-interval denominator. `eligible_only=True` produces a separate result conditional on the intersection of unmasked coverage. Emission must be present at a detectable exposure-averaged flux in every exposure. Intermittent transients are outside this selection.

A refit callback can propagate the injected signal through the actual stellar-template selection and fitting. A veto callback can replay all candidate checks, including the sky fibres. Without these callbacks, results are restricted to recovery conditional on a fixed stellar model and/or through the spectral search stage. Native-column interpolation is an approximation to the subpixel LSF. This simulation does not include pre-extraction operations such as cosmic-ray clipping, cross-talk subtraction, or the contribution of the artificial photon's shot noise. Document the range where background/source continuum noise dominates and the calibration assumptions before interpreting power limits.

`summarize_recovery` reports recovery fractions and exact one-sided Clopper–Pearson lower bounds, Bonferroni-adjusted for the number of fluxes tested per star/arm/window. The first flux whose lower bound and every brighter tested level's lower bound clear the target is returned. An insufficient trial count or recovery fraction leaves the limit empty. Trial wavelengths on measured residuals characterize the fraction of randomly chosen wavelengths detected in that interval for the recorded exposures. They do not produce a uniform guarantee for every spectral pixel. Use separate wavelength bins for limits that vary over an arm.

For received integrated line flux F in DESI units of 10⁻¹⁷ erg s⁻¹ cm⁻² and distance d in parsecs, the apparent exposure-averaged isotropic-equivalent line power is 4π(d × 3.08567758149 × 10¹⁶ m)² F × 10⁻²⁰ W m⁻². Convert lower/upper distance quantiles independently. Intrinsic emitter power additionally requires an extinction model and radiation pattern. A pulsed signal yields only its exposure-averaged received line flux. Record the distance source, Gaia identifier, quality flags and available uncertainty for each star.
