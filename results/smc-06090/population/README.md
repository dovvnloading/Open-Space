# Rarity among well-matched binaries

The criteria in design.md were fixed before extracting photometry for the comparison population, after the target itself was known. They selected 676 non-contact SMC binaries. For 548, the two modern epochs had adequate coverage and fitted precision. The target and 19 others had an absolute change of at least 0.07882 cycles. Among 442 with all four usable epochs, the target and four others showed opposite directions in the two independent eras and exceeded both of its absolute shifts (0.03237 cycles in OGLE-III and 0.07882 in OGLE-IV). This is an observed comparison count. The table includes real astrophysical variations, observational artifacts, and effects of differing cadence. The target was originally selected for its unusual behavior. This comparison cannot supply a false-alarm probability.

The other four were OGLE-SMC-ECL-03655, 03825, 04501 and 04595. The OGLE-III catalog specifically flagged 3825 for visible apsidal motion and the OGLE-IV catalog flagged 04501 as uncertain classification. The screen therefore recovers a published example, and additional candidate behavior requires individual checking. The literature status of the other two was not established.

Per-epoch fits use two phase-wrapped Gaussian magnitude dips plus a second orbital harmonic. Shape, centers, amplitude and baseline are independent for each time window. Formal covariance uses the robust effective Jacobian, a standard deviation floor and an empirical residual scale. These formal errors are used as cuts, without interpreting them as calibrated coverage limits. Trying five maximum-error cuts from 0.0075 to 0.030 cycles preserved the same five objects, with eligible populations ranging from 401 to 446. Each of the 2,704 attempted star/window combinations, including missing crossmatches, appears in epoch_measurements.csv.

Run OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python measure_population.py --prepare --workers 3 with NumPy and SciPy. The preparation step downloads both OGLE photometry archives (about 189 MiB compressed) and the three catalog tables. Per-file hashes for these inputs are recorded in sources.json. OGLE-III field.star identifiers were joined against the OGLE-IV crossmatch entries. Target times and periods use the OGLE-IV ephemeris in both eras. Dates are in heliocentric Julian days.
