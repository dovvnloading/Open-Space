# GBM conditional joint-detector screen: calibration did not support a physical test

Status: the separately authorized exploratory characterization of the seven fixed windows was completed; target TTE times and channels were read for that phase. The significance phase was not executed. The test is not ready for real-photon execution: a valid burst-envelope/PSD null, time-dependent response and Earth-occultation validation, saturation checks, empirical background and full false-positive calibration remain unresolved. A guarded joint-data extraction stage exists, but it is NOT authorized to run. The code in this folder is a bounded simulation and FITS-reader prototype, not an authorized science pipeline. No physical QPO, novel engine, or calibrated p-value is claimed.

## Fixed bounded study population

The 2026-10-01–07 UTC archive snapshot contains 12 trigger directories. Six have catalog-summary files; the deterministic cut T90 5–60 s and 1024-ms peak ≥5 photons cm−2 s−1 selects `bn261002867` (T90 8.96016 s, peak 8.16094) and `bn261007236` (22.7844 s, 43.5294). Four with summaries fail; six missing summaries remain unknown eligibility, including a GRB-classified trigger. This is a two-burst study of those two known selected bursts, not a complete rate, representative population or absence limit. Catalog brightness/duration was examined before this proposal; no oscillation power was inspected. [Inventory](inventory.json) and [archive snapshot](metadata/trigger-index-receipt.json) retain missing cases.

For bn261002867 the GBM team's later localization RA 155°, Dec −1.9°, statistical 5.5° is in https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45809 . For bn261007236 the Swift/XRT location RA 11.61403°, Dec −17.69197°, 90% radius 2.7 arcsec is in https://gcn.nasa.gov/circulars/45850 . At sampled trigger-data spacecraft attitudes, the nearest two detector directions are n5/n2 (14.9–15.3° / 41.9–42.1°) and nb/n8 (18.7–19.1° / 33.8–34.4°) respectively. These are jointly analyzed detectors A and B; neither is a held-out independent confirmation. `metadata/refined_geometry.py` uses the official detector axes and trigdat quaternion, after matching the quaternion convention to the spacecraft X/Z header axes; [geometry](geometry.json) records epochs and limitations. Four detector response headers and 128 EBOUNDS maps, bcat and trigdat files and hashes are deposited as metadata. Exact Earth blockage, response evolution, data gaps, detector quality, uncertainty across localization region and source detectability must be verified before adopting these splits. The 5.5° value is statistical only, not the full localization systematic. A detector that fails an a priori sensitivity/coverage gate gives an inconclusive burst; do not replace it based on oscillation strength.

## Proposed trials and null

Each burst contributes non-overlapping 4-second windows starting at its bcat T90START, with only complete windows contained in T90: 2 and 5 windows respectively. Time sampling 1/64 s gives Fourier frequencies 5–32 Hz in 0.25 Hz steps, 109 bins per window. One nominal recorded-energy band uses EBOUNDS channels entirely inside 50–300 keV. This specifies 2 bursts, 7 windows, 109 frequencies and 763 frequency-window hypotheses; no tuning additional energy bands or window offsets. Recorded channel energy is not a deconvolved true photon energy; detector EBOUNDS/response vary. Eventual trigger-level statistic is the maximum across all 763 hypotheses of the lesser of the two detector residual powers, using predeclared normalization and fitting rules. The two measurements share source envelope and spacecraft clock and are not independent astrophysical trials; do not multiply individual detector p-values.

A valid null must draw one common, temporally correlated intrinsic burst process per burst, observed through the two responses, plus detector-specific counting processes, background, deadtime, GTIs and timing faults. Fit and test more than one nonstationary envelope and red-noise model, preserving nuisance uncertainty and the full joint search including all 763 trials; include pulse-train alternatives and separate off-burst detector backgrounds. Off-burst data cannot establish that the burst itself lacks red noise. Nominal GBM nonparalyzable deadtime is 2.6 Î¼s per normal event and 10 Î¼s for overflow; pileup, saturation and timing glitches require measured per-detector quality checks and response/clock caveats https://fermi.gsfc.nasa.gov/ssc/data/analysis/GBM_caveats.html . If needed housekeeping, overflow or calibrated rate is unavailable, declare inconclusive rather than assume no effect. No numerical saturation threshold has yet been established.

`simulator.py` is a toy demonstrator: a shared latent AR(1) process within each burst is seen by both detectors with separate Poisson draws and per-detector nonparalyzable deadtime. It includes two chosen illustrative envelopes; this is not a fitted real-burst null. With 100 training and 100 held-out toy simulations, a 95th percentile training threshold gave 3/100 crossings in an independent same-model null (approximate 95% Wilson interval 1.0–8.5%), 4/100 with the alternative toy envelope (1.6–9.8%), and 100/100 with a 15% injected modulation (96.3–100%). See [synthetic receipt](metadata/synthetic-validation.json); the first development run had an incorrect rate/count normalization; its [result](metadata/failed-original-normalization.json) and [runner](metadata/failed-original-normalization-run.py) are retained. The faulty simulator source was not separately frozen before correction, so its exact code bytes are unavailable. This tiny experiment tests software behavior only: it neither calibrates low false-alarm tails nor proves any red-noise model applies. Rare-tail estimates require many simulations, finite-Monte-Carlo intervals and held-out false-positive checks; no formal significance threshold is frozen here.

`fits_reader.py` validates EVENTS/EBOUNDS/GTI, exact detector/trigger identity, TT MJD reference, units, monotonic event times and finite channel mapping. `observational.py` is a separately authorized joint-data entry point: before any EVENTS decoding it checks an exact committed manifest and all four input hashes, authorization phase/hash, output collision and all four headers/GTIs/EBOUNDS. No such authorization has been issued and it MUST NOT be run on target TTE files at this stage. Four selected input CDF-equivalent FITS hashes and metadata-only inspections are in metadata/tte-receipts.json and metadata/tte-metadata.json. Preliminary FITS OBJECT labels are SFL and DISTPAR, so identity is bound to suffix and trigger time, not those transient onboard classifications. `test_synthetic.py` tests bin-integrated 32 Hz injection, Poisson non-Nyquist/Nyquist normalization, independent counting with shared latent signal, ordinary and overflow deadtime, alternate envelope, synthetic FITS identity/units/GTI, byte mutation, phase and output collision guards. These are not real-background injections or physical validation. A published GRB 240825A 6.37 Hz candidate https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16538 is a comparison benchmark, not assured physical ground truth.

Before any target-photon access: root must review the committed phase/hash guard, frozen full input bytes and energy mapping, establish acceptable detector/source response and saturation checks, and validate or explicitly bound the joint nonstationary null on synthetic and empirical background controls. Fit/marginalization details and common-noise family must be fixed before interpreting target data. No claim that the two nominal detectors independently confirm a physical oscillation follows merely from a peak in both.

`envelope.py` implements a provisional common-envelope smoothing fit (width 9, 33 or 65 bins), common AR(1) latent red-noise process with explicitly varied phi ±0.1 and amplitude ×0.5–1.5, detector-specific Poisson/deadtime, refitting each surrogate and maximum over both bursts. These engineering ranges are not confidence-calibrated. An irregular, deliberately aperiodic synthetic pulse train returned the minimum attainable p=0.05 with 19 simulations for all three widths (artifact SHA 090bce501d3e4ccf257ca92aa845ea1f0f42c525a3723d4ffc9811a6b94d83e7). This single deliberately chosen case is a warning at the coarse Monte Carlo floor, not an estimated false-positive rate; the three smoother outcomes are correlated. Selecting the smallest p across widths would add an uncalibrated trial. No real-data significance is justified by this test. The previous normalization bug remains recorded; the corrected simulator integrates sinusoidal intensity over finite bins and treats the real Nyquist bin separately.

## Exploratory characterization — separate authorization

`exploratory.py` is a distinct phase, `exploratory_characterization`, requiring an exact reviewed manifest and separate root authorization before any target EVENTS access. After verifying all four hashes, headers, GTIs and channel maps, it first computes full-band recorded counts, overflow channel 127 counts and nominal nonparalyzable deadtime (2.6 μs for ordinary events, 10 μs overflow) for each fixed 4-second window. It then reports the predeclared 50–300 keV recorded-channel light curves, PHA histograms, and all 109 per-window detector and joint Fourier powers. It does not calculate p-values, rank bursts/frequencies or label detections. Deadtime is an approximation based on recorded events; cross-window boundary effects, pileup, unrecorded photons and housekeeping/saturation remain unverified. Nominal live time is not a proven complete detector exposure. Full-band and band curves share the same observational source and spacecraft clock.

Exploration and any subsequent null-model revisions are not blind significance validation. Separate from the original single adversarial example, 20 independently generated irregular-pulse synthetic two-burst cases with 19 null replicates each and one fixed 33-bin smoother gave 13/20 at the coarse p=0.05 simulation floor. This is an empirical warning for that artificial generator, with substantial finite-sample and Monte Carlo uncertainty; it is not an astrophysical false-positive rate. The [ensemble result](metadata/pulse-ensemble.json) and its code are preserved. The alternatives, real-background and saturation checks remain incomplete; no physical QPO or significance follows.

## Completed exploratory characterization and retrospective calibration

The [exploratory result](exploration/characterization.json) was produced once under the separately recorded [root authorization](exploration/root-authorization.json), with [execution](exploration/execution.json) and exact [manifest](exploration/manifest.json). The same raw events were independently reprocessed by [independent_check.py](exploration/independent_check.py); its [result](exploration/independent_check.json) reports exact agreement in counts, channel histograms and Fourier powers. The arrays are descriptive only: no p-value, ranked peak, detection or physical QPO interpretation was produced. The four spacecraft-detector streams share source emission and clock; recorded-event live-time approximations do not establish absence of saturation, pileup, missing photons or timing faults.

After this exploratory inspection, [retrospective synthetic calibration](calibration/README.md) tested a distribution of aperiodic pulse cases and found the proposed screen unable to distinguish one alternate pulse family from injected oscillations. The model changes and simulations are retrospective, not blind validation. The screen failed its calibration challenge; no supported physical oscillation test or zero-QPO claim follows. The unexecuted joint-significance phase remains sealed. Raw TTE FITS files remain external on D:.
