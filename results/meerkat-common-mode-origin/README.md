# Origin tests for the MeerKAT shared Faraday-rotation term

**Status: reproducible common-mode residual structure, not an identified physical cause.** This follow-up is distinct from [PR #47](https://github.com/dovvnloading/Open-Space/pull/47): it decomposes the ionospheric correction, contrasts sky sectors, and tests generalization to unseen dates and disjoint pulsars. All source data remain in `../meerkat-shared-faraday-rotation/`. No original files are changed.

## New empirical results

1. **J1057−5226 is strongly coupled to the predicted ionosphere.** On the same 53 matched observations, the measured (pre-ionosphere-subtraction) RM trend is **−0.25134**, the ionFR/JPL ionospheric prediction trend is **−0.38443**, and the released corrected RM consequently trends **+0.13308 rad m⁻² yr⁻¹**. The contemporaneous leave-target-out peer offset trends **+0.13303**; the remainder is **+0.000059**. Pearson correlation of the peer offset with the *published* ionospheric RM prediction is **−0.90868**. This is an observational decomposition, not an independent validation of the TEC map.

2. **A single global ionosphere scaling error fails to describe the full sample.** Across 304 matched pulsars, 168 have peer-offset/published-ionosphere correlation below −0.5, while 49 have correlation above +0.5. In RA 9–12 h, **55/57** are below −0.5; in 12–15 h, **29/29**; in 18–21 h, only **5/103** are below −0.5 and **38/103** are above +0.5. These pulsar counts are *not independent trials*: pulsars in the same sessions share observing conditions. The change of sign strongly suggests sky-, epoch-, or scheduling-dependent terms that a universal scalar correction cannot absorb. `diagnostics.py` reproduces the partitions from the fixed input rows.

3. **Within-day/direction test.** A two-way fixed-effect regression of `measured RM − published ionospheric RM` on the published ionospheric prediction, controlling separately for each pulsar and UTC day, gives coefficient **−0.723 ± 0.088** (day-cluster sandwich SE; 12,357 precision-filtered visits, 362 pulsars, 157 sufficiently populated days). The partial R² is only **0.032**. Restricting to local solar hour 08–16 gives **−1.111 ± 0.071**, and remaining times give **−0.443 ± 0.076**. These differences are associations conditional on a scheduling and sky-selection process, *not* a calibrated TEC multiplier. They could also reflect beam, polarization, elevation, or subarray-dependent systematics. Solar time is approximate (longitude-corrected UTC, ignoring the equation of time). Geometric elevation uses the Meeus-style approximate sidereal rotation at MeerKAT (30.71322° S, 21.44306° E).

4. **Strict held-out calendar-year validation.** A group-A daily residual predicts the independent group-B daily residual on **68 held-out days in 2022–2023**, with neither source group overlapping and only **56 training days in 2020–2021**. Using `A` directly, without a fitted coefficient, reduces B's test RMSE **0.33156 → 0.23061 rad m⁻²**, a **30.4% decrease** (day-bootstrap 95% interval **16.7–41.7%**). An A→B gain trained on 2020–2021 is **0.8416** (intercept **−0.0016**) and yields test RMSE **0.22209**. By contrast, a correction predicted from group-A's *published ionospheric RM alone*, with coefficients trained on 2020–2021, raises held-out RMSE to **0.38305**. Including iono alongside A is also worse out of time (**0.24149**) than A alone. Thus residual common mode is *predictable across pulsars and years*, but does **not** reduce to a stable scalar correction of the originally predicted ionospheric RM.

5. **Risk to individual published gradients.** In the 304-pulsar matched subset, 38 have >3σ *full-data* slopes; 18 of those have <3σ after the same-visit differential correction. Independently, 15 of 35 same-visit >3σ slopes fall below 3σ, and 10 other pulsars cross above. These are nominal *conditional* significance tests, not revised independent detections: comparison uncertainty and session correlation are not in the formal SE. See `published_risks.csv` for exact target-by-target changes and coverage limits.

## Causal alternatives and falsification

| Mechanism | Expected discriminant | Finding so far / falsification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Residual or excessive ionFR/JPL ionospheric correction** | Residual tracks independently computed CODE/IGS IONEX + IGRF slant RM and local-time/elevation trends; discrepancy varies with TEC and geomagnetic conditions | Strong J1057 relationship and differential local-time coefficients favor investigating this. **A single all-sky scalar discrepancy is contradicted** by reversed sky-sector associations and the held-out failure of an iono-only model. The independent CODE/IGRF comparison below does not improve held-out RM residuals, so an ionosphere-only explanation is unvalidated. |
| **Receiver polarization / RM-synthesis or calibration common mode** | Epoch-specific step, common subarray or calibrator; persists after independent TEC corrections; little physically predicted slant-TEC dependence | Plausible. Original `.rmtable` rows have no receiver/subarray/calibrator IDs. They cannot establish a hardware attribution; match to MeerKAT schedule/quality/calibration logs. |
| **Observing-time / elevation selection** | Bias follows sidereal hour angle, altitude or fixed RA/declination after allowing for UTC date; disappears in matched elevation/time controls | Sky-sector reversal and hour-of-day differences make this an important confound. Test within the same observing hour and subarray, versus matched zenith-angle contrasts. |
| **Real spatially coherent astrophysical Faraday screen** | Same angular pattern at independent observatories, not tied to local solar/geomagnetic states, survives independently computed ionosphere subtraction | Not excluded by one telescope's sample. Independent epoch-matched RM at a different observatory or frequency band is decisive. |

**Not yet a causal attribution:** no CODE/IGS IONEX, IRI, IGRF, independent RMextract/ionFR rerun, F10.7, Kp, Dst or operational receiver/subarray logs were present in the PR #47 measurement table. Never describe the published `RMiono` column as an independent TEC-model comparison. The new diagnostic code preserves this distinction. External products and telescope metadata are explicitly identified as necessary inputs for a final cause claim.

## Scope and provenance

Read-only input: `../meerkat-shared-faraday-rotation/measurements.csv` (20,365 released RM-table rows), `dates.csv` (124 disjoint-group UTC dates), `slopes.csv` (304 matched pulsars) and `J1057-5226.csv` (53 visits). The original publication states the RM tables report *as-measured* RM and predicted ionFR-derived ionospheric RM separately; it used JPL TEC maps. Sources: [Keith et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stae937), [MeerTime data DOI](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.8430591), [ionFR](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201220729), [IGS IONEX products](https://igs.org/products), [GFZ indices](https://kp.gfz.de/en/data), [NASA OMNI](https://data.nasa.gov/dataset/omni-daily-data-set).

The held-out date-level comparison is *separate* from the same-date, leave-source-out correction in PR #47. It uses pulsar-disjoint daily residual estimates and later years as an independent test period. Its confidence interval is a simple resampling of held-out **days**, not 6,351 correlated visits. No model fits to held-out 2022–2023 days.


## New independent solar/geomagnetic-index check — completed

The NASA CDAWeb OMNI2 hourly archive supplied actual **2020–2023** F10.7, Kp and Dst, joined to **all 124** independently split MeerKAT dates. File SHA-256s and all date-level join receipts are in `space_weather_checks.json` and `space_weather_days.csv`. Using the signed mean of the disjoint-group residuals, Pearson correlations were **Kp(max) −0.025**, **Dst(min) +0.117**, **F10.7(median) +0.007**. For the absolute mean residual they were **+0.041, −0.086 and +0.077** respectively. The median |offset| was **0.2144** for 12 storm-classified dates (Kp≥5 or Dst≤−50 nT) versus **0.1456** for 60 quiet dates (Kp<3 and Dst>−30 nT), an uncontrolled descriptive comparison.

A ridge-regularized linear predictor using *only these indices* and trained on the 56 dates in **2020–2021** gave a **2022–2023 test RMSE of 0.7479 rad m⁻²**, worse than using no offset (**0.3316**) and much worse than the peer-only predictor (**0.2221**). Peers plus indices tested at **0.3905** (also worse). These poor held-out predictions disfavor a simple *global-index* explanation, not local electron-column errors or geomagnetic responses. High solar/geomagnetic indices are not themselves slant TEC, and 12 storms do not establish an event-level effect.

`space_weather.py` re-fetches the original files with `--fetch`, preserves their hashes, and recreates all 124 joins; the same process completed on GitHub Actions. Bibliography: [NASA OMNI source](https://cdaweb.gsfc.nasa.gov/pub/data/omni/low_res_omni/), [OMNI documentation](https://omniweb.gsfc.nasa.gov/html/ow_data.html), [GFZ Kp](https://kp.gfz.de/en/data), [Kyoto Dst](https://wdc.kugi.kyoto-u.ac.jp/dst_final/index.html).

## Independent CODE TEC/IGRF diagnostic

`code_ionex_probe.py` downloads **CODE final GNSS TEC IONEX**, not the released JPL map, and computes a separate thin-shell slant RM using IGRF-14, the actual UTC times/directions and MeerKAT geographic position. It records original file URLs/hashes, pierce-point coordinates, altitude, predicted CODE RM and released JPL/ionFR RM. The initial pilot preselects two large-offset 2022 dates (October 8 and December 16), a 2022 intermediate date (November 12), and January 31 2023. The archived workflows completed those downloads and pilot calculations, but they do not validate a replacement ionospheric correction. This is not a full RMextract/ionFR reproduction; the shell assumption, electron height, finite IONEX resolution, ionFR corrections and two-decimal-MJD timing create model uncertainty. The archive index and filename transition are documented by [IGS](https://igs.org/products/) and [CODE](https://lists.igs.org/pipermail/igsmail/2026/008735.html).

## Full 124-date CODE/IGRF comparison — a negative holdout

The later `code_ionex_all_dates.py` experiment uses all **124 prespecified dates**, with **56 dates in 2020–2021** for centering the CODE-minus-JPL correction offsets and **68 dates in 2022–2023** held out. It keeps the original pulsar baselines fixed. On held-out dates, the group-B daily residual RMSE is **0.33156 rad m⁻²** with the released JPL/ionFR correction and **0.39326 rad m⁻²** after approximate CODE/IGRF substitution: **worse, not better**. The source-disjoint A-to-B differential RMSE likewise rises from **0.23061 to 0.24730 rad m⁻²**, while daily A/B residual correlation increases from **0.736 to 0.781**. These figures reconcile with [the full-date table](code_ionex_full_dates.csv) and [summary](code_ionex_full_checks.json); [124 archive receipts](code_ionex_full_sources.csv) record source products and SHA-256 hashes.

The smaller spread on four deliberately selected pilot dates therefore **does not generalize to the held-out full sample**. In addition, those four dates show negative across-sightline CODE-versus-JPL RM correlations, so geometry, sign convention, shell assumptions and differences from ionFR/RMextract must be validated before treating either calculation as a physical truth standard. Neither the pilot nor the full-date substitution identifies a causal ionospheric, instrumental or astrophysical origin, and neither supplies replacement per-pulsar RM measurements.
