# Independent referee report — public ultrashort Galactic-bulge alerts

**Scope:** PR #154, freeze 17 November 2026, branch `research/ultrashort-bulge`. This is a **survey of previously alerted, public light curves**, not a blind all-star census and not a free-floating-planet discovery. The numerical archive gives no monitored-star-time denominator, source-luminosity selection function or host-detection completeness. No planetary occurrence rate or upper limit in planets per star follows from these injections.

## Provenance and numerical checks

The independently executable checks are `referee_audit.py` (frozen-table and receipt reconciliation), `referee_replay.py` (download and SHA-256-verify the original light curves, independently evaluate the recorded parameter vectors, repeat optimization for the two most decisive events, and refit OGLE-2016-BLG-1928) and `validate.py --models`. The GitHub Actions workflow `.github/workflows/ultrashort-bulge-referee.yml` preserves logs and replay JSON as an artifact. Receipt-versus-receipt equality is *not* itself a raw-input hash verification: the replay downloads original KMT and OGLE bytes and checks their digest; the original survey manifests cover the remaining light curves.

## Four priority leads: literature/novelty

This review checked the [KMTNet event and publication portal](https://kmtnet.kasi.re.kr/ulens/), the [KMTNet planet/FFP archive](https://kmtnet.kasi.re.kr/exoKMTNet/index.html), the [OGLE EWS catalog](https://ogle.astrouw.edu.pl/ogle4/ews/ews.html), 2024–2026 name/alias searches in arXiv/ADS and the deposited `other/known_roster.csv`, `other/known_matches.csv` and `kmt/planet-archive-matches.tsv`. **No peer-reviewed or arXiv analysis explicitly identifying any of the four events was located as of 17 November 2026.** Absence from these sources cannot establish the events have never been studied privately, circulated under an undocumented identifier or prepared for publication. They are previously announced **survey alerts**, not new transients discovered by this project.

## The decisive missing observations for KMT-2024-BLG-3326

1. **Re-reduce the actual 2024 KMT CTIO I- and V-band image series**, around HJD 2,460,570.65 and at least the adjacent ±0.5–1 d baseline, with frame-by-frame seeing/PSF treatment, independent local difference imaging, a common registered reference scene and full propagated errors. Fit the difference-image **centroid trajectory** and simultaneously the alert position and the responding star 0.80 arcsec away. The existing two CTIO V points provide only weak color evidence; calibrated *variable-flux* V/I and source-flux ceiling are needed to identify what was magnified, rather than using total blended flux.
2. **Recover any genuinely independent 2024 images with time stamps bracketing the peak and shoulders** from KMT Chile's distinct frames, OGLE, MOA or other observatories (not merely an alternate reduction of the same exposures), if they exist. Constrain the rise/decay and wing flux over several hours up to approximately a day on both sides with precision adequate to separate the `tE=0.0848 d, rho=2.61` profile from the `tE≈0.522 d, rho≈0.456` profile. The KMT South Africa/Australia curves currently miss the narrow peak, and no amount of reinterpreting those unsampled epochs supplies an independent peak detection.
3. **High-angular-resolution, multi-filter AO/HST/JWST/Euclid imaging**, preferably at multiple epochs, to separate the sub-arcsecond objects and determine their positions, colors and angular sizes. Tie imaging astrometry to the *variable-flux centroid* before computing `theta_star` and thus `thetaE=theta_star/rho`. Later imaging and a calibrated contrast curve can search for a possible luminous host; a single high-resolution image cannot retrospectively determine the 2024 event duration.
4. **Test coherent long-timescale, binary-lens and intrinsic-variable hypotheses on the same original frames**, with correlated-noise and photometric-baseline sensitivity, demanding true optimizer convergence for alternative fits. Earlier/later seasons or a **future public release of KMT 2026 photometry** may test recurrence and baseline systematics, **but cannot retroactively add the missing 2024 peak epochs**.

## Disposition

**Accept as a carefully bounded public-data search and null/ambiguous result, not as a planet discovery.** Distinguish published alert notices from peer-reviewed analysis, identify nonconverged binary alternatives honestly, retain every input receipt and non-detection, and retain the full model/data limitations. The 0824/3278 source-assignment-dependent sub-microarcsecond values are conditional estimates only, not measured angular Einstein radii. No `[REMARKABLE]` label or `docs/REMARKABLE_FINDINGS.md` entry is justified.
