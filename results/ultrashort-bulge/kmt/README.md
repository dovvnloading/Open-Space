# KMTNet released event photometry

The 2024 and 2025 catalogs contain 6,789 event IDs. No positive catalog timescale falls below 0.50 d, and 16 are reported at exactly 0.50 d. This makes a cut on the catalog column alone unreliable: the published FFP candidates KB240792 and KB243237 appear at 1.25 and 1.01 d in the catalog. A strong sub-day feature in KMT-2024-BLG-0456 is present in both CTIO and SAAO photometry. The unconstrained 2.55-hour, large-source solution requires tens of times more source flux than the recorded total light. See the parent results for the constrained alternatives.

[2024](https://kmtnet.kasi.re.kr/ulens/event/2024/), [2025](https://kmtnet.kasi.re.kr/ulens/event/2025/) and [2026](https://kmtnet.kasi.re.kr/ulens/event/2026/) index responses were hashed before processing. The exact public HTML for both released catalogs is preserved, gzip-compressed, alongside the normalized TSV event tables. The 2026 page is an alert service. The survey permits work on future papers using posted curves but specifies the following July 1 as the end of the proprietary period. For KMT 2026, this search retains the public event-page roster and aggregate catalog coverage. Independent public photometry from other surveys can still be matched to those alerts. The KMTNet team's 2026 discovery of [two bound giant planets around an M dwarf](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12451) does not establish a release of the 2026 sample.

See `index-receipts.json` for per-field counts, index-body hashes, byte lengths and returned HTTP dates. The site encodes telescope/chip/subfield in its Field identifier. The 27 distinct two-digit field numbers are counted separately within each season. Nominal field visits vary with observing year and site. The [KMTNet team observing plan](https://kmtnet.kasi.re.kr/document-file/2025-11-18/11_BLG202511.pdf) shows the cadence range and field map. Overlapping fields can sample the same star. `shortlist-cadence.tsv` gives the exact fraction of candidate peak times with at least three distinct I-band timestamps in centered 3, 6, 12 and 24 hour windows, assuming uniform event centers between the first and last visits of the season. Across the full inventory, `field-coverage.tsv` groups the parent pipeline’s real timestamps into 54 field/season cells. It gives the median number of distinct exposures and the fraction of the common trial season with at least two observations on each side of a peak, over five time windows. Both measures concern timing alone; recovery of real signals also depends on flux and classifier thresholds.

Each manifest entry records the pySIS bundle URL, state, hash and the member filenames for successfully acquired archives. Each KMT I stream is a separate field/site reduction. In pySIS the input flux for a brightening is minus column 2, with uncertainty from column 3 and full HJD from column 1 (one-based). The same source brightens in the opposite sign in the alternative DIAPL products. Their epochs match pySIS after adding 2,400,000, typically within several seconds. DIAPL and pySIS use many of the same exposures. Their agreement tests reduction dependence, not statistical independence.

## Completed candidate checks

- **KMT-2024-BLG-0456.** The alternate DIAPL reductions give a strong peak at both CTIO and SAAO. The unconstrained FS template gives an improvement over constant of 981 and 804 in chi-square respectively, independently fitted in a 40 d window using per-season, per-stream baseline error scaling. The large-source template requires 54,248 differential-flux units in KMTC02 versus a total baseline of 1,595, and 69,489 in KMTS42 versus 1,596. `flux.py` recovers baseline flux on the DI scale from each I-band light curve's own reported corrected magnitudes. The three V streams actually sampling the source transit (six epochs in total) give no usable magnified source color. The only VIRAC2 neighbor inside 1.5 arcsec lies 0.59 arcsec away at catalog epoch 2014, with 159 Ks measurements and a median absolute deviation of 0.123 mag. Its association with the lensed star is unverified. No Gaia DR3 source lies inside 1.5 arcsec. SIMBAD and VSX give no match inside 2 arcsec. A SkyBoT 5-arcmin geocentric cone at the catalog peak yields one listed asteroid, 276.5 arcsec away. Centroid motion was not measured. The 2018–2024 DIAPL baseline has zero flagged off-peak nights in Chile and South Africa under the defined recurrence test and one in Australia. Chilean pySIS streams also show off-peak outliers on other dates. No calibrated angular radius or free-floating interpretation follows from these checks.
- **KMT-2025-BLG-0300.** Near reduced HJD 60951, the KMTS42 daily median exceeds its season median by 139 native quoted errors. The other sites' daily medians are near baseline. KMTS42 exceeds 142 on 60930. CTIO and SAAO instead both show a clear multi-night brightening around 60762–64. The long-baseline DIAPL flux at the putative second peak has the wrong sign at the principal site for this template.
- **KMT-2025-BLG-2510.** At reduced HJD 60849, the apparent short pulse is concentrated in KMTC42. Similar excursions occur elsewhere in that stream. A separate peak around 60939–40 appears in the 02 field across the network, including DIAPL.
- **KMT-2025-BLG-3173.** The sharp peak at 60951 shares its night and site (SAAO) with the spurious signal in 0300. Other nights show repeated, large brightenings in the same reductions.
- **KMT-2025-BLG-1446.** CTIO and SAAO see magnification on multiple nights spanning at least 60843–50. A sub-day fit follows the narrow peak but leaves a broad brightening requiring a different explanation.
- **KMT-2025-BLG-1507 and 1213.** Both show a peak in multiple sites. 1213 is also OGLE-2025-BLG-0867. Identification of a short pulse at multiple sites does not resolve whether it comes from a binary lens, a flare, or a finite-source single lens. See the main results for model comparisons and limits. KMT-2024-BLG-1157 has OGLE-2024-BLG-0944 and MOA-2024-BLG-149 aliases. KMT-2025-BLG-1760 is OGLE-2025-BLG-1245.

SIMBAD cone checks used the Harvard mirror. The [CDS service notice](https://cds.unistra.fr/) dated November 9 reported that this mirror includes data ingested through October 14, 2026. A negative match is limited to its available holdings.

The 17 named targets in `controls.py` have no other KMT event within 2 arcsec in the successfully parsed 2016–2025 annual tables. These catalogs contain alerts, not continuous light curves for every source. The published KMT planet catalog (updated November 5, 2026) has 11 FFP candidates. It includes KB240816, KB240792 and KB243237, but omits [KMT-2025-BLG-2093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27725), a published candidate with a 1.36 d timescale and a measured Einstein radius of 13.1 ± 2.8 microarcseconds. Archive membership alone cannot establish novelty. `control-checks.json` and `control-receipts.json` record the objects, exact queries and hashes.

The priority Gaia/VIRAC2 proximity screen targets all 124 positive catalog entries at or below 2 d plus four OGLE cross-identifications, two 2024 short-timescale grid leads, and three finite-source-profile leads above that cut (133 unique event positions, 266 successful queries). Both catalogs use their own reference epochs. `Gaia-neighbours.tsv` and `VIRAC2-neighbours.tsv` retain all neighbors inside 1.5 arcsec and their separations on the unpropagated J2000 coordinates. A missing Gaia variability classification is not evidence for constancy. VIRAC2 Ks-band statistics provide pre-event photometry; crowding and proper motion can affect positional association. Successful empty cone queries and service failures are separate in `neighbour-receipts.tsv`.

## Detector and position audit

`short-lead-audit.tsv` contains one row for every qualifying best-fit family in the blend-capped search. A PS row requires `tE < 0.5` d; an FS row requires `tE < 0.5` d or `rho > 1`. These fit-family rows do not form an astrophysical candidate count. The audit finds the full width at half the model's **excess** flux. It fits that shape over a local window, reports the gain for each site, counts observed sides of the peak, and repeats the fit after dropping invalid seeing or seeing above the stream's off-event 95th percentile. Neighboring fields from one telescope count as one site. The `sites_with_two_gt3` column requires two quality-filtered epochs exceeding three recalibrated errors, inside that model FWHM, in the same field/site stream. `second_site_quality_gain` measures the second largest fixed-shape improvement when grouped by telescope. It can be nonzero due to data on the wings even when a site did not sample the FWHM. `offpeak_site_night_flags` counts telescope/date combinations with at least three excursions above five recalibrated errors and at least half of the high-quality observations in that bin bright. Compare `t0_minus_catalog_d` to check whether a fit has landed on an unrelated spike.

The 2024 event 0909 provides a direct detector check: its grid spike falls 38 d after the catalog peak. The three brightest Australian frames have invalid seeing (-1), the next three have seeing of 10–12 pixels, and the independent DIAPL reduction gives essentially zero template gain. Only Chile sampled 3326 at half maximum in the narrow FS grid solution. Nine CTIO DIAPL exposures reproduce the signal, and two V measurements give weak corroboration. Same-detector photometry measured nine positive CTIO epochs at the event position and at a neighboring extraction 0.80 arcsec away (median recalibrated residuals +9.96 and +8.14). Two comparison stars offset by approximately 7 arcsec had median residuals of −0.32 and +0.23. The bump was local on the detector, with unresolved source identity. The target LCID has ten seasons of photometry (2016–2025), with two other flagged Chilean nights. Three topocentric SkyBoT checks across the peak give minimum nominal separations of 78, 107 and 136 arcsec. One fainter predicted object has an ephemeris uncertainty of 101 arcsec, so the search alone cannot exclude every moving source. A variable or blended source and binary- or single-lens models remain viable. For 3429, classified `possible` by EventFinder, the putative finite-source peak falls mostly in an observing gap and lacks independent-site corroboration.

`historical-alert-neighbours.tsv` tests the entire released inventory against the KMTNet 2016–26 positions and the frozen OGLE 2025–26 coordinates with an angular radius of 2 arcsec. Multiple years at the same position can identify recurrence. Single-season differences in identification and blending can also produce matches. See `../other/matches.csv` for cross-survey MOA/PRIME/Gaia alert correspondences. `planet-archive-matches.tsv` compares all 6,789 alerts to the KMT planet archive by event name or a two-arcsecond positional match.

## Reproduction

From the repository root with the parent fitting dependencies and lxml installed:

```bash
python results/ultrashort-bulge/kmt/freeze.py --cache staging/kmt --download --jobs 4
python results/ultrashort-bulge/kmt/manifest.py --cache staging/kmt
python results/ultrashort-bulge/kmt/fallback.py --cache staging/kmt
python results/ultrashort-bulge/kmt/neighbours.py --cache staging/kmt
python results/ultrashort-bulge/kmt/controls.py --cache staging/kmt
python results/ultrashort-bulge/kmt/positions.py --cache staging/kmt
python results/ultrashort-bulge/kmt/asteroid_check.py --cache staging/kmt
python results/ultrashort-bulge/kmt/vet_streams.py --cache staging/kmt
python results/ultrashort-bulge/kmt/field_controls.py --cache staging/kmt
python results/ultrashort-bulge/kmt/audit.py --cache staging/kmt --grid staging/bound
python results/ultrashort-bulge/kmt/batch_neighbours.py --cache staging/kmt
python results/ultrashort-bulge/kmt/field_coverage.py
```

`vet_streams.py` uses the recorded geometry in `stream-geometry.tsv` as a fixed shape. Values of rho=0.001 serve as the point-source limit. Pass `--refined` to substitute updated fit JSONs. It fits a nonnegative amplitude and a constant offset in each stream over ±20 days of peak, with full-season baseline scatter used to inflate errors. It counts off-peak nights only when at least three observations exceed five recalibrated standard errors in the bright direction and make up at least half of the measurements in that one-day bin. Season-specific offsets are estimated outside a ±12 d (or longer for slow templates) window. The output flags can identify contamination and recurring structure, but do not form a comprehensive periodic-variable or flare search. Further checks include source centroid motion, image-level deblending, source color and size, high-resolution host constraints, a complete binary-lens degeneracy search and injection/recovery selection efficiency.
