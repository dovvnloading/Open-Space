# Independent checks of four extended-source flags

**KMT-2024-BLG-0824 and KMT-2024-BLG-3278 each show a distinct 2024 brightening in a second reduction at all three KMT observatories.** Their single-lens fits span about seven to eight days. Neither shows a comparable repeat in the 2016–2025 positional data under the same two-site test. Both are crowded, both have a brighter response at the catalog position than at our comparison positions, and neither has an independently identified lensed star or a measured angular Einstein radius. KMT-2025-BLG-1495's broad feature fails an independent optical check. KMT-2025-BLG-0220 lacks independent confirmation at its fitted hours-long peak.

The additional checks of 2024-BLG-1152, 1762 and 3413 and 2025-BLG-1797 and 2489 include an empirical flux calibration of a DIAPL-only large-source model and an independent OGLE light-curve fit. [Additional five-event report](additional-tier.md)

These pre-event infrared measurements neither identify the magnified star nor establish constant brightness to arbitrary precision. The pointings and product hashes are in [sightline-receipts.json](sightline-receipts.json).

The time series use positional KMT DIAPL photometry, an independent reduction of some of the same KMT exposures used in pySIS. Each site/season excludes the worst 5% of reference seeing. The long-event scan bins two or more exposures per integer day, and 0220 uses 0.05 d bins. At each trial time we fit an offset and signed amplitude to each site using the frozen pySIS template. Site/season median scatter and within-bin reported errors determine the weights. We cap flux deviations at eight site/season robust scatter units for both real and trial peaks. The second-highest signed site score reduces sensitivity to large single-site artifacts. Bin-count requirements, a grid in time, sparse periods and 2020 gaps limit temporal completeness. The results give comparative scores, without a global false-alarm calibration. In the results JSON each site array holds fitted peak extra flux, its nominal S/N, number of bins within one source-crossing time and number of reference bins in the local fit. ## Conditional radii for the two nonrepeating events

The local red-clump colors from 60″, 90″ and 120″ apertures are J−Ks = 1.021–1.029 for 0824 and 1.032–1.038 for 3278. We used VIRAC2 PSF photometry for 0824 because the VVV DR2 aperture-photometry artifact flags exclude every central 60″ J/Ks pair. For 3278 the quality-filtered VVV DR2 field supplies 307 clump-color stars at 120″. For 0824 VIRAC2 supplies 203. A Gaussian clump plus exponential background fits the Ks counts, and the clump color is the median within 0.22 mag of the modeled peak. An intrinsic clump J−Ks = 0.68 gives full-column color excesses of about 0.34 and 0.36 mag. [Gonzalez et al.](https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/pdf/2012/07/aa19222-12.pdf) · [VIRAC2 data release](https://www.eso.org/sci/publications/announcements/sciann17684.html)

The following cases assume that a single dwarf or subgiant supplies **all** of the identified catalog star's J and Ks light, lies behind the local red-clump dust column and is the lensed star. In Kervella et al. Table 4 the Johnson K/J relation is log₁₀(angular diameter in mas) = 0.3192 (J−K)₀ + 0.5089 − 0.2 K₀. The published dispersal is 5.87%, with detectable nonlinearity. The authors give A0–M2 dwarfs and A0–K0 subgiants as the reliably sampled domain. The central estimates approximate the catalog bandpasses by Johnson J and K. The sensitivity calculation allows ±0.10 mag in color and ±0.05 mag in K for bandpass differences. It also spans the aperture-to-aperture reddening with another ±0.05 mag, A_Ks/E(J−Ks) = 0.428–0.528, the individual-epoch scatter in both catalog bands, ±5.87% in the calibration, and the two fitted ρ values. We restrict these illustrative dwarf/subgiant cases to −0.02 ≤ (J−K)₀ ≤ 0.85. The ranges are extrema under those assumptions and carry no probabilities. [Kervella et al., Table 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/astro-ph/0404180) · [Alonso-García et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.04854) · [VISTA bandpass calibration](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/474/4/5459/4683252)

Sources below the VIRAC2 detection threshold are possible. The nominal full-flux cases imply unusually small relative proper motions, about 0.02 mas yr⁻¹ for 0824 and 0.015 mas yr⁻¹ for 3278. In these crowded fields the public images and uncalibrated instrumental color are insufficient to weight source identities or determine the joint distribution of flux and ρ. ## Associations and remaining checks

The 2024 OGLE EWS, 2025–26 OGLE EWS, 2024–26 MOA/PRIME alert inventory, and 34 entries with coordinates in the frozen literature roster give no counterpart within 3″ for 0824, 3278 or 0220. Only OGLE-2025-BLG-1000 matches 1495. The roster covers the primary Mróz, Ryu, Koshimoto/Sumi and Gould lists used in this study. Its exclusion radius and finite contents limit novelty claims. The published KMT alert and planet-archive neighbor files provide complementary 2″ checks. For all four sightlines our CADC HST/JWST and ESA Euclid ObsCore image-footprint queries returned zero rows. A footprint-query miss does not establish absence of archival imaging. The 300 × 300 pixel KMT JPEG finding charts for 0824 and 3278 show crowded reference fields with a crosshair. Neither includes a peak difference image or an astrometric WCS. IRSA independently finds 48 Euclid Q2 products at 3278. Six paired VIS exposures recover distinct peaks 0.33″ and 0.42″ from the alert, 0.640″ apart. The central KMT photometry blends these sources. Pending checks for 0824 and 3278 include subpixel registration of difference-image centroids against infrared sources, absolute V-band calibration of the differential signal, calibrated flux–ρ covariance including source blends, and wider alternatives for intrinsic or binary variability. A matched short single-lens event, a source-specific θ★ and an exclusion of a bound host are prerequisites for a free-floating-planet interpretation.

## Replay

Use the Python environment for the parent search including VBMicrolensing, SciPy, pandas and NumPy. The KMT archive argument contains the released 2024–25 pySIS tarballs named by canonical event ID. The OGLE parameters and photometry arguments designate the public data identified by URL and SHA-256 in the linked receipts.

```sh
python sightlines.py --cache /path/to/extended-cache
python recurrence.py --cache /path/to/extended-cache --archives /path/to/kmt-archives --ogle-1000 /path/to/2025/blg-1000.dat
python associations.py --cache /path/to/extended-cache --ogle24-par /path/to/2024/lenses.par
python angular.py --cache /path/to/extended-cache
```
