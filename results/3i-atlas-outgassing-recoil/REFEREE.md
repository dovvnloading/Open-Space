# Independent referee report — 19 November 2026

**Disposition: accept as a qualified, reproducible measurement and conditional recoil feasibility test; reject any discovery-grade anomaly claim.** The evidence shows a meaningful nongravitational astrometric improvement and enough *scalar* measured gas momentum to explain its size for certain smaller, less massive nuclei. It does **not** show a single time-dependent, three-dimensional jet momentum solution reproducing the astrometric acceleration, nor does it rule out ordinary outgassing for all plausible nuclei. No entry belongs in `docs/REMARKABLE_FINDINGS.md`.

## Independent audit of the deposited evidence

1. **Predeclared measurement rules and preserved input.** The claim-first [README](README.md) predates the runner. The 19 November runner retrieved 8,497 MPC 80-column rows, dated 2025 May 8 to 2026 Apr 14; 8,345 passed the previously declared ground/observatory selection, with 8,301 retained by the inverse-square RTN fit. It preserved the original gzip and SHA-256 under [generated](generated/), together with source URL, acquisition UTC, response digest, DE440s kernel digest, and source-code digests. The separate [earlier independent 3I orbit positive control](../inbound-hyperbolic-search/orbits/README.md) is reused as an implementation, not counted as new evidence; the [radial-crossing study](../3i-atlas-383p-radial-crossing/README.md) addresses a distinct geometric hypothesis.
2. **Fit versus a fixed gravity alternative.** The sky residual RMS falls from 0.753 arcsec/coordinate (gravity) to 0.637 (nine-parameter symmetric inverse-square RTN), with 8,308 and 8,301 retained positions respectively. The fitted RTN coefficients are `(+1.856±0.315, +3.865±0.240, −0.745±0.023)×10^-8 au/day²` (conditional formal 1-sigma). The independent water-ice-law coefficients `(+2.157,+3.820,−1.157)` give a similar ~0.638-arcsec residual, but **not** the same acceleration vector. These are nontrivial model-selection sensitivities, and reduced weighted chi-square below unity does not mean the quoted errors span catalog biases.
3. **Temporal challenge kept in the record.** Holding out the latest 10% by date gives 0.875 arcsec/coordinate, versus 0.620 on the training subset. A nine-parameter **preperihelion-only** fit *failed to converge* within the optimizer budget; the postperihelion-only fit yields `A2=−8.39±50.39` in the same units, with 3.92-arcsec cross-phase withheld residual. These attempts **cannot** independently validate pre/post coefficients or reject a time-variable recoil law. The final report must not silently turn failures into detections.
4. **JPL comparator is not a same-data reproduction.** [SBDB solution #55](generated/sbdb.json) was solved 2026 Oct 21 for temporary JWST support, uses 117 selected observations from 2025 Oct 3 through **2026 Oct 16**, DE441 and fitted delayed peak `DT=+28.47 d`. Its `A=(+4.618,−0.956,−0.829)×10^-8 au/day²` differs by ~74.7° in RTN direction from this **zero-delay** 8,345-position public-MPC DE440s fit ending 2026 Apr 14. This is a serious **comparison non-equivalence**, not evidence for a mysterious transverse thrust or a failure of conservation of momentum. Refit with identical time law and identical chosen astrometry before interpreting an excess.
5. **Independent physical unit/size check.** The code uses `M=(4/3)πρR³`, `F=Σ Q_i m_i v_i η`, and `a=F/M`. At ρ=500 kg/m³ and exhaust=500 m/s, the [recoil grid](generated/recoil_grid.json) requires η≈0.305 at R=0.42 km for **JWST's nucleus-centered** 6 Aug gas, but η≈9.04 at R=1.30 km, which that *particular gas-only, fixed-parameter scenario* cannot supply. The terminal-coma alternative requires η≈0.166 and 4.92 respectively, and is **not** to be summed with the inner-coma extraction. At JUICE 2 Nov, the corresponding values are 0.0487 and 1.44; at JWST 22–23 Dec, 0.138 and 4.10. The R³ scaling and dimensional units are internally consistent. Because gas is measured in aperture-averaged production and dust-entrained water can be a distributed coma source, η=1 is a favorable *scalar upper budget*, not a measured directed jet.
6. **Independent reference and selection audit.** The earlier Swift July preprint had a different reddening assumption from the published marginal July rate; the CSV now separates and labels that revision. JWST nucleus-centered and terminal Q are separate alternatives; CO/H2O production inferred from SPHEREx ratios is labeled derived; SPHEREx Aug upper limits are **not detections**; OH-water factors are explicit; CN and Ni tracers are excluded from bulk gas momentum. The [source ledger](SOURCES.md) identifies primary telescope papers, HST radius/albedo assumptions, and earlier orbit/outgassing comparison papers instead of claiming priority.

## Major caveats and tests needed for a stronger inference

- The returned public MPC source is complete **only through 2026 Apr 14**, not JPL's later Oct 16 selected arc. No claim of fitting all observations used in SBDB #55 is justified. Astrometric weights, stationary coma photocenter offsets, observational-mode exclusions, calibration/debias, and non-grav lags can affect the fitted A1/A2/A3 substantially.
- The analysis compares a *constant-coefficient inverse-square astrometric law* with gas measurements at selected times; it does not simultaneously fit nucleus mass, rotation, directed jets, sublimation delays, species velocities, coma-source coupling or stochastic outbursts. Measured scalar Q cannot establish the sign of the radial/transverse/normal force components.
- The low-mass R≈0.42 km models that allow η≤1 may be inconsistent with a HST-inferred R≈1.3±0.2 km for pV=0.04, while some R≈1.3 km rows require efficiency exceeding unity. That is **real conditional tension**, potentially resolved by uncertain albedo/density, true nucleus-coupled gas flux, speed and force-law/delay, not a proof that gas is insufficient under every plausible physical interpretation. For much larger nuclei the tension grows as R³.
- A decisive future test needs the same astrometry in pre-registered fixed-lag and variable-lag N-body solutions, force-direction fits to physically constrained coma images, contemporaneous gas momentum by species, a constrained 3D nucleus size/mass, and temporal cross-validation across perihelion. An anomalous residual must survive that full comparison with explicit physical upper bounds.

**Referee finding:** the analysis is a useful replication and falsifiable *conditional* bound. It supports normal outgassing as a plausible explanation for the measured acceleration magnitude, but cannot demonstrate a complete vector/time match. **No validated unexplained acceleration and no discovery-level claim.**
