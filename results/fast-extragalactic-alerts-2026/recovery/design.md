# Recovery design

Evaluate the photometric selection on the measured alert-epoch sampling. Use the eligible discovery cohorts, including objects without suitably spaced pairs in the denominator. For each sampled object draw an independent peak time uniformly from half a day before to half a day after its first recorded epoch. Inject an achromatic transient with apparent AB peak magnitude 18, 20, or 22 and a linear magnitude slope of 2 or 4 mag/day either side of peak. Include constant-flux controls. Use the actual flux uncertainty (Rubin) or the alert difference-image limiting magnitude (ZTF) to add Gaussian flux noise. Enforce the frozen two-visit S/N, error, amplitude, interval and slope tests. This measures conditional recovery on the received epochs only. Image detection, masked area, the distribution of explosion times across the observing season and host vetting require separate calibration.

The implementation generates 16 peak phases per object per model and uses the same seeds across the model grid. It merges repeated band/epoch measurements (or Rubin visit) using the largest reported noise. In ZTF 146 repeated same-band/time groups included 81 with differing depths. The output is before quality flags, source-context vetting and any survey exposure correction. It assumes independent Gaussian flux errors. The constant-flux controls address random measurement noise alone.

## Can the recorded visits show both a rise and a fade?

**No observed object had a significant rise and a subsequent significant fade in the same band within one day.** The 47 passing Rubin pairs comprise 20 rises and 27 fades. The nine ZTF pairs comprise four rises and five fades. Joining at the identical peak measurement returned zero triples in each survey, even before image-quality or astrophysical cuts. Allowing the rising and fading legs to meet at two chronologically ordered visits in the same band also returned zero objects. This supplementary test was defined after the primary two-point screen. A qualifying triple establishes a sampled peak. Establishing the full duration would also require observations before and after the outburst. [Input hashes and observed counts](turnover_audit.json)

For each same-band triple the middle visit must be brighter than both outer visits. Both legs must independently pass the frozen pair cuts and the outer visits must lie within 1.00 day. The calculation reuses the archived visit times, depths, first-epoch definition, and the primary grid of peak magnitudes and slopes. It draws 2,048 independent peak phases per timing-capable object, uniform within ±0.5 day of the first archived epoch. Gaussian noise for the middle visit is drawn once and shared by both legs. Objects without an eligible triple contribute exactly zero for every phase. This gives a common denominator per model of 2,048 times the full cohort size. Each of the 2,197 successful Rubin trials at AB 18 and 4 mag/day contained a triple that bracketed the simulated peak. The `measured_turnover` column counts each successful object/phase once even if multiple triples passed. [All models](turnover_recovery.csv)

The reported Monte Carlo standard errors condition on this fixed cohort, cadence, noise model and phase law. For zero recoveries, the CSV supplies a one-sided 95% bound on the simulated recovery fraction. For `C` capable objects out of `N`, with `T` independent trials per capable object, the bound is `(C/N) × (1 − 0.05^(1/(C T)))`. It follows by bounding the probability of zero successes with the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality over the heterogeneous object probabilities. At AB 18 and 4 mag/day the ZTF bound is 0.00000351% of these object/phase combinations. A physical event rate still requires sky positions sampled independently of known alerts, a measured area–time or volume–time, and the efficiency of alert generation and vetting.

## Public exposure check for the ZTF injections

All 1,711 distinct filter/epoch combinations that can participate in the ZTF injection pairs have exposure program ID 1. The timing-only simulation admits 2,180 pairs before any synthetic flux cut. The observed-data program check covered the 1,020 visits entering its positive S/N ≥5 pairs. Reading the forced histories and packet arrays from the earlier evidence supplement identified 1,509 of the 1,711 exposures. Four supplementary TAP queries supplied previously unchecked exposures, leaving 64 for original or parent alert packets. The packet comparison uses the filter and timestamp rounded to seven decimal places in MJD. All 69 supplementary HTTP replies are deposited, including the parent-ID query. Each has its complete URL, UTC retrieval time, HTTP status, byte size and SHA-256 digest. [Validated visits](ztf_program_visits.csv) · [Replies and receipts](ztf_program_replies.zip)
