# Fast extragalactic optical transients in the November 2026 public alerts

**No new extragalactic transient is validated in the primary search.** The strongest unresolved Rubin source is 171198361324486700. Its difference flux faded from 5,900 ± 372 to 4,358 ± 307 nJy in the i band in 67.5 minutes on November 12 UTC. The PSF-fit change was 0.329 ± 0.103 mag in the difference images and 0.251 ± 0.065 mag in the direct images. Fixed-aperture difference photometry gave 0.152 ± 0.225 mag on the same exposures. A later independent direct-image check of an i-band fade reached only 0.67σ, so the apparent PSF-fit fading lacks independent confirmation. Legacy Surveys resolves no host at the position and places a pre-existing point source 0.11 arcsec away. No distance, rise time or total duration has been measured. Lasair/Sherlock predicts a variable star on the basis of that same point source. [Lasair record](https://lasair.lsst.ac.uk/object/171198361324486700/)

Crossmatched Gaia DR3 and, within its footprint, PS1, preserving per-query status, SHA-256 and an object-by-object ledger. ## Pending limitations, not pending candidate dispositions

No primary candidate showed a significant rise followed by a significant fade in the same band within one day. Only 244 Rubin objects and nine ZTF objects had three appropriately spaced same-band visits needed for that test. [Timing and injection audit](recovery/design.md)

The two unresolved Rubin alerts require independent image/longer-baseline data or spectroscopy to establish a physical class. AT 2026aidn has a TNS registration, not an extragalactic validation. A rate bound remains pending a measured effective survey exposure and detection/vetting efficiency. Supplementary older-broker screens do not enlarge the frozen primary cohort. All claims above are limited to the November 1–16, 2026 UTC alert selection, not a census of every optical transient in the sky. ## Frozen replay and flux interpretation

Rubin PSF fluxes and ZTF positive `magpsf` are difference-image quantities. Their magnitude changes describe the difference flux; they are not necessarily changes in the total source brightness. Visits can share a reference template, so their systematic errors need not be independent. Nondetection records describe only their particular observation and limiting measurement. The 17 unflagged Rubin objects and nine ZTF photometric rankings are screening outputs; individual physical identifications, localization and residual alternatives must be assessed with their evidence.

The Rubin screen verifies the byte count and SHA-256 of the frozen parent and every daily response before writing to a new output directory: `python rubin/screen.py rubin/data /path/to/new-output --flux-unit nJy`. The unit is an operator declaration based on the [ALeRCE LSST schema](https://alerce.readthedocs.io/en/latest/models/lsst_detection.html), not a unit validated from the archived CSV metadata. The ZTF screen verifies all 16 raw detection bodies and count responses against receipts in `ztf/ztf_evidence_supplement.tar.gz`: `python ztf/scripts/ztf_screen.py /path/to/new-output`. Both reject existing output directories. Rubin live retrieval requires a new directory. Set `FAST_ALERTS_NEW_CAMPAIGN` to a new directory for ZTF live retrieval; this creates distinct responses and receipts, not a replacement of this frozen cohort. Other follow-up scripts and the recovery script still require separate scratch copies and have not yet been made immutable replay tools.
