# Spectral measurements and interpretation

The blind line finder takes PSF-weighted 1D flux densities and their supplied 1σ errors. It never uses the catalog redshift or the catalog's classification to detect a line. The rest wavelengths in the code are vacuum wavelengths. Measured wavelengths are reported on the native air grid and converted to vacuum for approximate observed-frame redshifts. There is no additional barycentric velocity correction. Integrated flux is in erg s⁻¹ cm⁻², flux density in erg s⁻¹ cm⁻² Å⁻¹, and equivalent width and Gaussian widths in Å.

## Detection

1. Invalidate nonfinite fluxes, nonpositive or nonfinite errors and published bad-calibration air intervals 3534–3556, 5194–5197, 5200–5205 and 5456–5466 Å. Additional proximity-to-sky and single-dominant-pixel indicators remain visible on detections.
2. Subtract an inverse-variance-weighted local boxcar continuum (65 Å half-width). Convolve each spectrum with Gaussian profiles of observed σ = 2.0, 3.7 and 6.5 Å, propagating wavelength-dependent errors. Inflate errors by the robust scatter of the normalized residual spectrum when this exceeds unity. Retain local maxima with screen S/N ≥ 5.5 and refine the center using the local score curvature.
3. Refit at this center with Gaussian σ = 1.8, 2.4, 3.5, 5.0, 7.5 and 10 Å. Estimate the local continuum in two sidebands beyond ±38 Å, each 48 Å wide, applying a 4.5σ clip and propagating continuum uncertainty. At least 82% of the modeled profile area and two half-maximum samples must be usable. Accept the best profile at fitted S/N ≥ 7. Strongly blended, asymmetric, very broad or detector-edge lines can fail. A multi-template peak S/N is a selection statistic and has a trials factor.
4. Under a Lyα identification, estimate continuum redward of the line from max(25 Å,4σ) through 245 Å in the observed frame. Require at least 32 usable channels. The continuum variance floor uses the larger of propagated errors and scatter across eight-channel blocks. The point EW exists only at positive measured continuum. The lower bound equals max(0, F−1.96σ_F)/[(1+z)(max(0,fλ)+1.96σ_f)]. The bounds have nominal joint ≥95% coverage for a prespecified line under the Gaussian extraction-error model. Selection on the same spectra, uncertain sky/continuum calibration and image blending affect the interpretation. The extreme-EW screen requires the lower bound strictly above 240 Å. A continuum nondetection alone does not establish extreme EW.

## Line identities

For He II within z = 1.9–2.4, measure UV companions at predicted wavelengths, including both components of C IV, O III] and C III]. The inexpensive Lyα precursor uses the maximum blind screen statistic in the velocity interval −300 to +600 km s⁻¹; detailed Lyα checks use a four-point velocity grid. The grid addresses typical offsets and is incomplete for displaced/absorbed Lyα. Report each component's forced Gaussian area, 1σ uncertainty and conservative one-sided limit max(0,F)+3σ. Mark inadequate profile coverage and out-of-band transitions explicitly. Component measurements of blended doublets are correlated. Limits assume the best-fit detected line width, fixed systemic velocity for metals and local continuum model. Metal lines or broad AGN components at other velocities or widths may evade these limits. C III] only enters the optical band for a very small interval near the low-redshift boundary, with additional coverage losses at the edge.

At each plausible optical identity, force the other covered transitions at the corresponding redshift. An isolated unresolved [O II] doublet can have undetectable optical companions. Consistency with the [O II] hypothesis remains an identification ambiguity unless independent data resolve it. A single line in this entire observed band always admits a Lyα redshift. A catalog-mismatched line therefore cannot establish a novel transition by exclusion. A 1D Gaussian width is spectral and provides no evidence of spatially extended Lyα. Sky lists and the fraction of line counts in the brightest pixel are screening aids. Individual exposures, adjacent fibres/sky extractions and spatial measurements are needed to establish a robust astronomical origin and spatial extent.

## Control and completeness definitions

The shuffle function independently permutes spectrum identities at each wavelength, moving each flux together with its error. This retains wavelength-dependent residuals and breaks within-spectrum line coherence, spectral covariance and source-specific noise patterns. Grouping by shot retains additional shot-dependent sky structure when the data permit it. Contamination by real lines is possible. Results from this control alone do not measure the complete false-positive rate of an exposure-level search.

Injection trials insert Gaussian emission on copies of actual spectra, run the same blind detection code and match a recovered centroid within max(4 Å, injected σ). The input amplitude spans nominal single-template S/N of 5, 7, 10, 15 and 25; simulated widths are 2.0, 3.5 and 6.0 Å. The code retains attempted trials with poor baseline coverage, records nearby pre-existing peaks, and reports whether the EW lower bound passed. Pure Lyα trials add a constant flux-density shift to the spectrum to set its estimated red-side continuum to nominal rest EW of 120, 240, 360, 600 or 1000 Å where covered. This conditional recovery experiment preserves the original wavelength-dependent noise and uncertainty in the baseline. He II trials inject Lyα at three times the He II flux; a second family also injects a C IV doublet at total flux equal to He II (2:1 doublet ratio). These companions model specific signals, not a population distribution. A detection completeness is conditional on the released spectra, passed masks, flux, width and continuum and is not a survey volume or a sky-abundance constraint.

## Independent visits

Within each release, group multiple observation rows by positive source ID; choose one row (first original row, independent of line strength) per distinct shot. Require positions within 1 arcsec of their group centroid. Among the other rows, include close (≤1 arcsec), distinct-shot pairs only when both members have exactly one neighboring catalog position within this radius. Coadd the original density samples by inverse variance on their common native wavelength grid and propagate the per-visit variances. Run the same blind search and channel selection. Retain an additional coadd lead only if at least two distinct shots give forced S/N ≥3 at its coadd center and width. Archive every supporting or non-supporting visit measurement, the association rule, row IDs and shot IDs for those leads. Mark whether the same channel was already present near the recovered wavelength in any standalone visit. A forced measurement after selecting a coadd peak does not have the null distribution of an independently chosen wavelength.

For each real stack, keep the first visit and substitute unrelated catalog spectra for every later visit. Each replacement must be from the corresponding original shot and at least 60 arcsec from the target, with distinct source IDs. Apply the same coadd, channel and two-shot criteria. The control can contain genuine astronomical lines and shot-dependent sky structure. The search neither double-counts shared-shot rows as independent confirmations nor combines overlapping public releases into extra visits. Uncorrected differential heliocentric motion can move peaks by a fraction of a wavelength bin. The positions refer to catalog extractions and permit unresolved neighbors.

## Previously published positive controls

For each of the eight published HETDEX He II sources in Table 1 of Mirza Khanlari et al. (2026, DOI 10.3847/1538-4357/aea6d9), retain every catalog observation within 3 arcsec of the coordinates or carrying its exact detection ID. Search each full spectrum with the same blind detector. Match a detected He II peak afterward within ±500 km s⁻¹ plus 0.85 Å to allow rounding of the published redshift. Apply the same sky, pixel-shape, redshift and Lyα screen (precursor ≥4 unless anchored by the catalog within Δz <0.008). Include published objects missing from a release in the denominator of all eight, and identify separately how many have usable matches. Persist direct fits to Lyα, He II and the UV metals at the published redshift with fixed observed σ = 2.4 Å. Report a descriptive maximum over 21 He II centroid/width alternatives separately. For a check of independent optical fitting, use the first eligible DESI VAC catalog-order [O II] object with visual quality ≥3 and 0.05≤z≤0.45, forcing the doublet and companions at the visually assigned redshift.

## Extended-source injections

The blank aperture nearest the IFU centre is selected without considering its flux in the test windows (3950 and 4850 Å). Use the observed masks, continuum sidebands, local outer-background estimate, seeing-derived Moffat PSF fit, and the same 1.5–3 seeing-FWHM annular residual statistic as for a real isolated source. Add line flux to the narrowband image before those spatial fits. The spectral model has observed Gaussian σ=2.4 Å with no flux in either continuum sideband or the disjoint offset controls. Keep the seven-bin fraction of that line when forming the injected image; this is equivalent to perturbing its usable cube samples before the narrowband sum. Use source-free baseline, pure PSF, and 35% point-source plus 65% PSF-convolved Gaussian halo models, with intrinsic Gaussian σ of 1.5, 3 or 5 arcsec. The source flux takes four levels defined by the nominal 1.5-arcsec point-source aperture significance of 5, 10, 20 and 40. Report the actual physical flux of each trial, mean injected annular line surface brightness in erg s⁻¹ cm⁻² arcsec⁻², annular ≥5σ recovery and joint central/annular ≥5σ recovery. Sky and PSF mismatch already present in each blank are retained; injected profiles assume the modeled PSF and omit additional source photon noise. Results apply at these tested positions and wavelengths after source placement and do not calibrate blind spatial grouping or asymmetric bridges.

## Reassessment of unassigned peaks

For a line displaced from the common catalog-redshift transitions, record independent counterpart redshifts from high-quality DESI visual classifications or warning-free SPARCL spectroscopy. Compare its inferred rest wavelength with the SDSS MaNGA emission-line database and the linetools UV/optical transition atlas. Record the exact reference blob URL, SHA-256, and number of rows actually parsed, as well as any reference service failures. The coincidence window is the greater of 400 km s⁻¹ and 1.5 observed fitted line sigmas. Atomic coincidences can involve weak lines or transitions usually seen in absorption. They are possible assignments pending spectroscopy.

For each plausible alternative primary (Lyα, He II, C IV, C III], Mg II, [Ne V], [Ne III], [O II], the optical [O III] doublet, or a Balmer line), force the predicted visible companions. Report the signed flux, error, limit, coverage and the presence of a separate-wavelength ≥5σ companion. Test the [O III] 5007/4959 flux ratio including the uncertainty of the primary. Measure red-minus-blue wing flux within 2.5 fitted sigmas and the continuum discontinuity between observed 20–145 Å sidebands. These are conditional shape diagnostics. A low-S/N continuum, unresolved line profile or uncorroborated counterpart redshift commonly leaves several identities viable.

## Weaker helium lines paired with strong hydrogen

A complementary scan requires a clean Lyα anchor at fitted S/N ≥7 and a helium line at 4.5≤S/N<7. It takes all spectral rows in both releases, independently of source class. The velocity window is −300 to +600 km/s. Each line must be narrower than 900 km/s observed FWHM. He II/Lyα must reach 0.1, and the He II rest EW must reach 3 Å when the continuum has S/N ≥3. A measured component of C IV, C III] or O III] at ≥3σ vetoes a pair. Wavelengths outside the spectrum remain unconstrained. Cyclic ±76 Å displacements of the predicted helium wavelength measure accidental pairings under the same spectral cuts. The evidence includes opportunities, observed and displaced yields, synthetic-pair recovery, and the eight published positive controls. Surviving observed pairs enter the same source vetting as primary helium leads.

## Matched same-shot control

The matched control was added after the first-pass SC2 run showed an excess of peaks in the raw shuffle. The raw shuffle mixes absolute flux measurements from different sources at each wavelength. A bright stellar continuum beside a faint galaxy can produce an artificial peak in the mixed spectrum even with correctly paired errors. Keep all raw-shuffle spectral results and subject 1,024 spectra per release to the full downstream checks. Allocate that sample proportionally across the originally evaluated blocks and choose spectra without using their scores or number of peaks.

A second control draws approximately one recipient per 64 observations by shot. Select up to eight donor rows from the same shot at positions more than 10 arcsec away, requiring at least three donors with comparable continuum colors, errors, robust gains and masked fractions. Fit each continuum by interpolation between median 32-channel windows. Subtract and center the continuum, divide by the native uncertainty and robust median-absolute-deviation gain, and resample residuals in contiguous four-channel donor blocks with random phase. Each block retains its original observed wavelength. Restore the recipient continuum, empirical gain and error. Use the same valid pixels for its paired real spectrum. Persist recipient/donor row IDs and drawing seeds, and report attrition and lag-one covariance diagnostics. Genuine donor emission may enter this control and correlations spanning block boundaries can be interrupted. Retain the original cube blanks and the separate wrong-sky, independent-shot tests alongside both shuffles.
