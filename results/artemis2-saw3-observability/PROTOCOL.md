# Artemis II lunar flash observability in the SAW3 archive

This audit asks what Orion's Solar Array Wing Camera 3 actually supplies around the two timed crew reports of lunar flashes. The observing unit is a decoded source frame with a presentation timestamp. The independently reported cues are the crew's speech references at 00:41:01 and 00:51:15 UTC on 2026-04-07. A speech timestamp can follow a sighting. The mission report supplies these cue times, rather than a calibrated camera-to-crew timing model. Test the nominal time windows before interpreting any brightening. [NASA Preliminary Lunar Science Report](https://science.nasa.gov/feature/artemis-ii-preliminary-lunar-science-report/) · [PDS mission archive](https://pds-geosciences.wustl.edu/missions/artemis2/) · [Data User Guide](https://pds-geosciences.wustl.edu/artemis2/urn-nasa-pds-artemis2_mission/document/artemis2_data_user_guide.pdf)

## Frozen inputs and windows

Use the PDS channel-1 downlink product starting at 23:38:11 UTC and the separate onboard 30-fps recording starting at 01:19:13 UTC. The registry uses `art002m1200970119` for the onboard record. The guide's narrative shows a different prefix. Resolve by PDS label, duration and timestamp. Verify native MP4 sizes and PDS byte checksums. Retain returned labels and record the decoder and time base. Channel 2 is another ground decoder of the same source. It does not provide an independent camera exposure.

In the downlink inspect half-open intervals of 30 seconds centered on the two speech cues and another 30 seconds starting when the onboard video starts. The ±15-second interval is a repeatable search convention, not a measured timing error. Evaluate the entire onboard record separately as a cadence and image-content comparison. Use the PDS product start plus each decoded frame's elapsed presentation time as its nominal UTC. Compare container duration with label span before drawing timing conclusions. Record relevant still-label exposure times in the same window, with missing stop times separate from measured metadata. The first cataloged timestamp is assumed to refer to exposure start only when reporting a nominal still integration interval.

## Detector checks

Count presented frames, exact successive luma repeats and changes in an interior detector region. Evaluate raw decoded luma on its stored scale without a display transfer or gain correction. Preserve first and last timestamps, monochrome frame summaries and enough native statistics to test a different repeat definition. A distinct decoded frame can still differ only because of compression or overlays. Returned packet cadence and visual updates are separate observables.

Inspect whether the lunar disk is localizable and whether intensity changes arise from lunar pixels, spacecraft structure, framing changes, telemetry fills or decoder artifacts. A camera file covering the nominal time alone does not demonstrate an image of the lunar surface at that instant. A positive instrumental transient must be localized on the lunar disk, have a plausible optical profile and be separated from changes elsewhere in the same image. An upper limit requires location, calibrated sensitivity or an explicitly instrument-scale injection test with its limitations. The mission team already examined the short onboard video for flashes.

Keep synthetic tests and alternate decoder checks separate from the observing windows. Treat nonlocalizable or low-information intervals as an observability result. A failure to find an image counterpart under those conditions cannot characterize actual impacts.
