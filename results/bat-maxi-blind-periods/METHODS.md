# Period search and detection limits

The search measures repeatable modulation of daily mean X-ray rate. A period alone does not establish an orbital, superorbital, or outburst origin. Each BAT roster entry contributes one trial family, including entries without a usable MAXI counterpart. Each available band is scanned independently before periods are compared.

## Two registered search specifications

The first scan used the published inverse variances, a minimum of 40 epochs, 20 effective samples and 60 days, with direct vetoes of predicted clock sums and differences. This excluded some long, densely observed curves because a small number of formally precise epochs dominated their weights. It also rejected injections near predicted combinations that lacked an independently measured window line. The second scan applies a single revised specification to the whole roster. The two scans together consume twice the single-scan frequency-by-source trial allowance. Original results, including failures and published control detections, remain separately recorded.

## Blind grid and noise model

* Keep finite rates, including negative rates, with positive finite uncertainties and distinct epochs. Time and reported uncertainty alone determine a lower error bound for each instrument's curve. Within each elapsed-time half take the larger of the 10th error order statistic and the ceiling of the 10th percentile rank, capped at the number of available epochs. Use the greater of these two half-specific error values as a common floor. This limits the influence of a few formally precise bins and retains the relative ordering of errors above the floor. Save the raw and bounded Kish sample sizes, the floor, the count of changed errors and corresponding diagnostics for each half. Use the inflated errors both in the weights and for conservative independent measurement-noise simulations. The original quoted errors remain in the frozen inputs. A preliminary spectrum requires at least 16 epochs, six effective samples and a baseline of at least 10.25 days. Records too short for two independent half tests cannot confirm a signal. Search from 2.05 days to the shorter of 2,000 days and one quarter of the observed baseline. Individual grid edges and effective sample counts are saved.
* On each instrument's own grid, fit a floating constant plus a sinusoid with inverse-variance weights. Spacing is at most one quarter of the inverse baseline. Gaussian interpolation of actual timestamps onto a zero-padded half-day FFT grid speeds the Fourier sums. A finer quarter-day grid supplies the sine/cosine Gram matrix. Final fits and tail probabilities use the original timestamps directly. The daily input sets a real limit on sub-day and near-two-day recovery.
* Model the rate residuals using independent measurement errors, intrinsic white noise and a nonnegative mixture of eight stationary Ornstein–Uhlenbeck processes. Their correlation times run from one day to one third of the actual baseline. At the actual epochs, forward recurrences give the exact covariance of each component projected onto the weighted sinusoid fit. Use these responses at seven frequencies in each of up to eleven logarithmic bands to fit the observed broadband median periodogram. Medians reduce the influence of isolated coherent lines. A lag-variogram fit provides a fallback on short or ill-conditioned records. We save the component variances, timescales and method per curve.
* Simulate independent realizations of the fitted process on the original irregular timestamps. Each Gaussian process starts in its stationary distribution and advances with its exact gap-dependent transition variance. Add simulated measurement noise using every observation's reported error. Sixty-four realizations give the mean power at each grid frequency. Divide each observed and simulated power by that reference. Another 255 independent simulations give the distribution of the maximum normalized power over the entire grid. Save all local peaks with Monte Carlo rank probability below 0.01, together with counts of all lower peaks and window rejections. Seed streams derive from the source identifier, instrument and simulation purpose.
* Save observed median normalized power per band alongside the simulated first and 99th percentiles. If fewer than two diagnostic bands are available or at least two bands fall below half the simulated first percentile or above twice the 99th, the fitted continuum fails the adequacy check and the curve cannot confirm a period. These broad-bin checks cannot rule out nonstationarity, heavy tails, localized model failures, or coherent contamination from another sky source.

## Instrumental clocks and inference

* Calculate both the unweighted timestamp window and the inverse-variance-weighted window. Include resolved peaks containing at least 1% of the squared DC amplitude through 1.45 cycles/day. Direct vetoes include the first four harmonics of the solar and sidereal day, the native spacecraft precession (53 ± 2 days for Swift, 70 ± 2 days for ISS), four lunar months (synodic, sidereal, anomalistic and draconic), and the year. The other spacecraft's precession and all two-clock fundamental sums and differences generate advisory flags. If an observed timestamp or weight window line containing at least 1% of the squared DC amplitude supports a combination, veto the actual measured line within one inverse baseline. An unsupported combination cannot on its own reject a real period. Each threshold peak records proximity, predicted frequency and any measured window power. Use one inverse baseline of tolerance plus the specified clock-width allowance. Save the number of all local maxima vetoed.
* Rank significant peaks by normalized power. Check all local maxima for sidebands of stronger Monte Carlo-significant peaks at both signs of each clock harmonic and observed window line. Reject unresolved neighbors within 1.25 inverse baselines, and harmonics 2–6 of stronger signals. Vetoes reduce sensitivity to genuine modulations near the listed frequencies.
* For strong peaks, compute the exact two-dimensional covariance of the weighted, mean-subtracted sine/cosine projection under the fitted continuous-time noise model. With the model fixed, its squared Mahalanobis length has a chi-square distribution with two degrees of freedom. Multiply the resulting tail probability by the number of frequencies on that instrument's grid, the full BAT roster size, and two survey specifications, capped at one. Discovery, sinusoidal injections, outburst injections and the conditional residual audit share the same multiplicity function. Require this conservative survey bound to be at most 0.05 **and** the simulated full-grid rank probability to be below 0.01 in each instrument. The Monte Carlo value is (1 + number of simulated maxima at least as large)/(1 + 255), with resolution 1/256. The survey bound uses the model's analytic tail. The simulations do not measure probabilities below their rank resolution.
* BAT and MAXI must each pass on their own. Accept their frequencies as agreeing within half the sum of their inverse baselines. Use the larger of the two survey bounds for their conjunction. This avoids an assumption that intrinsic source variability is independent across bands. The model probabilities are conditional on the estimated continuum and reported errors. Their interpretation concerns stochastic fluctuations under that continuum, including a lookup over the whole source/grid search. They do not give an unconditional probability of astrophysical novelty or eliminate aliases generated by real modulations.

## Coherence, estimates and injections

* Split each instrument at its elapsed-time midpoint. Require at least 16 observations, 10 effective observations, and 1.9 trial cycles per half. Search ±2 inverse half-baselines locally. Both half maxima must be within one inverse half-baseline of the full frequency. Each half must pass a fixed-frequency red-covariance test at 0.05 and the two phases must agree within 0.20 cycle at a shared epoch. Save separate period, amplitude, phase and local probability for all tested halves.
* Refine each surviving instrument period by maximizing weighted least-squares improvement on the native timestamps within one grid step. Report BAT as the primary measurement. Save the MAXI estimate independently. Report rate semiamplitudes and local one-standard-deviation uncertainties from the sandwich covariance of the weighted sinusoid fit including the frequency derivative and the fitted red covariance. These are local errors for a single coherent mode. Sixteen-bin inverse-variance folds include coverage and measurement-only errors, which exclude correlated source variability.
* Inject sinusoids into the real, unmodified measured rates for a separate recovery experiment. Assign each source one eligible period from 7.37, 41.81, 173.3 and 683.3 days with a stable seeded draw. At that period test eight uniformly drawn phases and three semiamplitudes, 0.10, 0.30 and 1.0 times the observed weighted RMS in **each** band. Each row includes the actual rate amplitude in each instrument's own units. Hold the original curve's fitted continuum, simulation thresholds and model-adequacy decision fixed. Rerun both blind spectra, alias vetoes, full-survey analytic threshold, independent agreement and both half tests. Count a recovery only within one inverse baseline of the planted frequency in each instrument. Single-band recovery is also saved for uncovered sources. Rates are conditional on this waveform, grid, band scaling and sampled source/phase population. The same phases are reused at the three amplitudes, so those amplitude comparisons are paired. A source without a usable second instrument contributes no joint sensitivity. Injections measure statistical selection before counterpart contamination and literature review.

## Targeted residual audit

After the blind scan, `residuals.py` reopens the frozen daily curves of numerical survivors. At each candidate it checks stronger same-source maxima from the retained blind peak list, including peaks originally flagged as instrumental. A peak enters the nuisance fit if it exceeds the candidate's raw explained variance or normalized score and remains significant under the frozen covariance after the previously fitted lines are projected out. Each such family is profiled on the native timestamps within one inverse baseline. The fit includes up to six harmonics and holds the candidate in the model while optimizing the nuisance frequency. A pivoted QR decomposition then projects out the jointly fitted nuisance family columns. If more than eight significant families remain, the audit marks the result unresolved.

The audit reports the original-grid and refined-period semiamplitudes and conditional model bounds before and after projection. It projects the fitted nuisance waveform into the candidate sinusoid at the actual sample times. This measures leakage due to arbitrary gaps, weight variations and finite-baseline sidelobes even when they were absent from the original clock veto list. For each nuisance harmonic, the audit also reports prominent native window powers at the sum and difference frequencies. A weak residual, unresolved neighbor, close harmonic relation or large predicted leakage triggers interpretation. The residual bound conditions on nuisance frequencies estimated from the data. It is a diagnostic and adds no independently calibrated detection probability. A separate table groups numerical survivors of the same source with frequencies consistent with integer multiples through order six.

## Short-duty outburst sensitivity

A separate recovery experiment reopens each dual-instrument source from the corresponding pass and replays the blind calibration on its frozen rate measurements. It checks the original continuum, grid, model-adequacy decision, peak indices, scores and Monte Carlo ranks before injecting. Neither the real-source detections nor their reported probabilities are re-estimated by this experiment.

Each eligible pair retains its original assigned period stratum. At eight new seeded phases the injected source spends 10% of each cycle at a fixed excess rate. Each modeled daily measurement averages that top hat over a nominal one-day bin centered at the recorded epoch. The injected peak excess is 1, 3 or 10 times each band's original weighted RMS. These heights produce first-harmonic semiamplitudes of approximately 0.20, 0.59 and 1.97 times RMS before daily binning. Phases are shared between instruments and paired across the three heights. This assumes equal periodic spacing, a constant duty cycle and no varying outburst strength. Unavailable intra-day exposure histories preclude an exact exposure-weighted pulse integral.

The original sinusoidal search scans the full frequency grid of each injected light curve. Recovery requires the original continuum check, Monte Carlo threshold, native window and alias vetoes, survey-wide red-noise bound, half-period and phase stability, and BAT/MAXI frequency agreement. A joint recovery must pass these tests within one inverse baseline of the planted fundamental in both instruments. A harmonic by itself does not count. The output saves each source's tested phases and physical excess rates. Percentile intervals resample whole sources within a period and amplitude cell, keeping their phases together. A collapsed bootstrap interval at zero or one only describes the observed source set. These experiments assess the numerical gates before literature, localization and targeted residual review.
