# Period search and detection limits

The search measures repeatable modulation of daily mean X-ray rate. A period alone does not establish an orbital, superorbital, or outburst origin. Each BAT roster entry contributes one trial family, including entries without a usable MAXI counterpart. Each available band is scanned independently before periods are compared.

## Blind grid and noise model

* Keep finite rates, including negative rates, with positive finite uncertainties and distinct epochs. Require at least 40 samples, 20 effective inverse-variance-weighted samples and 60 days of coverage. Search from 2.05 days to the shorter of 2,000 days and one quarter of the observed baseline. Individual grid edges and effective sample counts are saved.
* On each instrument's own grid, fit a floating constant plus a sinusoid with inverse-variance weights. Spacing is at most one quarter of the inverse baseline. Gaussian interpolation of actual timestamps onto a zero-padded half-day FFT grid speeds the Fourier sums. A finer quarter-day grid supplies the sine/cosine Gram matrix. Final fits and tail probabilities use the original timestamps directly. The daily input sets a real limit on sub-day and near-two-day recovery.
* Model the rate residuals using independent measurement errors, intrinsic white noise and a nonnegative mixture of eight stationary Ornstein–Uhlenbeck processes. Their correlation times run from one day to one third of the actual baseline. At the actual epochs, forward recurrences give the exact covariance of each component projected onto the weighted sinusoid fit. Use these responses at seven frequencies in each of up to eleven logarithmic bands to fit the observed broadband median periodogram. Medians reduce the influence of isolated coherent lines. A lag-variogram fit provides a fallback on short or ill-conditioned records. We save the component variances, timescales and method per curve.
* Simulate independent realizations of the fitted process on the original irregular timestamps. Each Gaussian process starts in its stationary distribution and advances with its exact gap-dependent transition variance. Add simulated measurement noise using every observation's reported error. Sixty-four realizations give the mean power at each grid frequency. Divide each observed and simulated power by that reference. Another 255 independent simulations give the distribution of the maximum normalized power over the entire grid. Save all local peaks with Monte Carlo rank probability below 0.01, together with counts of all lower peaks and window rejections. Seed streams derive from the source identifier, instrument and simulation purpose.
* Save observed median normalized power per band alongside the simulated first and 99th percentiles. If at least two bands fall below half the simulated first percentile or above twice the 99th, the fitted continuum fails the adequacy check and the curve cannot confirm a period. These broad-bin checks cannot rule out nonstationarity, heavy tails, localized model failures, or coherent contamination from another sky source.

## Instrumental clocks and inference

* Calculate both the unweighted timestamp window and the inverse-variance-weighted window. Include resolved peaks containing at least 1% of the squared DC amplitude through 1.45 cycles/day. Direct vetoes include the first four harmonics of the solar and sidereal day, Swift precession (53 ± 2 days), ISS precession (70 ± 2 days), four lunar months (synodic, sidereal, anomalistic and draconic), and the year. The direct veto also covers in-band fundamental sum and difference frequencies of those clocks. Use one inverse baseline of tolerance plus the specified clock-width allowance. Save the number of all local maxima vetoed.
* Rank significant peaks by normalized power. Check all local maxima for sidebands of stronger Monte Carlo-significant peaks at both signs of each clock harmonic and observed window line. Reject unresolved neighbors within 1.25 inverse baselines, and harmonics 2–6 of stronger signals. Vetoes reduce sensitivity to genuine modulations near the listed frequencies.
* For strong peaks, compute the exact two-dimensional covariance of the weighted, mean-subtracted sine/cosine projection under the fitted continuous-time noise model. With the model fixed, its squared Mahalanobis length has a chi-square distribution with two degrees of freedom. Multiply the resulting tail probability by the number of frequencies on that instrument's grid and the full number of BAT roster entries, capped at one. Require this conservative survey bound to be at most 0.05 **and** the simulated full-grid rank probability to be below 0.01 in each instrument. The Monte Carlo value is (1 + number of simulated maxima at least as large)/(1 + 255), with resolution 1/256. The survey bound uses the model's analytic tail. The simulations do not measure probabilities below their rank resolution.
* BAT and MAXI must each pass on their own. Accept their frequencies as agreeing within half the sum of their inverse baselines. Use the larger of the two survey bounds for their conjunction. This avoids an assumption that intrinsic source variability is independent across bands. The model probabilities are conditional on the estimated continuum and reported errors. Their interpretation concerns stochastic fluctuations under that continuum, including a lookup over the whole source/grid search. They do not give an unconditional probability of astrophysical novelty or eliminate aliases generated by real modulations.

## Coherence, estimates and injections

* Split each instrument at its elapsed-time midpoint. Require at least 16 observations, 10 effective observations, and 1.9 trial cycles per half. Search ±2 inverse half-baselines locally. Both half maxima must be within one inverse half-baseline of the full frequency. Each half must pass a fixed-frequency red-covariance test at 0.05 and the two phases must agree within 0.20 cycle at a shared epoch. Save separate period, amplitude, phase and local probability for all tested halves.
* Refine each surviving instrument period by maximizing weighted least-squares improvement on the native timestamps within one grid step. Report BAT as the primary measurement. Save the MAXI estimate independently. Report rate semiamplitudes and local one-standard-deviation uncertainties from the sandwich covariance of the weighted sinusoid fit including the frequency derivative and the fitted red covariance. These are local errors for a single coherent mode. Sixteen-bin inverse-variance folds include coverage and measurement-only errors, which exclude correlated source variability.
* Inject sinusoids into the real, unmodified measured rates for a separate recovery experiment. Assign each source one eligible period from 7.37, 41.81, 173.3 and 683.3 days with a stable seeded draw. At that period test eight uniformly drawn phases and three semiamplitudes, 0.10, 0.30 and 1.0 times the observed weighted RMS in **each** band. Each row includes the actual rate amplitude in each instrument's own units. Hold the original curve's fitted continuum, simulation thresholds and model-adequacy decision fixed. Rerun both blind spectra, alias vetoes, full-survey analytic threshold, independent agreement and both half tests. Count a recovery only within one inverse baseline of the planted frequency in each instrument. Single-band recovery is also saved for uncovered sources. Rates are conditional on this waveform, grid, band scaling and sampled source/phase population. The same phases are reused at the three amplitudes, so those amplitude comparisons are paired. A source without a usable second instrument contributes no joint sensitivity. Injections measure statistical selection before counterpart contamination and literature review.
