# Scope and interpretation

The BAT monitor's public source roster defines the population. A source can enter the BAT search regardless of whether BAT reported an individual detection. Independent confirmation requires an identified MAXI/GSC light curve, a blind peak in both instruments and repeated modulation in disjoint time intervals. Exposure gaps and the change in observing span between monitors are part of the selection function.

A sinusoidal modulation in two energy bands establishes a repeating X-ray signal. An orbital interpretation needs evidence linking the signal to the binary orbit. Harmonics, superorbital modulation, outburst recurrence and contamination by other objects can produce competing explanations. A previously published fundamental or harmonic, an unresolved source identification, or incomplete literature coverage precludes a discovery claim.

The survey uses daily products. Sub-day or strongly transient clocks, severely non-sinusoidal outbursts, wandering cycles and systems faint in one energy band can escape detection. Sensitivity from injected sinusoids applies over the tested amplitudes and periods. A negative literature query alone cannot establish that a period is unpublished.
