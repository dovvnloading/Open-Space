# C/2004 H6 (SWAN) already had a published, mildly hyperbolic inbound orbit

Four published SWAN directions precede the 2004 May 12.68 perihelion. They fall within 0.67–0.85° of the modern SOHO-centered ephemeris at 00:00 UT. Two published ground-based orbit solutions, one constrained to a parabola, predict directions only 6.5–13.6 arcsec apart on those dates. The historical SWAN directions cannot distinguish those solutions. The hydrogen coma covers an extended area, the native instrument resolution is about 1°, and the discovery circular supplies an integer day for each map. [Original discovery circular, IAUC 8346](https://tamkin3.eps.harvard.edu/iauc/08300/08346.html) · [ESA SWAN data description](https://doi.org/10.5270/esa-k1pfbpn)

IAUC 8346 credits independent May 13 reports by X.-M. Zhou, K. Černis and M. Mattiazzo. The Central Bureau reported failed early searches and suspected some early reports had been misidentified. The table above uses only the four directions actually published by the Bureau. Its first reported ground positions are visual estimates on May 14, followed by a 135-mm telephoto CCD position on May 16. All were obtained after perihelion. [IAUC 8346](https://tamkin3.eps.harvard.edu/iauc/08300/08346.html)

The preliminary June 2004 MPC solution used 14 ground positions and fixed e = 1. In July 2004 Brian Marsden published original barycentric 1/a = −124 ± 48 in units of 10⁻⁶ au⁻¹. Its nominal excess speed is 0.33 km/s, 2.58 standard deviations from zero in reciprocal semimajor axis. The residual table lists 44 directions, with ten parenthesized exclusions. This establishes that the small negative original reciprocal semimajor axis had already been reported in 2004. [MPEC 2004-L42](https://www.minorplanetcenter.net/iau/mpec/K04/K04L42.html) · [MPEC 2004-N33](https://www.minorplanetcenter.net/iau/mpec/K04/K04N33.html)

The [calculation](h6-archive/check_swan.py) propagates the two published, rounded sets of elements with two-body solar gravity and light time, using Horizons vectors for the SOHO spacecraft. Both predictions are compared at 00:00 TDB. The SWAN-to-Horizons comparison uses JPL solution 19 and 00:00 UT. Unknown scan times contribute to these differences. We verified nine public NASA 640 × 480 quicklook GIFs dated April 28–May 17, including all four discovery dates. The [query receipts](h6-archive/swan-receipts.json) give URLs and SHA-256 values for each image, both Horizons responses, the archive listing and all three circulars. A preperihelion nuclear position accurate enough to test these orbit solutions remains unverified. No derived SWAN point entered the orbit fit.
