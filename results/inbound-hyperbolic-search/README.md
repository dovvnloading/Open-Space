# Search for inbound hyperbolic small bodies

**No previously unrecognised interstellar object was validated.** The search found zero real three-night high-speed links in 2,130 eligible Pan-STARRS 1 tracklets from October 19 through November 16, 2026. It recovered 35 of 37 simulated objects whose paths came within 1.5° of actual detections on three nights. Across the full simulation, only 37 of 20,000 paths met that sky-and-time requirement. Across 2,290 catalogue IDs, independent orbit fits produced 603 numerical far-inbound estimates, including 416 actual inbound crossings of 250 au with formal uncertainties. The fastest formal outliers were known objects, fragments, or short arcs admitting better parabolic fits.

The [referee replay and source-drift audit](REFEREE.md) independently checks the frozen astrometry, orbit solutions, recovery intervals, source receipts and remaining limits before merge.

### Apparent high-speed outliers and controls

Four short spacecraft arcs gave some of the largest apparent speeds. C/2004 D4, C/2005 D3, C/2006 S8 and C/2006 S9 are known SOHO Kreutz comets. The unconstrained Find_Orb solutions gave 47–325 km/s and high residuals and reappeared with a 120-second CPU budget. Independent parabolic fits used all 123 observations and all accompanying spacecraft coordinates. The per-coordinate RMS was respectively 22.47″, 28.17″, 35.49″ and 37.61″, lower than the high-speed solution for every object. [Same-observation parabolic fits and published comet identities](catalog/soho/)

The initial-orbit score in Find_Orb penalizes high eccentricity for unrecognised names. On the same blinded observations, the default procedure returned bound orbits for 1I and 2I and a spurious 14.40 km/s orbit for 3I. With the high-eccentricity penalty removed, a 120-second CPU budget reproduced all three incoming speeds. The 18-second screen timed out on every blinded control. [Paired control observations, settings and residuals](batch/evidence/blinded-controls.md)

### Original orbits and C/2025 V1 (Borisov)

Some formal outliers can be tested against the history of the object. The quick gravity fit to C/1996 J1-A gives 0.448 km/s and 18.8 formal eccentricity errors using 115 positions from May–December 1997, all after breakup. Of 131 explicitly parent-designated observations before the breakup, a separate fit retained 130. It gives incoming `1/a = (−109 ± 224) × 10⁻⁶ au⁻¹` at 250 au, and remains compatible with zero energy under radial or Marsden acceleration. Another formal positive-energy trajectory is C/2025 K1-D, observed after the parent comet fragmented. [Parent and component comparison](orbits/fragment/) · [Long-budget fits](batch/fast-priority/)

Among the 17 archival gravity outliers tested with denser data, C/2023 A3, C/2024 E1 and C/2021 O3 change to bound in preperihelion-only fits. The published preferred short-arc solution for the disintegrating comet C/2002 O4 is likewise bound under an activity model. [Independent force-law and withheld-data comparisons](orbits/robust/) · [Published C/2002 O4 solutions](https://apollo.astro.amu.edu.pl/CODE/orbit.php?int=2002o4n1&orb=original)

The independent event-based fit recovered incoming speeds at 250 au of 26.34 km/s for 1I/‘Oumuamua, 32.28 for 2I/Borisov and 57.97 for 3I/ATLAS. C/1980 E1 was bound before its Jupiter encounter and C/2024 L5 before its Saturn encounter. A/2024 U2's well-determined heliocentric hyperbola also becomes bound on entry, with `1/a = +0.00002521 ± 0.00000395 au⁻¹` from all 159 positions. C/2011 M1 switches to bound when the last night is omitted. C/2020 W5 has an unbound excess of just 1.32 formal errors in our gravity fit. [Twelve independent fits, model definitions and controls](orbits/README.md) · [Three slow objects](orbits/slow/README.md)

C/2004 H6 (SWAN) remains a slow, previously recognised hyperbolic candidate. Its 307 ground positions all follow perihelion. The denser gravity fit gives incoming `1/a = (−370 ± 29) × 10⁻⁶ au⁻¹`. Allowing outward radial and transverse water-ice acceleration gives `(−334 ± 93) × 10⁻⁶ au⁻¹` (about 0.54 km/s, 3.6 formal errors). The July 2004 MPC circular already reported `(−124 ± 48) × 10⁻⁶ au⁻¹` on the shorter arc. Fitting a third acceleration component gives 2.69 formal errors for an unbound origin, with strongly correlated accelerations. A near-parabolic solution costs Δχ² = 7.24 in that model. Four published preperihelion SWAN directions fall 0.67–0.85° from the current SOHO-centered ephemeris at midnight. The predictions from the contemporary parabolic and free-eccentricity orbits differ by only 6.5–13.6 arcseconds. The one-degree pixels and day-only reported times cannot distinguish these orbits. [Activity models and data holdouts](orbits/robust/) · [Contemporary circular and early imaging](catalog/2004-H6.md)

C/2025 V1, a known active comet distinct from 2I, had a catalogue osculating excess speed of 3.85 km/s. All 117 current ground positions span 9.62 days. A gravity-only fit gives incoming `1/a = −0.01598 ± 0.00316 au⁻¹` at 250 au (5.05 formal standard deviations). Fitting radial and transverse water-ice outgassing gives `−0.02682 ± 0.02618 au⁻¹` (1.02). Withholding the seven ZTF observations weakens the gravity-only result to 1.71. Constraining the incoming energy at 50 au to zero costs Δχ² = 25.37 under gravity, 1.06 when both Marsden coefficients may have either sign, and 3.39 with outward radial acceleration required. [Astrometry, fitted states, covariance and holdouts](orbits/README.md)

All ten ZTF exposures with footprints predating the published arc were tested using difference images or same-night subtraction. The October 25 single-exposure excess failed to repeat along the predicted path. The two better October 27 pipeline differences produced a peak with empirical off-track probability 0.047. Independent subtraction of the corresponding science frames peaked 6.4″ away, with probability 0.243 in the same type of control. A separate fit to the expected trailed point-spread function gave a two-frame peak of 3.15 empirical standard deviations. The empirical off-track probability was 0.059. Only one of the two frames supported the shared position. The local five-sigma point-source depth for the trailed template was approximately r=19.50. Recovery of a diffuse comet at this brightness was unmeasured. No earlier moving position was validated. The known ZTF positions included r=17.49 and 17.70 on October 29 and r=15.41 on November 1, 2025. The DECam search tested 19,899 exposure centers and the Rubin DP2 search tested 21,738 dated visits. Neither search found a possible exposure at the predicted position. ### Pan-STARRS and Rubin tracklets

The full MPC Isolated Tracklet File contained 7,170 F51 Pan-STARRS 1 observations in the 29-day window, October 19–November 16, 2026 UTC. Quality cuts retained 2,130 tracklet-days on 17 dates. The frozen full trial-state prefilter proposed zero real three-night orbit triples (`real_3night_proposals: 0`); no candidate reached the final six-coordinate orbit fit. The final orbit screen used a nominal excess speed above 10 km/s. Five independently rotated skies gave zero accepted links. [Selection, trial-state search, orbit fit and frozen observations](tracklets/README.md)

| Simulation in the real background | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Supply three groups of synthetic observations on a real cadence | 99/100 recovered (exact 95% interval 94.55–99.97%), including 51/52 inbound objects. In this experiment the later positions were supplied irrespective of telescope pointing. None of these 100 paths had nearby real detections on all three nights. |
| Start 20,000 independent paths at real F51 tracklets and check the actual sky directions and times | 37 paths had a real detection within 1.5° at every modeled exposure on three nights. Of these, 35/37 were recovered (94.6%, exact 95% interval 81.8–99.3%), including 14/15 inbound objects. Thirty-five of the 37 paths relied on November 1–3. At 0.5° just one path qualified and was recovered. At 0.1° none qualified. |

No accepted injection association mixed real and synthetic identities. The simulated geocentric distances were 0.45–2.8 au, excess speeds 15–60 km/s, initial heliocentric velocity directions isotropic, and astrometric noise 0.2″ per coordinate. With independent Poisson arrivals already supplying three tracklets drawn like the forced experiment, zero real links yields a conservative 95% upper bound of **3.90 expected such objects** in this sample. The calculation combines a Poisson upper bound and a binomial lower recovery bound. A nearby real detection does not determine which detector pixels were exposed or whether an object was bright enough to be measured. [Injection draws, intervals and conditional limit](tracklets/README.md)

The latest 29-day ITF window contained zero observations from Rubin station X05. The last populated 29-day X05 slice ended July 14, 2026. It had 36 measurements across eight submitted tracklet-days and only two nights after motion cuts. The 29 recent Rubin submission partitions held 45,440 distinct rows whose actual observation dates spanned May 2025–July 2026. ## Unresolved questions and limits

- Across all independent methods, 1,687 of the 2,290 catalogue IDs lack a numerical far-inbound estimate. A published CODE comparison is available for 26 of those. Even among the 603 with a numerical estimate, several short-arc high-speed solutions fit poorly or fail the alternate-model tests. Of the 1,763 exactly parabolic catalogue orbits, 1,422 had reported arcs shorter than two days. Some individual solutions are sensitive to weights, fragmentation, historical reductions, or unmeasurable comet activity. Complete six-dimensional uncertainty propagation under alternate force laws was feasible only for selected objects.
- This is a dated slice of unlinked tracks and NEOCP history. The single-opposition comparison covers `0.95 ≤ e < 1`. Historical NEOCP astrometry outside the specified selection, other stations and cadences, and bodies with only one or two eligible tracks remain largely unconstrained.
- A survey-wide encounter rate or space-density bound needs true detector footprints, limiting magnitude, photometric and trailing efficiency, and the probability of entry in the ITF. The forced-astrometry and sky-proximity tests measure separate parts of this chain.
