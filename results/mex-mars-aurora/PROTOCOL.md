# Mars Express radio occultations around the March 2024 aurora

Frozen on 18 November 2026 on main 45f6cf4f before opening the Doppler or EDAC values. NASA added MaRS volumes 4433, 4436, 4443, 4445, 4451 and 4452 on 26 October 2026. Earlier archived 4432 and 4434 are a same-station comparison straddling the late March 17 EDAC increase reported by Knutsen et al. The latter pass is inside the model arrival interval, ahead of the published particle enhancement and visible aurora. March 17 at 11:13–11:49 UTC is several hours before the earliest published model window. ## Observing units and controls

- An observing unit is one ground-station tracking pass. Use all six October DSN releases in a metadata and geometry inventory. Select their native L2 S-band DPS and X-band DPX samples. For the same-station March comparison use New Norcia volumes 4432 and 4434, both receiver chains' 0.1-second D4S and D5X files. Keep the receiver chains separate at every step. They observe the same signal and provide a receiver check. Preserve native counts, timestamp supports, missing data, quality/sigma fields where present and archive receipts. Native L2 points can be derived from coarser receiver sampling, so bin to full UTC seconds and require five populated native rows per band per bin. Form matched bins only on the shared support, without filling gaps. Use arithmetic seconds from UTC, not rounded header limits.
- Reconstruct the finite space-to-Earth downlink using public SPICE spacecraft and planet kernels, with reception epochs and one-way light time. Use a spherical 3,396.19 km Mars reference for selection, and preserve volume-provided geometry separately. Identify ingress and egress from the geometry, where the closest point is inside the segment. Use apparent geometry relative to geocenter as a bounded approximation and report the effects of geometry/height conventions on interpretations. Verify that the line of sight approaches the limb inside the joint time interval before interpreting atmosphere. For nonintersecting tracks retain the closest approach and nominal label classification. Compute tangent latitude, solar zenith angle (SZA) and range where geometry permits.
- Use the native calibrated frequency residuals and compare them to observed minus predicted where both exist. Retain the archive differential field separately for convention checking. Compute D = residual(S) − (3/11) residual(X) for the coherent two-way mode. Form each same-second paired value from per-band medians. The ratio removes a common achromatic fractional frequency term to first order. The same X uplink and line of sight enter both receivers. The difference can contain plasma anywhere along the downlink, receiver glitches or remaining model error. Record insensitive and failed cases.
- For each uninterrupted limb branch use [350,650) km as the frozen off-limb background and [120,210) km as the analysis band. Require at least 15 common seconds in each to score a branch. Remove the linear trend fitted in the off-limb samples from D. Record median absolute D and its 90th percentile on both altitude bands, trace slope and sign, counts and altitude support. Compare the residuals in the same way after using a median-only background and [300,600) km off-limb as sensitivity tests. Calculate changes in a matched altitude band for the earlier and later New Norcia passes only if both chains agree in direction, the branches cover a common altitude range and the tangent tracks' median SZA and latitude are within 15° and 20° respectively. Any reached layer must have at least three common 10 km bins, each supported by at least three paired seconds in each pass. A twofold limb-to-background contrast in both receiver chains is the minimum for a follow-up. Opposite sweep directions must be compared through absolute gradients or by tracking the altitude direction explicitly. Keep the high-altitude windows, repeat passes and receiver difference as confounder checks.
- The March 18 EDAC and published SEP/aurora series constrain chronology. EDAC records radiation-induced memory errors, not plasma density, and an optical aurora samples another location and altitude range. No storm attribution from temporal proximity or one ground ray. For the March 17 DSN pass compare contemporaneous independent GNSS vertical TEC and its hourly variation near the station as context. Use a slant mapping only if the pierce-point geometry is validated, and state that coarse global TEC cannot exclude faster or more local changes. Do not equate a same-signal L1B/L2 comparison with independent physical support.
