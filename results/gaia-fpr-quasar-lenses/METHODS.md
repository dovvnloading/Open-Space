# Selection, controls and interpretation

## Gaia fields and image pairs

We use the October 2023 Gaia Focused Product Release as distributed by CDS, tables I/361/lenscand and I/361/lenscatn. The first gives the positions and onboard G magnitudes of clusters of Gaia detections around input quasars and quasar candidates. The second identifies the input catalogue for each field. The calculation checks the declared row counts of both files. A field in the environment catalogue becomes a prior lens report only when a publication or a dedicated lens input list identifies it. Gaia input catalogue IDs 2 and 3 identify such lists. [CDS dictionary](https://cdsarc.cds.unistra.fr/ftp/I/361/ReadMe) · [ESA catalogue definitions](https://gea.esac.esa.int/archive/documentation/FPR/chap_datamodel/sec_dm_focused_product_release/ssec_dm_lens_catalogue_name.html) · [GravLens paper](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202347273)

The parent population comprises fields with 2–8 FPR components at absolute Galactic latitude of at least 20°. Components need five detections, component flag 0 or 10, and onboard G ≤ 21. Accepted pairs are separated by 0.7–5.2 arcseconds and differ by at most 3 magnitudes in FPR G. All positions are celestial coordinates in degrees. DR3 positions use the Gaia reference epoch J2016.0.

| Search | Target region | Random comparison region |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary | Circle of radius 2.6″ about the input quasar position | Equal circle 3.35″ away at a seeded random position angle |
| Wider search | Circle of radius 5.2″ about the input, intersected with a circle of radius 5.98″ about a point 5.7″ away | The same two circles with the radii exchanged at the same centres |

Every search region is inside the original 6″ FPR footprint. Rotating the wider target mask by 180° about the midpoint between the centres gives its comparison mask. We discard comparison regions containing an FPR input position and use the same image-selection rules in both arms. The offset positions can still contain uncatalogued quasars or images from a nearby lens. The comparison measures false triggers per local random aperture conditional on having selected a multiplet field. The two searches share fields and measurements. Their denominators and control rates must be reported separately.

Each component needs a single DR3 counterpart within 0.30″. Two FPR components that match the same DR3 identifier cannot supply two images. We test the vector (parallax + 0.017 mas, pmRA, pmDec) against zero using the Gaia covariance and a 0.03 mas or mas/yr systematic floor added in quadrature. The pair difference uses the sum of its two covariance matrices. Each of the three tests requires χ² ≤ 14.156 for three coordinates. We require RUWE ≤ 1.6, parallax uncertainty ≤ 1.5 mas, uncertainty in each proper motion ≤ 2 mas/yr, BP and RP flux signal-to-noise ≥ 5, and an absolute BP−RP difference ≤ 0.35 mag. Close images can contaminate each other's Gaia colour windows. The separate fits to the survey pixels provide the second colour check. [Gaia photometric validation](https://gea.esac.esa.int/archive/documentation/GEDR3/Data_processing/chap_cu5pho/cu5pho_sec_photProc/cu5pho_ssec_photVal.html)

All pair decisions are retained. Within each aperture, the image configuration is the largest set in which every pair passes. Ties are resolved by total pairwise colour difference and then FPR row order. A three-image configuration can therefore be selected even if a fourth image lacks usable astrometry. Repeated fields that yield the same DR3 source set share one pixel measurement and lens fit. The field-level ledgers retain the separate aperture identifiers.

## Imaging and mass model

At the Gaia positions the imaging code fits point sources against a sloping local sky and compares the residuals with a third point source and a PSF-convolved, extended light profile. The PSF is calibrated from compact off-target detections in the same survey field. Band diagnostics include point magnitudes, local PSF measurements, residual scores, fitted size, central coordinates and fit failures. The free extended surface-brightness component is an empirical Gaussian. Its fitted size and flux depend on that choice. Resampling correlates neighbouring pixels. The fit inflates the noise using observed blank-sky correlations. [Legacy Surveys DR10 files](https://www.legacysurvey.org/dr10/files/) · [Pan-STARRS image service](https://outerspace.stsci.edu/spaces/PANSTARRS/pages/298812251/PS1+Image+Cutout+Service)

Two different filters must prefer extended central light, agree on its position within 0.35″ and give a relative colour spread ≤ 0.55 mag for the point images. An apparent foreground galaxy can also be quasar-host light, a projected galaxy or a mismatch of the PSF. For each positive band we refit the extended component at each Gaia point source and report the score against these quasar-host alternatives. A model with extended light at both quasars remains possible. The per-band diagnostics, residual images and catalogue checks retain these alternatives.

The lens equation uses a singular isothermal ellipsoid and uniform external shear. Sky-plane offsets are east and north in arcseconds. Position angles run from east toward north. The fit reports the area-equivalent Einstein radius, axis ratio, mass position angle, shear components and orientation, mass and source coordinates, predicted image positions, signed magnifications, worst positional residual, and additional bright predicted images. We solve the forward equation and compare the predicted positions with the distinct observed images. The assumed astrometric scale, light-centre prior, ellipticity prior and shear prior are explicit in [imaging.py](imaging.py). A double supplies four positional measurements for nine lens and source parameters. The fitted parameters of a double describe a regularized example, and its geometry alone has little power to distinguish unrelated objects.

## Spectra, prior reports and recovery

The catalogue screen associates spectra with the nearest component. A spectrum centred within 0.65″ of one component and at least 2.5″ from the other candidate images can contribute to the independently resolved redshift check. We retain stellar classifications, discordant redshifts and single blended fibres. A single spectrum can establish a quasar in the field. Two compatible redshifts also occur in physically associated quasars. A foreground redshift distinct from the source, resolved spectra with matching line profiles, or repeated variability with a stable time delay can discriminate further. FPR epoch photometry for qualifying leads is descriptive because scan-angle-dependent blending can correlate neighbouring Gaia fluxes.

The primary lists use a 10″ search around each candidate image, with 6″ for the supplemental close-pair and HSC lists. A match flags a prior or possible identification and its separation is recorded. Catalogues of binaries, nearly identical quasars, uncertain lenses and published candidates count toward this veto. Each source has its own acquisition status and checksum. A missing catalogue leaves its novelty check open.

The reference population is the GraL X compilation of 364 confirmed lenses. We match each named system to eligible field centres within 6″ and count it once at each stage. The nearest field can be centred on an image rather than on the lensing galaxy. Recovery is measured before the publication veto, both against the full benchmark and conditional on an eligible FPR parent. Faint images without Gaia astrometry, small separations, high-magnification contrast and unresolved foreground galaxies all limit completeness. Wilson 95% intervals give binomial sampling scales. Shared sky fields and catalogue selection also affect their interpretation. [GraL X paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12810) · [GraL X benchmark table](https://cdsarc.cds.unistra.fr/viz-bin/cat/J/A%2BA/707/A345)
