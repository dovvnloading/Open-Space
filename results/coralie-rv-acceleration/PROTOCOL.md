# Frozen CORALIE radial-velocity acceleration screen

Does a velocity slope in the early CORALIE14 record predict a same-direction slope in later observations of the same star? Is unmodeled motion also visible in independent Hipparcos–Gaia astrometry? The observing units are one spectrum per La Silla night per target name and one velocity median per calendar-year bin. Following a stellar identifier to the wrong source, failing the independent time split, or recovering the slope from a changing spectral line shape would weaken a companion interpretation. [ESO data release](https://doi.org/10.18727/archive/106) · [Astrometry method](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4365/abf93c)

- Use only the November 2026 ESO CORALIE Phase 3 release. Freeze the ObsCore query and header selection before extracting any sample radial velocities. The single preflight spectrum is from 2010 and outside the selected sample. Use 1 July 2015–31 December 2019 for prediction and 1 January 2020–14 October 2024 for the holdout. The July cutoff avoids the June 2015 switch to Fabry–Perot referencing. End the holdout before 15 October 2024, the date of the reported ThAr lamp change. Record other reference and mask changes separately.
- Require at least 12 distinct observing nights and four calendar-year bins in both splits at selection. Freeze by exact trimmed, uppercased archive target name and use the median archive pointing for the positional crossmatch. A night starts at 12:00 UTC. Choose the highest metadata S/N spectrum each night, breaking ties by descending dataset ID. If a target has more than 18 nights in a split, choose the first, middle and last night of each year, then fill to 18 by choosing the night farthest from any selected night. No velocity informs eligibility or the subsample. Retain alias and coordinate-match risks.
- Read ESO cross-correlation RV, formal RV error, mask, global science QC, bisector and width from the primary FITS header. Require global science QC of 1, valid barycentric RV, a named mask and error greater than zero and at most 20 m/s. Use the most common acceptable mask across the two splits, breaking ties by mask name. Demand at least 10 good nights and four years after quality filtering in each split. Preserve failed and missing header downloads. Require at least 700 days spanned in each phase. Keep separate fast-known and quiet benchmarks if eligible.
- Collapse the observations to independent-year bins for the statistic. Fit an unweighted straight line to the median of the selected nightly velocities for each year at that bin's median date. Use all permutations of the yearly velocities within each phase for an exact two-sided training p value and a one-sided held-out p value in the direction fixed by training. A passing trend has absolute slope at least 10 m/s/yr in each split, training p at most 0.05, holdout p at most 0.05, and training direction preserved in both splits after deleting any one year in turn. Record all eligible tested targets. Compare the Fisher-combined p values using Benjamini–Hochberg at 0.10 as a sensitivity to selection across stars. This null assumes exchangeable year bins under no trend. Long-term covariance or shared zero-point drift can violate it.
- Check bisector and FWHM using rank correlation within each phase after removing the fitted RV trend. Also measure each indicator's slope using the same year bins. Report missing line-shape coverage and available reference modes. These checks can flag blends or activity. A lack of correlation cannot rule them out. Compare the median slope across the observed sample, recognizing that the target population may contain genuine time variability.
- Match the passed sample to HGCA EDR3 only after freezing observations. Use a HIP identifier when available and check coordinates. Otherwise use a unique counterpart within 30 arcsec of the median pointing at Gaia reference epoch, preserving multiple matches, object-name and high-proper-motion ambiguities. A Gaia proper-motion anomaly can support additional acceleration. It is a separate measurement over different epochs and does not verify an instantaneous joint orbit. Use the published covariance-aware constant proper-motion statistic and preserve cases where perspective correction assumes zero RV. Check for known visual, spectroscopic and planetary companions and for an independent public velocity series where possible.
- Run synthetic linear and sign-reversal cases before inspecting the observational fit. Track eligible control stars HD 10700 and HD 20794 as quiet benchmarks at the 10 m/s/yr threshold, and HD 219077 and HD 211847 as known long-period companion benchmarks. A curved known orbit need not pass the monotonic two-phase screen. Preserve all threshold failures, candidates with existing interpretations and follow-up limitations.
