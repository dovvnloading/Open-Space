# A reconstructed crossing of Haumea’s two moons

On **2006 August 13**, two orbit solutions put Hiʻiaka in front of Namaka as viewed from Earth. The 2024 non-Keplerian ephemeris gives a minimum projected center separation of **123 km at 11:30 UTC**. JPL Horizons gives **140 km about 64 minutes later** from an older orbit. At the adopted radii (185 and 75 km), both paths pass inside the 260 km sum of the radii. The closest archived optical position we found was 34 days earlier. This is a historical prediction of overlapping silhouettes. It has no measured light loss.

## Search and checks

We interpolated the two published right-ascension and declination offsets separately over all 33,967 epochs from 2005 January 1 to 2036 January 1. We searched every sampled local minimum of their squared difference and refined it with a cubic spline. There were 803 interior minima. The [event table](events.csv) includes every minimum inside a deliberately broad 26 milliarcseconds, more than three times the nominal limb-contact distance. All 14 occurred between 2005 and 2009. The closest post-2026-November-15 nominal solution in this table of positions is 182 mas in August 2030. The late extrapolation has large astrometric uncertainties.

For the 14 epochs we queried both satellite IDs in JPL Horizons from Earth's center. We used extra-precision astrometric coordinates (quantity 1), distance, cosine-of-declination correction to the RA differences, and the closer range for ordering. On 2005 June 24 both ephemerides also give a potential crossing (186 and 246 km), close to the limb in the older model. Other dates illustrate sensitivity to orbit choice. In particular, the solutions disagree about a February 2009 HST visit. They share historical astrometry, so their agreement is not independent observational confirmation. The published marginal positional errors do not give the four-dimensional joint covariance needed for a contact probability. The assumed 150 km diameter of Namaka alone carries about 50 km uncertainty. Solar-shadow eclipses require a separate geometry. [Proudfoot et al. 2024, Table 3](https://doi.org/10.3847/PSJ/ad26e9) · [JPL Horizons](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/) · [Hiʻiaka stellar occultations](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-65749-1) · [Proudfoot et al. 2026, updated orbit and radii](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36037)

The CADC moving-object search returned 1,246 image entries from 2005 through 2010. The MPC OBS80 response had 6,308 parseable dated records for Haumea. Both lists are included as compact time and ID extracts. A query only covers the collections it indexes. Unindexed ground-based observations could still test the historical prediction.

## The targeted Hubble visit

HST program 12004 **already predicted** the July 2, 2009 encounter. A CADC record starts within six minutes of the 2024 ephemeris minimum. Burkhart et al. reported that the shutter was closed for 6½ of the seven HST orbits. We checked all 70 calibrated STIS products, each containing three science images. The first 18 images have 40-second exposures and a clear dithered source. The other 192, including every image around the nominal minimum, have headers near 0.77 seconds. The image statistic in the receipt is the maximum after a 3 × 3 median filter in a fixed detector box, in detector counts. It is a shutter/coverage diagnostic, not photometry of a satellite. The 2009 February program 11971 and the effects of the moons being blended at that epoch were also previously published. [STScI observing program](https://www.stsci.edu/hst/phase2-public/12004.pro) · [Burkhart et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.3847/0004-6256/151/6/162) · [Hastings et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.3847/0004-6256/152/6/195)

## Reproduction

Run `python replay.py` with NumPy and SciPy to retrieve checksum-pinned Table 3 and regenerate all 14 rows. `--table FILE` uses a previously downloaded copy. Run `python verify_stis.py CACHE` with Astropy and SciPy for the 70 original FITS files and all 210 extension checks. The source manifest includes exact Horizons requests, extraction time windows, original file hashes, both archive requests, and the MAST product URL template. The compressed input tables use UTC MJD and degrees. The JPL searches use both 10-minute (±5 h) and 15-minute (±14 h) grids as recorded in the manifest. Boundary flags in the event table identify a window ending before a local minimum, if any. The sign of an archive offset is positive for observations after the 2024 orbit prediction.
