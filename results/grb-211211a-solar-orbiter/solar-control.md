# The Sun at the time of STIX catalog entry 2112111310

GOES-16 measured full-disk solar irradiance independently of STIX. In 26 one-minute records from 13:00 through 13:25 UTC on 2021-12-11, the GOES-16 XRS 1–8 Å irradiance stayed between 3.85 × 10⁻⁸ and 4.49 × 10⁻⁸ W m⁻². Both XRS channel quality flags were zero in every record. Even the highest one-minute value is below one sixth of the flux corresponding to the STIX catalog's estimated B3 class (3 × 10⁻⁷ W m⁻²). The STIX catalog derives B3 from its own count rates. The wide interval allows for the Sun-to-observer travel-time difference, about 14 s in the daily STIX FITS header. Solar Orbiter's heliographic longitude in that header is only 4.6° from the Sun–Earth meridian. The observations strongly constrain an ordinary Earth-visible solar B3 flare. A faint, short-lived, or narrowly occulted solar contribution remains possible. Source attribution of the hard pulse comes from the interplanetary timing and the Fermi profile.

The release is NOAA/NCEI GOES-16 EXIS/XRS science-quality Level 2, daily one-minute product v2-2-2. Run `python solar_control.py` with h5py, or pass a local copy of the daily NetCDF file. The script verifies the SHA-256 before writing goes16.csv. The CSV stores the UTC time labels, both irradiances in W m⁻², and both quality flags. Retrieved 2026-11-15.
