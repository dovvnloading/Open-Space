# Prespecified ASPIICS continuum timing comparison

The observing unit is one released v03 Level-3 Fe XIV exposure with its recorded wideband continuum donor and the first later v03 total-brightness exposure bracketing its representative time. We test whether the choice of plausible continuum timing materially changes the sign of displayed positive Fe XIV pixels. We separately record the timing and sequence lineage of all active Fe XIV products in two frozen orbit queries. The calibration question concerns these released data. It does not search for coronal transients or infer intrinsic narrowband emissivity.

## Sample

Orbit 795 (17 September 2026) is the newest active Level-3 orbit in the checked archive. The metadata returned 63 Fe XIV records with QUALITY=2 and two with QUALITY=0. Orbit 772 (29 August 2026) is a comparison orbit with 65 quality-zero Fe XIV records. A quality bit describes whether the continuum came from another sequence iteration. It does not itself give the exact lag. The day-level samples and metadata links are fixed in [selection.json](selection.json), built from [orbit 795](orbit795.json.gz) and [orbit 772](orbit772.json.gz) by [select_pairs.py](select_pairs.py). Before loading science arrays, for each selected line file require that all its recorded wideband FITS parents exactly equal the earlier total-brightness product's parent set. The corresponding QUALITY, cycle IDs and file epochs must agree with the archived metadata.

In each orbit take the five equally spaced ranks in the eligible time-ordered set, including both endpoints (integer floor). Use only QUALITY=2 line images in orbit 795 and QUALITY=0 line images in orbit 772. Eligible examples have a before-donor and a following quality-zero total-brightness image bracketing the line DATE-AVG, each within 90 s. Reject mismatched lineage or geometry and report missing inputs without choosing substitutes. DATE-AVG is a representative exposure epoch from the merge, not a weighted per-pixel time.

## Counterfactual and masks

The release subtracts wideband intensity Wd from a calibrated narrowband intensity. Under the counterfactual, replace Wd with its linear interpolation at the line epoch between Wd and the following wideband image. Level-3 total brightness is a K-corona product, where the model F-corona was subtracted. For these short same-orbit pairs with common image geometry, the corresponding change in W is estimated by the difference between delivered K products. This assumes their modeled F-corona terms agree sufficiently. Keep fractional brightness statistics expressed relative to the donor K-corona brightness. Record the change of the solar angular radius as a check, but do not assert exact F-model cancellation.

If the line time is a fraction w of the interval from donor to following image, the signed change to the line is w times (donor K minus following K) in mean solar brightness. Convert the shift with the line FITS HISTORY conversion from MSB to photons/s/cm²/sr. Compute the new signed line value only at originally finite positive line pixels. A v03 NaN may be censored negative signal or missing coverage. Its unknown value is never treated as zero or restored. Require coincident positive finite K pixels. Retain the annuli 1.20–1.40 and 1.40–1.70 apparent solar radii from FITS solar center and scale. Within them use 30° sectors measured counterclockwise from positive helioprojective X toward positive Y. The radial cut avoids the nominal occulter edge. Mask NaN and saturation and report all denominators.

Measure the median and 95th percentile of the absolute K perturbation fraction, the fraction of originally positive line pixels that would become nonpositive, and the fraction still nonpositive after each of four ±0.5-pixel axis shifts of the following image. An interval with at least 5% such robust flips in either annulus is counted as material relative to this counterfactual, with recurrence defined as two or more sampled intervals. The same diagnostics apply to the quality-zero comparison. No pixel is an independent observing epoch, no population p-value is reported, and the comparison orbits differ in solar state and cadence. The synthetic static-pair and signed-change sanity tests are separate from data. The result cannot recover the true continuum, identify individual astrophysical artifacts, or establish a new line-emitting structure. Instrument ghosts, residual registration and the linear time assumption remain.
