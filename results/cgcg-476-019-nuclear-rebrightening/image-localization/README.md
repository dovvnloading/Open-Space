# Locating the 2023 variable light in difference images

In 19 ZTF difference images the variable source falls a median **0.122 arcsec** from the Pan-STARRS DR2 centre of CGCG 476-019 (individual distances 0.050–0.191 arcsec). Its median distance from the reported coordinates of SN 2025aghl is **1.681 arcsec** (1.553–1.827 arcsec). The sky position of an optical galaxy centre can depend on colour and morphology. The images support a nuclear location for the 2023 source. They cannot distinguish the central black hole from a stellar transient within the nuclear region. The SN 2025 comparison uses its reported coordinates and carries their astrometric uncertainty. [TNS 2025aghl](https://www.wis-tns.org/object/2025aghl) · [Pan-STARRS catalog at MAST](https://catalogs.mast.stsci.edu/panstarrs/)

We selected positive ZTF21abouxex alert detections in 2023 (59945 < MJD < 60310) with real/bogus deep score above 0.8, reported PSF uncertainty below 0.12 mag and a stored stamp. Across the two filters we retained the lowest-uncertainty exposure per integer MJD, and then the ten lowest-uncertainty nights in each band. We matched the IRSA direct photometry to obtain the exposure file fraction, detector and quadrant, and requested contrast-preserving, WCS-calibrated difference-image cutouts. The manifest records the URLs and the SHA-256 digest of the decompressed FITS response. One of twenty selected products returned HTTP 503 across retries. The packet covers ten g and nine r images. [ZTF image API](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/program_interface/ztf_api.html)

In each image the position fit uses an elliptical Gaussian, free centroid, and locally sloping background in a radius of 5.5 pixels around the Pan-STARRS centre. An outer annulus supplies the initial sky and noise scale. We apply the native distortion-corrected WCS to the fitted position and measure spherical offsets. The median offsets are +0.006 arcsec east and +0.058 arcsec north. The median radial distance differs from the length of the median offset vector because it includes exposure-to-exposure scatter. The pixels and alert positions derive from the same ZTF exposures.

Run `python centroids.py` here with NumPy, pandas, SciPy and Astropy. The two compressed arrays preserve the difference-image pixels and WCS used for these fits. Compare each row of `centroids.csv` to its source product in `manifest.csv`.
