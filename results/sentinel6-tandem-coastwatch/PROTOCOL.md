# Sentinel-6 tandem agreement in NOAA's near-real-time coastal archive

Initial protocol frozen on 18 November 2026 from Open-Space main 6160144f. Only archive directories, file names, HTTP headers, instrument documentation and station locations were inspected. Freeze schema decisions before inspecting measured values.

## Question and prior measurements

How closely do the Sentinel-6A and Sentinel-6B sea-level anomaly (SLA) and significant wave height (SWH) estimates agree in NOAA CoastWatch's recently added stream along the contiguous United States Pacific coast? Test whether additional mismatch appears near shore, and whether either mission shows a reproducible wave-height difference against the same in-situ records. The satellites use similar hardware and harmonized processing and fly about 30 seconds apart in tandem. The same water is the primary control for comparing reported values. Shared retrieval errors can affect both instruments.

NOAA added Sentinel-6B CoastWatch files from September 9, 2026. CNES already reports tandem calibration consistency with Sentinel-6A and SWOT. The 2026 Ocean Surface Topography Science Team meeting covers coastal wet-tropospheric corrections in the tandem phase. This study targets the downstream NOAA near-real-time product, specific coast, and observation dates. It cannot establish absolute sea-level accuracy or identify a correction as the cause. ## Frozen observational sample

- Native satellite-specific CoastWatch NetCDFs for September 9 through November 15, 2026 UTC. Preserve every expected day, missing inputs, content digests, URLs and native schema. Include UTC boundary dates in matching. Use September 9 through October 29 as reference, October 30–31 as transition days reported outside scoring, and November 1–15 as holdout. Keep raw downloads and scratch on D.
- Use 32°–49°N, 132°–116°W, on the Pacific side of the contiguous US. Preselect coastal distance bins of 20–80 km and 150–300 km to the Natural Earth 1:10m coastline. Report counts outside those bins. Limit SLA inference to paired support and keep unmatched rows for coverage accounting. The study is conditional on producer-edited records. The archive description mixes Level 2 along-track and Level 3 grid text. Verify native dimensions, units and times. A gridded or untimed sample would be inadequate.
- Match same-track records uniquely using time and surface distance before selecting on wave height or SLA. The primary difference is Sentinel-6B minus Sentinel-6A. Use 10–70 seconds with B following A and separation at most 5 km. Independently verify a symmetric window and tighter spatial match. Coastline binning uses the pair midpoint. Report the distribution of lag and separation. Define visits by per-pass metadata or time and large spatial gaps. One visit and one repeat of a track are the meaningful units. Test whether a one-day displaced control can falsely pass the geometry.
- Use valid values according to product masks and documented ranges. For each visit covering both bins, compute median absolute SLA difference within each bin and subtract offshore from near shore. Report visit medians, signed B-minus-A offsets, quartiles and supported visit counts. Use repeat or day blocks for uncertainty. Separately compare high and low wave-height subsets from the mean satellite SWH, at least 3 m versus below 2 m. This is descriptive because the satellite estimates also define the strata. A noteworthy near-shore candidate needs an excess above 1 cm in both periods, recurrent visits, and robustness to tighter pairing.
- The independent wave control uses fixed NDBC buoy IDs 46011, 46013, 46014, 46015, 46022, 46025, 46026, 46027, 46028, 46029, 46041, 46042, 46050, 46053, 46054 and 46069. Freeze the current standard meteorological files and positions. Preserve all stations including missing inputs. Match each satellite pair to the same valid buoy wave height within 30 minutes and 50 km. Aggregate once per station and overpass, and repeat within 25 km. Compare paired signed and absolute residuals. Report at least three stations and twenty supported buoy visits before claiming a difference in performance. Site, timing and footprint differences and automated quality status limit inference. Buoys independently measure wave height. Testing absolute coastal sea level would require other evidence.
