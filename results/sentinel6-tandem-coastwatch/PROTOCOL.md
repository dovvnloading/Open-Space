# Sentinel-6 tandem agreement in NOAA's near-real-time coastal archive

Initial protocol frozen on 18 November 2026 from Open-Space main 6160144f. Only archive directories, file names, HTTP headers, instrument documentation and station locations were inspected. Freeze schema decisions before inspecting measured values.

## Question and prior measurements

How closely do the Sentinel-6A and Sentinel-6B sea-level anomaly (SLA) and significant wave height (SWH) estimates agree in NOAA CoastWatch's recently added stream along the contiguous United States Pacific coast? Test whether additional mismatch appears near shore, and whether either mission shows a reproducible wave-height difference against the same in-situ records. The satellites use similar hardware and harmonized processing and fly about 30 seconds apart in tandem. The same water is the primary control for comparing reported values. Shared retrieval errors can affect both instruments.

NOAA added Sentinel-6B CoastWatch files from September 9, 2026. CNES already reports tandem calibration consistency with Sentinel-6A and SWOT. The 2026 Ocean Surface Topography Science Team meeting covers coastal wet-tropospheric corrections in the tandem phase. This study targets the downstream NOAA near-real-time product, specific coast, and observation dates. It cannot establish absolute sea-level accuracy or identify a correction as the cause. ## Schema and analysis choices frozen before opening values

- The daily products are CF-1.7 NetCDF trajectory files. They contain one time dimension, with UTC seconds since 1985-01-01, packed latitude, longitude, SLA, SWH and wind. No coast distance, native pass number or per-record edit flags are carried. Preserve mask and scaling on extraction. Use finite SLA, and finite nonnegative SWH, conditional on the producer edits. Deposit native attributes, including the asymmetry in SLA latency filtering. Do not try to identify the contributing upstream radar mode or derive reprocessing effects from these files.
- Use great-circle distances on a sphere of radius 6,371 km. Represent the regional Natural Earth coastline with points spaced at most 1 km. Compute pair separation and distance to the coast separately. Form one-to-one matches by increasing separation, then by closeness to the nominal lag of 30 seconds, with stable sample order as the last tie-break. Sensitivities use a 2 km tolerance or either ordering with absolute lag from 10 to 70 seconds. Displace Sentinel-6B timestamps by one day for the control.
- Define visits from chronological primary pairs by a gap over 90 seconds or 120 km. Require three paired samples in each coastal bin before comparing a visit, and at least five supported visits in each period before escalating. Assign periods by UTC pair midpoint. Bootstrap UTC days with both supported measurements, 5,000 draws, seed 6226, while retaining the visit as the statistic. Day blocks reduce dependence from sequential samples but can still share repeat-track and weather conditions.
- Use archived geographic buoy positions, not positions inferred from wave data. Match the pair centroid to the nearest valid timestamp at a fixed site. Preserve rolling availability and estimated distance. Require three paired satellite samples per station-visit and apply the same condition at 50 km and 25 km. The difference in absolute wave-height errors is based on paired per-visit medians.

The Sentinel-6B headers inspected during this schema pass add "-Qsla=latency -Llatency=0,1" to the extraction command. The Sentinel-6A examples have neither option. This receipt difference is sufficient to define a separate per-day coverage audit, and it does not identify which edit removed individual samples. Compare native row counts, paired coordinate coverage and one-day controls before interpreting the SLA test.
