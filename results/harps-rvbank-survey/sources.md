# Spectroscopic observations

The primary input is the CDS correction of 20 April 2026 to Table 4 of Perdelwitz et al. (2024). The catalogue describes 252,615 exposures of 5,239 targets observed before January 2022. Those are publication totals. The search coverage is the set of targets and epochs recorded by the run. The correction date refers to publication of the table, not the date of the last observation. [CDS catalogue and byte definitions](https://cdsarc.cds.unistra.fr/viz-bin/cat/J/A+A/683/A125) · [Perdelwitz et al.](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202348263) · [Trifonov et al.](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201936686)

The acquisition stage tries the Harvard and Strasbourg fixed-width mirrors, CDS’s original-file FITS conversion, paged VizieR ASU exports, and the Strasbourg TAP endpoint. Selecting CDS as the full time series requires the published row count. For a partial ASU catalogue, the reader checks each mirror’s catalogue metadata, requests its last record in descending order and probes from that record upward in ascending order. It verifies every retrieved record number from 1 through the observed endpoint exactly once. A truncated contiguous prefix is identified separately whenever the upper boundary cannot be verified. For each response the manifest records the requested URL, status, retrieval time, byte count and SHA-256. TAP and ASU hashes identify an exact serialized response and can vary between servers. [CDS mirror guidance](https://vizier.cds.unistra.fr/) · [VizieR ASU format](https://vizier.cfa.harvard.edu/doc/asu-summary.htx)

If those routes fail, the workflow uses Trifonov's public 3 March 2024 Ver.02b CSV distribution. This version predates the April 2026 CDS correction. The reader verifies the pinned Git blob, reads all CSV archive members using their headers and checksums, and reports the decoded exposure and target counts. It uses the author columns RV_mlc_nzp, e_RV_mlc_nzp, f_RV and TIMEID. The source and version used at execution are recorded in `ingest.json`. [Pinned author release](https://github.com/3fon3fonov/HARPS_RVBank/tree/09626a652ddc93df8771ce3d818ee04a292f7585)

When the complete CDS table is unavailable, the reader also checks any contiguous CDS Table 4 records against the full author release. Matching requires the same target, preserves component designations and uses a unique instrument timestamp within two seconds in BJD. A fallback requires a unique target and BJD within 0.022 seconds, with consistent timestamps if present. Duplicate associations and coordinate disagreements exceeding two arcseconds are rejected. All author exposures are retained. The comparison covers the RV, error, quality flag, diagnostic values, BJD and coordinates. The per-exposure change ledger includes numerical differences within the print precision as well as material changes. The workflow can use a changed finite measurement only when at least 95% of at least 20 global pairs agree within the stated numerical tolerance, and at least 95% and three pairs agree separately for that star and fibre era. Except for flags, three distinct matching values are required globally. Timing and coordinate changes are recorded only. Incompatible scales, ambiguous identifications and absent values retain the author measurements. The CDS history identifies a corrected Table 4 dated 20 April 2026 without itemizing changed fields. The comparison and coverage receipts cannot establish the update history of each mirror or certify uncovered epochs. See `correction-audit.json`, `correction-crossmatch.csv.gz` and `correction-changes.csv.gz`; the latter two are produced when a partial CDS table and the complete author release are available together.

The primary velocities are differential SERVAL RVs corrected for barycentric motion, secular acceleration, instrumental drift and nightly zero points. The reader uses DRVmlcnzp and e_DRVmlcnzp, both in m/s, and requires a finite BJD, finite RV, positive finite error and a zero SERVAL quality flag. It retains one measurement per target and BJD, choosing the smallest reported error if a pair repeats. The fibre split uses BJD 2457169, in the intervention gap identified by Trifonov et al. Separate zero points are essential because the upstream templates and corrections were derived separately. FWHM and BIS have units of km/s, CRX has units of m/s, and dLW is in (m/s)(km/s). H-alpha, S and R′HK are dimensionless. The time-resolved R′HK column and S-index column remain separate.

The S-index supplement uses Harada et al. (2025), SPORES-HWO II, Table 5. The reader retains positive S-indices on HARPS, HARPS-pre and HARPS-post exposures with consistent fibre-era labels. It normalizes HD prefix separators and zero padding while preserving stellar components. For alternate designations it also checks Table 3’s SIMBAD positions, requiring one survey target within two arcseconds and one SPORES source at that target. Both directions of the exposure match must have exactly one neighbour within two seconds in absolute BJD. The receipt `sindex-coverage.json` records the instrument labels and finite S counts, catalogue overlap, nearest-time distances before thresholding, and accepted associations. [SPORES-HWO II tables](https://cdsarc.cds.unistra.fr/viz-bin/cat/J/AJ/170/343)

For RVBank stars with no accepted SPORES S-index, the workflow checks the 35-star catalogue of Ibáñez Bustos et al. (2025). It uses positive, unflagged southern HARPS S-indices in Table B.1. A match requires the same stellar identifier or a unique J2000 position within two arcseconds. The published MJD must agree within one second with the UTC exposure start encoded in the HARPS filename, and matches must be unique in both directions. The workflow retains the chosen activity reduction per star. `ibanez-sindex-coverage.json` and `ibanez-sindex-matches.csv` give the counts and the exposure matches. [Ibáñez Bustos et al.](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202450348) · [CDS tables](https://cdsarc.cds.unistra.fr/viz-bin/cat/J/A+A/696/A230)

If both versions of the RVBank are unavailable, the survey uses the ESO Phase 3 HARPS RV catalogue DR1. Explicit `--source eso` selects the same ESO reduction for an RV-only holdout and skips the optional S-index supplement. This independent DRS reduction contains 289,843 archive records through May 2023. The reader identifies the documented table by its columns and row count. It uses the SIMBAD designation, drift-corrected CCF RV in km/s converted to m/s, and drs_dvrms in m/s. It selects high-accuracy mode and each star's most frequent CCF mask among valid exposures. It retains BIS and FWHM from the DRS. This release lacks the SERVAL activity products and the RVBank nightly zero-point correction. These differences limit activity and trend interpretation for a fallback run. Based on data obtained from the ESO Science Archive Facility with DOI [10.18727/archive/33](https://doi.org/10.18727/archive/33). [ESO column definitions](https://www.eso.org/qi/catalog/show/416)

A signal whose only remaining internal flag is missing S-index receives a targeted query for public DACE standard-processing Mount Wilson S measurements. The workflow requests the latest DRS and passing quality control, requires the southern HARPS high-accuracy observing mode and matches unique frame timestamps. When a timestamp is absent, the match must be unique in both directions within two seconds in barycentric time. Conflicting timestamps or stated fibre eras exclude the association. With 10 or more matched nights and a variable S-index, it repeats the RV search using DACE S alone, preserving the original flags and activity results. Periods, semiamplitudes, FAPs and injection/recovery counts must reproduce before it accepts the new diagnostic. The query URL, UTC, response checksum, match counts, and individual matched exposures are deposited in `sindex-receipts.csv` and `sindex-epochs.csv.gz`. Unavailable or insufficient DACE coverage leaves the original disposition in force. [DACE spectroscopy API](https://dace-query.readthedocs.io/en/latest/dace_query.spectroscopy.html)
