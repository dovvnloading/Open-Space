# RV search and selection function

## Population and time coverage

Read every named target in the ingest inventory. Record the input epoch count, accepted observing nights, number of nights in each fibre era and first-to-last baseline. A search requires at least 12 nights spanning at least 30 days. Use the integer part of barycentric Julian date to group exposures within each era. This day boundary falls in daylight at La Silla. Adopt the inverse-variance mean BJD and RV per night and the median of each available activity indicator. Adopt the larger of the propagated formal error and the unweighted within-night standard error. Add 1 m/s in quadrature for a nightly noise floor.

## RV model

At each trial frequency, solve weighted least squares jointly for a constant, linear drift, a post-upgrade offset when both eras are present, and sine and cosine terms. The search weights are inverse nightly variances. The periodogram is the fractional decrease in weighted residual sum of squares. Frequencies range from 1/baseline to 1/1.5 days in increments of 1/(5 × baseline). The first and last grid periods for each star are in the star ledger. The nuisance model follows the form of the general linear periodogram described by [Delisle, Hara & Ségransan (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201936905).

Refine the highest grid peak for every eligible star, including insignificant peaks. Fit an eccentric Keplerian within 0.8/baseline of the grid frequency and inside the search band. Fit eccentricity from 0 to 0.85. Vary frequency, eccentricity and mean anomaly at the first observation, solving the two orbital amplitudes and nuisance coefficients by weighted least squares at each iteration. Start from three eccentricity/phase combinations. Output the primary's RV convention, with equation v = K [cos(ν + ω) + e cos ω] plus the nuisance terms. The longitude of periastron ω is in radians and the periastron epoch is an absolute BJD. For nearly circular orbits the two phase parameters are degenerate.

Repeat the blind search up to three times if the previous component has bootstrap FAP ≤ 0.01, ΔBIC ≥ 10, converged, has eight or more independent time blocks under the noise model, and sufficient residual degrees of freedom. Previously detected components retain their nonlinear shape parameters. Both linear quadratures refit at every frequency. Significant activity and binary signals can enter this conditional model. This procedure can miss a companion masked by a stronger component, several interacting companions, eccentric periastron passages in observational gaps, and periods outside the band. The trend reported in m/s/year is conditional on the final set of included components. Its quoted error is the larger of a formal regression error and a leverage-corrected block sandwich estimate, excluding uncertainty in previously fitted orbital shapes.

## False alarms

Apply random signs by 14-day block to the fitted null residuals, separately within each era, and project out the nuisance terms again. Repeat the entire frequency scan for each of 255 realizations. The stored FAP is (1 + number of simulated maxima at least as large as the observed maximum)/(255 + 1). The resolution is 0.00390625. The statistic is the maximum GLS power before nonlinear refinement. The randomization assumes approximately sign-symmetric, independent blocks. Activity extending over several blocks, leverage from concentrated sampling, and fitted-null uncertainty can invalidate that approximation. The FAP is per star and conditional on prior components.

For a second screen under independent Gaussian errors with variances proportional to the nightly variances, compute the partial-F tail for adding two fixed-frequency quadratures and multiply by the number of grid frequencies. Cap the product at one to obtain `fap_gaussian_grid`. Multiply by the number of named targets and by three search rounds, again capping at one, to obtain `gaussian_trials_screen`. Require this value below 0.05 for an internally passing signal. This Bonferroni treatment applies to finite grids with fixed linear null models. Its interpretation on later, adaptively selected residual searches is conditional. Correlated noise can invalidate the Gaussian screen. ΔBIC uses a fitted common noise scale and a five-parameter penalty for period, amplitude, eccentricity and two orbital angles.

## Activity, sampling and stability

For FWHM, BIS, CRX, dLW, H-alpha and S-index, require at least 10 usable nightly measurements and finite variance. Apply the same tests to R′HK whenever available. For each time series, remove its era offsets and linear trend and calculate rank correlation with the contemporaneous RV series after removing prior components. Flag |ρ| ≥ 0.4 with Spearman p < 0.01/7. At the RV frequency and its half and double, fit an indicator sinusoid with offsets and trend. Flag partial-F p < 0.01/(3 × 7). Search a broad rotation-proxy band of 1.5 to 200 days, constrained by the indicator baseline, and record the highest indicator peak and its Gaussian grid-adjusted FAP. Flag 0.5, 1, 2 or 3 times a significant indicator frequency if it agrees with the RV to within 1/baseline. An activity peak can originate from instrumental systematics or a magnetic cycle. Literature rotation periods require a separate check. Indicator regressions use equal nightly weights; p values are screening diagnostics.

Measure the weighted spectral window through 2.05 cycles/day. Retain up to 16 highest local maxima of normalized window power ≥ 0.12 beyond two Rayleigh widths from zero. Flag an RV peak that coincides with a retained window line, or a competing peak ≥ 80% as strong, separated by more than one Rayleigh width, whose frequency sum or difference matches a window line within one Rayleigh width. For statistically selected signals, also refit the top three such rivals as Keplerians and record the BIC difference. Unexamined frequencies outside the search band can still alias into it. Require coverage of 1.5 fitted periods and a maximum gap below or equal to 0.35 cycles. Check the fixed-shape amplitudes in chronological halves and each sampled fibre era. Flag a missing or ill-conditioned split fit or a >3σ disagreement. An optimized eccentricity above 0.82 or failed convergence also prevents a pass.

All six required activity series must be testable to pass. `rv_status=pass` refers only to these internal RV tests. Celestial identification, rotation from the literature, known or proposed companions, stellar mass, binarity and astrometric information belong in the external adjudication. A rejected strongest peak remains in the signal ledger, including its provisional Keplerian parameters. Such a fit does not establish an orbit.

## Injection and recovery

For every eligible star, add synthetic Keplerian RVs to its actual nightly residuals after fitting the final conditional model. Test periods of 3, 10, 30, 100, 365.25, 1000 and 3000 days that fit within the star's baseline. Use K of 1, 3, 10, 30, 100 and 300 m/s, eccentricities of 0 and 0.35, and eight stratified random mean anomalies per cell with randomized arguments of periastron. Reuse the phases across amplitude values within a cell family. Draws use a SHA-256-derived seed for each star, independent of workflow sharding.

Each injection must produce the strongest blind GLS peak within the smaller of 1/baseline and 10% of the injected frequency. It must exceed the second-highest of the 255 null maxima computed on the final residual model, corresponding to the 1% screening threshold with the add-one convention. Count trials and successes, allowing an aggregate recovery fraction to be recomputed at any reported grid cell. Eight phases give coarse individual-star fractions. Recovery is conditional on the observed cadence and residuals, fitted components, finite injection grid and specified detection rule. Activity, literature and astrometric vetting have separate selection effects. The injection table measures sensitivity of the period search and does not estimate an occurrence rate.
