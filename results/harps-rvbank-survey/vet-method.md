# Companion identification and mass constraints

Every fitted RV signal receives a row in `vetting.csv`. Each row records the internal RV disposition, period, semiamplitude, eccentricity, search FAP, minimum mass where available, identity, catalog conflicts and missing checks. A missing indicator, rotation period, unreviewed paper or inaccessible catalog leaves an unresolved check. A catalog nondetection cannot establish novelty. The automatic search produces a literature-review shortlist. A discovery claim requires an individual comparison of the signal with published orbital solutions, candidate announcements, activity periods and other companions under the host’s aliases.

## Identity and prior signals

The search resolves each target with a fitted signal or significant RV trend through SIMBAD identifiers. It rejects name matches with a positional discrepancy greater than 30 arcseconds. If the name lookup fails, it accepts one SIMBAD object within 3 arcseconds of the input position. Crowding and epoch mismatches can leave targets unresolved. SIMBAD supplies aliases and object types. Exact Hipparcos and Gaia identifiers are required for astrometric inference. The literature ledger includes object-indexed papers with titles mentioning planets, brown dwarfs, companions, orbits, spectroscopic binaries, Doppler/RV studies, HARPS or rotation. It preserves bibcodes, titles and DOIs. Indexing delays, proceedings, preprints and signals mentioned only within broad surveys require additional per-target review. [SIMBAD](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/)

The NASA composite planet catalog and all published periods in its planetary systems table are searched alongside the Extrasolar Planets Encyclopaedia. Position matches within 4 arcseconds also flag potential conflicts in crowded fields. Composite stellar masses require matching identifiers. The direct-period tolerance is the larger of 1.2% in frequency and one Rayleigh interval, capped at 5% for the Rayleigh term. Half/double, solar and sidereal day, and annual aliases are retained as possible conflicts. Different periods on known hosts stay in the ledger. [NASA archive](https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/API_PS_columns.html) · [Extrasolar Planets Encyclopaedia](https://exoplanet.eu/API/)

The two 2025 SPORES-HWO tables provide prior periods with classifications and previously reported drifts, including stellar activity and candidate planets. The public HARPS-RVBank search by Pinho and the earlier Open-Space results supply additional prior leads. HD 32564 and the published HD 25673 drift are specifically marked. [SPORES-HWO II](https://vizier.cds.unistra.fr/viz-bin/VizieR?-source=J/AJ/170/343) · [Prior HARPS scan](https://github.com/rpinho/planet-hunt) · [Open-Space catalog](https://github.com/dovvnloading/Open-Space)

## Rotation, multiplicity and minimum masses

Published NASA rotation periods and the best Gaia DR3 rotational-modulation period are compared at one-third, one-half, one and two times each rotation period with a 12% fractional tolerance. Multiple periods and differential rotation can complicate that comparison. SIMBAD binary types, NASA stellar multiplicity, Gaia DR3 non-single-star solutions and RUWE above 1.4 are flagged. A high RUWE can arise from the substellar companion itself. Missing Gaia rows and ambiguous identifiers leave the binary check incomplete. [Gaia DR3 data model](https://gea.esac.esa.int/archive/documentation/GDR3/Gaia_archive/chap_datamodel/)

For an identified primary the search solves the exact binary mass function at inclination 90° for the minimum secondary mass. It uses the NASA stellar mass, or the Gaia FLAME mass if its quality flag is 00. FLAME assumes solar metallicity and can underestimate the uncertainty. Each mass retains its source. [Gaia FLAME documentation](https://gea.esac.esa.int/archive/documentation/GDR3/Data_analysis/chap_cu8par/sec_cu8par_apsis/ssec_cu8par_apsis_flame.html)

## Long-period companions

`accelerations.csv` records every absolute fitted RV slope of at least three formal slope errors and any Hipparcos–Gaia proper-motion anomaly. The likelihood uses the four components of Hipparcos and Gaia proper motions minus the long-baseline scaled position difference. It retains the catalog’s within-epoch RA/Dec covariance and the covariance shared by the two differences. The HGCA EDR3 edition uses the astrometric solution underlying Gaia DR3 and includes nonlinear-motion corrections. Inspect the RV slope and PMA at their respective epochs. A linear trend and a tangential anomaly can arise from different bodies. A trend without a resolved period does not determine mass or separation. For fallback ESO DRS RVs, unremoved perspective acceleration in nearby high-proper-motion stars can mimic a long-term trend. [Brandt (2021)](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4365/abf93c)

For a single plausible dark companion with an RV baseline covering at least 1.25 periods, a period of at least four years, FAP ≤0.01, ΔBIC ≥10, and joint four-dimensional PMA p<0.001, the code refits the nightly RVs, parallax and the four anomaly components. Published competing periods, secondary significant RV signals, cataloged multiplicity and missing binarity coverage prevent single-companion attribution. Elevated RUWE alone permits a diagnostic fit. The model fits a common Keplerian orbit, inclination, node and separate era offsets. It adds a robust fixed era-specific excess-noise term and propagates the catalog parallax error. It approximates each mission by uniform temporal sampling about its coordinate-dependent central epochs, with 3.25-year and 34-month windows. Orbital positions give the mission-averaged displacement, long-baseline motion and least-squares window slopes. Real scan times, scan-angle weights and photocenter shifts can differ. A remaining physically significant RV acceleration invalidates attribution to a single orbit.

The fit tries both inclination hemispheres and multiple nodes. It withholds an adopted mass for poor fits or boundary solutions. A 55-point log-mass profile plus the best-fit mass reoptimizes orbital elements, parallax and host mass under both inclination branches. It reports the envelope one grid step beyond the approximate 95% profile-likelihood set (Δχ² ≤3.84). A broad or unbounded set, competitive unaccounted optimum or upper bound reaching 80 Jupiter masses prevents adoption. Any reported dynamical mass is conditional on the single dark companion, white-noise RV model, uniform observing-window approximation and unimodal period solution. The mass profile is a diagnostic interval and lacks calibration against the actual Gaia scanning law. The survey FAP describes RV detection; the PMA p-value describes acceleration.

## Reproduction

Run `python vet.py --work "$WORK" --out "$OUT"` in the HARPS survey workflow after ingestion and RV search. Inputs are `$WORK/epochs.csv.gz` and `$OUT/{targets,stars,signals}.csv`. The stage records every attempted external query, endpoint, retrieval status, UTC timestamp and successful response SHA-256 in `catalog-inputs.csv`. It caches responses in the run workspace. Updated catalogs or revised bibliographies can change future answers. `literature.csv.gz` contains the indexed bibliography. Queries that fail twice are recorded, and three consecutive failures for a source stop further uncached requests to that source.
