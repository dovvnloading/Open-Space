# Literature adjudication of HARPS RV signals

These are individual comparisons for periodic signals that warranted a second look. The full RV and catalog dispositions are in the [fit ledger](revised/signals.csv) and [external checks](revised/vetting.csv). A previously announced candidate counts as prior work even when the confirmed-planet catalog omits it.

| RVBank series | Identification and published evidence | Disposition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HD5976 | TOI-261 c was announced with a 10.2-day period, alongside planets near 3.4 and 22.9 days. [Grziwa and the KESPRINT and CHEOPS teams, EPSC 2026](https://doi.org/10.5194/epsc2026-891) | The 10.285-day RV peak has a prior counterpart. |
| HIP112414 | Mayor et al. reported HD 215456 b near 192 days and c near 2,268 days. NASA lists these objects among prior candidates excluded from its confirmed catalog. [Mayor et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.2497) · [NASA](https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/removed_targets.html) | The 192.8-day and 2,258-day peaks recover previously announced periods. |
| GJ634.1 | Mayor et al. reported HD 150433 b at 1,096 days. [Mayor et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.2497) · [NASA](https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/removed_targets.html) | The roughly 1,011-day fit needs an explicit comparison with the published orbit. A separate companion is unestablished. |
| MCC150 | Tuomi et al. reported a 661-day candidate for GJ 3822 with a 514–778-day interval and poor phase coverage. A separate HARPS-N analysis reported a 25.15-day candidate. [Tuomi et al., Appendix A.64](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.04644) · [Pinamonti et al.](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202142828) | The 682.9-day fit lies inside the earlier candidate's reported period interval. |
| HD157338 | Rickman et al. detected HD 157338 B directly and fitted the orbit of this low-mass star with RVs and astrometry. [Rickman et al.](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202244633) | A proper-motion anomaly at this target cannot be assigned to a proposed additional substellar companion without accounting for the known stellar orbit. |
| GJ550 | Published HARPS solutions for HD 126525 b give 948.1 ± 22 days and 960.41 days. [Barbato et al., Table 15](https://boris-portal.unibe.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/009da830-c2c1-4fea-9cb4-243d39837200/content) | In a joint fit the inferred periods changed substantially with the bounds, and one eccentricity reached 0.80. The additional-body interpretation remains unresolved. |
| HD216770 | HD 216770 b has a published 118.45-day orbit. [NASA record and discovery reference](https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/overview/HD%20216770) | The joint comparison recovered 116.11 days for the known companion under both outer-orbit models. An extra term worsened both BIC and chronological predictions. |
| HD22049 | Chromospheric monitoring of ε Eridani measured rotation between 12.3 and 12.5 days in one observing season. [Primary spectroscopy study](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202245201) | The 12.455-day peak falls in the measured rotation range despite clearing the automated catalog-period screen. |
| TYC7971-207-1, TYC964-1432-1 and TYC11-1042-1 | The EBLM survey published 14.456-, 10.964- and 7.235-day eclipsing systems under the names J2040−41, J1630+10 and J0040+01. Published secondary masses are 0.165, 0.238 and 0.102 solar masses, respectively. [Individual records and paper links](survivor-review.csv) | All three RV periods belong to known stellar binaries. |
