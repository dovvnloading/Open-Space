# Later HARPS observations as a fixed-orbit test

The corrected RVBank release defines the discovery sample. For the separate prediction test, the ESO Phase 3 catalog supplies drift-corrected DRS velocities from dates after the latest dated exposure in the complete RVBank release, including flagged exposures. The cutoff is the next integer BJD, so the last discovery night cannot contribute. All earlier ESO measurements are excluded. Both catalogs use HARPS at La Silla. New photons provide a temporal test, but instrument-specific periodicities can recur. The broader ESO archive was searched separately during the preceding fallback run, so this is a fixed-prediction retrospective comparison. The holdout does not establish historical blinding, an independent instrument, or a planetary origin.

## Selection before comparing velocities

Select every RVBank fit with internal status `pass` or `inconclusive`, per-star FAP ≤0.01 and ΔBIC ≥10. Include every blind fit matching one of the seven prespecified published controls, even a below-threshold fit. Retain the disposition of all seven controls, including a discovery miss that supplies no predicted orbit. Every selected signal counts toward the multiplicity correction, including targets with no later observations. A source at the ESO position must match the discovery identifier or a SIMBAD alias assigned during discovery, and the positional match must be unique in both directions within 3 arcseconds. The name match preserves component suffixes. This procedure can miss high-proper-motion stars with catalog positions given at different epochs. Ambiguous identities are excluded.

Use the same ESO quality cuts, single CCF mask per star and weighted nightly binning as the survey. Later fits have independent zero points by fibre era and a free linear trend. Give other significant discovery components free signed amplitudes at their frozen phases and periods. Require at least 12 new nights, eight 14-day noise blocks, a baseline of one signal period and a maximum phase gap ≤0.35 cycles. Require a full-rank nuisance model and at least six observations beyond its dimension. Exclude a case if one block contains >40% of the residualized template information. These requirements limit sensitivity to long periods in the short ESO extension.

## Comparison to independent nights

The RVBank period, eccentricity, periastron direction, phase and semiamplitude define a signed waveform; the holdout never searches over them. Fit one signed amplitude at that phase as a diagnostic. Compare the exact predicted waveform to a zero-amplitude model, profiling the same linear nuisance terms independently for each. A positive `fixed_minus_null_improvement` means the fixed waveform reduces weighted squared residuals. The amplitude standard error uses the larger of a scatter-scaled formal uncertainty and a leverage-corrected, 14-day block sandwich error.

At the fixed phase, compare the observed signed score to blockwise sign reversals of the fitted null residuals. Enumerate all sign combinations for up to 16 blocks. Otherwise draw 65,535 reproducible random sign vectors using the SHA-256 of the target and fit index, with an add-one correction. The one-sided p-value assumes sign-symmetric, approximately independent blocks; long-lived stellar variability can violate that assumption. Apply Bonferroni over the entire selected family.

Propagate the discovery orbital uncertainties to the test dates using a local five-parameter Jacobian at the published fit. Vary frequency, log amplitude, the eccentricity vector and mean longitude at the mean discovery time. Marginalize over discovery offsets, drift and other fixed-shape amplitudes. Use the larger projected waveform error from scatter-scaled white and 14-day clustered covariance. If the model is ill-conditioned or the projected RMS uncertainty exceeds 35% of the testable waveform, record the comparison as ephemeris-limited. This local calculation conditions on a single period mode and the shapes of earlier components; disconnected aliases, orbit interactions and long-term evolution can remain.

The classification **fixed waveform corroborated on later dates** requires adjusted p ≤0.05, improved fit at the fixed discovery amplitude, and consistency of the signed later amplitude within 2.58 combined standard errors. A >3-standard-error amplitude discrepancy is a diagnostic specific to this prediction. Otherwise the result is inconclusive. A later-date repetition of an activity signal or a known companion remains a repetition of that signal. Discovery flags and separate catalog adjudications remain attached to each compared peak.

`holdout.csv` records measured checks, skipped targets and outcomes. `holdout-controls.csv` includes discovery-stage and later-date outcomes for the prespecified control periods. `holdout-nights.csv.gz` retains matched later nightly velocities, uncertainties, diagnostics and original ESO exposure identifiers. `holdout.json` identifies the release-wide BJD cutoff and SHA-256 digests of the inputs, including the ESO receipt file. The workflow rebuilds the ESO epoch table from its source; compare input receipts before interpreting a replay.
