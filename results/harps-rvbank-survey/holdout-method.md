# Later HARPS observations as a fixed-orbit test

The corrected RVBank release defines the discovery sample. For the separate prediction test, the ESO Phase 3 catalog supplies drift-corrected DRS velocities from dates after the latest dated exposure in the complete RVBank release, including flagged exposures. The cutoff is the next integer BJD, so the last discovery night cannot contribute. All earlier ESO measurements are excluded. Both catalogs use HARPS at La Silla. New photons provide a temporal test, but instrument-specific periodicities can recur. The broader ESO archive was searched separately during the preceding fallback run, so this is a fixed-prediction retrospective comparison. The holdout does not establish historical blinding, an independent instrument, or a planetary origin.

## Selection before comparing velocities

Select every RVBank fit with internal status `pass` or `inconclusive`, per-star FAP ≤0.01 and ΔBIC ≥10. Include every blind fit matching one of the seven prespecified published controls, even a below-threshold fit. Retain the disposition of all seven controls, including a discovery miss that supplies no predicted orbit. Every selected signal counts toward the multiplicity correction, including targets with no later observations. Use SIMBAD J2000 coordinates when an archive designation produced a unique name-based SIMBAD identity during discovery. Other targets retain their archive coordinates. A single ESO identifier must equal the discovery name or one of its established aliases, and that same object must be the only ESO source within 3 arcseconds. The reverse cone must contain only the discovery entry or entries sharing its verified SIMBAD object ID. Component suffixes and system designations are preserved. Source rows with conflicting aliases, close distinct catalog components, or irreconcilable positions remain unresolved. The ledger records which coordinate source was used, the comparison position and object ID. Coordinate epochs and high proper motion can still cause misses.

Independently fitted RVBank series sharing the same uniquely identified control host, such as GJ 302 and HD 69830, receive individual discovery and later-date dispositions. Report a control planet once if any series recovers its period above threshold. Conflicting SIMBAD identities exclude the association. These duplicate series are not independent control planets; every tested discovery template still contributes to the holdout multiplicity penalty.

Use the same ESO quality cuts, single CCF mask per star and weighted nightly binning as the survey. Later fits have independent zero points by fibre era and a free linear trend. Give other significant discovery components free signed amplitudes at their frozen phases and periods. Require at least 12 new nights, eight 14-day noise blocks, a baseline of one signal period and a maximum phase gap ≤0.35 cycles. Require a full-rank nuisance model and at least six observations beyond its dimension. Exclude a case if one block contains >40% of the residualized template information. These requirements limit sensitivity to long periods in the short ESO extension.

## Comparison to independent nights

The RVBank period, eccentricity, periastron direction, phase and semiamplitude define a signed waveform; the holdout never searches over them. Fit one signed amplitude at that phase as a diagnostic. Compare the exact predicted waveform to a zero-amplitude model, profiling the same linear nuisance terms independently for each. A positive `fixed_minus_null_improvement` means the fixed waveform reduces weighted squared residuals. The amplitude standard error uses the larger of a scatter-scaled formal uncertainty and a leverage-corrected, 14-day block sandwich error.

At the fixed phase, compare the observed signed score to blockwise sign reversals of the fitted null residuals. For up to 32 nonzero block scores, compute the exact tail by enumerating the two halves of the sign combinations and counting sums by binary search. This preserves all signs without allocating the full joint array. Beyond 32 blocks, use reproducible random sign vectors and an add-one correction. Set the number of draws before comparing outcomes to max(65,535, ceil(64 × family size / 0.05) – 1). At the family threshold the simulations then have at least 64 expected exceedances. Random seeds derive from the SHA-256 of the target and fit index.

Record the resampling mode, effective block count, exact pattern count or Monte Carlo draws, exceedances, and minimum attainable unadjusted and adjusted p-values. With nine effective blocks, a one-sided exact test has at most 512 distinct sign patterns, so a family of 258 has a best possible adjusted p of at least 258/512. More draws cannot improve that limit. For exact tests the reported floor also counts ties at the maximum score within numerical tolerance. For Monte Carlo tests the floor includes the add-one resolution and the finite sign-pattern bound. A comparison lacking enough independent patterns can establish no family-significant repetition under the adopted criterion. 

The one-sided p-value assumes sign-symmetric, approximately independent blocks; long-lived stellar variability can violate that assumption. Apply Bonferroni over the entire selected family.

Propagate the discovery orbital uncertainties to the test dates using a local five-parameter Jacobian at the published fit. Vary frequency, log amplitude, the eccentricity vector and mean longitude at the mean discovery time. Marginalize over discovery offsets, drift and other fixed-shape amplitudes. Use the larger projected waveform error from scatter-scaled white and 14-day clustered covariance. If the model is ill-conditioned or the projected RMS uncertainty exceeds 35% of the testable waveform, record the comparison as ephemeris-limited. This local calculation conditions on a single period mode and the shapes of earlier components; disconnected aliases, orbit interactions and long-term evolution can remain.

The classification **fixed waveform corroborated on later dates** requires adjusted p ≤0.05, improved fit at the fixed discovery amplitude, and consistency of the signed later amplitude within 2.58 combined standard errors. A >3-standard-error amplitude discrepancy is a diagnostic specific to this prediction. Otherwise the result is inconclusive. A later-date repetition of an activity signal or a known companion remains a repetition of that signal. Discovery flags and separate catalog adjudications remain attached to each compared peak.

`holdout.csv` records measured checks, skipped targets and outcomes. `holdout-controls.csv` includes discovery-stage and later-date outcomes for the seven prespecified control planets. `holdout-control-series.csv` gives the individual series, including misses and below-threshold fits. `holdout-nights.csv.gz` retains matched later nightly velocities, uncertainties, diagnostics and original ESO exposure identifiers. `holdout.json` identifies the release-wide BJD cutoff and SHA-256 digests of the inputs, including the ESO receipt file. The workflow rebuilds the ESO epoch table from its source; compare input receipts before interpreting a replay.
