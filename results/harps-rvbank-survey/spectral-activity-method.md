# Instrumental Ca II H&K follow-up

This check revisits the spectra behind RVBank measurements when the sole unresolved internal RV flag is a missing S-index. It selects statistically significant signals with no cataloged period/alias conflict, rotation-period conflict, binary flag, or stellar minimum mass. Missing external coverage stays unresolved. HD 17970 has first priority in this targeted follow-up. Order additional stars by the discovery ΔBIC, with target name and fit index as tie breakers. At most three hosts and 24 nights per host enter spectral retrieval. This selected diagnostic uses the original HARPS exposures, so it provides no independent-date confirmation.

Start with the ESO high-accuracy, accepted-mask exposure ledger. Require exactly one matching component-preserving ESO identifier among the target names and vetted aliases. Match BJD in both directions within two seconds, verify the same fibre era, and compare the FITS start UTC with the RVBank exposure timestamp to one second. Also check FITS DRS BJD where supplied. Use at most one exposure per night, choosing the smallest reported RV uncertainty. Before looking at flux, sample by uniformly spaced chronological ranks. When both eras have at least ten nights, allocate about half to each; otherwise use the better sampled era. Retain archive product identifiers and download digests as well as unsuccessful and rejected products.

The ESO primary product contains a wavelength-calibrated, order-merged, uncalibrated flux spectrum from the stellar fibre. Its ERR vector is a dummy. Use the DRS barycentric air wavelengths, converting the recorded wavelength unit to Å, and shift into the stellar rest frame using the absolute DRS RV of the same exposure. Reject disagreement with the expected frame, wavelength coverage, exposure identity or an explicit DRS drift failure. Reject nonfinite flux, substantial gaps, strongly nonpositive flux, and a blue-continuum median below eight times the local point-to-point difference noise estimate in either continuum window. The continuum statistic is an empirical quality diagnostic. [ESO HARPS Phase 3 release, §Data Format](https://www.eso.org/rm/api/v1/public/releaseDescriptions/72) · [ESO HARPS DRS wavelength scale](https://www.eso.org/sci/facilities/lasilla/instruments/harps/doc/manual/HARPS-UserManual2.3.pdf)

Compute the wavelength-weighted mean flux in triangular 1.09 Å FWHM filters at 3933.664 and 3968.470 Å and rectangular 20 Å windows centered at 3901.070 and 4001.070 Å. Record all four mean fluxes, their ratio (H+K)/(V+R), and V/R for color-throughput sensitivity. Matching order throughput and wavelength response across observing seasons is uncertain. The ratio has no Mount Wilson calibration. The instrument’s merged-order flux normalization can evolve. [Bandpass definition](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202039765)

Within each era independently, require at least ten nights, one full fitted period of time coverage, and a maximum phase gap of 0.40 cycles. Remove an intercept and a linear time trend from the simultaneous RV and activity series. Record Spearman correlation between their residuals, correlation with color, and partial-F tests for sinusoidal modulation of the activity ratio at the RV frequency, its half and its double. Flag |ρ|≥0.4 at p<0.01/7 or a sinusoid at p<0.01/21. These are screening probabilities conditional on the selected target, frequencies, and approximate noise model. Correlated activity, sparse phase coverage, noise in the uncalibrated blue flux, residual throughput variations and unmeasured rotation periods limit inference. All fitted component, nightly-exposure and archive-coverage checks remain necessary before any candidate promotion. A negative test leaves the formal S-index check unresolved.

The workflow's `spectral-activity-epochs.csv` retains input receipts, four-band fluxes, simultaneous RVs and quality dispositions. `spectral-activity-targets.csv` retains per-era tests and insufficient-data outcomes. Based on data obtained from the ESO Science Archive Facility, DOI [10.18727/archive/33](https://doi.org/10.18727/archive/33).
