# Spectroscopic results

The November 2026 public LAMOST DR12 v2.0 low-resolution white-dwarf table contains **zero objects with raw tangential velocity ≥1,000 km/s among 9,965 exact Gaia DR3 matches with parallax S/N ≥5 and RUWE <1.4**. Its maximum is 542.0 km/s (Gaia 89617795461596544). None of the 20 comparison stars from the three survivor/remnant families appears in the LAMOST WD table under its Gaia DR3 identifier. This catalogue count places no useful constraint on the space density of supernova survivors.

## LAMOST parent and denominator

| Stage | Number |
|---|---:|
| Published LRS WD-table spectral records | 18,286 |
| Records with a positive published Gaia DR3 ID | 17,713 |
| Distinct positive IDs, all retrieved from Gaia DR3 | 13,850 |
| Available parallax, positive uncertainty, proper motion and RUWE | 13,721 |
| Positive parallax among those solutions | 13,448 |
| Parallax S/N ≥5 and RUWE <1.4 | 9,965 |
| Raw tangential speed ≥500 / ≥1,000 km/s in that subset | 2 / 0 |

The numerator is a measurement on the observed catalogue. A target can be absent because of targeting, sky coverage, classification, failure to match to Gaia or failure of the astrometric cuts. The 573 observations with missing IDs were excluded. The statistic uses catalogue parallax before zero-point correction. Taking the parallax down by 1.645 quoted standard errors at fixed proper motion moves the largest value to 757.4 km/s. This sensitivity calculation omits systematics and correlations. The Gaia-first search in the sibling directory treats zero points, Bailer-Jones distances, correlations and source integrity explicitly.

Inside this directory run `python lamost_parent.py` (standard library). The code verifies the full LAMOST release file against SHA-256, sorts distinct IDs as integers, runs 40 ordered TAP batches, checks for duplicates and records every result hash and missing ID. `lamost_high_velocity.csv` lists the broader tail at S/N ≥3 and ≥500 km/s, including rows that fail the tighter astrometry gate. The associated visit IDs are in `lamost_high_visits.csv`; their pipeline redshifts were not used. [LAMOST DR12 catalogue](https://www.lamost.org/dr12/v2.0/catalogue) · [International release notes](https://lamost.org/dr12/v2.0/doc/release_note)

## DESI positive control

J1109+0001 (Gaia DR3 3804182280735442560) has a barycentric Mg I line shift of **+96.5 ± 7.5 km/s** in the DESI DR1 coadd. Keck LRIS/VLT X-shooter spectroscopy gives +100 ± 10 km/s in the literature. Individual DESI spectra give +100.0 ± 11.2, +79.7 ± 11.9, and +98.1 ± 10.3 km/s. The first two visits were on 2022-01-14 and the third on 2022-02-02. The coadd is derived from those same exposures and is not an independent fourth measurement. This reproduces a **known** LP 40-365 spectrum. [Swan et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stag1701) · [Warwick spectral archive](https://cygnus.astro.warwick.ac.uk/phsdaj/WhiteDwarfs/) · [El-Badry et al. 2026, Tables 2 and 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11293)

`python fit_control.py --cache .cache` retrieves the three DESI arms for all three epochs and their coadd in one release tarball. Every input has a hash and pixel count in `spectrum_receipt.json`. NumPy and SciPy are required. We fit local linear continua and Gaussian absorption lines with individual depths and widths, zero mask and positive inverse variance, using fixed 26 Å windows. Four Mg I rest wavelengths (4167.271, 4351.906, 4702.991, 5528.405 Å in air) share a relativistic Doppler shift. Rest wavelengths are converted to the DESI vacuum system before fitting. Three starting velocities search the bounded ±350 km/s interval. This interval is appropriate only for this radial-velocity control. A D6 discovery fit must allow shifts of several thousand km/s. Error bars use the linearised fit covariance multiplied by max(1, reduced χ²) before taking standard deviations. They omit atmospheric line shifts, gravitation and calibration systematics. [NIST Mg I](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/Handbook/Tables/magnesiumtable2.htm) · [Mg I laboratory wavelengths](https://academic.oup.com/view-large/16987146) · [DESI wavelength reference](https://data.desi.lbl.gov/doc/releases/dr1/)

The Mg I 4703, Ca II K, O I triplet at 7772–7775 and Si II 6347 windows give pseudo-equivalent widths of 1.80 ± 0.38, 3.17 ± 0.41, 3.05 ± 0.68 and 2.67 ± 0.56 Å respectively. The shorter-wavelength Si II window has much stronger absorption than the 6371 window. At fixed Mg velocity each uses direct integration, a local linear continuum with propagated slope/intercept uncertainty, and windows padded by 7 Å. These are flux deficits in potentially blended regions. Ca H/K fitted on their own give +43.3 ± 11.4 km/s, so those resonance lines alone are a poor estimator of this star’s photospheric velocity. Interstellar material, blends and pressure shifts are possible contributors. Hα is −0.57 ± 0.59 Å, Hβ is 0.81 ± 0.61 Å. The negative He I window is susceptible to neighbouring features in its continuum windows. Other features and per-line fits are retained in the output, including weak or negative ones. These line-window measurements do not constrain a photospheric abundance vector without atmosphere, temperature and pressure modelling. [NIST oxygen](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/Handbook/Tables/oxygentable2.htm) · [NIST calcium](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/Handbook/Tables/calciumtable2.htm)

## Gaia-first targets

All nine of the 12 astrometric hits with a published XP spectrum have a calibrated 41-bin optical spectrum archived in `xp_sampled.csv`. Seven of the nine are outside the published comparison set. Gaia GSP-Phot gives 4,933–5,946 K and log g of 4.54–4.75 for these seven under its stellar model. These are conditional on the model and priors. Known D6-1 itself receives a normal-star GSP-Phot gravity, illustrating the danger of classifying a survivor from a pipeline value or colour alone. `xp_apsis.csv` contains the catalogue outputs.

For the highest-priority distance-sensitive object, Gaia 6094201393481158656, mean flux per unit wavelength at 430–480 nm divided by that at 680–730 nm is **0.885**. A 20,000 K Planck continuum behind the full mapped Galactic dust column, E(B−V)=0.1134, gives 3.47. Reproducing the observed ratio with that continuum requires E(B−V)=1.02 for CCM89, R(V)=3.1. Its Gaia normal-star fit gives 4933 K, log g 4.57, [M/H] −1.55. These results disfavor a hot, featureless continuum under the mapped sightline extinction. They cannot exclude a cool D6/LP40 remnant, unusual line blanketing or additional local extinction. There is no atmospheric abundance or RV measurement from XP here. Run `python xp_shape.py` to reproduce the flux ratios and reddening comparison. [GAVO sampled spectra](https://dc.g-vo.org/tableinfo/gdr3spec.spectra) · [Gaia XP calibration](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202243880) · [IRSA Galactic Dust Reddening and Extinction](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/applications/DUST/)

Direct survey spectroscopy identifies two additional ordinary F-type spectra among the nine astrometric alternatives. For Gaia 2588254701112130048, SDSS-V DR20 BOSS (a spectrophotometric standard), legacy SDSS and LAMOST DR12 classify it as F0 IV, F5 and F0 respectively. Independent Balmer-core fits in these spectra give −7 ± 7, −3 ± 8 and −5 ± 8 km/s. SDSS legacy and LAMOST independently show Hα pseudo-equivalent widths of 2.97 ± 0.17 and 3.03 ± 0.20 Å. For Gaia 297028668197924352 a legacy BOSS spectrum has an F3/F5 V pipeline template and Hβ absorption of 3.02 ± 0.16 Å. Its measured Hβ shift is −186 ± 6 km/s. The one-line formal error omits atmospheric, wavelength and profile systematics. The spectra support normal F-star classifications over H-deficient remnants. Neither star passes the final Gaia velocity confidence requirement.

Run `python fit_screen_spectra.py` with Astropy, SciPy and NumPy. The script downloads the four matched FITS products in `screen_spectra_receipt.json`, retains unflagged valid pixels and fits the Hγ/Hβ/Hα cores with local Gaussians as above. A feature needs 12 valid pixels across its window. Flux integration uses individual pixel widths on the logarithmic wavelength grid. Missing EW values denote insufficient coverage of the line/continuum windows. The pipeline subclasses come directly from the input headers/tables.

## Coverage and release scope

`python coverage.py` queries every object in the union of the 12 loose Gaia astrometric hits and 20 literature controls (29 unique sources). It tests an epoch-2016-centred 15 arcsec cone. This radius admits their proper-motion offsets over the survey span and unrelated neighbours. All returned records, their angular positions or archive-provided offsets, URLs, byte counts and hashes are preserved in `coverage.jsonl`. LAMOST DR11 and DR12 LRS and MRS, SDSS-V DR19 and DR20, the SDSS legacy archive, and DESI DR1 are separate queries. A Valis catalogue row can mean a target record with a legacy spectrum only. For J0546+0836 the Valis match is 12.7 arcsec away and must not be attributed to that control. The two matched DESI controls are J1109+0001 and the related J1240+6710 object. Survey counts overlap by release. A successful empty cone is local survey coverage information, not a constraint on astrophysical type. TAP returns catalogue records rather than an inventory of every independent exposure ever taken. No exhaustive inventory of individual-observatory holdings or every external archival spectrum is claimed.

DESI DR1 is the newest public **spectral** release. Underlying DR2 spectra are still withheld although DR2 science products already exist. SDSS DR20 adds southern and northern optical BOSS spectra and carries the DR19 APOGEE data forward. LAMOST DR12 v2.0 became public in September 2026. Additional positive-control and novelty references include the 2026 systematic Gaia follow-up, the 2026 LP40 discovery J1022−3414, and the 2025 study of SDSS J1637+3631.
