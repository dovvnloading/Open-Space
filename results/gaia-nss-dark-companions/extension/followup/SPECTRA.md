# UCAC4 720-033289 has room for a luminous companion

Actual DR11 MRS spectra of the 470-day binary allow substantial early-type companion light. Four phase-selected coadds give 12–33% local hot-star continuum around Hα across the tested models. In the blue arm, a narrow-lined hot template gives 7–13% apparent light at the predicted secondary velocities. These conditional fits, combined with the coeval luminous-companion SED fits, preclude a claim of darkness. Neither result establishes that the hot spectrum follows an independently fitted second orbit.

**Hα wings.** We directly retrieved four vacuum-wavelength, heliocentric coadds with obsids 764807168 / 719508155 and 776307168 / 707107168. In each pair the visible star was measured on opposite sides of its systemic velocity. For a comparison covering 6526–6602 Å we normalized the observation and shifted templates using two flanking continuum bands. We fitted the wings at 2.5–16 Å from Hα (vacuum rest wavelength 6564.61 Å), excluding pixels where either giant template predicts a metal-line blend deeper than 6%. Each spectrum contributes 130–136 wing pixels. The PHOENIX grid spans a 4800 K, [M/H] = −0.5 giant and a 5000 K, solar-metallicity giant, each at log g = 2.5. The candidate hot companion has 10,000 or 12,000 K, log g = 4.0, solar abundance and projected rotation of 15 or 150 km/s. We adopted R = 7500 and varied both the giant template and the continuum percentile (85th or 95th). The assumed secondary RV follows the primary in antiphase with mass ratio M1/M2 = 1.25 and systemic velocity −51.526 km/s.

The median best-fit hot fractions are 17%, 24%, 18% and 17% for the four spectra (in the obsid order above). Across 64 model/epoch choices the median wing RMS decreases from 4.2% of the continuum in the giant-only model to 2.6% with free companion light. These are descriptive RMS values. Giant atmospheric parameters, Hα line formation, continuum normalization and template mismatch can alter wing strengths. The model range is not a confidence interval. **Antiphase spectral search.** Independently measured blue-arm shifts between the observations are +33.3 and +25.4 km/s. We use the line-difference procedure documented in the [HD 264291 experiments](../../vetting/README.md), applied to synthetic hot-star spectra. For slowly rotating models (15 km/s) the fitted local blue fractions are 12–13% and 7–8% across the two epoch pairs, exceeding the empirical 5–8% threshold from 54 off-systemic control velocities. At 150 km/s projected rotation the same thresholds rise to 27–42%. Injecting a broad-lined star at 15% of the local continuum recovers it in only 9–22% of trials at the specified control thresholds. The maxima in a search over ±150 km/s drift between pairs, including 3–33 km/s from prediction even for the slowly rotating case. Shared absorption in the visible star, broadening, and primary-alignment systematics can give responses near the target velocities. The blue-arm fractions and Hα fractions refer to distinct local wavelengths. [Injection and velocity results](hot_ccf.json)
