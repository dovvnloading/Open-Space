# Individual timing features seen by both arrays

**Three pulsars had a narrowband threshold crossing that also passed the fixed-epoch EPTA directional test.** J1012+5307 had 45 of 367 primary crossings pass that test, J1713+0747 had 157 of 240, and J1909−3744 had 73 of 234. These are counts of overlapping templates. In 3, 70 and 18 fits respectively, every check before the final residual-noise test passed. All 91 failed that final test. All three external reductions used EPTA DR2full. [All individual dispositions](evidence/individual-dispositions.csv)

**J1713+0747.** Both neighborhoods coincide with published timing dips accompanied by pulse-profile changes. Around the first dip, at MJD 54750–54757, the blind search placed the 60-day tail at 54731. Removing the narrowband visit at 54765 reversed its sign (+0.264 µs, two-sided p = 0.291). Wideband had no observing day in the 60 days immediately after 54731. Its fitted direction also reversed. At the separately fixed MJD 54750, narrowband had two observing days on either side within 60 days. Day-level leverage and sparse cadence limit how precisely the blind timing fit can locate the onset. [Individual record](evidence/single/J1713+0747.json) · [Fixed-epoch measurements](evidence/known-event-controls.csv) · [Published pulse-profile analysis, §IV.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05453v1)

Near the second dip at MJD 57510, the MJD 57514.5 fits in the table passed the red-noise/DM refit, most-influential-day deletion, wideband and EPTA checks. The 14-day dispersive tail also appeared in the wideband DM measurements, with ΔDM = (−2.33 ± 0.98)×10⁻⁴ pc cm⁻³, directional p = 0.0087. Its ν⁻² component survived a simultaneous fit of frequency exponents 0, −2 and −4 (partial p = 0.00022). The 60-day profile-like tail had an ambiguous ν⁻⁴ component (partial p = 0.105). EPTA amplitudes were more negative, differing by 5.1 and 8.1 combined formal standard errors respectively. After subtracting either template, narrowband residual outliers exceeded all 256 projected-noise simulations (p = 1/257). The profile-like fit also failed the lag test. Several temporal models respond to this known event. Timing measurements alone do not identify a unique pulse-shape or propagation mechanism. [Fits and noise diagnostics](evidence/single/J1713+0747.json)

**J1012+5307.** Its strongest narrowband fit that reached the final noise test was the 14-day Doppler-shaped response at MJD 58196.5. EPTA supported the sign but its coefficient was smaller by 7.27 combined formal standard errors. The independent frequency components were ambiguous, and the post-fit outlier test gave p = 1/257. A compact-object interpretation would require a consistent physical amplitude and a better residual-noise fit. [Individual record](evidence/single/J1012+5307.json)

**J1909−3744.** Its strongest fit reaching the final test was a dispersive recovery at MJD 56617. Direct wideband dispersion measurements gave ΔDM = (+1.95 ± 0.53)×10⁻⁴ pc cm⁻³, directional p = 0.00011. EPTA supported the same sign with a smaller coefficient. This repeated frequency-dependent structure remains inconclusive as a discrete event because subtracting the tail left an outlier p = 1/257. [Individual record](evidence/single/J1909-3744.json)

**J1643−1224 provides a sensitivity check.** At its published MJD 57074 profile event, the 60-day narrowband profile-like tail gave −0.107 ± 0.242 µs (fixed two-sided p = 0.657), with no matching blind crossing within 30 days. The published pulse-shape response strengthens toward higher frequencies. The timing template's ν⁻⁴ response gives greater weight to lower frequencies. The calibrated limits apply to the specified timing waveforms and frequency laws.
