# Array coherence checks after the blind search

The original array survivors motivated these checks. Every fit remains in the original trial table. The checks ask whether unusually strong alignments survive whole-pulsar randomization, a previously defined noise screen and separate telescope reductions. The one-based `survivor_ordinal` in each output identifies the row in the original array summary's `survivors` list.

## Whole-pulsar randomization

One draw assigns each narrowband pulsar a sign and applies it to its entire residual series. The same sign applies to all times, receiving systems, temporal templates and sky positions for that pulsar. The operation preserves each pulsar's cadence, extreme values, noise diagnostics, and observed time and frequency correlations. A common reversal leaves all statistics invariant, so the first sign is fixed. The recorded seed generates 4,095 independent draws with replacement.

For each original eligible template, the code reconstructs the projected scalar scores, GLS contrast and its conservative covariance. It checks that the measured statistic agrees with the archived trial. Sign flips multiply the pulsar scores while leaving the per-pulsar residual sums of squares, spatial covariance and contrast weights fixed. A draw scores the largest negative base-10 log chi-square tail among all eligible templates. The tail conversion makes the one-, two- and three-component searches comparable. The tail ranks fits here even when its Gaussian interpretation is imperfect.

Each surviving fit is compared with the maximum from every draw. A Monte Carlo family-wise p-value is (exceedances + 1) / 4,096. The bounds in the events table are exact binomial intervals for the exhaustive randomization tail conditional on the measured data. A Bonferroni allocation jointly covers the full and subset tail calculations for all survivors plus both bank maxima at 95%. The procedure can be replayed with the per-draw maxima.

Independent sign reversals are a valid randomization null if the joint residual distribution is invariant under them. Cross-pulsar correlations, including a stochastic Hellings–Downs signal or shared calibration errors, can invalidate that assumption. A passing result indicates alignment across pulsars. It cannot distinguish a deterministic gravitational-wave burst from a coherent systematic or background fluctuation.

## Pulsar and telescope checks

The subset bank intersects the original bank with pulsars passing the previously declared single-pulsar outlier and lag diagnostics before event subtraction. Those screens are invariant under a whole-pulsar sign change. Each subset template still requires at least eight eligible pulsars and identifiable geometry. The subset preserves the original conservative white-scale allocation. Its sign maxima are recomputed across the entire eligible subset bank. Fixed-template parametric probabilities are also reported with the original full-bank trial count; data-dependent pulsar selection makes them diagnostic. The combined robustness flag requires the 95% upper Monte Carlo bounds for both banks to be at or below 0.005, the subset's nominal array-wide probability to be at or below 0.005, and its amplitude projected along the original discovery vector to retain the original sign. This is a post-discovery check.

The pulsar table decomposes the fitted amplitude **along the observed vector direction**. These signed contributions sum to the norm of the discovery amplitude, including for a negative clock coefficient. Individual pulsar delays and predictions are in microseconds (or microseconds per year for a ramp). The system table partitions each pulsar's scalar product between projected, whitened data and template by the backend of its input row. The contribution is evaluated over the entire series; a separate column counts actual neighboring observing days. Shared nuisance fits couple different rows. The backend partition is an accounting identity, whereas rerunning the fit with just one telescope directly tests dependence on that instrument.

Telescope checks repeat the timing/noise projection and local cadence cuts using only that telescope's reduced rows. They fit the original family, time dependence, discovery sky and signed vector and compare a clock response at the same epoch against a dipole nuisance. EPTA and NANOGrav narrowband each have telescope-specific fits; all three releases have combined fits. Sparse or rank-deficient subsets retain explicit statuses. Telescope-only projections can have different sensitivity. The original wideband processing shares the narrowband observations.

The events table gives the original EPTA directional probability, Holm-adjusted and Bonferroni-adjusted for all internally surviving original templates, including unavailable tests as p = 1. It also gives the EPTA amplitude minus the original narrowband amplitude along the discovery direction with matched units. Their difference divided by their errors added in quadrature is descriptive. Both releases share an ephemeris and can share stochastic timing signals. The blind selection can also amplify the discovery estimate. Inspect both the amplitude and the adjusted directional probability for external corroboration.

Outputs are `events.csv`, `pulsars.csv`, `systems.csv`, `telescope_checks.csv`, `null-maxima.csv`, and `summary.json` under the directory supplied to `--out`.
