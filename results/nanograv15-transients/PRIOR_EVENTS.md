# Published events and observational checks

These published epochs were collected for interpreting threshold crossings. They do not enter the blind template bank, change the detection threshold, or supply injections. An onset and the maximum of a finite-width template can differ. Compare the surrounding observing dates, residual shape, band dependence and observing systems before assigning a counterpart.

Arecibo had a malfunctioning local oscillator during MJD 57984–58447. Both archives' observation windows and the distribution of Arecibo, Green Bank and European telescope participation matter for apparent steps near either boundary. The nine-pulsar NANOGrav profile study also publishes lower-ranked candidates in its Appendix A, with separate entries for interpulses. [Observing cuts and full candidate list](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05453v1)

The NANOGrav 15-year search for compact substructure tested Doppler and Shapiro delays and reported limits on the abundance of compact objects under its population assumptions. A transient timing coefficient in microseconds requires an explicit geometry, velocity distribution, mass model and selection function to yield a corresponding abundance constraint. [NANOGrav new-physics search, §6](https://doi.org/10.3847/2041-8213/acdc91)

## Reading coherent timing fits

The published NANOGrav Earth-term memory search marginalized its limit at each epoch over sky direction and polarization. Figure 2 places the 95% upper limit at a few times 10⁻¹⁴ around MJD 57810 (read from the plotted curve). Figure 4 instead averages over a window of epochs for each sky position. A limit conditioned on a specific epoch, propagation direction and polarization would be required for a direct exclusion at that grid point. [Agazie et al., Figures 2 and 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.18599)

The NANOGrav 15-year narrowband and wideband releases share telescope clocks, receivers and backend transitions. At Green Bank the transition from GASP to GUPPI began in 2010. At Arecibo the transition from ASP to PUPPI began in 2012. The released older-backend TOAs carry measured timing offsets in the `-to` flags. The timing paper documents additional sampling changes, including new Green Bank targets joining regular campaigns in early 2017. It records the Arecibo local-oscillator cuts over MJD 57984–58447. In that interval a coherent fit can have different telescope participation from neighboring epochs even if the primary reduction correctly applied every cut. [Agazie et al., §2, Table 1 and Appendix A.2](https://doi.org/10.3847/2041-8213/acda9a)

A separate Arecibo hydrogen-maser failure occurred on MJD 58482–58483. The primary clock memo records a large drift during the failure, a permanent change in the measured 1 PPS offset and a switch to a rubidium frequency standard. The measured short-term clock behavior before and after the two days was similar. A timing feature near these dates would need to be tested against observations from the affected instrument and the corrections actually applied to them. The clock record alone does not identify its cause. [Nice, Arecibo clock memo, §§1 and 3](https://naic.nrao.edu/arecibo/phil/hardware/clocks/aotime_memo_19Jan13.pdf)

Both NANOGrav 15-year timing and EPTA DR2 use the JPL DE440 Solar System ephemeris. Their published terrestrial-time realizations are TT(BIPM2019) and TT(BIPM2021), respectively. Independent telescopes therefore test observatory-specific errors, while a DE440 error would enter both reductions. A fitted dipole describes the angular dependence of arrival times. Identifying a planet, mass error or ephemeris defect requires a physical model for its time dependence and a comparison with alternative ephemerides. [Larsen et al., §2](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/ad5291) · [NANOGrav noise budget, §§2.5–2.7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16218)
