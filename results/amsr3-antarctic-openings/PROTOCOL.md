# Frozen search for offshore Antarctic sea-ice openings

Can a low-concentration patch in the November 2026 GOSAT-GW record be recovered on consecutive days and in another satellite product? The observing unit is a daily 10 km OSI SAF grid cell. An opening is an eight-connected set of low-concentration cells surrounded by valid compact ice. A connection to the open ocean, a break in the observed ring or a contradiction from a second satellite can defeat the interpretation. The concentration cutoffs define this experiment and follow the general flood-fill approach used for Maud Rise. [Historical method](https://doi.org/10.1029/2022JC019148)

- Screen the complete OSI-408-g AMSR3 Southern Hemisphere grid for 1–17 November 2026 UTC. Freeze the daily products by source URL and SHA-256. Use the same-date OSI-408-a AMSR2 and OSI-401-d SSMIS daily products as comparisons. AMSR2 was staged for 1–16 November. A missing date stays missing in this frozen comparison.
- Use the filtered concentration only where it is finite, between 0 and 100 percent and has nominal status (flag 0) or the open-water flag only (flag 2). The raw field stores estimates edited away by screening. If a flag-2 cell has a raw estimate, use the higher of filtered and raw estimates for enclosure and core tests. Mask flag-2 cells without a raw estimate. Reject every other flag combination. Use land, lake and near-coast flags to exclude cells within 50 grid-projected km of the coast mask.
- Label eight-connected eligible cells with effective concentration below 70%. Keep a component only if it stays offshore, does not touch the grid edge, and every immediate neighboring cell is a valid, offshore cell at or above the enclosure cutoff. A component must contain at least five cells at or below 30%. Record the median and maximum quoted concentration uncertainty among core pixels with available uncertainty. The nominal five-cell projected area is 500 km². This is a grid count with no correction for projection or instrument footprint.
- Link core footprints on consecutive days when at least 30% of the smaller core lies within the other core expanded by one cell in both axes. Keep all branches in the links. A persistent opening must have a chain covering at least three consecutive UTC dates. The primary analysis window is the whole 17-day span.
- Evaluate each primary candidate's fixed core and surrounding ring in AMSR2 and SSMIS without moving its footprint. Require valid coverage of at least 80% in both regions, effective concentration at or below 50% in at least half the valid core, and compact concentrations of at least 70% in at least 80% of the valid ring. Require support from at least one other sensor on two days along the same three-day chain for a cross-sensor candidate. A daily comparison can be unavailable, insufficient, contradictory or supporting.
- Repeat the primary discovery screen with enclosure cutoffs of 60% and 80%, holding the five-cell core and all other gates fixed. Also repeat the 70% screen with a minimum three-cell core. Keep outcomes of each sensitivity separate.
- Before reading the November concentration arrays, run controls with a synthetic sealed six-cell depression, the same depression connected to the edge, a gap in the ring, a false hole formed by screening, and near-coast exclusion. A separately documented 2017 Maud Rise observation is a historical positive control if a digestible public file is available. Record any differences in product, grid and available quality flags before interpreting it.
- If a supported candidate survives, query for an optical or radar view at its footprint and record available observations and their timing. Passive-microwave products in this experiment share processing and can share retrieval errors, and SSMIS platform metadata lists a family without identifying the contributing satellites for each day. A supported microwave candidate needs independent imaging or other evidence for a physical claim. Preserve single-day signals, failed comparisons and threshold sensitivity.
