# Follow-up V: independent Venus dynamics, observability, and an explicit falsification protocol

**New additive analysis, 14 November 2026.** This report is not a modification to [the original occultation investigation](README.md). It challenges PR #21 with a *different* planetary calculation and introduces four telescope positions plus mechanical, receiver and horizon constraints. The predicted event is still future as of this report.

## Independent planetary model and frozen test

The original calculation uses Skyfield/DE440s and Horizons/DE441. Both share JPL numerical planetary dynamics. This follow-up instead calls **Swiss Ephemeris 2.10.03's built-in Moshier semi-analytical planetary theory** via `pyswisseph==2.10.3.2`, forcing `FLG_MOSEPH`. This theory was fitted to the older DE404, so it is independent in computation and approximation, **not independent of all JPL historical observations**. We request J2000 equatorial, suppress stellar aberration and solar deflection, retain planetary light travel time, apply topocentric Earth rotation and subtract the radio timing position in the pulsar tangent plane. The Venus limb uses a physical radius of 6,051.8 km and the returned topocentric range. We solve the uninterrupted 30-minute track rather than joining one-minute points.

At 16:55 UTC, across each of the eight original comparison sites, Moshier differs from the saved Horizons ICRF direction by approximately **0.046 arcseconds**. The script independently computes the predictions first, then reads *all 248 frozen minute rows* from `horizons.csv` to verify a maximum position separation below 0.12 arcseconds. If that check fails, the independent model is not corroborating the original and the report must be revisited. The nominal Moshier contacts agree with the original ones to a few seconds; do not interpret hundredth-second printed model output as contact-time accuracy. The difference between the 2024 and 2023 timing declinations is **9.83 arcseconds**, orders of magnitude larger than the inter-ephemeris discrepancy.

A radio pulsar being behind the solid disk at a **site unable to see Venus** is a valid *geometric* prediction, not an observing opportunity. The 2024 Warkworth chord has a **7.197″ nominal inside-limb margin**; the 2023 Warkworth solution has a **2.610″ nominal miss margin**. The 2024 position's quoted ~2.816″ declination uncertainty, unknown proper motion and possible correlated timing errors mean this is not a calibrated posterior odds ratio or guaranteed disappearance. A nearby contemporaneous pulsed interferometric position is the most useful upstream experiment.

## All competing explanations, checked to their evidential limit

1. **Wrong pulsar timing position / timing systematic**: the 2024 and 2023 MeerKAT solutions share data, differ by 9.83″ in declination and cannot be treated as independent votes. The much older Parkes position has a large uncertainty. A fresh astrometric localization is mandatory to distinguish predicted site paths.
2. **Unknown proper motion and epoch propagation**: all three are zero-proper-motion nominal tracks. The decades-old position especially requires epoch propagation; an unmodeled positional change can shift a grazing chord. The site probabilities are **not** established by marginal RA/Dec error bars alone.
3. **JPL-only planetary model bias**: Moshier independently retains the event classification at tested sites; 0.046″ disagreement cannot explain 9.83″ of pulsar position disagreement. This does not bound future unknown Earth-orientation errors or pulsar errors.
4. **Radius/limb and atmospheric effects**: the reported ingress/egress are solid-disk geometry. Venus's ionosphere and radio refraction, scattering, solar-wind modulation, and solar elongation (~44°) may alter apparent pulse behavior at different frequencies. Atmospheric plasma cannot produce ordinary pulsed flux from directly behind Venus's solid body.
5. **Unobservable configuration**: the Earth-centered and South Pole predictions are *not* guarantees of a working telescope or sufficient sensitivity. Parkes, ATCA, MeerKAT and ASKAP fail site-feasibility tests. Warkworth's 30-m receiver inventory is not shown to cover the ~L-band pulsar flux used for this prediction.
6. **Sensitivity, confusion, pulse dropout, RFI and calibration**: a nondetection in a low-SNR six-minute window is not evidence of an occultation. Request a simultaneous on–off pulse baseline and telescope system/beam calibration. Pre- and post-event positive detections are essential.

## Prospective registered decision tree: exactly what would falsify what

**Before 2027-02-06:** acquire a new `<=0.5″` error radio **pulsed** position propagated to the event epoch, including an explicitly bounded proper-motion uncertainty and RA/Dec covariance. If the source lies more than its full uncertainty beyond 8.985″ from the Moshier Warkworth track, **the nominal Warkworth solid-body occultation prediction is falsified**. If it lies more than its uncertainty inside, the predicted Warkworth **miss** under the 2023 coordinate is falsified. A continuum centroid alone is not proof of the pulsar's position because confusion and shape biases remain.

**During the event:** preregister a site with valid tracking elevation, L-band pulsed backend and baseline pulses detectable in short time bins. Record continuous data from at least 16:40–17:10 UTC, topocentric clocks, two or more radio subbands and adjacent field controls. Under a confirmed solid-body chord, high-SNR pulses **continuing at mid-occultation** would falsify the source association or the geometric model after checking clock synchronization and position. A failure to detect pulses at any time cannot confirm the occultation. Under a confirmed miss, a clean, timed and frequency-consistent full-pulse shutdown would falsify the solid-disk-only miss model, but plasma eclipses or RFI must first be excluded.

**Independent model stability:** if the saved DE441 and Moshier positions depart by >0.12″ across the frozen window, or a new full numerical ephemeris changes a site's inside/outside-limb classification away from positional uncertainties, the claimed ephemeris-independent result fails its frozen test. The test is intentionally numerical, not tuned after inspecting outcomes.

## Replay

From the repo root (the input data are *already* in PR #21, no files overwritten):

```sh
python -m pip install pyswisseph==2.10.3.2
python results/venus-psr-j1813-2113/independent_validation.py > /tmp/venus-moshier.json
```

The command performs all assertions and prints machine-readable contacts, 248 JPL comparisons, and altitude for every site; it exits nonzero on any broken geometric or model-concordance condition. The original frozen `horizons.csv` and `site-predictions.json` are unchanged. Physical observability is not implied by the calculated solid-disk intersection.
