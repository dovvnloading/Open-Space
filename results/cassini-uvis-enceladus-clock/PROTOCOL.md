# Frozen Cassini UVIS timing and spectral replay

Can the same 27 March 2017 Enceladus occultation be placed consistently on the clock of the far-ultraviolet spectrograph and the high-speed photometer? Does mixing archive generations materially change which photometer counts belong to a spectrograph integration? Test the metadata against NAIF clock kernels and the observations against each other. The observing units are individual FUV exposures, HSP integration samples, and predefined groups of FUV wavelength bins. The source and plume were previously studied. [NASA observation account](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/cassini/cassini-significant-events-3221732817/) · [Prior plume model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.02663) · [October 2026 PDS release](https://pds.nasa.gov/datasearch/subscription-service/SS-20261031.shtml)

- Verify full FUV PDS3/PDS4 data identity by independent retrievals and SHA-256. Preserve exact files and both archive labels. Preserve the HSP label and counts separately. Read the label-declared 465 × 64 × 1,024 big-endian FUV cube and 466,032 big-endian HSP samples. Mask FUV value 65,535 and extract spatial rows 30–34. The 2 s FUV and 2 ms HSP record durations come from the deposit. Test the FUV support against its BAND_BIN=1 declaration and the contradictory prose about binning. Keep the published 1 s statement as a separate description.
- Decode SCLK fine components as integer ticks and compare the published start/end references with both the documented PDS4 conversion kernel cas00171 and the final NAIF 2017 archive kernel cas00172. Use the UVIS guide's end-of-first-sample convention. Treat each channel's midpoint as the end minus half its integration. Calculate differences for the paired PDS3 stamps, the migrated FUV with legacy HSP, and two channels converted with the same final kernel. Use both staged 2016 epsilon Orionis pairs as metadata controls, accepting event-dependent results.
- For the timing test, sum all valid counts across the five FUV rows and 1,200–1,800 Å. Use the manufacturer's published native wavelength centers. Repeat with the three adjacent spectral rows centered on the brightest row in the pre-occultation 14:31–14:36 UTC control. Treat the outer rows separately as stellar wings or background monitors. Integrate the exact overlapping HSP sample fractions into each FUV interval. Test an artificial one-bin impulse to verify the endpoint convention. Use the published 14:38:30–14:39:10 UTC stellar normalization. Select frames once on the final-kernel time grid and hold frame membership fixed across time alignments.
- Measure timing consequences in the externally selected 14:39:10–14:40:45 UTC encounter window and in the pre-event 14:31–14:36 control. Compare the median and maximum absolute HSP count changes across the competing alignments. Test fast variations by differencing consecutive normalized frames within eight-second blocks, fitting a single nonnegative factor from HSP to FUV in alternating blocks, and scoring the others. Report squared errors and blockwise win counts even when the result is underpowered. Whole-block sign-flip ranks are descriptive and assume independent, symmetric block differences. A free lag outside these clock choices is exploratory. Two channels share starlight and geometry and weight different wavelengths.
- For a separate spectral check, freeze 14:39:15–14:40:05 UTC from the independently reconstructed stellar ray near Enceladus. Check the laboratory water template within 1,200–1,800 Å, excluding gaps in the deposited calibration. Form blocks of 16 adjacent native bins, require at least 40 baseline counts per block, and fit alternating wavelength blocks under a gray-plus-linear-color model with and without a nonnegative water optical depth. Fit after allowing for the exposure count ratio. Compare held-out Poisson deviances conditional on the measured reference covariance being neglected. Repeat a 50 s control at 14:35:00–14:35:50 UTC. Treat the water temperature, exposure averaging, detector compression, finite reference, and spectral response as limits. A good fit is a repeat check of known wavelength dependence, with no new plume composition or flux inferred.
- Preserve the exact source arrays or a documented lossless subset, archive clocks and assumptions. Report SPICE spherical line-of-sight geometry as an independent approximate map from time to altitude. The clock and comparison findings do not date individual jets without a validated shape, pointing and radiative-transfer model.
