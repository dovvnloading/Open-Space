# WISE J0458+6434 AB: held-out nulls, stationary blends, and a decisive future test

**Follow-up to PR #41 | 2026-11-15 | status: candidate, not confirmed.**

## Result beyond PR #41

The strongest inference is **a probable moving 2–4 GHz component, not yet a demonstrated 144 MHz brown-dwarf emitter**. PR #41 already found the four VLASS fluxes, an apparent proper-motion track, and the independent LoTSS-DR3 positional coincidence. This extension tests three *additional* explanations with a fixed LOFAR-derived sky position, on native frozen VLASS pixels, plus empirical off-source controls.

1. **A single fixed source at the independently measured 144 MHz centroid fails to account for the 2017 and 2025 GHz peaks as well as a moving template.** In a common 19 × 19 pixel patch, the joint moving-plus-fixed fit recovers (moving, fixed) = **(+0.799, −0.095) mJy/beam** in 2017 and **(+0.488, −0.009) mJy/beam** in 2025. Robust control-template scales are ~0.16 mJy for either component in both years. The two endpoint source-site contrasts each exceed the same comparison in all 64 co-translated off-target controls; the combined endpoint diagnostic is **rank 1 of 65**, not a search-wide p-value. The 2023 dual fit is nearly degenerate (moving +0.148, fixed +0.316 mJy/beam), so its source identity is **not** independently resolved.
2. **Two stationary, independently variable GHz sources remain a viable adversarial model.** Lock two Gaussian beams at the *known infrared positions from 2017 and 2025*, without letting either move. The 2023 image then fits +0.350 and +0.302 mJy/beam at these fixed locations, with a slightly *smaller* pixel residual than one beam at the 2023 moving position. These are fit coefficients of highly overlapping beams, **not two detections**. At the endpoint epochs, the model behaves as expected for an alternating-source blend: 2017 (+0.725, +0.041) and 2025 (+0.061, +0.487) mJy/beam. It uses extra epoch-by-epoch flux parameters and is not evidence in favor of two real stationary emitters; critically, the present data do not falsify it.
3. **The LoTSS 144 MHz catalog profile is too broad to attribute solely to the dwarf's motion during the nearby survey pointings.** Its reported deconvolved Gaussian FWHM is **4.712 ± 0.801 arcsec** along PA 69.46 ± 15.41° (east of north). The five LOFAR pointings within 3° span **2022-12-28 to 2023-08-20**. Catalog motion of sqrt(218² + 300²) = **370.8 mas/year** spans only **0.238 arcsec** in that interval: the reported major axis is **19.8 times larger**. The proper-motion PA is **36.0°**, a 33.5° difference from the fitted major-axis direction. The catalog total/peak ratio is 1.291. A blend, background structure, imperfect ionospheric calibration, or mosaicking artefacts are possible. Unknown contributions/weights from more distant pointings prevent a definitive intrinsic-size measurement. Even the full reported 2017–2023 mosaic date range permits only ~2.3 arcsec of proper-motion drift. **This is evidence against the simple argument that an extended 144 MHz source is merely the moving dwarf smeared across nearby visits, not proof of a background galaxy.**

The proper conclusion is therefore **possibly a real radio counterpart at GHz frequencies, but a distinct low-frequency source and/or a radio blend are live alternatives**.

## Native pixel model and results

The existing PR #41 contains four individually archived 91 × 91-pixel, 2–4 GHz VLASS Quick Look images with FITS observing dates and elliptic restoring beams. All fits below independently recompute a beam-template plus flat/linear background on a common *per-image* patch. One source follows the **catalog IR motion**, without optimizing its position on these radio images; the second is fixed to **ILTJ045854.31+643455.8** at (RA, Dec) = (74.726302774°, +64.582182554°). Each epoch has independently adjustable amplitudes. Allowing an unconstrained negative amplitude is intentional, to avoid censoring noise.

The z in the *new* column is (dual moving coefficient − control median) / (1.4826 × control MAD), with 64 identical two-beam fits translated by 17–31 pixels, in six-pixel increments. These are **local robust amplitude diagnostics**, not independently sampled Gaussian significances. The two templates are 1.49, 0.43, 0.49, and 1.44 arcsec apart, respectively. In particular, the near-coincidence of fixed and moving coordinates in 2020/2023 gives weak deblend identifiability. Patch half-widths 7, 9 and 11 pixels leave the 2017 moving coefficient ~0.78–0.80 and the 2025 one ~0.47–0.49 mJy; the fixed source stays near zero there.

The fixed-LOFAR single-template fits by epoch are +0.417, −0.140, +0.455, and +0.241 mJy. The model residual contrasts (SSE_fixed − SSE_moving) are +1.319, +0.012, −0.029, and +0.510 (mJy/beam)² on the common 19-pixel patch. **Do not convert these to a chi-squared likelihood ratio**: adjacent image pixels share the synthesized beam, and the original source was selected from a 1,526-object survey. Both 2017 and 2025 exceed all 64 local co-translated comparisons in their individual residual contrasts; the joint endpoint normalized contrast also exceeds all 64. This is an **image-specific falsification of the one-object fixed-LOFAR fit**, not a global false-alarm calibration.

### A deliberately hard blend null

The independent two-stationary-endpoint model is reported separately because a single stationary background emitter is too easy a strawman. Its two fixed centroids are the IR positions in 2017 and 2025, ~2.93 arcsec apart. Independent epoch amplitudes can produce an **apparent moving centroid** in the intermediate epoch. It has approximately twice the number of free flux terms of the moving-only model. A physically meaningful model comparison requires realistic priors on steady/variable radio source densities, number of independent beams and epoch flux distributions, with interferometric covariances and survey selection propagated. Those ingredients have **not** been measured here. Calling the two fixed-source fit unlikely without them would be overstatement.

## Quantitative interpretation: spectrum, variability, size, power

**Variability:** weighted constant-flux fit to the four published moving-coordinate amplitudes and their empirical single-epoch MAD scales gives a weighted mean **0.432 mJy**, with a **nominal χ² = 22.68 for 3 degrees of freedom**. Dropping 2017 yields χ² = 12.35 for 2 degrees of freedom; dropping the 2020 non-detection leaves χ² = 3.64 for 2 degrees of freedom. These are **diagnostics**, not corrected p-values; the four noise estimates can contain imaging artifacts, calibrations differ and the 2025 selection was a posteriori. A single 2020 non-detection is compatible with auroral burst intermittency *or* multiple variable background sources.

**Spectrum:** IF the LoTSS total flux and each VLASS unresolved peak described the same steady emitter, the implied 144 MHz–3 GHz two-point spectral indices would be **−0.15 (2017), −0.32 (2023), −0.29 (2025)** for S∝ν^α. These are **cross-epoch, cross-beam illustrative ratios, not measured spectral indices**; the source appears variable and LoTSS has extended fitted morphology. Conversely, a stationary steep-spectrum contributor with α = −0.7 and 144 MHz integrated flux 1.167 mJy would yield **~0.139 mJy at 3 GHz**, well within the ~0.16 mJy local static-component deblend scale in 2017/2025. This is a quantitative *blend hypothesis that the current data cannot exclude*.

**Brightness temperature and luminosity:** using the parent catalog parallax π = 108.3 mas gives d ≈ 9.23 pc. If the 2025 +0.487 mJy at 3 GHz belongs to the binary, its isotropic spectral luminosity is **Lν ≈ 5.0×10¹³ erg s⁻¹ Hz⁻¹** and νLν ≈ **1.5×10²³ erg s⁻¹**. A **uniform disk of radius 1 Jupiter radius**, viewed at 9.23 pc, would have brightness temperature **~8.9×10⁹ K** at 3 GHz; scaling the entire +0.903 mJy peak at 144 MHz to the *same* dwarf-sized area yields **~7.2×10¹² K**, favoring coherent emission under that strong assumption. These are conditional and **do not demonstrate a source size or ECM**. If the LoTSS catalog's 4.712″ deconvolved major FWHM is truly intrinsic and at the dwarf distance, it represents **~43.5 AU**, not a compact Jupiter-radius aurora. Its 6″-beam diluted brightness is only order 10³ K. A genuinely extended 144 MHz background component is therefore a concrete competing possibility.

For fundamental cyclotron maser emission, ν_c ≈ 2.8 MHz per gauss would associate 144 MHz with **~51 G** and 3 GHz with **~1.07 kG** at their respective emission sites. Neither field measurement is warranted without a polarized spectral cutoff and common-source evidence. For a useful confirmed comparison, the T5.5+T7 binary **WISEP J101905.63+652954.2** exhibits **strong, rotationally modulated circularly polarized 144 MHz pulses**, with an approximately 3-hour modulation; long-term LOFAR observations also detected phase-averaged Stokes V. The recent cutoff analysis estimates a polar field of about **126 G** for that *different* binary. This candidate has **none of those decisive polarization/time-resolved diagnostics yet**.

Benchmarks: [Vedantham et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202244965); [Yiu et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202554844); [methane-dwarf spectral cutoff study, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202558234). WISE J0458+6434 AB has a published ~43-year orbit and a projected ~0.5″ infrared component separation, so different component positions and optical/IR photocentres can drift at a few-tenths-of-an-arcsecond level; parallax is likewise ~0.108″. Neither correction can mimic a multi-arcsecond track, but both limit component identification. [Binary orbit analysis](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/ab3393).

## Which specific observation would settle it?

**Most discriminating near-term experiment: a deep, full-Stokes, phase-referenced VLA A-configuration S-band (2–4 GHz) campaign centered on 2028 September 6, with a same-setup repeat and field astrometric calibrators, ideally paired with LOFAR high-resolution 120–168 MHz full-Stokes imaging.** Sample several hours per visit in short time bins to capture burst intermittency. Aim for **≤15 μJy/beam Stokes-I rms**, accurate in-field differential astrometry ~0.05–0.1″, and angular resolution **≤0.8″**; verify capability in proposal planning. Dates separated by a year or more distinguish slow motion and duty-cycle effects. Reprocess the suspect 2017 visibilities for a clean independent check.

Propagate the parent fixed catalog coordinate and proper motion to **2028-09-06**, three years after the last VLASS date (matching season roughly suppresses differential annual-parallax shift). The approximate binary-track prediction is:

- **RA 04h 58m 54.5397s, Dec +64° 34′ 57.954″ (J2000/ICRS)**, or (74.72724859°, +64.58276496°), **before parallax and orbital offset corrections**.
- The stationary LOFAR DR3 source remains at about **RA 04h 58m 54.31s, Dec +64° 34′ 55.86″**, **~2.56″** southwest of that predicted moving location by 2028.
- A 2028 radio detection at the propagated binary position, compared with 2025 and earlier epochs and *stationary in-field reference sources*, would make the one-stationary-source hypothesis untenable. Repeating the measurement reduces the probability of an alternating blend. An emitter stuck at the LOFAR location or at an earlier fixed spot while the dwarf moves away would falsify the moving GHz interpretation.
- A nondetection at a single visit alone does **not** falsify a flaring dwarf. Observe over hours and repeat. **Circularly polarized, periodic, moving GHz or 144 MHz pulses** would be much more convincing evidence of a dwarf aurora. **Steady, extended, steep-spectrum emission fixed in position** would support a background galaxy.
- To establish that the **144 MHz source specifically** belongs to the binary, obtain ≥2 well-separated LOFAR long-baseline, high-resolution, time-resolved full-Stokes epochs with absolute/in-field registration sufficient to track **~0.37″ per year**. If 144 MHz stays at the 2023 catalog position while GHz continues with the dwarf, the *single common emitter* interpretation is falsified even if the dwarf emits at GHz.
- To identify component **A vs B**, use a high-frequency VLA A-array/VLBI detection with angular resolution significantly finer than the **~0.5″** binary separation and an IR orbital solution. A system association alone cannot say either component or both radiate.

**Decision status:** strong *interesting candidate* under a moving-GHz model; **not yet a secure brown-dwarf radio detection**, **not an established 144 MHz connection**, **no defensible polarization or magnetic-field detection**, and **two-variable-stationary-source blends and 2017 Quick Look artifacts remain open**.

## Reproduce and review

The only repository changes in this PR are **four new files in this directory plus one new CI regression test in `tests/`**. No file introduced before this PR is modified; PR #41's sources, computations, and other science projects are untouched. After PR #41 is merged (or with this PR stacked on PR #41), run:

~~~sh
python results/wise-j0458-heldout/recheck.py
~~~

This independently reads the frozen four source FITS images (NumPy/Astropy only), LOFAR catalog row and five local pointing dates in the pre-existing parent folder, recalculates all patches and matched off-source controls, and writes a full-precision local **measurements.json**. The new regression test `tests/test_wise_j0458_heldout.py` executes the analysis in CI with temporary output and checks source coefficients, null ranks, morphology and the 2028 astrometric prediction. The checked-in [native-pixel-tests.csv](native-pixel-tests.csv) reports independently recomputed rounded results. The [protocol](protocol.md) records the competing hypotheses and pre-specified falsifiers. No new full-sky false-alarm probability, calibrated visibilities, or archival non-VLASS data reduction is claimed.
